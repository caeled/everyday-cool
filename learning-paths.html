LEARNING PATHS / A SUGGESTED ROUTE

Three ways in.
One way of thinking.

Portable Workshops for Curious Minds

Every workshop stands on its own, and the front page still says start anywhere. These paths simply suggest an order: gentle first steps, then deeper benches, then the workshops that ask the most and give the most back. Every stop teaches how a system works.

Levels are a route, not a gate. Skip around freely — curiosity does not need permission.

PATH 01 / BEGINNER

Look closely, try small things

New, young, or just warming up.
No setup, no accounts, no wrong answers.

Decisions Everywhere goes first: it teaches the observe, question, test, decide, review approach that every other workshop quietly uses, and it asks for no prerequisites at all. Beginner workshops ask you to observe and experiment: compare two places, throw a ball and watch its arc, turn classroom math loose in the wild. The reference shelf belongs at every level, so it starts here — keep it open alongside everything else. When you want a gentle next step, colour a page, mix a lamp, play a cabinet in Flynn's Arcade, or open The STEM Room and pick a workshop.

Everyday Light

The fastest thing there is — slowed down, split, and mixed two ways.

Split sunlight the way a prism does and mix colour the two opposite ways — lamps adding toward white, pigments subtracting toward a near-black that leaks (which is why printers carry black) — then strike colour chords on a twelve-hue wheel (complements straight across at 180°, triads at 120° steps: the circle of fifths' twin), bend light through a thin lens and watch the image land upside down, read the blue sky and the red sunset off the single 1/λ⁴ scattering rule, walk a stick's shadow around a compass through the day, and slide the spectrum past the ends of sight into infrared and ultraviolet, which are labelled, never painted.

Start here Turn all three lamps up and make white — then mix all three paints at full and watch white disappear into a warm near-black.

PATH 04 / THE MACHINE PATH

Learn computers by meeting them

Eleven real machines, in teaching order.
Bring your own software — the Box ships the machines, never the games.

The consoles live in Nerd Heaven and travel together as the Emulator Box. This path walks them in the order that teaches: begin with a bare processor and meet every idea — memory, cartridges, mappers, BIOS, emulation itself — as the machines add it.
