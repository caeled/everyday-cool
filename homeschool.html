HOW FAMILIES USE IT

One workshop, one sitting

Each workshop card names a “Start here” activity — that is a complete short session on its own. Follow the collection’s rhythm: Ask → Try → Notice → Understand. Ask the question together, let your learner drive the activity, ask what they noticed, then read the explanation together.

The learning paths turn the collection into a gentle sequence. Beginner workshops suit younger learners and first encounters; intermediate and advanced workshops reward older kids — and give younger siblings something to grow into, or an older sibling something to teach. Teaching it is the deepest way to learn it, and every workshop is really about the same thing: how a system works.

Keep a simple notebook beside the computer: the question, what we changed, what happened, what surprised us. It becomes a portfolio of real thinking, and on hard days it is proof of how far curiosity has carried you.

PRACTICAL NOTES

Good to know

  • Offline is the default. The eight bundled workshops include their core activities in the download. Videos, the external destinations on the A Library shelf, and live weather need internet when you want them.
  • No accounts, no tracking. Activities process locally in the browser. Examples are fictional; nothing asks for your child’s personal information.
  • Yours to adapt. Hub code is MIT and original lessons are CC BY 4.0. Print a worksheet around an activity, reword an explanation for your learner, or skip ahead — keep credit, and note your changes if you share your version onward.
  • An honest boundary. Everyday Cool is a growing educational collection, not an accredited school. How it counts toward your local homeschool requirements is yours to confirm with your district or state.
  • A few features want a secure context. Some cryptography features require HTTPS or localhost; the collection’s Serve.cmd helper serves the folder on a home computer using an existing Python 3 installation.
