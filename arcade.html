WHAT'S INSIDE

Twenty games, five rooms, one table

The whole Game Table,
packed to carry.

The Arcade holds the twenty games of the collection's Game Table, gathered from the rooms that built them:

EVERYDAY PHYSICS

The main table

Orbital rendezvous and gravity golf, a two-stage rocket and a ballast-tank submarine, lunar landers, bridge building, falling sand, soft-body jelly, air hockey, a pool table, and more — the physics is real enough that a scripted pilot had to prove each Muse-built game could be won before it shipped.

LIGHT · SOUND · ENGINEERING

The guest seats

Laser & Mirrors from Everyday Light, where every level is provably solvable by the game's own beam tracer. Tune the String from Everyday Sound, tuned by ear against real beating tones. Shake Table from Everyday Engineering, where a tower meets its resonant frequency — and the tuned mass damper is genuinely the only way out.

THE GAMES ROOM

Tensor-Tris

The Games Room's falling-block tensor puzzle takes the twentieth seat, so the Arcade is the whole collection's play in one place — not just the physics wing.

And the lessons ride along. Every one of the twenty games carries its full back-door lesson inside the package. A lesson is a page of its own: the physics in plain language, the math with its formulas taken apart, worked examples computed from the game's own numbers, and a builder's account of the coding choices. In the games built for the collection, the lesson also opens as a pop-up while you play — the explanation is on the table at the exact moment someone asks why.

FOR TEACHERS

Easy to try. Easy to justify.

  • Try it yourself first. The games are just legitimately fun to play — Gravity Golf will kick your butt, and that is the point. Five minutes at the table tells you more than any brochure.
  • Experimental by design. Play comes first; the back door is there when the questions start. A student who asks why the pendulum swings faster when you reel in can open the lesson and find the formula, a worked example, and the same numbers the game is using.
  • The receipts are in the room. The Muse-built games were published only after a scripted controller proved them winnable against the shipped code, and the lessons say plainly where a game simplifies the real physics. Nothing here asks to be taken on faith.
  • No accounts, no ads, no cost. The Arcade is free, installs once, and plays offline — the games and lessons are self-contained pages that answer to nobody's server.

Honest notes before you install

The installer is unsigned — it was built by an independent curator, not a software company. Where Windows Smart App Control is enforced, it may block unsigned installers outright, with no “run anyway” offered; turning Smart App Control off is a one-way door, so don't do it for our sake. The same games play free in your browser either way, and nothing about the Arcade phones home. The Arcade is free.
