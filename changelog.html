THE COLLECTION / RECEIPTS

What changed, and when

Every dated addition.
Newest first.

  • 2026-11-26Join our Forum — Steven opened the Everyday Cool forum — a Google Group where anyone may ask to join and members post: questions, finds, and talk about the collection. The link now rides the hub footer and the collection's main doors.
  • 2026-11-26Pass it on — share links — Plain sharer links (Facebook, X, Reddit, Flipboard, Bluesky) on the hub, the STEM Room, and Everyday Physics. No trackers and no loaded scripts — each link opens the service’s own share box.
  • 2026-11-26The Machine Path — The eleven Emulator Box consoles became Path 04 on the Learning Paths page (eleven stops in teaching order, each with its lesson), the hub chooser became Four Ways In, and the STEM Room gained a visiting Emulator Box card beside Everyday Computing
  • 2026-11-25Everyday Money — Meta AI’s missing system, built: interest running against you and for you, a paycheck split that keeps its own sum, and four messages to judge. Its audit recomputed both loops to the dollar.
  • 2026-11-25Everyday Home — Meta AI’s house-as-a-system, built: a breaker that trips honestly at 2,700 W of heater and microwave, four leaks with one right first move each, and triage checklists that know when to say call. Its audit caught and fixed a tripped-breaker deadlock before shipping.
  • 2026-11-25How to Make a Workshop — Meta AI’s proposed meta workshop, built from the collection’s own practice: the five moves, the plumbing, the real wiring checklist, and a contribution door that asks only for an idea.
  • 2026-11-25The X-Ray Time Machine — A flight recorder for little games, designed by ChatGPT and built by Muse: record a run, rewind it, branch it, verify it. Both recorded demos finish the job.
  • 2026-11-25Shardstorm gets its flight recorder — The Time Machine’s Phase 2 begins: Steven’s chord-split Asteroids now runs on a fixed tick with a seeded field, and the Recoil Experiment runs on the game that asked the question.
  • 2026-11-25Is the Earth Flat? — Five flat-earth proofs, steelmanned and put on the bench, ending with the 24-hour sun at Union Glacier.
  • 2026-11-25Calculus Without Fear — Grok’s build: limits, derivatives and integrals on one liftoff curve, every number checked.
  • 2026-11-25Hear the Hydrogen Line — Grok’s build: sweep a pretend dish, watch the 1420 MHz peak slide with Doppler shift — then hear the real line through PICTOR, the free public radio telescope.
  • 2026-11-25DipoleLab — Antigravity’s antenna bench — the science division’s first card in the Radio Room.
  • 2026-11-25SpacetimeLab — Gravitational waves on the bench: chirps, ringdowns, and a Kerr wake that flips with spin.
  • 2026-11-25LuxLab grows — The optics bench’s second half: eleven chapters, with the Arago spot’s honest caveat printed on its face.
  • 2026-11-25Skip closes its deltas — The web Skip gains the Python original’s seeded station order and its fixed-distance practice target; its proof table grows to 34 rows.
  • 2026-11-24The Robot Room — Nine audited robotics workshops join as catalog 37 — motors, control, sensors, and one wall-follower that fails its island on purpose, in print.
  • 2026-11-24The Baton Box — Catalog 38 is plumbing, not a workshop: a relay server that knows no games and just carries the baton.
  • 2026-11-24The Relay — The Baton Box’s first passenger. You don’t have to finish it. You have to set up the next person.
  • 2026-11-24Morse — Steven’s Python Morse sender, ported line for line: PARIS timing, Farnsworth spacing, and a WAV recipe with no clicks.
  • 2026-11-23Skip — Steven’s own skywave game, written in Python in the Bordeom workshop and ported to the web: bounce shortwave off the sky and work the country. The one that started the chain.

Maintained by hand in the hub generator, one line per integration — the same discipline as the rooms’ own COLLECTION.md notes.
