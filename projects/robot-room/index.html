The Robot Room

Nine workshops on how a robot actually works: the motor that moves it, the sensors that inform it, the control loop that corrects it, the arms, eyes, chips, code, and message-passing that make it more than a toy. Every workshop here was audited the collection's way — driven headlessly, its numbers measured against its own claims — and every lesson prints what passed, what bends, and what was false. Three builders share this room: Qwen (Kimi) designed the founding bundle, Antigravity delivered the sensors arena, and Muse built the rest and repaired what needed repairing, each under Steven Powell's direction and sanction.

Everyday Motors

Qwen (Kimi) · repaired by Muse under sanction

One small brushed DC motor on a bench. As shipped, its dials implied a motor 428% efficient; the repair rebuilt all three constants from one real Mabuchi RF-370CA datasheet until Kt = Ke exactly — worst-case efficiency now an honest 60.77%.

Everyday Sensors

Google Antigravity · repaired by Muse under sanction

A rover arena of sonar, lidar, IR, encoders, and an IMU. The Kalman position fusion is genuine — 12.1 px RMS against odometry's 30.6 — and the heading fusion was repaired to trust its own compass instead of quietly reading the truth.

Everyday Control

Qwen (Kimi) design · completed by Muse

A line-following robot and the PID loop that steers it. Qwen's copy arrived with no simulation at all; Muse built the missing engine, then measured her presets — two labels were false until the gains were re-tuned to make them true.

Help ▾ — how this room works

What this is. A room of nine self-contained workshops for beginners and hobbyists starting with robots. Each workshop runs entirely in the page — no account, no installation, no network — and each carries an in-page Help section and a companion lesson (the 📖 links above) that shows its work: the formulas, the measured numbers, and the honest limits.

How the room was audited. Before any workshop joined the room, its shipped code was driven headlessly and its claims measured: motor constants against a real datasheet, PID presets against their own labels, a Kalman filter against ground truth it was not allowed to peek at. Where a claim was false, the lesson says so. Where Steven sanctioned a repair, the repair is listed in the lesson and the audit record, with before-and-after hashes.

Who built what. Qwen (the Qwen AI Studio agent team, whose model Steven calls Kimi) proposed and designed the founding bundle of nine; it delivered Motors (repaired under sanction), the design and copy of Control (completed by Muse), and could not deliver Sensors, which Antigravity built instead (repaired under sanction). The other six workshops are Muse originals. Provenance for every file is in COLLECTION.md and the room's provenance map.

Start anywhere. Motors and Sensors need nothing before them. Control assumes the idea of a loop, which its own first benches teach. Python Robotics and Simulation share one motion model, so either order works. ROS 2 lands best after any one of the others.
