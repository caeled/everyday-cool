# Collection origin

The Robot Room, added 24 November 2026 at Steven Powell's direction. The room grew out of the collection's Java Young Robot Builders room and out of Steven's audition, that morning, of the Qwen AI Studio agent team (create.qwen.com; the model Steven calls Kimi) on the topic "Expanded Robotics Resources." Qwen's PMO proposed a bundle of nine workshops plus a landing page for beginners and hobbyists just starting with robots. Steven chose the full bundle. What each builder actually delivered was then measured by audit, and the room was completed by reassignment and repair under his sanctions, as recorded below and in each lesson's provenance section. This folder is the maintained original; guest deliveries are preserved as received in Steven's own files, outside the collection.

**Everyday Motors** — `everyday-motors.html`, lesson `everyday-motors-lesson.html`. Qwen's delivery arrived with a fatal defect: Bench 4's `evaluate()` was truncated mid-function, a SyntaxError that killed the whole page, and its three motor constants (900 RPM/V, 150 g·cm/V, 150 mA/V) were un-derived — measured efficiency reached 428%, falsifying the page's own framing sentence. Steven sanctioned an in-house repair (24 November 2026): `evaluate()` reconstructed from the surviving helper code and the page's own hint texts, and all three constants re-derived from one real motor, the Mabuchi RF-370CA-15370, so that Kt = Ke (1.0000); worst-case efficiency after repair is 60.77%. The repaired file was placed in the room byte-identical to the repair record (sha256 ddb664e3…9199). The audit and its repair addendum are the record; the lesson prints the as-shipped numbers, the datasheet anchors, and the bends that remain.

**Everyday Control** — `everyday-control.html`, lesson `everyday-control-lesson.html`. Qwen's delivery was truncated harder: the file contained no script at all — no simulation, no Bench 6, no closing. Its copy and pedagogy audited as sound. At Steven's direction ("use what it had and fix the bugs in that one or scrap at start over"), Muse preserved Qwen's copy and design and built everything missing: the fixed-step 20 Hz line-follower, the discrete PID (derivative on measurement, stated anti-windup), Bench 6, and the closing. Her preset gains were then measured in the finished simulation; two labels were false as shipped (the PI preset never settled at Ki = 0.5; the "Over-damped" gains were the fastest on the bench) and were re-tuned so the labels tell the truth, with the before/after gains recorded in the lesson. Eight documented copy corrections (loop rate, PWM frequency, sibling filenames) are likewise listed there.

**Everyday Sensors** — `everyday-sensors.html`, lesson `everyday-sensors-lesson.html`. Qwen could not deliver this one at all; Steven routed it to Google Antigravity, which delivered a rover arena (sonar, lidar, stereo, GPS, a 5-element IR array, bump, encoder odometry, IMU, Kalman fusion) that passed liveness with zero script errors and whose cores measured real: sonar geometry exact, lidar a true 360°, Kalman position fusion genuinely beating odometry (12.1 vs 30.6 px RMS). Two claims were false: the "PID" line follower is proportional-only, and the Kalman heading update corrected from ground truth (`rover.theta`) rather than the page's own magnetometer. Steven sanctioned four in-house fixes (24 November 2026): the PID labels corrected to P-control; the heading update rewired to consume the page's own compass heading, after which the fused heading honestly absorbs compass error near magnets (settling ≈67° off in the audit's magnet case, where the truth-fed version showed 0.00°); the "95%" covariance ellipse redrawn at 2.45σ; the stereo fan widened to its labelled 65°. Total repair diff: six hunks. The audit and its repair addendum are the record.

**The six Muse builds** — `everyday-kinematics.html`, `everyday-vision.html`, `everyday-embedded-c.html`, `everyday-python-robotics.html`, `everyday-ros2.html`, `everyday-simulation.html`, each with its `-lesson.html`. Built by Muse at Steven's direction to complete the nine when Qwen's delivery failed, each self-audited by driving its own shipped code headlessly before integration: Kinematics (exact-trig FK/IK, unreachable targets clamp with the shortfall printed; proofs 7/7), Vision (a real threshold/blob/centroid pipeline; proofs 12/12, including the named RGB-under-shadow collapse), Embedded C (a simplified ATmega328P register model with datasheet-formula PWM; 41 independent checks, 0 failed), Python Robotics (a real tiny interpreter for a beginner Python subset, stated on the page as not CPython, with its 2,000-statement watchdog printed; proofs 11/11), ROS 2 (a discrete pub/sub model on a fixed clock with seeded QoS drops; proofs 7/7), Simulation (raycast sensors, exact arc integration, odometry drift measured equal to predicted at 0.3586 m; its wall-follower's two solves and one honest failure printed on the bench's face; proofs 9/9).

**Room furniture** — `index.html` (the front page: nine cards at the top, a full card for Java Young Robot Builders carrying Steven's framing "where this room came from — the code before the wheels," a card for Satellite Watch, and the Help documentation), `README.md`, this file, `LICENSE` and `LICENSE-CONTENT.md` (the collection-standard license files, byte-identical to the other rooms'), and `robot-room-banner.png` (the collection hub's robot art, reused as the room banner). The Java room's front page gained the return door to this room in the same pass; the Satellite Watch curator's note — a communications satellite as a remote robot — is the sentence this room teaches.
