What you are looking at

A little world made of cubes — voxels — on a 32 × 32 plot of grass, up to 16 blocks high. Pick a block, click the world to place it, and build anything: a house, a tower, a castle, your name in giant letters. Then switch to Explore and fly through what you made. Nothing uploads anywhere; the world lives in this browser, on this machine.

This bench is a tribute to the block-building game Flynn and David love. It is not that game, and it uses none of its pictures — every block here is a flat colour drawn by this page's own code. There is no digging for treasure, no crafting, and no monsters. Building is the game.

Build mode — placing blocks

  • Choose a block from the palette — Grass, Dirt, Stone, Wood, Planks, Leaves, Brick, Glass, Sand, or the bright Glow Block.
  • Click a block's face to place the chosen block against that face. Click the ground to start a wall; click a wall to grow it.
  • Drag to swing the camera around your plot. Scroll (or pinch) to zoom. Shift-drag or right-drag to slide the view sideways.
  • Break tool: pick ⛏ Break, then click a block to remove it — or right-click any block to break it on the spot. On a touchscreen, tap to place and use the Break tool to remove.
  • The white outline shows the block your pointer is touching; the amber outline shows where the new block will land.
  • Rules the world keeps: nothing below the ground floor, nothing above 16 high, and one block per cube — a click only fills empty space.

Explore mode — flying through it

  • Drag to look around. W A S D or the arrow keys fly you forward, back, and sideways.
  • Space rises, C sinks. Hold Shift to fly faster. Scroll zooms you forward and back.
  • Exploring is flying, not walking: there is no gravity, no falling, and no bumping — you can pass through blocks like a ghost. That is stated plainly because the real game would stop you; this one lets you fly inside your own walls to see how you built them.

Keeping your world

  • Autosave: every change is saved to this browser's storage box (localStorage) and your world is where you left it when you come back. The box belongs to this browser on this device — clearing the browser's site data empties it.
  • Share Code: packs the whole world into one line of text. Copy it, send it to someone, and they can paste it into Import Code on their machine and stand in your world. Importing replaces the world on screen — autosave first if it matters.
  • Export a Page: downloads a real .html file with your world baked inside it. Open it anywhere and fly through your build — no internet, no account. The file carries the same renderer this page uses, so the world in the file is the world you built.
  • Start Over wipes the plot back to flat grass. It asks first.

How it works, in one breath

The world is a box of 32 × 32 × 16 cubes, and each cube holds one number: 0 for air, or the number of its block. Everything else is that box of numbers wearing different clothes — the picture on screen is the numbers drawn as cubes (skipping every face that touches another block, because nobody can see those), the share code is the numbers squeezed into text, and the exported page is the numbers with a window wrapped around them. The lesson shows the receipts, including the face-count arithmetic. The Glow Block, honestly: it is bright paint, not a lamp — this page's light is baked into the cube faces, so the Glow Block glows by being painted brightly, not by lighting its neighbours.
