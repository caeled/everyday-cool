Transformer Attention Lab — the lesson

A transformer reads a sentence by letting every word look at every other word and decide how much each one matters. That looking is attention: each token asks a question (its query), advertises what it offers (its key), and passes along what it means (its value). Match the questions to the keys, turn the matches into weights that sum to one, and blend the values by those weights — that is the whole mechanism, and this lab lets you watch it resolve a pronoun in the classic Winograd pair: "The animal didn't cross the street because it was too tired" versus "…too wide." This page is the other half of the lab: the concept and the math, and the audit that recomputed the shipped attention matrices independently — and found the arithmetic exact, and one of the page's labels false.

Verdict: The attention arithmetic is exact — within 2.2×10⁻¹⁶ of an independent NumPy recomputation, and the Winograd demos are genuine ("tired" sends it → animal at 40.4%) — but the "Concat" head is FALSE: it is an elementwise mean with no concatenation and no W_O, and its displayed scores do not softmax to its displayed weights.

The concept

Queries, keys, values

Each token is first turned into a vector — its embedding, with a positional encoding added so the model knows where in the sequence it sits. From that vector come three roles. The query (Q) is what this token is looking for. The key (K) is what each token advertises. The value (V) is the content a token hands over if it is attended to. A pronoun like "it" carries a query that, in a well-trained model, matches the key of its antecedent — "animal" in one sentence, "street" in the other, with only one word changed. That flip is the Winograd test, and the lab's presets drive it live.

Weights that sum to one

Score every query against every key, scale the scores down so the softmax that follows stays well-behaved, and softmax each row: the result is a set of attention weights, one distribution per token, each summing to exactly 1. Multiply those weights by the values and each token walks away with a context vector — a blend of the whole sentence, weighted by relevance. A causal mask zeroes the upper triangle before the softmax, so a token in a GPT-style model can only look backwards; remove it and, BERT-style, every token sees the full sequence. Run several heads in parallel and a real transformer concatenates their outputs and mixes them through an output projection, WO. Hold that sentence — the audit comes back to it.

Sampling: what comes next

The lab's second studio turns a distribution over next tokens into a choice. Temperature reshapes the distribution before sampling: low temperature sharpens it toward the single most likely token, high temperature flattens it. Top-k keeps only the k most likely candidates; top-p (nucleus sampling) keeps the smallest set whose probabilities add up to p. The order those two filters are applied in matters — another sentence the audit comes back to.

The concept/math

Scaled dot-product attention

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( QKᵀ / √dk ) · V Each row of the softmax is one token's attention distribution. In the lab dk = 8, so scores are divided by √8. The audit recomputed this formula independently in NumPy from the shipped Q, K and V — see the receipts below for how close the two computations land.

Positional encoding — the standard, and the lab's variant

PE(pos, 2i) = sin( pos / 100002i/d )  ·  PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos( pos / 100002i/d ) That is the Vaswani et al. formula. The lab's encoding is genuinely multi-frequency sin/cos and does inject order, but it is a variant: it uses base 100 instead of 10000 and multiplies every angle by an extra 1.35. Measured PE(1) in the lab is [0.976, 0.219, 0.414, 0.910, 0.135, 0.991, 0.043, 0.999] against the standard's [0.841, 0.540, 0.0998, 0.995, 0.0100, 1.000, 0.001, 1.000]. The encoding is also attenuated ×0.12 and added to only 6 of the 8 embedding dims; dims 7–8 carry separate cos/sin(pos·0.55) position features.

Entropy: how spread out is a row?

H = − Σ p · log₂ p  (bits) A row attending to one token only has H = 0; a row spread evenly has high entropy. The lab's entropy HUD is honest: the "it" row reads 2.56 bits, and the audit recomputed 2.5555 bits. Drag the sharpness slider and the entropy moves exactly as the label promises — at 2.40× on head 1, it → animal rises to 69.8% and the row entropy falls to 1.05 bits; at 0.35×, it → animal falls to 17.7% and entropy rises to 3.36 bits.

The sampling studio's math holds too. Temperature softmax, top-k and entropy were recomputed independently at six (T, k, p) settings: base probabilities match to ≤ 1.1×10⁻¹⁶, sample probabilities sum to 1, and entropies match to all printed digits (at the T = 0.85 default, H = 1.7307 bits, nucleus 4). The page's own note that low temperature sharpens onto the greedy argmax is literally true in the code: at T = 0.1 the top candidate takes probability 1.0 and the nucleus collapses to 1 token; at T = 2.5 the top probability flattens to 0.283 and the nucleus grows 4 → 5. Driven end-to-end, Sample appends a drawn token, Auto-Gen appends exactly 4, a candidate-bar click force-appends, and the 18-token cap refuses with a banner — the tokenizer caps sequences at 18 tokens, matching the cap.

FALSE — the "Concat" head is not a concatenation

The fifth head is tagged "Concat." It does not concatenate anything. Verified exactly (difference 0.0): the blended head's weights are the elementwise mean of the four heads' weight matrices, and its context vector is the mean of the four context vectors. There is no concatenation and no output projection WO anywhere in the file.

Worse, the blend disagrees with itself. The blended head's displayed scaled scores — mean raw dots / √8 × sharpness — do not softmax to its displayed weights: the maximum discrepancy is 0.0775 across presets. In "All Heads" mode, the Live Scaled Dot-Product Trace shows numbers whose softmax is not the α the page displays beside them.

The W1…W4 framing overclaims the mechanism. The heads are tagged W1…W4 and the notes say each token "projects into" Q, K and V. For Q and K that is not what the code does. They are hand-written rule tables keyed on the token's identity: head 1's query for "it" is a constant vector selected by whether the sentence contains "tired" or "wide"; pronoun keys are suppressed by hard-coded factors (×0.15, ×−0.35); code tokens get literal constants (2.1 / −0.4). Only V is a genuine linear map of the embedding, and it is diagonal. There are no learned weights. The attention arithmetic downstream of those tables is exact — but the tables are rules, not projections, and the page does not say so.

What bends

Top-p is computed after top-k, on the renormalized survivors: standard nucleus sampling takes p-mass from the full distribution; here the cumulation runs over the top-k renormalized probabilities, so with the default k = 6 the "0.88 nucleus" is 88% of six candidates, never of all ten. Internally consistent, nonstandard order.

The sampler's logits do not come from the attention model: next-token logits are a hand-written table of 14 regex continuation rules plus fallbacks. The attention state and the "Autoregressive Decoding" studio are two separate machines sharing a page; nothing in the transformer computation feeds the sampler.

Full-width heads: each of the four heads runs at full width dk = 8. In a real 4-head model with dmodel = 8, each head would have dk = 2 and the four outputs would be concatenated. The page's dk = 8 label is self-consistent, but the architecture drawn is four parallel full-width attentions averaged — not multi-head attention as the term is used.

Positional encoding is a variant: base 100 instead of 10000, every angle multiplied by an extra 1.35, attenuated ×0.12 and added to only 6 of 8 dims — measured numbers in the formula box above. Embeddings themselves are a hand lexicon for known words plus a deterministic hash (FNV + sin) for unknown words.

Stale first-paint placeholders: the static HTML states "Q:'it' → K:'animal' (68%)" where the solver says 40.4%, "Attn Entropy 1.62 bits" where the solver says 2.56, and a sampling "H = 1.94b" where the solver says 1.73b. All three are replaced the moment the script runs — but a no-JS render states three wrong numbers.

One clean negative, for the record: the page makes no parameter-count and no complexity (O(n²)) claims — grep-verified. There was nothing there to check, and nothing was invented to check it against.
