What you are looking at

A real Atari 2600, emulated in this page by Javatari — the open-source emulator written by Paulo Peccin, embedded here unmodified, exactly as upstream ships it. This page is only the cabinet around it: the collection ships the console and an empty cartridge slot. There are no cartridges on this page, none to download, and none hiding anywhere in Everyday Cool.

Bring your own cartridges — the slot

  • Drag & drop: drag a cartridge file (.bin, .a26, .rom, or a .zip/.gz containing one) from a folder and drop it on the screen. The console powers on and the game starts.
  • Open File: click the console's power button (bottom-left of the screen bar) and choose Open File, then pick your cartridge.
  • Where the file lives does not matter: a folder on this machine, a USB stick, or a mapped network drive — a mounted share is just a folder, and the file picker can browse it like any other.
  • Your cartridges never leave your machine. The emulator reads the file locally, in this browser. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is sent anywhere, and this page itself makes no network requests of its own — the audit on the lesson page counted them: zero. The one exception is the public-shelf block under the console, and only when you use it: looking up a shelf link talks to the Internet Archive, and nowhere else.

Playing

  • Player 1 joystick: the arrow keys steer. The fire button and every other key can be seen — and remapped — in the emulator's own Settings (the gear menu at the bottom of the screen), which is also where gamepads and touch controls are configured. Real gamepads work if your browser can see them.
  • Player 2 has its own key set, also listed in Settings — two players can share one keyboard, the way two kids once shared one television.
  • Console switches: the panel under the screen is the real console panel — Power, Game Select, Game Reset, difficulty switches, black & white — and every switch on it is clickable. Keyboard shortcuts exist too (Power is F1, Select F11, Reset F12); the full list lives in Settings.
  • Pause, speed, full screen, CRT effects: all in the screen's bottom bar and menus. They are the emulator's own controls, documented by its own help.

Save states — freeze a moment

  • Open the power-button menu and choose Save State to store the exact moment — the whole machine, frozen — in one of ten slots in this browser (Ctrl+1…0 saves a slot, Alt+1…0 loads it back).
  • In the same dialog, File… exports the moment as a small .jst file you keep. Another player loads it with Load State → File…, or by dropping the .jst file on the screen.
  • That file is the challenge baton: freeze a brutal wave, hand the file to the next player — by email, USB stick, or a shared folder — and their turn is to survive it. States travel as files, not as copy-and-paste codes; the lesson page says plainly what that means for the Baton Box.

One honest note

Javatari also has a NetPlay feature for playing over the internet. It is the emulator's own, it only wakes up if you open its dialog and host or join a session, and it talks to Javatari's session service when you do. Nothing on this page starts it for you, and this console is happiest treated as a local machine: your cartridges, your screen, your saves. The 2600 Console — bring your own cartridges

An Atari 2600 that lives in a web page, running on the open-source Javatari emulator by Paulo Peccin — embedded unmodified, under its own license. The collection ships the console and an empty slot. The cartridges are yours: they live on your shelves and your drives, they are read on your machine, and they never leave it.

The cartridge slot — three steps, then you're playing

1. Find a cartridge file. Any .bin, .a26 or .rom you own — homebrew you wrote, a backup from your own shelf, a file from a folder or a mapped network drive. A mounted share is just a folder.
2. Drop it on the screen — or use the console's power-button menu → Open File. The file is read locally, in this browser. Nothing uploads.
3. Play. Arrow keys steer player 1. The console panel under the screen works — Power, Select, Reset, difficulty — click the switches, just like leaning over the real woodgrain box.

Or borrow from the public shelf — the Internet Archive's cartridges

The cartridges on this shelf live at the Internet Archive — a public library of software — and are served by the Archive, from the Archive. We host no games here; this page only opens the door. Your own library never leaves your machine, and nothing you borrow is kept here either.

Why a door, and not the slot? The Archive's file shelves don't currently let other pages take books down — their servers send no permission header, and browsers enforce that absolutely — so a shelf cartridge can't be loaded into this console directly today. Press a title and it opens on the Archive's own page, where the Archive's own player runs it in their house. The day the Archive opens that header, these same buttons will load the game right here.

The challenge baton — hand someone your worst moment

Twitch games don't take turns the way The Relay does — but a frozen moment does. Play until the screen is full of trouble, open the power menu → Save State → File…, and you hold a tiny .jst file: the whole machine, mid-disaster. Hand it to the next player. They drop it on this screen and inherit exactly your mess — your turn to survive this. The full story, including the honest part — states are files, not copy-and-paste codes, so they travel hand to hand or through a shared folder; the Baton Box passes codes, and this is not one — is on the lesson page.
