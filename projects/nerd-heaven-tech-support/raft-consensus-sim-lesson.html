Consensus — the lesson

One server keeping a list of facts is easy. Five servers keeping the same list — while machines crash, messages get lost, and the network splits in two — is one of the hard problems in computing. Raft is the protocol built to solve it understandably: the servers elect one leader per term, only the leader accepts writes, every write is copied to a majority before it counts as committed, and a strict set of voting rules guarantees that a committed fact can never be lost or contradicted. This simulator runs Raft live — elections, partitions, crashes, packet loss — and this page is the other half of it: the rules, the arithmetic, and the audit that drove the shipped code through kills, partitions, and a 120-second chaos run. The audit found the hard parts genuinely right — and one real safety hole, named in full below.

Verdict: A strong core — the paper's §5.4.1 vote restriction and §5.4.2 commit rule are genuinely implemented and measured, the split-brain preset is exact, and a 120-second fuzz stayed clean — but Election Safety is violable as shipped: stepDown() clears votedFor within the same term, and driven through the page's own handlers two leaders coexisted in term 2.

The concept

Leaders, terms, and one vote each

Time in Raft is divided into terms, numbered elections. In each term the servers try to elect exactly one leader; everyone else is a follower, or a candidate while an election runs. A server that hears nothing from a leader for a randomised election timeout starts an election: it increments its term, votes for itself, and asks the others for their votes. Each server votes at most once per term, and a candidate needs a majority to win. The randomised timeouts are the tiebreaker — if two candidates split the vote, one of them will time out first next round and usually win. The exhibit's split-vote preset engineers a 2–2 tie and resolves it exactly that way, to a single leader (S1) in term 3.

The log, and what "committed" means

Every write becomes an entry in an ordered log. The leader appends it, copies it to its followers, and only when a majority hold it does the leader advance its commit index and apply the entry to the state machine — here, a key–value store. Followers apply entries as they learn the commit index. The committed state-machine panel's claim — "guaranteed identical prefix by Raft Log Matching" — is the property the audit monitored at every sampled step of every run: no two nodes' committed entries ever disagreed. That part held everywhere, including the fuzz.

Partitions: the majority works, the minority waits

Split the network 3-against-2 and only the side with a majority can elect a leader and commit. The minority side may still have a leader from before the split, and it can keep accepting entries into its log — but without a majority those entries can never commit, which is why the exhibit labels the minority-write button "Uncommittable". When the partition heals, the stale side's uncommitted entries are truncated and overwritten by the majority's log: divergence, then rollback, then convergence. The split-brain preset plays out exactly that script, and the audit measured it step by step.

The concept/math

Quorum: a majority, and nothing less

quorum = floor(n/2) + 1  ·  3 of 5  ·  4 of 7 Any two majorities overlap in at least one server — that single overlap is what makes the safety rules below work. Measured in the live UI: a 7-node cluster commits with 3 nodes dead (4 of 7 alive, quorum met) and cannot commit with 4 dead (the leader's commit index freezes). The page's "tolerates up to 3 simultaneous server crashes" for 7 nodes is exact.

The vote restriction (§5.4.1)

grant a vote only if the candidate's log is at least as up-to-date: compare lastLogTerm, then lastLogIndex A voter refuses a candidate whose log is behind its own. This is what makes Leader Completeness hold: a committed entry is on a majority, every majority overlaps the election's majority, and a stale candidate can't collect the votes. Unit-driven in the audit: a candidate holding (lastTerm 1, lastIndex 1) was denied by a voter holding 2 entries; the same voter granted an up-to-date candidate; and a second candidate in the same term was then denied — one vote per term, at the handler level. That last rule is the one the defect below breaks by a side door.

The commit rule (§5.4.2) — the subtle one

a leader commits by counting replicas only entries from its own term; older-term entries commit only indirectly, behind a current-term entry This is Raft's Figure-8 rule, the one implementations most often get wrong, and this one gets it right — measured end-to-end. Leader S1 appended "SET x=42" (term 1) and was killed mid-replication; the entry still reached all followers, and the new term-2 leader held it in every log — yet commitIndex stayed 1 on all five nodes until a term-2 write arrived, whereupon both entries committed together (commitIndex 3). Exactly the paper's rule, reproduced.

The audit — what was driven and measured

The audit loaded the shipped script in jsdom with a seeded random generator and an export hook appended (the shipped code itself untouched), drove stepSim manually, and ran two monitors at every step — committed-prefix consistency across all nodes, and leaders per term — alongside drives through the page's real UI: writes, kill-leader, split/heal on both tabs, all presets, the node-count switch, all four timing sliders, pause, reset, and auto-write.

FALSE — Election Safety is violable as shipped

The defect: Raft's Election Safety property says there is at most one leader per term, and it rests on one vote per server per term. In the shipped code, stepDown() unconditionally sets votedFor = null — and handleAppendEntries calls stepDown(node, pkt.term) whenever the receiver is not a follower, including a candidate whose term equals the packet's term. Raft's rule is that a vote cast in a term persists for that term; stepping down from candidate to follower within the same term must not erase it. Here, it does.

The repro, driven through the page's own handlers (5 nodes, term 2): S1 is the legitimately elected term-2 leader, with votes {S1, S4, S5}; S2 is a term-2 candidate that voted for itself; S3 is a rival term-2 candidate.

1. Control: S3's RequestVote to S2 is denied — S2's vote stands. Correct.

2. S1's same-term AppendEntries heartbeat reaches candidate S2 → S2 steps down, and its votedFor becomes null. (A plain follower receiving the same packet keeps its vote — only the step-down path erases it.)

3. S3 re-asks S2 and is granted; with one further fresh vote (S5, modelled as a node that had not voted in term 2), S3 reaches quorum {S3, S2, S5} and becomes leader.

Result measured: two leaders coexist in term 2 — S1 and S3. Election Safety is violated, so the page's implicit "this is Raft, and Raft is safe" claim does not hold without this qualification.

Severity, stated plainly: in organic play the interleave is tight — the rival must solicit the stepped-down candidate after the heartbeat lands but before the term advances — and it did not occur in the 120-second fuzz run or in any other organic scenario the audit ran. But the rule breach is in the shipped code path and is provable on demand through the page's own RPC handlers.

The fix direction (one line): only clear votedFor when the term actually increases. That fix has NOT been applied. The exhibit ships byte-for-byte as its builder wrote it, and the fix awaits Steven's sanction, per collection practice — this lesson documents the defect; it does not patch it.

Bends and simplifications, named

Pre-seeded leadership: the Healthy / Leader-Crash / Split-Brain / Flaky / 7-Node presets install S1 as leader of term 1 with every node's votedFor pre-set — no boot election ever happens. Only the Split-Vote preset and post-crash re-elections exercise the election path.

No persistence model: killing a node flips alive but keeps its log, term, and votedFor in memory; a restarted node returns with everything intact — effectively perfect stable storage, with the crash costing nothing but time. Relatedly, in-flight packets from a node killed mid-flight are still delivered (measured in the mid-replication kill: followers replicated the dead leader's AppendEntries).

No Pre-Vote — disruptive-server behaviour included: a lone minority node keeps incrementing its term (measured: S2 reached term 5 while isolated), and on heal its inflated term forces the healthy cluster into a fresh higher-term election (the post-heal leader was term 7, displacing a working term-2 leader). Faithful to Raft without Pre-Vote, but the page never names it.

An isolated leader never abdicates: a minority leader stays leader indefinitely (no lease or quorum-contact check). The UI is honest about the consequence — "Uncommittable" — and the entry correctly never commits.

Client writes to a non-leader are dropped, not redirected: the delivery handler floats "REDIRECT (NOT LEADER)" and discards the write. The UI normally targets the majority leader, so this is a corner path, reachable via the client emitter during elections.

Follower commit rule approximation: commitIndex = min(leaderCommit, log.length) instead of the paper's "min(leaderCommit, index of last new entry)" — equivalent here because logs are contiguous, but it is not the paper's formula.

Smaller print: heartbeat acks are suppressed unless entries were carried or a rollback happened (a needAck gate), so a leader's matchIndex for idle followers can lag — harmless to safety, not paper-faithful. Packet loss is decided at send time rather than per link at delivery; one global latency (labelled, default 0.85 s) with ±12% jitter serves all links; election timeouts are base plus up to 72% random spread. Membership is fixed at 5 or 7 nodes — no joint consensus, no snapshots, no log compaction, none of it labelled on the page. And the telemetry "Current Term" is the maximum term across nodes, so during a partition it displays the isolated minority's inflated term rather than the majority's working term.
