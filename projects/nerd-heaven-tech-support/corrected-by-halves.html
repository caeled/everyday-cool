📘 Field Guide — Corrected by Halves

You don't read the whole station book from page 1—you split the problem in half, ask "which way?", and throw away 50% of the possibilities every single step. Each clean halving earns 1.00 bit of Shannon information.

⚡ 1. Line Fault (Binary Search Troubleshooting)

A signal trunk has one broken relay. Tap the middle of the active bracket: a CLEAN reading eliminates everything to the left ("Go Thataway →"); a DEAD reading eliminates everything to the right ("← Go Thataway"). Even 1,024 stations takes at most 10 probes (⌈log₂ 1024⌉ = 10)!

🌳 2. Checksum Tree (Merkle / CRC-8 Packet Bisection)

A network frame arrived with a bad CRC-8 checksum. Click nodes in the binary checksum tree to hash half of the packets at a time. Clean halves lock out immediately; follow dirty halves to the exact corrupted byte, then flip the flipped bit to repair the frame.

📡 3. TCP Halver (AIMD Congestion Control)

Real internet congestion control (TCP Reno/CUBIC) ramps up your send rate (×2 Slow Start or +1/+4 Additive Increase) until packets drop, then cuts the window in half (÷2)! Narrow your Clean/Drop bracket to hover right below hidden link capacity without triggering heavy retransmit stalls.

⊞ 4. Parity Matrix (Hamming Code Half-Space Rescue)

Hamming ECC cuts memory in half in space instead of time! Each parity check (P1, P2, P4, P8, [P16]) covers exactly half the cells. Odd parity = bit is 1; even parity = 0. Reading the failing parity masks in binary spells the exact address of the flipped bit—4 checks pinpoint 1 bit in 15; 5 checks pinpoint 1 bit in 31!

〰 5. Zero Crossing (Bolzano Bisection Oscilloscope)

Calibrate a hidden black-box curve where f(a) < 0 < f(b). Every bisection zooms the oscilloscope 2× tighter around the zero crossing. Even on deceptive steep cliffs that fool giant-jump extrapolators, bisection is guaranteed to converge exponentially fast.
