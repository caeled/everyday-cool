The Printer Did It — a deduction game

Somebody killed the house computer at midnight. The Printer has an alibi. The Printer always has an alibi.

One card each — a suspect, a cause, a room — is sealed in the Case File. The other eighteen are dealt to you and three houseguests: Ada, Grace and Linus. Roll, move through the house, and in a room make a suggestion: that room, a suspect, a cause. The named suspect is brought to the room (house rules, and it hates that). The other players, in turn, must show you one matching card if they hold one — privately. If nobody can show, that silence is evidence too.

Everything that happens goes into the case log, and your detective notebook keeps the deductions the log entitles you to — no more, no less. A pass is three facts. A card shown is a receipt. When the notebook leaves exactly one suspect, one cause and one room with nowhere else to be, name them in an accusation. Right: case closed. Wrong: you are out — you keep showing cards, but the houseguests finish the case without you.

It is Find the Fault at board scale: a suggestion is a test, elimination is the engine, and the notebook is a ledger. The only difference is that here the suspects can talk back.
