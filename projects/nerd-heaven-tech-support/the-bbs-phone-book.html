The phone book — boards that still answer

The BBSes did not all die. Hundreds of them still answer on telnet, kept by sysops who never saw a reason to stop. This is the bench's short list: twelve boards and one legendary game, drawn from the master list at the Telnet BBS Guide — and the client that can call them is already sitting in your C:, in the NET folder, where mTCP's TELNET.EXE has been waiting since Pack 002.

Before you call

Two honesties, up front, the way this room keeps them:

  • The wall stands. This page's own DOS console fits no network card, so TELNET inside this window can only introduce itself to nobody — the same wall Pack 002 teaches against. On a machine that fits a card — DOSBox-X with its NE2000 turned on, or real hardware — the exact same drive, stack, and client below connect for real.
  • Read the house rules first. These are people's homes, run the old way: a sysop, a set of rules, a community that remembers. The bench's own guide — Online Interaction and Communication, as Told in the Age of the BBS — is the etiquette book for exactly these rooms. Everybody was new once. Be the guest it describes.

How a call goes

On a card machine — DOSBox-X, or the real thing — with Pack 002's drive or your Template C: mounted, the whole call is the stack you already built, plus one word:

CD \NET
PNPPD 0x60
DHCP
TELNET AGENCY.BBS.NZ
TELNET 20FORBEERS.COM 1337
TELNET XROADS.MACRO10.COM 7777

The packet driver loads because the card is there this time. DHCP's question gets an answer. And then a login banner from Dunedin or Portland or Oak Ridge scrolls up a DOS screen, and it is 1993 again, except the phone bill is not ticking. On a modern machine instead, any telnet client dials the same numbers — the telnet:// links in the tables are live for clients that speak the scheme, and BBS-native clients like SyncTERM draw the ANSI the way it was meant to be seen.

The file

This page has a DOS twin: BBS.TXT, riding in the NET folder of Pack 002 and of Steven's Template C:, readable at the prompt with TYPE BBS.TXT. That is deliberate. Steven, remembering how it worked back then: the list itself was the artifact — it was a WALL, or a CompassRose, passed hand to hand. A phone book you could hold, copy, and carry to the next machine. This page is the shelf copy. The file is the real one.

The honest box

  • Source. Every board above stood on the Telnet BBS Guide's list on 26 November 2026; the Guide maintains the master list — hundreds more boards — and re-verifies it. The Crossroads doors come from Steven's screenshot of the community's current word, not from the Guide.
  • What was not done. This bench's own network cannot open telnet connections, so no board on this page was rung from here, and no banner is quoted as captured. Boards also sleep, move, and retire between any list's printing and your call — that was true of every phone book ever passed hand to hand, and the Guide is where a moved board's new number lives.
  • Addresses are the boards' own public listings, published by their sysops to be called. Nothing here is anyone's private number.
