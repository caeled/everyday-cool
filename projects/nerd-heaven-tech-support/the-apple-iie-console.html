The Apple IIe Console — how to play

This page is a working Apple IIe, emulated in your browser by Apple2TS — a complete Apple II family emulator written in TypeScript by Chris Torrence and contributors, MIT-licensed and embedded unmodified in the folder beside this page; its license rides there (see the lesson for the receipts). The machine below is the enhanced IIe: 128K, 80 columns, two Disk II drives and a hard drive, exactly as its maker ships it. What the page deliberately does not contain is a single disk. The console ships empty, the way the real one did: you bring the disks.

Insert a disk

  • Click Insert a disk… below the machine and pick a disk image — .dsk, .woz, .2mg, .po, .do or .hdv (the machine also runs .bin and .bas programs, and reads .a2r flux images) — or drag the file onto the console. The page hands the file to the machine's own loader, and it lands in Drive 1 (S6D1, in the machine's drive row).
  • Point the picker anywhere your files live — a folder on this machine, or a mapped network drive. A mounted share is just a folder; the disk is read right there.
  • The file is read on your machine only. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is sent anywhere, and once this page has loaded it works fully offline — the emulator itself is served from this site, never from a CDN. Your library stays yours.
  • A disk in a drive is not a booted disk. Press Boot the machine (or click the machine's screen and press Alt+B) to start from it — the same two steps the real machine asked for. Some disks, inserted before the machine settles, start on their own; both behaviours are the machine's, not a fault.
  • To change disks, insert the next one the same way, or use the machine's own drive bays — each drive in its drive row takes a disk directly, and its menu offers the machine's whole toolbox.

The keyboard — the machine's own, as delivered

Click the machine's screen first, so it has your keyboard. From there your real keyboard is the IIe's keyboard: letters, numbers, Return, Esc and the arrow keys go straight to the machine. Two period details ship on: the machine starts with Caps Lock on, like a real IIe (the toolbar's A button toggles it), and the Alt keys do double duty, below.

Save states — the baton is a file

  • Freeze any moment — a wagon fully loaded, a boss at one hit point — with Save State in the baton section below, or the machine's own toolbar button, or Alt+S. The whole machine (CPU, memory, drives, screen) packs into one .a2ts file that downloads to your machine. The lesson measures a real one.
  • Hand the file to the next player however you like — a message, a shared folder, a USB stick, in the best sneakernet tradition. They insert the same disk, press Restore State (or Alt+O), pick your file, and the machine resumes at that exact moment.
  • A state is a moment from one specific disk, not a disk in itself — the same disk has to be in Drive 1 first.

The Oregon Trail door

  • The button below the machine opens The Oregon Trail (MECC, 1985 — the Apple II original) in the Internet Archive's own player, in a new tab. The shelf is theirs, and for now the reading happens in their house: the Archive's file servers don't currently give web pages permission to fetch the disk itself, so no page — this one included — can honestly load it from there directly. The lesson carries the evidence.
  • When this page is served by your Baton Box on your own network, that same button becomes a one-click loader: the box fetches the disk on your side of the wall and hands it to the machine here. Until then, the Archive's own player is one click away, and your own disks never needed the door at all.
  • The machine itself also carries its maker's own Internet Archive browser (in its Disk Collections panel). It works as its maker designed it — its catalogue searches the Archive's open index, and its disk loads route through the maker's chosen CORS proxies. This page's door doesn't use those proxies; it opens the Archive's own house instead. The lesson lays out both roads.

The fine print

  • This console must be served over HTTP — from this site, or from your Baton Box. Opened as a lone local file it cannot start: a browser won't fetch the machine's parts for a file:// page, so the console says so in plain words instead of sitting mute.
  • No disks are included, linked, or downloaded by this page's local slot — by design, and permanently. Homebrew you wrote, backups you made, disks you imaged: those are yours to insert.
  • The machine's own cloud-drive buttons (in its drive menus) are its maker's furniture: they reach Google or Microsoft only if you press them and sign in. The local slot never does.
  • “Apple II” and “Apple IIe” are trademarks of Apple Inc.; “The Oregon Trail” belongs to its publisher. All are used descriptively — this is a fan-made console page for playing your own disks.

🏃 The baton is a file

Playing something brutal? Freeze the exact moment — the wagon loaded, the river ahead — and hand it to the next player as one .a2ts state file. They insert the same disk, restore your file, and the machine picks up exactly where you left it. No codes to copy, nothing to retype: a file, hand to hand, the way the 2600 console next door passes its moments. The lesson measures a real state file and tells the whole story.
