Bug Hunt — it works on my machine

Steven's second variation from the same afternoon: Duck Hunt, re-cast for tech support. The ducks are famous software bugs, and every bug behaves like the error it's named after — the HEISENBUG moves when you observe it, the OFFBYONE's hitbox is not where the bug is drawn, the MEMORYLEAK fattens while you watch. Your debugger fires real shot vectors that ricochet off the walls of the code window, and anything you miss doesn't fly away. It ships to production — where Clippy is waiting to hold it up, grinning.

Field guide, short version: LOOKERROR freezes while your cursor is near it and darts when you look away. HEISENBUG teleports the moment a shot's line passes close. OFFBYONE is hit beside itself, not on itself. NULLPOINTER is only a name tag pointing at nothing. STACKOVERFLOW copies itself when you nearly miss. MEMORYLEAK grows — and pays — the longer it lives. DEADLOCK travels frozen in pairs; free one and the other runs. And the gold one is a FEATURE: shooting it costs you. Clippy ships whatever escapes — and from round 3 he occasionally takes the field himself as a boss bug worth 750, running one of the other bugs' behaviours, chosen at random. The game. Bugs cross the code window. Click to fire the debugger's shot from the bottom of the screen toward your cursor: the shot is a travelling vector, not an instant zap. It pierces every bug on its line and ricochets off the side and top walls up to three times before it dies at the floor — line up two bugs, or bank a shot off a wall into a third. Each round gives you a handful of shots; bugs left when the shots run out make a run for it. A bug that crosses the far edge ships: you lose one of your five releases, and Clippy presents the escapee. Lose all five and the build has shipped broken — game over. Clearing a round pays a 250-point bonus; an extra release every 10,000 points.

The roster, honestly. Each bug's behaviour is its definition, playable: a heisenbug really does change when observed (it teleports when your shot's line passes within range); the off-by-one's hitbox really is displaced from its body by one body-width; the lookerror only moves unobserved — park your cursor near it to hold it still for the shot. The memory leak's value grows with its body. The stack overflow spawns a smaller, faster copy of itself (up to three generations) whenever a shot passes close without hitting. The deadlock pair cannot move while both live. The FEATURE is a decoy: −150 points and a wasted shot if you hit it, and if it escapes, nothing bad happens — features ship as designed. And from round 3, CLIPPY himself occasionally crosses the field as a boss bug worth 750 points: he has borrowed one of the other bugs' behaviours at random — this Clippy might freeze under your cursor, that one might teleport when observed. The label under him only says CLIPPY. The X-Ray, alone, can see which behaviour he's running.

The X-Ray layer (F2). Draws the shot you are about to fire: the full predicted path, ricochets and all, following your cursor. It also draws every bug's true hitbox — including the OFFBYONE's real one, with a marker where its body pretends to be — the LOOKERROR's sight radius, the link between a DEADLOCK pair, and the MEMORYLEAK's maximum size. After a shot, its travelled path stays on screen briefly, so you can compare the prediction with what the shot actually did. The X-Ray changes nothing; it only shows what the game already knows.

Honesty notes. Shots are simulated in small steps so fast shots can't tunnel through bugs; the prediction and the shot use the same wall rules, and the lesson's audit compares them point by point. Clippy's eyes follow your cursor. He would want it that way. Sound: none. This page makes no network requests and runs from a lone local file.
