Corrected by Halves arrives (21 November 2026): `corrected-by-halves.html`, a five-in-one halving suite built by Gemini Neon (Google Antigravity) at Steven Powell's direction and integrated byte-for-byte unchanged (sha256 8d0e8f19a37f538abc5680039463c3a7552ac350a0b71ff0ea0799270c6eb050) — Line Fault (binary-search fault isolation), Checksum Tree (CRC-8 bisection and bit repair), TCP Halver (AIMD by hand), Parity Matrix (Hamming syndrome decoding), and Zero Crossing (bisection root-finding), with its own in-page Field Guide. `corrected-by-halves-lesson.html` is the collection's audit of it, written from the code: the CRC is genuine CRC-8/SMBUS (check value 0xF4), the Hamming claim is exactly true and proven exhaustively, and the divergences are named in its honest box — "Merkle" is the tree's shape, not its arithmetic; the TCP guide's +1/+4 matches nothing (the coded steps are +2/+8, and nothing is automatic — the player is the control loop); and there is no seed system despite the button names. `index.html` gained a banner section for the game between the Find the Fault banner and the Back Doors shelf. The same day, the expanded Educational Arcade Edition replaced it in place, again byte-for-byte unchanged (sha256 7bbceaa347e78a5ebb7463248bca531271989c8347dde82ff5e5c2db326619c1): five new ops — Git Bisect, Quadtree Radar, Backoff Arena, Subnet Slicer, and the timed Arcade Shift — 81 campaign stars, a new Packet Grandmaster rank, and a local leaderboard. The lesson was re-audited and extended against the new code: the CRC and Hamming proofs still hold, the Field Guide now prints the TCP steps correctly (+2/+8), the seed buttons still only re-deal, Git Bisect's skip tiers are winnable about nine times in ten rather than guaranteed, the Backoff Arena is slotted ALOHA on one shared window rather than per-station backoff, and the Subnet Slicer is a genuine buddy allocator whose usable-host arithmetic checks out for every size. With the edition final, the wiring landed once: a guest card on the Physics Game Table, the lesson shelved here and at Java Young Robot Builders (eighteen back doors each), and a card on the Arcade shelf (33 games, 32 lessons).
