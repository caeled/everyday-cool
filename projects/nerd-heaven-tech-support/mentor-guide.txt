NERD HEAVEN — A GUIDE FOR LEARNING TOGETHER

Begin by asking what the learner already understands and wants to accomplish.
Let him use the keyboard. Ask for his reasoning before supplying your own.
This is adult craft practice. No timer, no comparison with someone else's pace.

FIRST MEETING
Open index.html together. Try Starting check. Choose one lesson he wants to
demonstrate. Create Support-Practice/Tickets, Files, and Evidence in Documents.
Show how progress export/import works. Keep a spare copy of the course folder.

LOW-COST PRACTICE EQUIPMENT, IF AVAILABLE
A Windows PC, a mouse, two known-good Ethernet leads, a spare monitor, and a
printer are useful. A basic cable tester and disconnected practice cable can
support cabling lessons. No purchase is required to use the offline benches.
Do not use a production network as an experimental lab.

SESSION PLAN
1. Ask him to show one thing from last time (10 minutes).
2. Work through one lesson (20–30 minutes or longer as needed).
3. Do the lab; let him predict what a test would show (15–30 minutes).
4. Record evidence and answer the checkpoint (10 minutes).
5. Export progress, agree on the next demonstration.

USEFUL QUESTIONS
What was the person trying to do?
Who else is affected?
What did you actually observe?
What would disprove that idea?
What is the smallest safe next test?
How will you know their original task works?
What would another technician need to continue?

BEFORE REAL SUPPORT
He should demonstrate safe file copying and recovery, identity verification,
permission boundaries, and documentation. Keep initial tasks noncritical and
supervised. Agree on scope, preserve work, and stop at suspected compromise,
data loss, electrical hazards, or privileged changes outside authority.

CAPSTONE SUGGESTED REASONING — NOT A UNIQUE REQUIRED SCRIPT
1. 169.254 IPv4, colleague working:
   Scope is one endpoint. Automatic IPv4 configuration appears unsuccessful.
   Inspect active adapter, link, DHCP configuration and relevant policy; compare
   with working device. IPv6 is unknown; do not claim all connectivity failed.
   Use an authorized alternate connection only if policy allows. Escalate if
   DHCP/network administration is needed beyond the technician's authority.
2. Viewer needs approved editing:
   Verify intended managed account, approved request, owner/manager authority.
   Grant the smallest required editing role if allowed. Retest the original
   editing task and document the access change. Do not make the file public.
3. Printer works for Windows test page but not one document:
   Compare another document and another application. Record app version, file
   type and print settings. Work on a copy; preserve original. Do not reset the
   entire printer or network when evidence points toward document/app behavior.

RUBRIC: 0 not yet demonstrated, 1 with prompting, 2 demonstrated independently.
Score scope, evidence, controlled testing, protection/authorization,
verification, and handoff. Maximum 12. Use scores to choose more practice, not
as a credential. Repeat weak areas. Do not accept invented results or secrets
in exported artifacts.

PORTFOLIO
A how-to document, asset sheet, network diagram, cable record, and three
practice tickets. Label simulations honestly. Prefer clear demonstrated work
over broad claims of expertise. Ask for a 90-second explanation of one case.
