What you are looking at

A game studio in one page. On the left side of the bench is an editor: a grid where you paint blocks and spikes to build a running level. On the right side is a player: press ▶ Play and a cube runs your level by itself — your only job is to tap, click, or press Space at the right moments to jump. Build a level, play the page. It is a learning tribute for kids who love Geometry Dash (RobTop's game) — the same idea, rebuilt small enough to see through: one button, one cube, and physics you can read.

First steps

  • Press ▶ Play my level first and try the starter level. Tap, click the level, or press Space to jump. Hold to keep hopping.
  • Press ✎ Back to the editor, pick the Spike tool, and paint a spike on the ground. Play again and meet it.
  • When your level is yours, press ⬇ Export — save my level as a page. You get a real .html file that plays your level all by itself, anywhere.

The editor

  • Paint: pick a tool — ▦ Block, ▲ Spike, or ⌫ Erase — then click or drag on the grid. Blocks are solid: land on top and you can run across them. Spikes are trouble wherever they sit.
  • The ground is the bottom row of blocks. Erase some of it and you have made a pit — fall in and the run is over. The green flag at the far end is the finish; it always stands in the last column.
  • Scroll: a level can be much wider than the screen. Drag the scroll slider under the grid to travel along it.
  • Level length: the −10 / +10 buttons shrink or stretch the level (20 to 200 columns). Stretching adds fresh ground at the end; shrinking cuts the end off, so export first if you love what is there.
  • Clear: wipes the level back to bare ground. It asks first, because it clears your work.

Playing — the rules, all of them

  • The cube runs by itself at one fixed speed. You cannot slow it down or steer it. Jump is the only move: tap, click, or Space. Hold the button and the cube hops again the instant it lands.
  • Touch a spike and the run ends — instantly back to the start, and the attempt counter ticks up. That counter is the game's memory of your learning.
  • Land on top of a block and you are safe. Hit a block's side and the run ends, exactly like the game this honours.
  • Fall below the world — down a pit you dug — and the run ends.
  • Reach the green flag and the level is complete, with your attempts printed on the banner.

The numbers under the cube

  • One grid square (a cell) is 32 pixels. The cube runs at 320 pixels a second — ten cells a second.
  • Gravity pulls at 3200 pixels a second² and a jump launches at 800 pixels a second, so a jump rises exactly 100 pixels — 3⅛ cells — stays in the air half a second, and carries the cube 5 cells forward. That is why one spike is easy, two spikes are a good jump, and three spikes in a row cannot be cleared: the arithmetic says so, and the lesson page shows the working.
  • The simulation steps 120 times a second in fixed steps, so the game plays identically on every machine — the physics never depends on how fast your screen redraws.

Share codes — a level is just data

Your whole level — every block and spike — squeezes into one short line of letters and numbers, the share code in the box under the editor. Copy it and give it to a friend; they paste it into their Dash Builder, press Load, and your level stands in front of them. Nothing is uploaded anywhere: the level is the code, the same way the Web Page Workshop next door taught that the code is the page.

Export — your level becomes a web page

Export downloads a complete, standalone .html file with the player and your level baked inside it as data. Double-click it anywhere — another computer, a USB stick, an email to Grandma — and it plays your level by itself, no Dash Builder needed. It carries a small credit line: Made with Dash Builder in Nerd Heaven — Everyday Cool. This is the Web Page Workshop's Export trick, one door down: there, the page was the thing you made; here, the page is the game you made.

Your work is kept in this browser

Every painted square is autosaved into this browser's own storage (localStorage) as you build. Close the tab, come back tomorrow, and your level is still here. The honest limits: it lives in this browser on this device only, and clearing the browser's site data would clear it too. Export and share codes are how you keep a level forever.

One honest note

This is a learning tribute, not the real Geometry Dash: no music, no icons to unlock, no accounts, no online levels. Levels live in this browser, in share codes, and in exported pages. What it shares with the real game is the honest core — auto-run, one-button jump, instant retry — and that core is small enough to read end to end on the lesson page.
