# Collection origin

Original Everyday Cool workshop, added 22 November 2026 at Steven Powell's direction. He asked for a colouring book with preloaded pictures to colour, a way to load your own picture and have it converted to a colourable outline, and a print option on all of them so the pages can be coloured with real crayons — a room that also teaches eye-hand coordination. He added the subjects mid-brief: some of the preloaded pages are cute AIs (Robot Buddy, Robot Pup, Baby Bot), because the robots are this collection's house characters. Built by Muse (AI assistant) in the Everyday Cool side chat and added directly to the collection at his direction; this folder is the maintained original, and there is no upstream copy.

The room is one self-contained page, `index.html`: twelve hand-built vector picture pages (SVG, graded Little hands / Getting steady / Fine detail by patch size and count), a 24-crayon box with an eraser crayon, undo and start-over, print-the-outline and print-coloured via a print stylesheet, and PNG save. The photo bench converts a dropped or chosen image to an outline entirely on the device — a Canny-style pipeline: grayscale, Gaussian blur, Sobel gradients, non-maximum suppression (thinning), hysteresis thresholding, and a one-pixel sealing dilation — and colours it with a scanline flood fill under the lines; the page states plainly that the picture never leaves the device, and nothing in the room makes a network call. The image-processing core is written as pure functions over pixel arrays and is checked by a script that extracts it from the shipped file: the twelve pages parse with every patch a closed shape (13–14 patches on the Little hands pages, 27–31 on the last three), the flood fill colours exactly the 216 squares inside a drawn box without leaking, fills up to an anti-aliased fringe at the shipped tolerance, the Sobel step scores 255 on a test square's boundary and 0 in flat areas, and the threshold is monotonic in line strength.

Field note, 22 November 2026: the Canny-style upgrade above was driven by the curator's own test the day the room shipped — Steven converted a family photo (an outdoor portrait: a man holding a newborn, stone steps and foliage behind, soft natural light) and the original grayscale → Sobel → single-threshold pipeline "didn't do so well": the stone-and-leaf texture read as thousands of edges while the subjects' low-contrast contours fell under the same bar. The upgraded pipeline was proven on synthetic stand-ins for that photo's properties before shipping: on a grain-textured test image the stray line pixels in the texture region fell from 12,031 (old pipeline) to 0 while the smooth subject's boundary stayed detected (74% of its circumference band); a weak edge connected to a strong one survived hysteresis (12/12 pixels) while the identical weak edge isolated was dropped (0/11); a contour with a deliberate one-pixel gap leaked the flood fill (896 squares painted outside it) before sealing and none after; and stronger blur monotonically reduced texture lines on a high-contrast grain (17,024 line pixels at the Detail range's weakest blur, 0 at its strongest). Two controls now steer the conversion: Line strength (the definite-line bar) and Detail (blur strength plus the faint-line bar). The sealed line map is what the page displays, prints and fills against, so printed lines sit a touch bolder than the raw detected edges — named in the lesson's honest box.

`coloring-book-lesson.html` is the back door: eye-hand coordination as the real curriculum — Fitts's law stated properly, the graded patch sizes as deliberate practice, and the lineage Steven states for this collection (Solitaire taught drag-and-drop, Minesweeper taught the mouse buttons; these fills teach aim) — plus how the photo conversion and the paint-bucket walk work, and an honest box naming the limits (converted outlines are only as clean as the photo's contrast; the preloaded pages are vector and the photo pages are raster, filled by two different engines for that reason).
