Shake Table

Every building has a natural wobble — the rhythm it swings at when it's shoved and left alone. A quake has a rhythm too. When the two match, each push from the ground lands just as the building is already swinging that way, and the sway piles up, floor over floor. That's resonance, and it knocks down more towers than raw strength ever saves. Build to the target height, spend the budget well — then start the quake.

Simplifications, named: each floor is one lumped mass on a spring, where a real building bends in a curve; the quake here is a single steady note, where real quakes are a jumble of frequencies that swell and fade; a storey fails at one drift number, where real frames tear in complicated ways; and the tuned mass damper is a hefty quarter of the tower's mass — real ones are a percent or two of it, working alongside other damping, so this one is oversized to let you watch it work. The resonance is not simplified: it falls straight out of the masses and springs, the same arithmetic a real tower obeys. Quake playback runs at 1.5× speed.
