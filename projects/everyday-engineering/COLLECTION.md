# Collection origin

Original Everyday Cool workshop, added 19 November 2026 at Steven Powell's direction. In the Everyday Cool ideas side chat that evening he named the gap himself — "I keep having a nagging feeling that there is a STEM subject we are missing" — and from the four candidates laid out (Math, Engineering, Earth, Computing, a list he ordered saved so all four will be built) he picked this one first: "lets do Engieering. That links to/from survival too." Built by Muse (AI assistant) and added directly to the collection at his direction as the twenty-sixth catalog entry and the seventh workshop inside The STEM Room; this folder is the maintained original, and there is no upstream copy.

The room follows the Science block's shape (benches that work right on the page and show their working, a verified shelf of legitimate places, Help ▾ menu with full documentation, banner verified on the page and inside the desktop app). It enters the catalog under "Science" with `hubParent: stem-room`, immediately after Everyday Measure, so the STEM Room's bench now reads Physics, Chemistry, Electronics, Measure, Engineering, Body, Radio. The Survival Guide cross-link runs both ways, as Steven specified: this room's "Out in the field" section links to the Guide, and the Guide's Links Out section gained a card naming this room the machine shop behind its field skills (recorded in the Guide's own COLLECTION.md). The updated Everyday Cool desktop app ships as app v1.2.1 through the app repo's existing build workflow.

Everything interactive is computed in the visitor's browser from rules baked into `engineering.js` — the page sends nothing anywhere. The arithmetic is ideal-machine physics, stated as such at every bench: lever MA = effort arm ÷ load arm; pulley effort = load ÷ supporting segments with rope pulled = segments × lift; ramp effort = load × sin(slope), with the wheelchair-ramp standard 1:12 (≈ 4.8°, ADA §405.2) computed from the same rules; the frames bench is pure geometry (a square racks by the push, a triangle and a braced square lean zero). `test.cjs` re-checks all of it on every run. Facts, sources, and the deliberate scope lines (ideal machines, one-to-four segments, geometry-only frames) are recorded in THIRD-PARTY.md.

The rules live in `engineering.js` (no DOM), checked by `test.cjs`.

Add, the same evening, at Steven's word while the build was in flight ("Logarithms were the engineer's pocket calculator for three centuries — a compact log bench belongs in this room"): **the pocket log table** — ninety four-decimal mantissas for 1.0–9.9, the characteristic-plus-mantissa rule that stretches the small table to any number, a bench that looks numbers up by the table's own method (linear interpolation — proportional parts — with its one-unit fourth-decimal wobble shown, not hidden), a worked interpolation example (log 2.53), and the slide rule named in one line as the same table turned into lengths on a stick. It folded into the same app release, v1.2.1, at his instruction.

Second add, the same evening, also at Steven's word: the **Links Out shelf** grew from four links to twelve in three groups, on the Survival Guide's shelf pattern — the six machines one by one (Wikipedia), the arithmetic behind the benches (Mechanical advantage, Logarithm, Slide rule), and one plain-English explainer (Explain That Stuff). Every destination was verified live (HTTP 200) before shipping; Britannica's simple-machine article was considered and left off because it answers automated checks with a 403 and so could not be verified the collection's way (recorded in THIRD-PARTY.md).

**Recorded, not done — the Everyday Math cross-link.** The log table is mathematics wearing an engineer's hat, and its natural sister room is Everyday Math — second in Steven's STEM queue, not yet built, so no forward link exists (a link to a missing room would fail the collection check). When the Math room is built, it will link here, and this room's log section gains the return link in the same pass: the loop closes both ways in that build, the way the Survival Guide loop closed in this one.

Banner: `everyday-engineering-banner.jpg`, AI-generated watercolor generated 19 November 2026 as part of adding this room, in the style and dimensions (2736×912) of the collection's other banners: the plush-style robot in a yellow engineer's hard hat holding a rolled blueprint, in a warm workshop beside a wooden lever beam on a stone fulcrum and a rope pulley hoisting a crate, with chalk diagrams of a lever, a pulley, and a triangle frame on the board behind it. It carries no readable lettering — the chalk diagrams are drawings, not words — and it shipped on its first generation.
