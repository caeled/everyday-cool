The claim

The workshop page claims the collection is made by a five-move method — question, bench, audit, wiring, a human decides — carried on three pieces of plumbing, and that the wiring checklist printed on its face is the real one. A method page is exactly the kind of page that can drift into describing an aspiration instead of a practice, so it gets the same audit as a bench.

What was checked

  • The checklist is the real one. This workshop's own integration was run against it line by line the night it was built: catalog entry 39, generated hub card, provenance map, package ZIP, sitemap lines, changelog line, documentation row, gate pass, and a byte-compare of the live page against the audited file before the word "live" was used.
  • The examples are real. Every named exhibit on the page — Is the Earth Flat?, the X-Ray Time Machine and its Recoil Experiment, AI-Delivery, The Baton Box — exists in the collection, and every link on the page was resolved against the files on disk before shipping.
  • The audit story is checkable. The "law falsified by its own audit" anecdote in Move 3 is Shardstorm's chord law, corrected the same night it failed; its lesson page tells the story with numbers.
  • The counts moved honestly. Adding this entry changed the generated homepage line to 39 projects and 37 bundled workshops — produced by the generator from the catalog, not typed.

What bends

It is one collection's practice. The method is described from inside Everyday Cool, by its builders. Another collection would bend the moves to its own material; the page says so, in its Help ▾.

The plumbing summaries compress. AI-Delivery in particular is a working arrangement with more texture than a card holds — the per-builder boxes, the copy-never-move rule, the sync cycle. Each tool's own page governs; the cards are doors, not documentation.

"Anyone can contribute" is an invitation, not a volume claim. The door is real and the issues link works; how many people walk through it is not this page's to promise. The curator's word for it stands on the page: if anyone is really watching, this is the door.
