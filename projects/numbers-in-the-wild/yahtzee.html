The scorecard

The number on each open box's button is what the dice in the tray would score there right now. Click it to bank that box and end the turn.

The rules, stated — including the joker

Thirteen turns, one box per turn. Upper boxes score the sum of that face; the upper bonus is +35 when the six upper boxes total 63 or more. Three and Four of a Kind score the sum of all five dice; Full House 25, Small Straight 30, Large Straight 40, Yahtzee 50, Chance the sum of all dice.

Yahtzee bonus and joker, played faithfully: if the dice land on a Yahtzee while your Yahtzee box already holds 50, you bank +100 at once, and that hand is a joker: if the upper box matching its face is still open you must score it there, at its full sum of five dice; otherwise you may score any open lower box at its full joker value — a joker Yahtzee counts as a Full House (25), a Small Straight (30), or a Large Straight (40); if no lower box is open you must take a zero in an upper box. If your Yahtzee box holds 0, there is no bonus and no joker: a Yahtzee hand in the Full House box scores 0, exactly as the classic rules say.

What the advisor does not see: the advisor prices every box at face value only. It does not price the +35 upper bonus — a Sixes box late in the game can be worth more than its face when it carries you over 63 — and it does not price Yahtzee-bonus or joker effects. It is a per-turn advisor: it maximises the expected points banked this turn, which is not the same as whole-game optimal play. The lesson page says why, plainly.

The dice are a random number generator: every face 1 in 6, every roll independent, no luck and no memory. The scorecard, though, remembers everything — and that is where the game is.
