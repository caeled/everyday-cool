What this is

A silicon p–n junction you can bias. Drag the voltage from −5 V (reverse) through 0 to +1.0 V (forward). Watch the pale depletion region in the middle widen under reverse bias and narrow under forward bias, and watch the operating point ride the I–V curve.

Controls

Bias slider / box: applied voltage V, in volts. Steps are 5 mV so you can creep up on the knee near 0.6–0.7 V. Doping: acceptor density Na (P side) and donor density Nd (N side), in cm⁻³. One-sided presets put almost all the depletion width on the lightly doped side. Plot: Linear shows the familiar knee; Log shows |I| on a log scale, where the straight-line stretch is the exponential — about 59.6 mV per decade at 300 K.

What to try

1) Sit at 0 V, then go to −5 V: current barely changes (it saturates near −Is) but W more than doubles. 2) Step forward in 59.6 mV increments above ~0.3 V and watch the current multiply by ten each step. 3) Switch doping and read Vbi: heavier doping raises the built-in potential, slowly — it is a logarithm.

The model

Shockley equation I = Is·(eV/(n·Vt) − 1), n = 1, Is = 1.0×10⁻¹⁴ A (a chosen representative value for a small silicon diode — see the lesson). Vt = kT/q at a fixed T = 300 K (temperature is fixed in this exhibit, not a slider). Vbi = Vt·ln(Na·Nd/ni²), ni = 1.0×10¹⁰ cm⁻³. W = √((2εs/q)(1/Na + 1/Nd)(Vbi − V)), εs = 11.7ε₀. Every number on this page is computed by the same pure core the on-page proofs test.

Honest bends

Abrupt, one-dimensional junction; no breakdown (real junctions avalanche or Zener well before −5 V in some dopings — this one just saturates); no series resistance, so forward current past ~0.8 V is larger than a real diode's; no high-injection or recombination current (real n is often 1–2). Near and above Vbi the depletion formula runs out of road, so W is floored at a small value rather than going imaginary.

Diode Junction

Two pieces of silicon, one boundary. The P side is rich in holes, the N side rich in electrons, and between them sits a depleted strip with a built-in voltage of its own. Bias it one way and the strip widens and nothing flows; bias it the other and the strip narrows and current grows — not proportionally, but exponentially, ten times for every extra 59.6 mV.
