What this is

Transient Lab is a working oscilloscope for the two circuits that teach you how electronics behaves in time, not just at rest. Flip a switch and a capacitor does not jump to the supply voltage — it climbs (or falls) along an exponential curve whose speed is set by one number, the time constant τ = RC. Add an inductor and the circuit stops creeping and starts ringing: energy sloshes between the capacitor's electric field and the inductor's magnetic field while the resistor bleeds a little off on every pass. Everything on the scope is integrated live from the circuit equations by the simulation core in this page — no pre-drawn curves, no formula pasted onto the screen.

Every control

  • RC / RLC mode toggle — switches the circuit. RC: source, resistor, capacitor in series. RLC: source, resistor, inductor, capacitor in series. Each mode remembers its own resistance setting, because the useful ranges are very different.
  • Source voltage V0 (1–12 V) — the step the circuit is hit with when you press Charge, and the voltage the capacitor starts from when you press Discharge.
  • R slider — series resistance, logarithmic. RC mode: 100 Ω – 10 kΩ. RLC mode: 2 Ω – 400 Ω. In RC it sets τ together with C. In RLC it sets the damping: small R rings for a long time, large R smothers the ring.
  • C slider (RC mode) — capacitance, 1 µF – 1000 µF, logarithmic. Bigger C = slower exponential: τ grows in direct proportion.
  • L slider (RLC mode) — inductance, 10 mH – 1 H, logarithmic. Together with C it sets the natural frequency f₀ = 1/(2π√(LC)); together with R it sets the decay rate α = R/(2L).
  • ⚡ Charge — connects the source (capacitor starts empty, source at V0) and sweeps the trace. ↯ Discharge — disconnects the source (source at 0 V) with the capacitor starting full at V0, and sweeps again.
  • Time scale — stretches or squeezes the scope window around its natural size (5τ in RC mode, about six ring periods in RLC mode). It changes only what you see, never what the circuit does.
  • Show current I(t) — overlays the series current on its own scale (amber). The current's own peak is printed under the scope, because volts and amps share the glass but not a scale.
  • Run proofs — re-runs the built-in proof harness against the exact core this page ships, and prints claim vs. measured for each check.

The physics

RC: Kirchhoff around the loop gives RC · dVc/dt + Vc = Vs. Charging from empty, the solution is Vc(t) = V0(1 − e^(−t/τ)) with τ = RC: at t = τ the capacitor has reached 63.2% of V0, at 2τ it is at 86.5%, at 4τ at 98.2% — always the same fraction of the remaining distance per τ, which is what makes it an exponential and not a ramp. Discharging into a short, Vc(t) = V0 · e^(−t/τ): 36.8% left after one τ. The lab does not plot these formulas; it integrates the differential equation step by step (exact exponential update per step, which is the exact solution of the linear ODE over that step) and the formulas are what the proofs check the integration against.

RLC: the series loop is a second-order ODE, L · dI/dt + R·I + Vc = Vs with dVc/dt = I/C, integrated here with RK4 on fine substeps. Its character is set by two rates: the natural frequency ω₀ = 1/√(LC) and the damping rate α = R/(2L). If α < ω₀ the circuit is underdamped and rings at the damped frequency ωd = √(ω₀² − α²), inside an envelope that decays as e^(−αt). If α > ω₀ it is overdamped and just creeps, like a heavier RC. ζ = α/ω₀ is the damping ratio; ζ = 1 is the critically damped boundary.

Simplifications, named

  • Components are ideal: no capacitor ESR or leakage, no inductor winding resistance beyond the R you dial in, no source resistance, no switch bounce, noiseless wires.
  • The source is a perfect step at t = 0. Real supplies slew; the interesting physics here starts after the step.
  • The scope is ideal too: infinite input impedance, no bandwidth limit, no quantisation — it reads the simulated state exactly.
  • RC steps use the exact exponential update for the linear ODE (exact per step, not an Euler approximation). RLC steps use RK4 with substeps fine enough that the proof harness — not optimism — sets the accuracy claim.
