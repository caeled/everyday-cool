What this is

One resistor and one capacitor — the simplest real filter in electronics — wired two ways. As a low-pass (output taken across the capacitor) it lets slow signals through and rolls off fast ones. As a high-pass (output taken across the resistor) it does the mirror image. The big chart is a Bode plot: how much the filter scales a sine wave (top, in decibels) and how much it shifts it in time (bottom, phase in degrees), drawn across frequency on a logarithmic axis. Everything on this page is computed live from the exact complex transfer functions — nothing is sketched or faked.

Every control

  • LP / HP buttons — choose low-pass or high-pass wiring.
  • R slider — resistance, 100 Ω to 100 kΩ, logarithmic.
  • C slider — capacitance, 1 nF to 10 µF, logarithmic.
  • Probe slider — picks one frequency to interrogate, 1 Hz to 1 MHz, logarithmic. You can also click or drag directly on the Bode plot to move the probe.
  • “Set probe = fc” button — snaps the probe to the cutoff frequency, the most interesting spot on the whole chart.

The physics

The capacitor's reactance falls as frequency rises, so the R–C pair acts as a frequency-dependent voltage divider. The corner where the two impedances are equal is the cutoff frequency fc = 1 / (2πRC). At fc the output is exactly 1/√2 of the input — −3.01 dB — and the phase shift is exactly 45°. Far past fc a single RC stage rolls off at 20 dB per decade (a factor of 10 in amplitude for every factor of 10 in frequency).

The transfer functions, with x = ωRC = f / fc:

  • Low-pass: H(jω) = 1 / (1 + jωRC)
  • High-pass: H(jω) = jωRC / (1 + jωRC)

The time-domain inset

The lower scope does not just rescale a sine by |H|. It integrates the actual circuit equation RC · dv/dt = vin − v step by step on the input sine (exact exponential update per step), waits for the transient to settle, and only then draws the input and output. The amplitude sag and phase lag you see are the simulation's own.

Simplifications, named

  • Ideal components: no capacitor leakage or ESR, no resistor parasitics, a perfect zero-impedance source and an unloaded output.
  • The Bode curves are the exact steady-state phasor result; the scope is a numerical integration of the same circuit, so the two agree to integration accuracy, not bit-for-bit.
  • One pole only: a real multi-stage filter rolls off steeper (20 dB/decade per pole).
