Don't Cook the LED — an Ohm's-law game

Seven circuits. One drawer of resistors. A bin of LEDs.

An LED will eat as much current as you let it have, and die of it. The resistor is the part that says no — politely, in ohms. Each job gives you a supply, an LED, and the current it wants. Pick a resistor from the drawer, press Power it, and land the current inside the band: too much cooks the LED, too little leaves it a night-light. Cook three on one circuit and the parts bin refills the LED, gently, on the same circuit.

The sums are one line long — the leftover voltage divided by the resistance — and the 📖 Lesson button up top opens the back door: the physics, the math, and the worked answer for every level, readable while you play.

Every one of those was the same single line of Ohm's law: the LED keeps its voltage, the resistor drops what's left, and the current is the leftover divided by the ohms. That is the whole secret life of the LED resistor bench in the room behind you — and the 📖 Lesson has the worked sums, the E12 story, and the honest simplifications, if you want the receipts.
