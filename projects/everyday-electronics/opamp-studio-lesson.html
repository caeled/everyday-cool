Op-Amp Studio — the lesson

Eight classic circuits around one op-amp — inverting, non-inverting, follower, summing mixer, integrator, differentiator, Schmitt trigger and Wien oscillator — drawn as one big animated schematic with a scope, a transfer monitor and a telemetry panel. This page is the other half: the formulas, the numbers measured by driving the shipped code itself, an honest list of where it bends, and a side-by-side with Muse's smaller Op-Amp Bench on the same configurations. Verdict up front: the Studio functions as shipped, and its core arithmetic is excellent — gains, clipping, integration, differentiation, Schmitt thresholds, Wien frequency and the slew limiter all measured within a fraction of a percent of theory, with the named exceptions below.

⚠ False and misleading claims — flagged first

FALSE — “Ideal (Fast)” slew. With the slew limiter switched off, the telemetry reads “Ideal (Fast)”, implying no slew limit at all. There is one: the code caps every output move at a hidden 1.5×10⁶ V/s. Driven on a Schmitt-trigger rail-to-rail jump, the measured maximum slope was exactly 1.500 V/µs (1,500 V/ms) — finite, and never disclosed on the page. It is fast enough that ordinary sine work never notices, but “ideal” it is not.

MISLEADING SCALE — the slew slider. The slew control is labelled in V/ms and runs 3–40 V/ms, i.e. 0.003–0.040 V/µs. Even a humble 741 slews at about 0.5 V/µs = 500 V/ms — more than ten times the slider's maximum. The limiter itself is honest (set 12 V/ms, measured exactly 12.00 V/ms; set 3 and 40, measured 3.00 and 40.00), but do not read those numbers as typical real-part values.

UNDISCLOSED — the integrator is not the pure integrator on its formula box. The page states Vout = −(1/RinC) ∫ Vin dt. The code quietly adds a bleed resistor of 35 × Rin across the feedback capacitor. At audio frequencies the difference is invisible (see the sine proof below), but on DC it is not: a 1 V DC input at RC = 1 ms should ramp at −1,000 V/s and instead measured −931 V/s over the 1–4 ms window (−6.9%), bending toward a −35 V asymptote instead of ramping forever. That is actually what real integrators do with a stabilising resistor — it just isn't told to you.

No other on-page claim failed. In particular the supply, gain, threshold and oscillator formulas printed in the Studio all checked out against its own driven output, below.

What this is

An operational amplifier outputs roughly A·(V+ − V−). The Studio models a finite open-loop gain AOL = 100,000 (100 dB, as its transfer monitor says), then lets negative feedback do the taming. Its two golden rules are printed in its own drawer and they are stated correctly: no current into the inputs, and — under negative feedback, while unclipped — V− ≈ V+, the virtual short (virtual ground when V+ is grounded). Because AOL is finite, the virtual short is not exact here: at the default inverting gain of −2 with a 5 V peak output, the measured V− error peaks at 0.0500 mV — exactly Vout/AOL, and exactly the 0.05 mV its telemetry placeholder shows.

The topologies, in one line each

Inverting: Vout = −(Rf/Rin) · Vin  ·  Non-inverting: Vout = (1 + Rf/Rin) · Vin Follower: gain exactly 1. Summing: Vout = −(Rf/R₁·V₁ + Rf/R₂·V₂). There is no difference (subtractor) amplifier — summing is the only multi-input topology offered.
Integrator: Vout = −(1/RinC) ∫ Vin dt  ·  Differentiator: Vout = −RfC · dVin/dt A sine into the integrator comes out a cosine shape of amplitude A/(2πfRC); a triangle into the differentiator comes out a square of amplitude RfC × (triangle slope).
Schmitt: VTH± = ±Vsat · Rin/(Rin + Rf)  ·  Wien: f₀ = 1/(2πRC), needs gain ≥ 3 Positive feedback in the Schmitt makes two thresholds instead of one; the Wien bridge makes a sine out of no input at all, if the gain condition holds.

How this was audited

The Studio's own shipped stepCircuit() engine — the exact code in opamp-studio.html (sha256 ac4fe52a…, guest file untouched) — was extracted and driven headlessly in Node, with its own topologies and values configured the way its sliders configure them, and the output read back off the simulated trace. “Measured” below means that driven output, not the formula re-typed. Independent references are the textbook formulas computed separately. One method note that mattered: the integrator and Wien results were measured in steady state (after 1–3 s of simulated time); early-window readings are polluted by start-up transients and were discarded — an early integrator-sine reading of 0.689 V settled to 0.63665 V once the circuit actually settled.

Where it bends — named honestly

Finite gain, no bandwidth. AOL = 100,000 at every frequency: gains are flat from 60 Hz to 800 Hz and beyond, with no gain-bandwidth roll-off or phase shift. A real part's gain falls with frequency; this one's never does.

Hidden slew cap when “off”. 1.5 V/µs, measured above. With the limiter on, the limiter is exact — but on a V/ms scale 10–100× slower than real op-amps.

Integrator bleed resistor (35 × Rin) and soft windup bound. The bleed bends DC and low-frequency integration (−6.9% on the DC ramp, −0.8% on the 220 Hz triangle, negligible at the 50 Hz sine test) and caps the DC asymptote at −35 × Vin; the internal charge is additionally soft-clamped at 1.45 × the rail, so a long-railed integrator recovers differently from a pure one.

Differentiator series resistor. A small series resistor (0.032 × Rf, minimum 80 Ω) tames the differentiator — standard real-world practice, and why its sine and triangle answers still land within 0.1% while its square-wave spikes stay finite (they clip at the rail rather than running away).

Saturation is a hard clip exactly at the rails. Same modelling choice as the Bench: real parts round off near the rails, often can't reach them, and recover slowly. The Schmitt trigger is the honourable exception — railing is its job, and the Studio correctly doesn't flag it as clipping.

No offset voltage, no bias current, no noise of its own. The “Noisy” input is a signal choice, not device noise. Nothing drifts, nothing offsets, components are exact.

Wien AGC is invented, kindly. A real Wien oscillator needs a lamp or JFET to settle its amplitude; the Studio uses a smooth mathematical envelope tracker instead. It works — 160 Hz against 159.2 Hz theory, sustaining near 8 V peak at gain 3.2, and correctly refusing to sustain at gain 2 — but no physical Wien bridge stabilises quite that cleanly.
