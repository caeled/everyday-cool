PN Junction — the lesson

This guest exhibit — “Diode Lab” — takes the same P–N junction as Muse's own Diode Junction bench and drives it much harder: six device presets (silicon rectifier, red and blue LEDs, Zener regulator, Schottky, solar cell), a bandgap slider that tunes the emitted colour, a temperature slider, a series resistor with a genuine load-line solve, photocurrent, and Zener breakdown. This page is the audit: the shipped code was driven headlessly in Node (extracted as-is, DOM and canvas stubbed, guest file untouched — sha256 a5055af9…), its controls were swept, and every number below is what the engine actually computes, checked against independent calculations.

The exhibit was built by Google Antigravity (Gemini) for Everyday Cool, at Steven Powell's direction. The verdict up front: the solver machinery is real and mostly sound — but the built-in potential is not the formula the page displays, and one preset, the Blue LED, cannot work as shipped. Details, with receipts, below.

⚠ False claims — flagged first, prominently

1. The displayed Vbi formula is not what the code computes. In equilibrium the exhibit's own explainer prints V_bi = (kT/q) ln(N_a N_d / n_i²). The shipped computeBuiltInPotential() never evaluates that logarithm and contains no ni anywhere. It computes a bandgap-linear heuristic: V_bi = E_g − 0.14 + 0.055·(log₁₀N − 16.8) − 0.0017·(T − 300), clamped. For silicon at Na = Nd = 1×10¹⁶ cm⁻³ it returns 0.936 V; the displayed formula (ni = 1.0×10¹⁰ cm⁻³) gives 0.7143 V — the guest is high by 0.222 V, about 31%. Its doping slope is also wrong: 55 mV per decade of doping where the real logarithm gives 119.1 mV per decade (both sides together). There is no single ni the guest “effectively uses”: forcing its Vbi back through the real formula implies ni ≈ 4.0×10⁷ cm⁻³ at 1×10¹⁵ doping, 1.37×10⁸ at 1×10¹⁶, and 3.0×10⁹ at 3.16×10¹⁸ — the implied ni itself slides with the slider, which is the signature of a heuristic, not the physics.

2. The Blue GaN LED preset cannot emit as shipped. The diode's exponential argument is clamped at 36 (clamp(vD/(n·V_T), −80, 36)). The blue preset (Eg = 2.78 eV, n = 2.1) has Is = 2.59×10⁻²⁰ A, so reaching even its own 8 mA calibration target needs an argument of 40.3 — past the clamp. Its Shockley current therefore caps at Is·e³⁶ = 0.111 mA at any voltage. Driven at its own preset (Vbias = +3.30 V, R = 47 Ω) the solver returns Vd = 3.295 V, I = 0.111 mA, and the regime badge logic lands on “Sub-Threshold” — never “LED Emitting!”. The blue preset is broken in the shipped file; per the audit rules the guest file was not edited, so this is reported, not repaired.

3. “EXACT SHOCKLEY” (the solver's own header comment) overstates the saturation current. Is is not derived from doping, diffusion or lifetimes. The code reverse-calibrates it from an invented knee, Vknee = clamp(0.88·Eg − 0.26) with a −2 mV/K drift, chosen so the diode passes exactly 8 mA at that knee. That calibration is admitted in a code comment but nowhere on the page, where the Shockley equation is presented without the caveat. (The sibling bench, by contrast, labels its chosen Is openly.)

4. The breakdown branch is unbounded outside the circuit solve. The Zener term is 0.0018·exp(−(Vd+VZ)/0.075) with the exponent clamped only at +30. Called directly, diodeCurrentAtV(−6 V) with VZ = 5.1 returns −293 A, and at −7 V about −1.8×10⁸ A. Only the series resistor in the load-line solver keeps real operating points sane (the Zener preset itself solves to a plausible −5.25 V at −12.8 mA through 82 Ω from a −6.3 V supply). The drawn I–V inset simply clips this.

5. Stale numbers in the static HTML. The telemetry values hard-coded in the page markup (+1.88 V, +8.38 mA, W = 54 nm, barrier 0.08 eV, 29 kV/cm, 15.8 mW) match nothing the solver produces: at boot the shipped code solves the red-LED preset to Vd = +1.44 V, I = +14.83 mA, W = 94 nm, barrier 0.33 eV, E = 70 kV/cm, 21.4 mW. The live HUD overwrites the stale values on the first animation frame, so a visitor never sees them — but they are wrong in the source.

6. “Photon λ” is displayed for devices that emit no photons. The telemetry always shows λ = 1239.84/Eg: the silicon rectifier reads 1107 nm and the Schottky 1722 nm. That is the bandgap wavelength, not an emission — only the LED presets emit, and only when the LED-mode toggle is on.

What this is

Press P-type and N-type semiconductor together and the boundary empties of free carriers — the depletion region — leaving fixed ionic charge, a built-in field, and a built-in potential Vbi. This exhibit wraps that junction in a real external circuit: a supply Vbias (−8 to +3.5 V) behind a series resistor R (15–1500 Ω), and a bisection solver finds the diode voltage Vd where the Shockley current equals the load-line current (Vbias − Vd)/R. That architecture is genuinely good — it is why the LED presets land at sensible currents instead of running away exponentially, and why the Zener preset regulates. Around the solver sit the bandgap/colour system (λ = hc/Eg), a photocurrent term for the solar preset, and a particle/band/I–V visualisation. Doping is a single symmetric slider, Na = Nd from 1×10¹⁵ to 3.16×10¹⁸ cm⁻³; temperature runs 200–450 K.

The physics — what the code actually runs

Thermal voltage — exact, and temperature is real here

V_T = kT/q — 25.852 mV at the default T = 300 K Computed from the exact SI constants k = 1.380649×10⁻²³ J/K and q = 1.602176634×10⁻¹⁹ C, the same values the sibling bench uses. Unlike the sibling, this exhibit has a temperature slider (200–450 K): driven, V_T = 17.235 mV at 200 K and 38.778 mV at 450 K, exactly kT/q. The page's stated default temperature is 300 K and its HUD prints V_T = 25.9 mV — correct.

Built-in potential — a heuristic wearing the logarithm's clothes

Code: V_bi = clamp( E_g − 0.14 + 0.055·(log₁₀N − 16.8) − 0.0017·(T − 300) ) Displayed on the page (equilibrium explainer): V_bi = V_T·ln(N_a·N_d/n_i²). The two agree nowhere across the doping slider — see the measured table below. The Schottky preset uses a separate heuristic, ≈ 0.36 V at its doping, which is at least the right neighbourhood for a metal–semiconductor barrier.

Depletion width — the formula itself is right

W = √( (2ε_s/q) · (2/N) · max(0.025, V_bi − V_d) ), ε_s = ε_r·ε₀ For the symmetric doping this exhibit offers (N_a = N_d = N), 1/N_a + 1/N_d = 2/N, so this is exactly the textbook abrupt-junction result — in SI units, correctly converted. It is driven by V_d, the solved diode voltage, not the raw supply, which is the physically right choice in a circuit. Because it inherits the guest's inflated V_bi, its absolute widths run large (next section), but its scaling is exact: measured below. Peak field E_max = 2(V_bi − V_d)/W is also the correct triangular-field result.

The Shockley branch — a true exponential, with a preset ideality factor

I = I_s · ( eV_d/(n·V_T) − 1 ) − I_photo − I_Zener Ideality n is a per-preset constant, not a user control: silicon 1.45, red LED 1.85, blue LED 2.1, Zener 1.35, Schottky 1.15, solar 1.35. The decade slope is therefore n × 59.53 mV at 300 K — and that is exactly what driving the shipped function measures, preset by preset (table below). There is no single “59.5 mV/decade” in this exhibit, and the page never claims one; the honest statement is that its exponential is internally exact given its n.

The one-sentence summary: the two engines agree on everything that is a ratio, a scaling or a thermal voltage, and disagree on the one absolute number the guest fakes — Vbi — from which its absolute depletion widths inherit the error.

Where this bends — named honestly

Vbi is a bandgap heuristic, not the junction formula. The largest bend, detailed as False Claim 1: Eg − 0.14 plus small doping and temperature terms. It lands in a plausible range for LEDs (where Vbi does track Eg), which is likely why it was chosen — but for silicon it is up to 0.29 V high and its doping slope is half the real one.

Is is calibrated to a knee, not derived. Vknee = 0.88·Eg − 0.26 and “8 mA at the knee” are inventions that make every preset turn on at a photogenic voltage. Absolute currents are therefore staged; slopes and load-line behaviour are the trustworthy outputs.

The exponential is clamped at argument 36. A display-safety guard that silently caps every forward current at Is·e³⁶. Harmless for most presets (the silicon cap is an absurd 1.35×10⁵ A), fatal for the blue LED (cap 0.111 mA — False Claim 2).

Depletion width is display-clamped to 12–2400 nm, and the barrier is floored at 0.025 V. Driven at 1×10¹⁵ doping in deep reverse, the formula's 4150 nm is shown as 2400 nm. In strong forward bias the width stops shrinking at the floor instead of following √(Vbi − V) to zero — the same guard the sibling bench declares for its own floor.

Symmetric doping only. One slider sets Na = Nd. Real junctions are usually one-sided, with the width living on the lightly doped side; this engine cannot show that (the sibling bench can).

Ideality is a preset costume, not a control. n ranges 1.15–2.1 by device and cannot be explored; the blue LED's 2.1 sits just past the usual 1–2 textbook span.

Breakdown is a soft exponential knee of fixed 75 mV width for every device and doping, with no distinction between Zener tunnelling and avalanche multiplication, and no bound outside the circuit solve (False Claim 4).

Photocurrent is a chosen scale. Iphoto tops out near 39 mA at full “sun” through an absorption-efficiency fudge; the solar preset's 0.768 V operating voltage is optimistic for silicon.

The Schottky preset reuses Eg = 0.72 eV as a barrier parameter — reasonable for its Vbi heuristic (≈0.36 V, solved drop 0.27 V), but it is not a bandgap, and it is what feeds the meaningless 1722 nm “photon λ” readout (False Claim 6).

What is genuinely right, for the record: V_T from exact constants with a working temperature slider; the depletion formula and its √ scaling, exact to four decimals; E_max = 2ΔV/W; λ = hc/Eg (653 nm red, 446 nm blue, both correct); the built-in field direction N → P in the legend; and a real bisection load-line solve against a series resistor — the circuit behaviour of the working presets is honest.
