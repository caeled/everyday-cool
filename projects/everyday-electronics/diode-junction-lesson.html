Diode Junction — the lesson

Every diode, LED, transistor junction and solar cell starts here: P-type silicon joined to N-type silicon. Nothing is connected, nothing is moving — and yet the boundary between them already has a voltage, a width, and an opinion about which way current may go. The exhibit lets you bias that boundary from −5 V to +1.0 V and watch both consequences at once: the depletion region breathing in and out, and the current answering exponentially. This page is the other half — the physics, the exact formulas the exhibit runs, and the proofs that the shipped code actually does what it claims.

What this is

Dope silicon with acceptors and it becomes P-type: full of holes — missing-electron spots that behave like positive carriers. Dope it with donors and it becomes N-type: full of free electrons. Press the two together and carriers near the boundary diffuse across and recombine, leaving behind a thin strip with no free carriers at all — the depletion region — and a layer of exposed, fixed ionic charge on each side of it. That fixed charge makes an electric field, and the field makes a voltage you never applied: the built-in potential, Vbi.

An external bias fights or feeds that built-in voltage. Reverse bias (negative V) adds to it: the depletion region widens, the barrier grows, and essentially no current flows. Forward bias subtracts from it: the region narrows, the barrier falls, and carriers pour over it — in numbers that grow exponentially with every millivolt you add.

The physics

Thermal voltage — the scale of everything

Vt = kT/q = 25.852 mV at T = 300 K Computed in the core from k = 1.380649×10⁻²³ J/K and q = 1.602176634×10⁻¹⁹ C — not typed in as 25.85. Temperature is fixed at 300 K in this exhibit; there is no temperature slider, and the game page says so.

Vt is the natural voltage unit of any semiconductor junction at a given temperature. Every exponential in this lesson is measured against it.

Built-in potential

Vbi = Vt · ln(Na·Nd / ni²) = 0.7143 V for Na = Nd = 1×10¹⁶ cm⁻³ With ni = 1.0×10¹⁰ cm⁻³ (the silicon intrinsic carrier density used throughout this exhibit): ln(10³²/10²⁰) = ln(10¹²) ≈ 27.631, × 25.852 mV ≈ 0.7143 V. Note the logarithm — multiplying both dopings by ten raises Vbi by only about 119 mV.

Depletion width

W = √( (2εs/q) · (1/Na + 1/Nd) · (Vbi − V) ) εs = 11.7·ε₀, with ε₀ = 8.854×10⁻¹⁴ F/cm so everything stays in centimetres. At 0 V with symmetric 1×10¹⁶ doping the core gives W = 0.430 µm; at −5 V it gives 1.216 µm. The square root is the signature: quadrupling (Vbi − V) only doubles W.

Where does W live? Mostly on the lightly doped side. In the one-sided P⁺N preset (Na = 1×10¹⁸, Nd = 1×10¹⁵), the term 1/Nd dominates the sum, so almost the whole depletion width extends into the N side. The exhibit's doping presets let you feel that asymmetry through the readout even though the diagram draws the strip centred.

The Shockley equation — why 59.6 mV matters

I = Is · ( eV/(n·Vt) − 1 ), n = 1 Is = 1.0×10⁻¹⁴ A is a chosen representative value for a small silicon diode, stated openly — it is not derived from the doping in this model. That is why the proofs below lean on ratios and slopes, which do not depend on the Is pick, rather than on absolute forward currents.

Because the exponent is V/Vt, the step that multiplies current by ten is Vt·ln(10) — about 59.5 mV at 300 K (often rounded to “about 60 mV per decade”). Measured from the core's own currents between 0.30 V and 0.60 V, the exhibit's slope is 59.53 mV/decade. At 0.60 V the core current is 1.201×10⁻⁴ A (0.120 mA) with the chosen Is. In reverse, the exponential dies away and I settles to −Is: at −1 V the core gives −1.000×10⁻¹⁴ A.

Where this bends — named honestly

Abrupt-junction, one-sided-style depletion model. Real junctions have graded doping profiles; this one steps cleanly from Na to Nd, and the W formula is the textbook abrupt-junction result.

Is is chosen, not derived. A fuller model would compute Is from doping, diffusion constants and minority-carrier lifetimes. Here Is = 1.0×10⁻¹⁴ A is a representative pick, labelled as such in the game; slopes and ratios are the trustworthy outputs.

No breakdown. Real junctions break down in reverse — Zener or avalanche — sometimes well before −5 V. This model simply saturates at −Is all the way to −5 V.

No series resistance. A real diode's bulk resistance bends the forward curve over at high current. With none here, forward currents past ~0.8 V are larger than a physical small diode would carry (and run off the linear plot — the page says so).

No high-injection, no recombination current. The ideality factor is fixed at n = 1. Real silicon diodes often show n between 1 and 2 at low forward bias, which would soften the decade slope toward ~119 mV/decade in the worst case.

Depletion formula floored near Vbi. W ∝ √(Vbi − V) goes imaginary for V > Vbi, where the depletion approximation has already failed. The core floors (Vbi − V) at 0.005 V instead of returning NaN — a display guard, not physics.

Temperature fixed. T = 300 K throughout; ni in reality is fiercely temperature-dependent, which this exhibit does not model.
