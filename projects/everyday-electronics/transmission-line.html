What you are looking at

A signal travelling down a wire is not instant and it is not magic. This exhibit models a coaxial line as a ladder of tiny inductors and capacitors — the telegrapher's model — and lets you fire a voltage step or pulse down it and watch what comes back from the far end.

How to play

Pick a load, then press Fire step (a step that stays up — the cleanest way to read a reflection) or Fire pulse (a step that turns off after the pulse width you set). Watch the glowing voltage profile run down the coax, hit the load, and — unless the load is matched — bounce. The scope at the bottom traces the voltage at the load end over time.

The loads

Open: the end of the line is cut. Nothing can leave, so the wave doubles back in phase: Γ = +1 and the load-end voltage doubles. Short: the end is a perfect conductor to ground; the voltage there must be zero, so the reflection comes back upside down: Γ = −1. Matched (50 Ω): the load equals the line's characteristic impedance Z₀, the wave is absorbed completely, and nothing returns: Γ = 0. Resistive loads in between give partial reflections, Γ = (ZL − Z₀)/(ZL + Z₀).

The line, stated plainly

Z₀ is fixed at 50 Ω. Each of the line's sections carries L = 2.5 µH and C = 1.0 nF, so Z₀ = √(L/C) = 50 Ω and one section of delay is √(LC) = 50 ns — the wave advances exactly one cell per simulation step. The source drives the line through its own 50 Ω source resistance (matched at the source end too, so a returning reflection is absorbed instead of bouncing again). The source amplitude is 2.0 V, so the step that actually launches into the line is the divider value, 1.0 V.

Reading the numbers

Measured Γ is not typed in — the page runs the same simulation core off-screen, reads the step height at a probe point before and after the reflection returns, and divides. Theory Γ is (ZL − Z₀)/(ZL + Z₀). One-way delay is the line length in cells × 50 ns per cell. Every number on this page is computed from the simulation core at the bottom of this file — the same core the proof harness runs in Node.

Honest bends

The line is lossless (no series resistance, no dielectric loss), so reflections never decay. The boundary currents at the source and load are solved implicitly each step, which is why the measured Γ lands essentially on theory. The fired step has a 10-step (500 ns) smooth rise, like a real generator: a mathematically perfect step excites a ripple at the lattice's Nyquist edge that decays only slowly (we measured it — see the lesson page). Γ is read on the flat top of the step, away from the edge, for exactly that reason.

Transmission Line — fire a step, watch it come back

Carriers all the way down: a ham operator's signal on a feedline and a packet on a network cable are the same physics — a wave on a wire, in a hurry, meeting an end it may or may not agree with. Match the end and the wave is swallowed whole. Miss, and it comes back to tell you.

Controls

Z₀ is fixed at 50 Ω. Source resistance is also 50 Ω, so the launched step is half the 2.0 V source — and a returning reflection is absorbed at the source, not re-bounced.
