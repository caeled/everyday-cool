RF Transmission Line — the lesson

The exhibit — titled “Waveguide” on its own screen — runs a continuous RF sine wave (or a TDR pulse) down a simulated 50 Ω coaxial line, draws the standing wave, the coax cross-section and a live Smith chart, and reports Γ, VSWR, return loss and delivered power for whatever load you hang on the end. Underneath, it is a real solver: a 260-cell FDTD integration of the telegrapher’s equations, not a cartoon of one. This page is the other half of the exhibit — the physics, the worked answers, and an audit of the shipped code, driven headlessly and measured, in the same spirit as the rest of the bench.

The exhibit is a guest build: it arrived as a finished page and is kept byte-for-byte as shipped (sha256 fca8c744…). Nothing in it was edited for this lesson. Where its numbers are right, the tables below say so with measurements. Where one of its readouts is wrong, that is flagged prominently — see the red box — not smoothed over.

What this is

A wave travelling on a line carries voltage and current in a fixed ratio set by the line itself — its characteristic impedance Z₀. When the wave reaches the end, the load either accepts that ratio or it doesn’t. The mismatch is summarised by one complex number, the reflection coefficient Γ: the reflected wave comes back scaled (and possibly phase-shifted) by Γ, the forward and reflected waves add up into a standing wave whose peaks and nulls sit still on the line, and the ratio of peak to null — the VSWR — is the number antenna and cable people actually quote. This exhibit lets you watch all of that at 1.5 GHz on a 54.17 cm length of simulated PTFE coax, drag the load around a Smith chart, fire a TDR pulse to locate the end of the cable by its echo, and insert a quarter-wave matching section to fix a mismatch. It is the continuous-wave, frequency-domain sibling of this bench’s own Transmission Line exhibit, which works the same physics in the time domain with a fired step; the two are compared head-to-head below.

Γ = (ZL − Z₀) / (ZL + Z₀) The reflection coefficient at the load. Open: +1. Short: −1. Matched 50 Ω: 0. 100 Ω: +1/3. 25 Ω: −1/3. A reactive load gives a complex Γ — for 50 + j75 Ω, Γ = 0.360 + j0.480, |Γ| = 0.600 at +53.13°.
VSWR = (1 + |Γ|) / (1 − |Γ|)  ·  return loss = −20·log₁₀|Γ| dB  ·  power reflected = |Γ|² |Γ| = 1/3 → VSWR 2.00, return loss 9.54 dB, 11.1% of the power reflected. |Γ| = 0.6 → VSWR 4.00, return loss 4.44 dB, 36% reflected. |Γ| = 1 (open or short) → VSWR ∞, everything reflected, nothing delivered.
vp = 2.0×10⁸ m/s = 0.667 c  ·  λ = vp / f The exhibit’s stated phase velocity for PTFE coax. At the default 1.50 GHz, λ = 13.33 cm — the page’s own readout says 13.3 cm, and the audit confirms it. The 260-cell line is 54.17 cm long = 4.0625 λ at 1.5 GHz, and one FDTD step is 10.0 ps.

How the audit was run

The shipped page’s script was extracted and run in Node with the DOM and canvas stubbed out — the exhibit’s own code, unedited, with a test hook added to the harness copy only so the internal state could be read. Loads were set through the exhibit’s own state fields (the same fields its sliders and preset buttons write), its own updateRFParameters() produced the telemetry it displays, and its own stepFDTD() was then run for thousands of steps. Measured Γ and VSWR below were not read off its telemetry: they were computed from the driven fields — a phasor (DFT) fit of the voltage at every cell over 2,000 steps of steady CW, giving the standing-wave envelope Vmax/Vmin directly, plus the positions of the envelope minima. Independent references were computed from the formulas above. The guest file on disk was not touched; its hash is the same after the audit as before it.

Propagation — speed, delay, TDR

A Gaussian TDR pulse was fired and its first arrival (and peak) tracked cell by cell on a matched line: the wavefront advances at 1.044 steps per cell, against the 1 / Courant = 1.0417 steps per cell the grid’s own constants predict (0.2% — ordinary FDTD dispersion at Courant 0.96). One way down 260 cells is therefore ≈ 271 steps × 10.0 ps = 2.71 ns, which is the physical answer for 54.17 cm at 2.0×10⁸ m/s (2.708 ns), and the round trip the exhibit prints in TDR mode — (2 × 260 / 0.96) × 10 ps = 5.42 ns — is the same number doubled. The claimed velocity factor checks too: 2.0×10⁸ / c = 0.667, the textbook figure for PTFE dielectric. Wavelength readouts were verified at five frequencies (0.5–3.0 GHz): λ = vp/f to the displayed digit every time. The open-circuit echo in TDR mode returns at full height (+0.997 measured in the reflected-wave array at the probe, Γ = +1) with the right sign; a short returns inverted.

The quarter-wave section

With a 200 Ω load and the λ/4 section inserted at 1.5 GHz, the exhibit computes Zm = √(50 × 200) = 100.0 Ω and a transformed input impedance of exactly 50 Ω. The driven fields back it: the standing wave inside the section is still there (envelope 2.00 / 1.00 across the section and load, as it must be — the section is itself mismatched to the load), but on the main line before the section the phasor-fit envelope is flat — Vmax/Vmin = 1.0012 / 1.0011, main-line VSWR 1.0001. The match is real, in the simulation and not just in the readout. Where it bends is named below: the section length is a whole number of cells, so off the design frequencies the match is only approximate.

Where they differ is in what they add around that core. The sibling is a step-and-watch time-domain bench: one speed (exactly one cell per step), resistive loads only, Γ read off a probe plateau. Neither contradicts the other; the guest build is the wider instrument, the sibling the plainer one.

⚠ A false claim, flagged

The Zin readout is wrong for mismatched loads (λ/4 section off). The panel’s “Zin” simply repeats the load impedance: set a 100 Ω load and it displays Zin = 100.0 Ω. But the input impedance of a lossless line is the load impedance rotated along the line, and this line is 4.0625 λ long at 1.5 GHz — not a whole number of half-wavelengths — so the rotation does not come back to where it started. The true input impedance of the exhibit’s own line, from Zin = Z₀·(ZL + jZ₀·tan βl) / (Z₀ + jZL·tan βl) with βl ≡ 22.5° (mod 180°), is 69.5 − j36.9 Ω for the 100 Ω load — not 100 Ω; for a 200 Ω load it is 62.6 − j83.0 Ω, not 200 Ω; for 50 + j75 Ω it is 186.0 + j49.3 Ω, not 50 + j75 Ω. The readout is only correct when the load is matched, open or shorted (the rotation-invariant cases), or when the line happens to be a whole number of half-wavelengths long. The same omission affects the phase of the displayed Γ if it is read as an input reflection coefficient: the phase shown is the load’s phase, while at the input the phase has rotated by −45° at the default frequency. The magnitudes — |Γ|, VSWR, return loss, power split — are unaffected, because a lossless rotation preserves |Γ|; those readouts remain exact, as the tables above show. With the λ/4 section on, the displayed Zin is the impedance looking into the section (correctly computed through it), which is a defensible reading — it is the no-section case that equates Zin with ZL outright.

Bends named honestly

The λ/4 section is a whole number of cells. Its length is round(λ/4) cells. At 1.5 GHz that is exact (16 of 64 cells) and the measured main-line VSWR is 1.0001. At 1.30 GHz, λ/4 is 18.46 cells, the section ships as 18, and the exhibit’s own telemetry honestly degrades to VSWR 1.061 — good, not the “VSWR = 1.00” the panel text promises. That promise holds at the design frequency, not across the frequency slider. The section impedance is also clamped to 10–240 Ω and, for reactive loads, is computed from |ZL| rather than RL — the panel text itself scopes the claim to real loads, and for real loads it is exact.

It is a transformer, not a stub, and the exhibit is not a waveguide. The preset labelled “Quarter-wave matching stub” inserts a series λ/4 section of different-Z₀ line — a quarter-wave transformer. A stub is a shunted length of line; this is not one. Likewise the exhibit’s title, “Waveguide”: the physics simulated is the TEM mode of a coaxial line, not a hollow waveguide (no cutoff frequency, no TE/TM modes). The names are loose; the maths under them is the coax maths, correctly done.

Telemetry ignores the loss slider. The attenuation control (α, Np/m) genuinely acts on the FDTD fields — at α = 1.6 Np/m the driven envelope near the source collapses toward a travelling wave, as a lossy line’s should — but the Γ / VSWR / return-loss readouts are computed from the load alone and do not move. On a real lossy line the input VSWR improves with loss; here the readouts are the load’s lossless values whatever α says. Read them as load quantities.

The TDR echo delay is computed, not measured. The echo readout’s delay is the code’s own (2N / Courant) × dt round-trip constant, filled in when the returning echo crosses a threshold — the threshold crossing is detected in the fields, but the number printed is the formula’s, so it cannot disagree with the formula. The audit’s independent tracking of the pulse (1.044 steps/cell) is what verifies the formula is right.

Boundary decomposition wobble. The forward/reflected split (V ± Z·I)/2 is exact in the interior but wobbles at the two boundary cells, where the current is a staggered-grid average: read at the load cell for an open, it gives Γ = 0.991 ∠−5.6° instead of exactly +1. The envelope and minima measurements — which is what the displayed traces rest on — are unaffected (interior |Γ| from the envelope: 0.9999). Don’t read Γ off the very last cell of its decomposition arrays.

“Unconditionally passive” — spot-checked, not proven. The panel text claims the complex termination is unconditionally passive. The audit ran the solver 20,000 steps on the hardest cases to hand (50 ± j75 Ω, 5 + j200 Ω, open with maximum α) and the fields stayed bounded at their steady-state amplitudes in every case — consistent with the claim, offered here as a spot check rather than a proof.

Small overshoots. Steady-state amplitudes sit ~0.1–0.25% high (matched line at 1.0012 V against an ideal 1.0000 V; open-line peaks at 2.0023 against 2.0000). That is the source boundary’s discretisation showing at the third decimal — visible because everything else is accurate enough for it to stand out, and immaterial to every readout the page displays.

Verdict

The exhibit functions as shipped and its core physics is right: Γ and VSWR exact in telemetry to six places and confirmed in the driven fields to ~0.1–0.6%; standing-wave minima spaced λ/2 and positioned by the load phase to within half a cell; propagation at the stated 0.667 c with the TDR round trip correct in physical time; the λ/4 transformer producing a measured main-line VSWR of 1.0001 at its design frequency. One readout — Zin with the λ/4 section off — is false for mismatched loads, for the reason given in the red box, and the remaining bends are naming, rounding and scope, each named above. That is a better score than most guest exhibits get, and the false readout is a display-side omission (the line’s rotation is simply never applied), not a defect in the solver underneath.
