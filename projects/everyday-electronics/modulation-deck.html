What am I looking at?

Three glowing traces, like a real communications bench. Top (amber): your message m(t) — the thing you want to send. Middle (cyan): the modulated carrier — a fast wave at fc whose size (AM) or spacing (FM) has been bent into the shape of the message. Bottom (mint): the recovered message, pulled back out of the carrier by the demodulator at the far end. If the bottom trace matches the top, the link worked.

Controls

Mode AM / FM — amplitude or frequency modulation. Message shape — sine, triangle, square, or a two-tone pair. fm — message frequency. fc — carrier frequency; the deck enforces fc ≥ 20·fm so the carrier is always a proper fast wave next to the message. μ (AM) — modulation index: how deep the message swings the carrier's size. μ = 1 is full; past 1 the envelope tries to go negative, the carrier flips phase, and a simple envelope detector recovers a mangled copy — try it. Δf (FM) — peak frequency deviation; β = Δf / fm is the FM modulation index. Detector RC (AM) — the time constant of the diode envelope detector. This slider deliberately spans the good regime and the failure regime.

The RC rule, and the failure you can dial in

An envelope detector is a diode that charges a capacitor to each carrier peak, and a resistor that lets it bleed down between peaks. It works when 1/fc ≪ RC ≪ 1/fm: long enough to smooth the carrier, short enough to follow the message. Make RC too small and the trace ripples at the carrier rate. Make it too large and you get diagonal clipping: on the falling edge of the envelope the capacitor simply cannot discharge fast enough, so the recovered trace saws diagonally across the message instead of following it. That is a real, named failure of real radios — here it is a feature you can dial in and watch, not a bug.

How it is built

Everything is synthesised sample by sample at fs = 48,000 Hz in a pure core with no DOM in it. AM: s(t) = (1 + μ·m̂(t))·cos(2πfct). FM: the phase is accumulated one sample at a time, φ += 2π(fc + Δf·m̂)/fs, so the instantaneous frequency is exactly the phase increment. The AM demodulator is the actual diode + RC circuit simulated per sample (instant charge to a higher peak, exponential RC discharge otherwise). The FM demodulator mixes the signal down to I/Q, takes successive phasor angles, and differentiates — a real discriminator. The proof panel under the bench re-runs the shipped core and checks the recovery correlations and the FM deviation against the maths. The 📖 Lesson page carries the full derivation and the proof table.

Modulation Deck — AM + FM

Radio is a trick for putting a slow message onto a fast wave. Bend the carrier's size and you have AM; bend its spacing and you have FM. This bench synthesises both, sample by sample at fs = 48,000 Hz, then demodulates them back — including the classic AM detector failure, which you can dial in yourself with the RC slider.
