# Collection origin

Original Everyday Cool workshop, added 18 November 2026 at Steven Powell's request. In the Everyday Cool ideas side chat that morning he proposed "Everyday Chemistry....atomic info on numbers calculating combinations simple experiments about the atom (visual?) maybe listings of fun kitchen experiments", decided it should be a NEW room in Everyday Cool (the 18th), and named it himself: "Everyday Chemistry". He added the banner's art direction in his own words: "also an image.. ai working a chemistry set? :)" — and said "Go — build Everyday Chemistry". Built by Muse (AI assistant) and added directly to the collection at his direction; this folder is the maintained original, and there is no upstream copy.

The room follows the Games Room / Radio Room shape (a verified shelf of legitimate places, things that work right on the page, a short workshop underneath) and the Find It / Radio Room pattern (Help ▾ menu with full documentation, banner verified on the page and inside the desktop app). The updated Everyday Cool desktop app ships as app v1.0.12 through the app repo's existing build workflow.

Everything interactive is computed in the visitor's browser from data baked into `chem.js` — the page sends nothing anywhere. Element data are the standard atomic weights (CIAAW conventional values) and textbook Bohr shell counts; neutron counts shown are for each element's most common isotope, and the page states that plainly, along with the fact that the Bohr picture is a teaching map, not a photograph. The eight kitchen experiments use only ordinary kitchen ingredients (vinegar, lemon juice, dish soap, salt, sugar, cabbage, milk, cornstarch, an egg); heat and hot-water steps are marked as a grown-up's job on the cards themselves.

The rules live in `chem.js` (no DOM), checked by `test.cjs`.

Banner: `everyday-chemistry-banner.jpg`, AI-generated watercolor to Steven's approved concept — a friendly robot with safety goggles pushed up on its head, working a chemistry set (test-tube rack, bubbling flask, beakers) at a kitchen counter, a small atom model beside a mug — generated 18 November 2026 as part of adding this room, in the style and dimensions (2736×912) of the collection's other banners.

Game (21 November 2026): at Steven Powell's direction, the room gained its first game — `balance-it.html`, **Balance It**: build molecules atom by atom against exact counts, then balance equations against a live per-element ledger. One recursive-descent formula parser (elements, subscripts, bracketed groups) serves the targets, the judge, and the proof; a balanced answer in non-smallest numbers is sent back to be reduced ("balanced, but reducible"). The build's proof caught an error in the commissioning brief itself — the doubled methane solution is 2,4,2,4, not 2,4,2,2, which does not balance — and the game and lesson teach with the corrected numbers, both facts asserted in the proof. `balance-it-lesson.html` is its back door (conservation of mass, the working balancing method as heuristics, worked levels, the ratio argument), opened from inside the game by a Lesson pop-up. `index.html` gained a game card in the Numbers Bench grid. The game also visits the Physics Game Table, the Arcade shelf, and both lesson shelves. Changed files: `balance-it.html`, `balance-it-lesson.html`, `index.html`, `README.md`, `COLLECTION.md`.

Staleness fix, 22 November 2026: `chem.js` — argon's standard atomic weight updated 39.948 → 39.95 (CIAAW's current conventional value).

The Chemistry Wave (23 November 2026): at Steven Powell's direction, Google Antigravity (Gemini) built nine full laboratories for this room, delivered as single self-contained pages and integrated byte-for-byte unchanged (sha256 verified against the uploads): `vsepr-molecular-3d.html`, `reaction-kinetics.html`, `acid-base-titration.html`, `electrochem-cell.html`, `spectroscopy-lab.html`, `bz-oscillator.html`, `organic-mechanisms.html`, `protein-polymer-fold.html`, `solvation-water.html`. Each was audited by Muse the way the physics exhibits were — the shipped code extracted and driven headlessly, its claims measured against real chemistry — and each gained a back-door lesson written by Muse printing the measured numbers and naming the bends: `vsepr-molecular-3d-lesson.html`, `reaction-kinetics-lesson.html`, `acid-base-titration-lesson.html`, `electrochem-cell-lesson.html`, `spectroscopy-lab-lesson.html`, `bz-oscillator-lesson.html`, `organic-mechanisms-lesson.html`, `protein-polymer-fold-lesson.html`, `solvation-water-lesson.html`. Headline audit results: the titration solver matches an independent charge-balance calculation to 1.1×10⁻¹³ pH; all 110 electrochem half-cell pairings and the Nernst slope are exact (its charge clock runs 12× fast, confessed in its own comment); spectroscopy's displayed lines sit within 0.05% of the real spectrum while 45 of 99 lines cannot be recomputed from their own level diagrams; the SN1 lesson in Organic Mechanisms flags a false "racemic mixture" label on achiral tert-butanol; Protein Fold's headline denaturation is announced but never simulated at any temperature. `index.html` gained the wave as its FIRST section (after the hero), plus a nav link. The wave also visits Dad's Arcade (new "Molecules & Reactions" shelf, counts 77 → 86), the Nerd Heaven lesson shelf, and the Java Young Robot Builders back doors (62–70, heading now "Seventy games").
