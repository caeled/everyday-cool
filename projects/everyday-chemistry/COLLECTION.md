# Collection origin

Original Everyday Cool workshop, added 18 November 2026 at Steven Powell's request. In the Everyday Cool ideas side chat that morning he proposed "Everyday Chemistry....atomic info on numbers calculating combinations simple experiments about the atom (visual?) maybe listings of fun kitchen experiments", decided it should be a NEW room in Everyday Cool (the 18th), and named it himself: "Everyday Chemistry". He added the banner's art direction in his own words: "also an image.. ai working a chemistry set? :)" — and said "Go — build Everyday Chemistry". Built by Muse (AI assistant) and added directly to the collection at his direction; this folder is the maintained original, and there is no upstream copy.

The room follows the Games Room / Radio Room shape (a verified shelf of legitimate places, things that work right on the page, a short workshop underneath) and the Find It / Radio Room pattern (Help ▾ menu with full documentation, banner verified on the page and inside the desktop app). The updated Everyday Cool desktop app ships as app v1.0.12 through the app repo's existing build workflow.

Everything interactive is computed in the visitor's browser from data baked into `chem.js` — the page sends nothing anywhere. Element data are the standard atomic weights (CIAAW conventional values) and textbook Bohr shell counts; neutron counts shown are for each element's most common isotope, and the page states that plainly, along with the fact that the Bohr picture is a teaching map, not a photograph. The eight kitchen experiments use only ordinary kitchen ingredients (vinegar, lemon juice, dish soap, salt, sugar, cabbage, milk, cornstarch, an egg); heat and hot-water steps are marked as a grown-up's job on the cards themselves.

The rules live in `chem.js` (no DOM), checked by `test.cjs`.

Banner: `everyday-chemistry-banner.jpg`, AI-generated watercolor to Steven's approved concept — a friendly robot with safety goggles pushed up on its head, working a chemistry set (test-tube rack, bubbling flask, beakers) at a kitchen counter, a small atom model beside a mug — generated 18 November 2026 as part of adding this room, in the style and dimensions (2736×912) of the collection's other banners.
