# Collection origin

Original Everyday Cool workshop, added 20 November 2026 at Steven Powell's direction. In the Everyday Cool ideas side chat that morning, Muse offered six candidate rooms; Steven picked four and placed them himself — "sound and time knots too, and light. (knots go in survival)" — then set the build order: Sound first, Time second, Light third. While Sound was being built he gave Light its design note in the same breath as Sound's music wing: Sound gets music theory because "music is basically math geometry with sound," and "same for light really with it's cord" — light gets colour theory, and its chords. Built by Muse (AI assistant) and added directly to the collection at his direction as the thirty-second catalog entry and the thirteenth workshop inside The STEM Room; this folder is the maintained original, and there is no upstream copy.

The room is built as a pair of twins. Its opening benches are twins: mixing light and mixing pigment, the same three sliders run by opposite rules — lamps summing toward white (red over green making yellow, the additive surprise), pigments multiplying toward dark, with real CMY's leak built into the model so the full mixture lands at a warm near-black instead of zero, and the printer's black cartridge explained by that failure. Its theory wing is a twin: where Everyday Sound arranges twelve notes by fifths into a circle and names the distances chords, this room arranges twelve hues around a wheel and strikes colour chords by angle — complementary at 180°, triads at 120° steps — with the pairing stated on the bench and the angles handed to Everyday Math. Around those twins stand the physics benches: a thin-lens ray diagram that lands its image upside down and turns into a magnifying glass inside the focal length; a Rayleigh bench that computes the (650/450)⁴ ≈ 4.35 scattering ratio and reads both the blue sky and the red sunset off it; a shadow bench walking a stick's shadow through the day on a compass rose, the same geometry as Everyday Time's sundial and the Survival Guide's shadow stick; and a spectrum slider that runs the visible octave and labels the infrared and ultraviolet it cannot paint.

It enters the catalog under "Science" with `hubParent: stem-room`, immediately after Everyday Time, so the STEM Room's bench now reads Physics, Sound, Time, Light, Chemistry, Electronics, Measure, Engineering, Math, Earth, Computing, Body, Radio. The room follows the Science block's shape: benches that work right on the page and show their working, a verified shelf of legitimate places, Help ▾ menu with full documentation, banner verified on the page. Its cross-links: Everyday Sound (the other circle of twelve — Sound's circle-of-fifths section now links here, and this room links back), Everyday Time (the sundial is this room's shadow drawn as a dial — Time's sundial bench now links back to the shadow bench), Everyday Earth (the weather on the scattered sky), Everyday Math (the wheel's angles, the scattering ratio, the lens equation), Everyday Body (the eye, the body's own three-channel additive bench), and Everyday Physics (light in its general form). The STEM Room's own page gained this room's card, and its counts moved from twelve to thirteen, recorded in that room's COLLECTION.md.

Everything interactive is computed in the visitor's browser from rules baked into `light.js` — the page sends nothing anywhere. The mixing models, the colour-theory scheme, the thin-lens equation, the Rayleigh rule, the solar geometry, and the spectrum facts are recorded in THIRD-PARTY.md. `test.cjs` re-checks the arithmetic — additive white and yellow, the subtractive model's leaking near-black, complements at exactly 180° for every root, the lens at f = 10 and do = 30 landing at di = 15 and m = −0.5, the 4.35 scattering ratio, and the equinox-noon shadow at Defiance's latitude equalling height × tan(41.3°) — on every run.

Files in this room: `index.html` (the room itself), `style.css`, `light.js` (the pure rules), `app.js` (the wiring: the lamp stage, the wheel, the ray diagram, the sky swatches, the compass shadow plot, the spectrum slider), `test.cjs`, `README.md`, `THIRD-PARTY.md`, `COLLECTION.md`, `LICENSE`, `LICENSE-CONTENT.md`, and `everyday-light-banner.jpg`.

Banner: `everyday-light-banner.jpg`, AI-generated watercolor to the concept in Steven's idea sketch — a friendly robot splitting sunlight with a prism, a rainbow across the bench, a colour wheel leaning nearby — generated 20 November 2026 as part of adding this room, in the style and dimensions (2736×912) of the collection's other banners. The robot holds a tall glass prism up into a sunbeam from the window; the spectrum fans from the prism across the bench, and the twelve-hue colour wheel stands propped at the bench's right end among pigment jars and brushes. The first generation matched the brief and ships.

The room ships web-first, as the recent STEM rooms have: it reaches the desktop app in a later release, not this one.
