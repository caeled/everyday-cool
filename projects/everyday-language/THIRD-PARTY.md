## Data sources (facts, not files)

- **Language classification** — the family tree follows the standard consensus classification of Indo-European as catalogued by **Glottolog** (glottolog.org) and **Ethnologue**-style references: Proto-Indo-European → Germanic → West Germanic → Anglo-Frisian → English; Romance from Italic (Latin); Balto-Slavic; Indo-Iranian; Hellenic; Celtic; Armenian; Albanian; and the extinct Anatolian and Tocharian branches. Branch facts (Grimm's law, Linear B Greek, the Frisian kinship rhyme, Lithuanian's conservatism) are the standard textbook accounts.
- **Cognates and reconstructions** — the eleven word families and their starred Proto-Indo-European forms (*ph₂tḗr, *méh₂tēr, *tréyes, *nókʷts, *ḱērd-, *wódr̥, *bʰréh₂tēr, *pṓds, *déḱm̥, *néwos, *h₁nómn̥) are the standard reconstructions found in Indo-European etymological references (the tradition of Pokorny's dictionary and its modern successors, as summarised in the linked Wikipedia articles on the Proto-Indo-European language and on cognates). Every listed descendant form is a genuine cognate; known non-cognates that resemble the family (Russian отец, Spanish hermano, Latin aqua/Spanish agua, Russian нога) are named as warnings, not listed as relatives.
- **The phrasebook** — sixty translations of ten everyday phrases into Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, and Dutch: standard phrasebook material, hand-checked for this room. They are one accepted rendering each; most phrases have polite/familiar and regional variants a phrasebook cannot hold.
- **Family sizes** — language and speaker counts for Sino-Tibetan, Niger-Congo, Afroasiatic, Austronesian, and Trans-New Guinea are approximations in the Ethnologue/Glottolog range and are labelled approximate on the page; sources differ because they count languages and second-language speakers differently.
- **The brain section** — the standard account: Broca's area and production (from Paul Broca's 1861 case), Wernicke's area and comprehension (Carl Wernicke, 1874), the arcuate fasciculus as their connecting bundle, the visual word form area in reading (its degree of specialisation is noted on the page as debated), the dual-stream model of language processing (Hickok & Poeppel), sign-language aphasias mirroring spoken ones (the Poizner–Klima–Bellugi research tradition), and early-childhood hemispheric re-mapping after injury. The diagram is an original schematic, not a scan.

## Nothing is called at view time

This page makes no network calls at all: the tree, the cognates bench, the phrasebook, and the brain bench run entirely in the browser from the data in `lang.js`, and the shelf links are only followed when a visitor presses one.

## Limitations

The tree is a deliberate simplification: main branches and sample languages, not all ~445 Indo-European languages, and debated corners of classification are left to the linked references — the page sticks to consensus. Proto-Indo-European is a reconstruction; its starred words were never written down, and its homeland and dates are the mainstream account rather than certainties, as the Help section states. Cognate spellings use the common scholarly transliterations; scripts are given alongside for Russian and Sanskrit where a transliteration alone would hide the word. The phrasebook is ten phrases, not a translator, and says so. The brain diagram is schematic and not to scale, and the neuroscience is the textbook account — the living research is more detailed and partly unsettled, which the page notes where it matters. Descriptions were written on 18 November 2026; each linked site's own pages are the authority.
