# Collection origin

Original Everyday Cool workshop, added 18 November 2026 at Steven Powell's request. "Everyday Language" grew from a single line of Steven's in the Everyday Cool ideas side chat — "You know language is a code too (ala secret squares)" — and he named its contents himself: a map of language families ("people always get tripped out when they learn English is Germanic"), some common cognates, a mini translator, and STEM articles. A fifth section, "Where language lives" (the brain's language network), was added to the build brief the same day. Built by Muse (AI assistant) and added directly to the collection at his direction; this folder is the maintained original, and there is no upstream copy.

The room follows the house shape of the recent rooms (Everyday Physics, Everyday Body): a verified shelf of legitimate places, benches that work right on the page, a Help ▾ menu with full documentation inside the page and this README in the room and the installer, and cross-links to its neighbours. The updated Everyday Cool desktop app ships as app v1.0.16 through the app repo's existing build workflow.

Cross-links, as built: this room links to Secret Squares (its sibling — nav, the "Language is a code" section, and Keep Going) and Secret Squares' "Go deeper" section links back; the brain section links to Everyday Body's brain explorer, and Keep Going also names Numbers in the Wild and Packet Playground. One cross-link leaves the collection deliberately: the phrasebook's "Take it traveling" card points to Steven's separate Travel Kit app (releases page, https://github.com/caeled/travel-kit-app/releases, verified HTTP 200 on 18 November 2026), framed on the card as a separate downloadable app bundling Find It, Tonight's Sky, and Weather Everywhere — not a room in this collection. The "Language is a code" section also links Steven's Spectrum Search (on his MIS portal site) where its idiom-explaining work is the worked example of compression failing without the shared dictionary.

Accuracy, and what was checked: the tree's classifications follow the standard consensus (Glottolog/Ethnologue-style); every cognate form listed is a genuine descendant of the starred Proto-Indo-European root given beside it, and the three known traps are handled as warnings instead of false entries — Russian отец (otéc) is not from *ph₂tḗr, Spanish hermano is not from *bʰréh₂tēr, and Spanish agua/Latin aqua are not from the *wed- root. The phrasebook's sixty translations are standard phrasebook basics, hand-checked; pronunciation hints were omitted rather than guessed. Speaker and language counts for the other great families are approximations and are labelled approximate on the page. Links: all thirteen shelf links were checked live on 18 November 2026 — twelve returned HTTP 200 directly; Omniglot answers scripted checks with HTTP 403 (bot protection) but serves readers normally, verified by reading the page itself, the same treatment the Radio Room records for QRZ, and the page notes it in the open. Nothing was dropped.

Everything interactive is computed in the visitor's browser from the data in `lang.js` — the page sends nothing anywhere. The data lives in `lang.js` (no DOM), checked by `test.cjs`.

Banner: `everyday-language-banner.jpg`, AI-generated watercolor — a friendly robot in a warm lamplit study, pointing at a glowing family tree drawn on a large chalkboard, speech bubbles around it holding different scripts (Latin ABC, Greek letters, Cyrillic letters, Arabic script, Chinese characters) — generated 18 November 2026 as part of adding this room, in the style and dimensions (2736×912) of the collection's other banners.
