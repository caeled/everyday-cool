    Open the lesson: how to learn a language from clues

    Every sentence here comes from one consistent miniature grammar. The locals describe objects as object, then color. A suffix marks two objects. For a request, they put an action word first. A separate word at the front reverses a request into “do not.” Nothing is randomly translated after you speak.

    At the experiment bench, hold two features steady and change one. If “stone red” and “stone blue” share one word, the changing word probably names color. Try a second object to test that idea. A single scene may fit several explanations; a good experiment makes them disagree.

    The notebook checks a proposed meaning against observed examples. “Consistent” means the evidence has not ruled it out; it does not prove the guess. The missions check complete sentences against the underlying meanings and word order. Wrong answers cost nothing.

    Each expedition changes the vocabulary using one of three fixed language packs. The grammar stays the same. The game works entirely offline; no speech recognition, live AI, tracking, account, or network request. This is a teaching model of language learning, not a claim that real languages are this small or easy to decode.
