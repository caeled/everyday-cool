# Collection origin

Everyday Home, added 25 November 2026 (catalog 41). Meta AI's homepage audit proposed it — "A house as a system: water in, water out, power, heat, drains, and what to check before you call someone" — and Steven Powell directed the build the same night ("Do Money and Home"), alongside Everyday Money. Muse built it as a house workshop.

**The page** — `index.html`: three benches. The breaker bench sums typical nameplate wattages on one 120 V × 15 A = 1,800 W circuit, marks the 1,440 W (80%) planning line electricians use for continuous loads, and trips — refusing a reset until the load is shed — past the rating. The water bench gives four leaks one correct first move each (the fixture valve twice, the main shutoff twice), with the tells printed after answering, including where a main shutoff usually lives and the calm-day homework of finding and tagging it. The triage bench walks four symptoms (silent furnace, slow drain, dead outlet, warm fridge) through the free checks first, each list ending in a two-part verdict: what it usually is, and the honest point at which it's a professional's call. Its Help ▾ carries the safety lines (gas smell = leave and call from outside; water near live electrics; never mix drain cleaners; panel/gas/structural work is a professional's) and the honest limits (nameplate wattages are typical, your label governs; houses differ — the benches teach the shape, the map of your own house is homework). **The lesson** — `everyday-home-lesson.html`: the receipts and bends. `README.md` is the portable guide. `LICENSE` and `LICENSE-CONTENT.md` are the collection-standard license files, byte-identical to the other projects'.

Files: `index.html`, `everyday-home-lesson.html`, `README.md`, `COLLECTION.md`, `LICENSE`, `LICENSE-CONTENT.md`.
