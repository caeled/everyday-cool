Energy in motion

Three classic cabinets, one subject: things moving, and what happens when they hit things. Breakout is reflection and momentum. Tempest is geometry fired down a well. Frogger is two moving systems on different clocks — and you, crossing both. Pick a cabinet and play.

Breakout

One ball, one paddle, a wall of bricks. The paddle's edge is a steering wheel — where the ball lands on it decides the angle it leaves at.

Move: mouse, touch, or ← → · Launch: click or Space

The physics: reflection & momentum

A ball bouncing off a flat wall keeps its speed and mirrors its angle — angle in = angle out. The paddle cheats that rule on purpose: hit dead center and the ball goes straight up; catch it on the edge and it leaves at up to 65°, trading vertical speed for horizontal speed.

KE = ½ · m · v²  —  double the speed, four times the energy

Watch the kinetic-energy readout in the HUD. Every level the ball gets faster, and because energy grows with the square of speed, a slightly faster ball is a much more destructive one. Broken bricks spray particles — the ball's energy, handed off.

How to play

  • Clear every brick to advance a level. The ball speeds up each level.
  • Top rows are worth more — and the ball gets livelier once it slips behind the wall.
  • Catch the ball low on the paddle's edge to send it sideways into a channel.
  • You have 3 balls. Miss one and the next serves from the paddle.

Tempest

You are the claw on the rim of a geometric well. Enemies climb the lanes toward you from the center. Shoot down your lane before they reach the rim — and don't be standing there when they arrive.

Move: ← → or A D · Fire: Space · Superzapper (once per web): Z

The physics: geometry is the board

Tempest isn't played on a shape, it is played inside one. Every web is a figure from geometry class, drawn as vector lines and divided into lanes:

d = 2r  ·  C = 2πr  ·  parabola: y = x²
  • Web 1 — Circle. 16 lanes around a center. The rim is a circumference; every lane is a radius.
  • Web 2 — Square. Same game, corners instead of curves. Watch how the lane lengths change.
  • Web 3 — Parabola. An open web bent along y = x². It has ends — you can't wrap around, and neither can they.
  • Web 4 — Triangle, then back to the circle, faster.

Tick Show the geometry above the notes to draw the radius, diameter, and center on the live web.

How to play

  • Flippers spawn at the center and climb your way, flipping between lanes as they come.
  • Shoot a flipper before it reaches the rim. One that makes it will walk the rim toward you — shoot it or move.
  • Clear the web's full count of enemies to warp to the next shape.
  • The Superzapper (Z) wipes every enemy on the web. Once per web. Spend it well.

Frogger

Five homes across the river. The road runs on one clock, the river on another, and the logs moving away from home still count as progress if you ride them right.

Hop: ← → ↑ ↓ or W A S D

The physics: relative motion

The road and the river are the same problem wearing different clothes: everything is moving, including the ground.

your velocity = hop velocity + velocity of what you're standing on

On the road, a car's momentum is the whole story — it will not stop for you, and contact transfers all of it at once. On the river, the rule flips: standing still on a log means moving at the log's speed. That's a moving reference frame, the same idea Einstein built on, at frog scale. Ride a log past your home, hop off late, and the current takes the decision out of your hands.

How to play

  • Cross 5 road lanes, rest on the median, then ride logs and turtles across 5 river lanes.
  • Land in an empty home bay at the top. Fill all five to advance a level — traffic speeds up.
  • Water is fatal unless you're on a log or a turtle. Turtles dive — watch for the warning sink.
  • The clock runs 60 seconds per frog. Running out is the same as getting run over.
  • Every forward hop scores. Reaching a home scores big; finishing all five, bigger.

All three cabinets run on the same idea the rest of Flynn's Arcade is built on: the lesson is in the mechanic. Reflection, geometry, and relative motion — learned in your hands, not from a diagram.
