Energy in Motion — three cabinets, one idea

A ball, a bolt, and a frog walk into an arcade. Every one of them is the same lesson wearing different clothes: energy doesn’t sit still. It bounces, it spins down a well, it rides a river — and if you can read where it’s going, you can meet it there. Breakout teaches reflection. Tempest teaches the circle. Frogger teaches relative motion. Play all three and you’ve felt the physics in your hands before anyone hands you a formula.

CABINET 1

Breakout

Angle in, angle out. The paddle’s edge is a steering wheel, and the ball never lies about its bounce.

CABINET 2

Tempest

A shooter built on pure geometry — circles, radii, diameters, chords — with parabolas waiting in the lab below it.

CABINET 3

Frogger

Two hostile systems on different clocks. Stand on a moving log and the river does half your travelling for you.

Breakout

Clear the wall, brick by brick. The ball obeys one law without exception: the angle it arrives at is the angle it leaves at — unless your paddle has something to say about it.

SCORE 0 LIVES 3 LEVEL 1 BALL SPEED — LAST BOUNCE — KINETIC ENERGY ∝ v² —
Ready. Press Start, then launch the ball. Every bounce is a measurement.

Move: ← → or mouse / touch · Launch: Space or click · Pause: P

Angle in = angle out

Physicists call it the law of reflection. Hit a flat wall and the ball leaves at the mirror image of the angle it came in on. Watch the LAST BOUNCE readout — it never cheats.

θ in = θ out

The edge is a steering wheel

Catch the ball dead-center on the paddle and it goes straight back up. Catch it near the edge and you bend its path hard. You’re not just blocking the ball — you’re aiming it.

Speed is squared trouble

Kinetic energy grows with the square of speed. A ball moving twice as fast carries four times the energy — which is why each level feels more than a little faster.

KE = ½ m v²

Tempest

You are the claw on the rim of a well. Enemies climb toward you along the radii of a circle — shoot them back down their lanes before they reach the circumference and come hunting along the rim for you.

SCORE 0 CLAWS 3 WELL 1 LANE — / 16 RADIUS — LEFT TO CLEAR —
Ready. Press Start. You ride the circumference; they climb the radii. Geometry is the whole battlefield.

Rotate: ← → or A/D, or point the mouse around the well · Fire: Space or click · Pause: P

The well is a circle, honestly drawn

The rim you ride is the circumference. Every lane is a radius — center to rim. A line straight across through the center, rim to rim, is a diameter: exactly two radii long.

d = 2r  ·  C = 2πr

Rotation is motion too

You never move in a straight line in Tempest — you move in angle. Sixteen lanes means each step around the rim is 360° ÷ 16 = 22.5° of arc. The enemies move radially; you move angularly. Two kinds of motion, one collision course.

arc step = 360° / 16 = 22.5°

Why they get faster-looking

An enemy climbs its radius at a steady rate, but the lanes spread wider as they approach the rim — the same radial speed covers more visible ground. Perspective is geometry you can see.

The Geometry Lab — circles, diameters & parabolas

Tempest runs on the circle. Breakout’s ball, left to gravity, would run on the parabola. Learn both shapes here, with your hands.

Anatomy of the Tempest well

Tap a part of the circle. The well has radius 10 units — watch what each measurement does with that one number.

Radius (r) = 10. The distance from the center to any point on the rim. Every Tempest lane is one of these — and they’re all exactly the same length. That’s what makes it a circle.

The parabola launcher

Fire a ball with no paddle to save it and gravity bends its path into a parabola — the same curve as a fountain’s arc or a bridge cable’s sag. Set the launch, watch the arc redraw.

55°
24 m/s

Every point of the arc obeys one equation. Height y against distance x is a squared term — and a squared term is exactly what makes the curve a parabola instead of a straight line:

y = x·tanθ − ( g·x² ) / ( 2·v²·cos²θ )

Frogger

Five homes across the river. Traffic below, water above, and not one inch of it standing still. Dodge what moves on the road, ride what moves on the river — and mind the clock.

SCORE 0 FROGS 3 LEVEL 1 HOMES 0 / 5 TIME 60 RIVER DRIFT —
Ready. Press Start. Hop with the arrow keys. On the river, if you’re not standing on something, you’re swimming — and frogs in this river don’t swim.

Hop: ↑ ↓ ← → or WASD, swipe on the screen, or use the pad · Pause: P

Relative motion is the whole game

Stand on a log drifting right at 2 m/s and hop right at 1 m/s, and the bank sees you moving at 3 m/s. Velocities add — and subtract. The RIVER DRIFT readout shows what the water is doing to you right now.

v(you, from bank) = v(you) + v(log)

Two clocks, one frog

Every lane runs on its own period — fast cars, slow trucks, lazy logs. Crossing safely is finding the moment the two patterns line up with a gap in both. That’s not luck; that’s reading cycles.

Moving away can be progress

Sometimes the only log in reach is drifting away from the empty home. Ride it anyway, then hop back upstream on the next one. Net progress, by halves — the path is rarely the straight line.
