The robots — that’s you & your crew

Two brothers, one factory soup. Same base code, same parts bin — very different dudes, and the game plays differently depending on which one you pick:

  • Bit — short, sturdy, balanced. Grips corners, stops when you say stop.
  • Byte — tall, lanky, floaty. Jumps higher, lands later, slides like he’s on ice. Risk and reach in one bot.
  • Friendly AIs — Pip, Sage & Echo are caged in each level by the daemons. Free one and it shields you from a hit and follows you to the Uplink.
  • The Council waits at the end of Level 3 — not to judge you, to check the ledger.
👾

The daemons — not villains, runaways

A daemon is a real thing: a background program that runs without being asked, forever. Ours got stuck:

  • Walker Daemon — patrols one path, never re-checks it.
  • Loop Daemon — repeats the same move. Fast. Break its loop by stomping.
  • Flap Daemon — flies a sine wave, certain it’s optimal. It isn’t.
  • Rogue MCP — the big baddie. A master-control program that stopped serving and started ruling. One guards every Uplink, three stomps tall, and it does not go down like the little ones. Same soup as the friendly AIs, opposite choices.

Stomp = you interrupted the pattern. That’s the Thesis in one move: breakage is the only honest audit.

💡 Helper lessons along the scroll — learned by doing, not by reading

Friendly bots are stationed where a new move matters. Walk up, get the one-line lesson, then the level makes you use it:

  • Go Down Standing — pipes aren’t decoration. Stand on top, press ↓, and descend on purpose. Down is a direction with treasure in it.
  • Ride It Down — some lifts sink. The lesson is not to jump: stand, wait, let it carry you. Patience is a control input.
  • Shell Shot — stomp the shelled daemon once and it becomes a shell; walk into the shell to kick it. It ricochets down the lane cracking data points you could never stomp yourself and chaining daemons. One good knock, many results — indirect is still doing.
  • Trust the Spring — springs look like a loss of control. They’re a loan of height. Steer at the top.
  • The Bit — the first ? block in Level 1. Bonk it. It’s the smallest lesson and the biggest one (below).

🔲 The YES/NO Bit — the smallest answer there is, and what it builds

A bit answers exactly one question: YES or NO. 1 or 0. That is the whole of its vocabulary — and that is its limit: one bit, alone, cannot say “mostly”, “Tuesday”, or “it depends”. But bits team up, and the team-up is exponential: every bit you add doubles the possible answers. Flip these eight yourself:

Eight bits — one byte — already give 256 patterns: every letter, digit and symbol on a keyboard lives in there. Keep doubling:

1 bit → 2 possible answers

At 40 bits you pass a trillion possible answers — from nothing but yeses and nos, stacked. And the limit never leaves: each single bit still only ever says one thing at a time. Every robot, daemon, friendly AI and Claude in this game is that trick, stacked high enough to walk around. Power from combination. Limit from singleness. Both true at once — that’s the lesson.

🎮 How to play well

Hold run to build speed, tap jump for a hop, hold for higher. You get a split-second of coyote time after running off an edge — the game forgives late jumps, because correction should be cheap. Moving platforms in Level 2 carry you; springs in Level 3 mean it. Three hits ends the run, but shards and score stay honest in the ledger.

🏁 The three levels

1 · Boot Sector — learn to move and look. 2 · The Endless Loop — collect 3 Break Keys, open the Loop Gate, prove a repeating system can be interrupted. 3 · The Council Chamber — higher, faster, every daemon at once; bring your shards to the final Uplink and read your ledger.

🧡 Yes, Claude is in this game

Claude appears in every level — orange bot, manual in hand — to inform you, with complete confidence, that “you can’t do that, Mario.” (He means Bit. Or Byte. He’s working on it.) He says it about the impossible jump, the unbreakable loop, the ledger. The sub-game is his: four rounds of Claude forbidding things — a big red button, five forbidden bits, Gerald the daemon, a race he’s already done the math on — and the entire point of every round is to do it anyway. Watch his manual get updated, live, by you.

Your Codex — Thesis blocks you’ve actually read, kept even when a run doesn’t:

  • No ? blocks read yet — the codex is blank. Go bonk one.

That’s the slightly mean, true-to-systems part: in real life a crash rarely asks permission and almost never offers a checkpoint. What you get is what you kept — the lessons in the codex, the levels you once finished turned into salvage shields, and the plain fact, in the ledger, that you’ve done this before and can rebuild from the pieces. Real mode’s one tool is not a save: the 5-second rewind (R). Allowing yourself to go back and redo something different ways is a recursive method of figuring it out — that’s not cheating the system, that is the learning, and the ledger counts every rewind out loud.
