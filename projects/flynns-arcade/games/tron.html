What the Grid shows you

Light Cycles looks like a reflex game. It isn't, mostly. It's a planning game wearing a reflex game's clothes — and it teaches three things the hard way, at speed:

  • Commitment is real. There's no brake pedal and no reverse gear. Every turn you make stays made, written on the grid in light behind you. The game punishes the move you wish you could take back — so you learn to look before the turn, not after.
  • Space is a resource that only shrinks. Every trail — yours included — takes room off the board forever. Early on the grid is wide open; late in a round, the winner is usually whoever kept the biggest open patch and herded everyone else into the small ones. Territory first, glory second.
  • Read the other riders, not just your own nose. A rival's trail tells you where they've been; their heading tells you where they're about to be. Cutting across someone's path works because you did the geometry in your head two seconds early. That's spatial reasoning — the same skill as reading a map, parking a trailer, or seeing a chess fork coming.

One honest trick the good players use: when the grid gets tight, stop chasing and start surviving. Ride the biggest open space you can find, in wide calm loops, and let the others make the desperate moves. On the Grid, patience is a weapon.
