What falls from above

The pond is not a closed jar — and this part is not controversial in the slightest: stuff falls from up there into down here, constantly, and always has. The biggest example is sitting in plain sight: a large share of Earth's ocean water itself is thought to have arrived from above, delivered by water-rich asteroids and comets during the early bombardment, because the early Earth was too hot to keep its own. No delivery, no oceans. No oceans, no soup. Meteorites still deliver amino acids today; cosmic dust still rains down by the ton. What is controversial is only the extra step — panspermia, the idea that living things made the trip too.

The controversial extra step

In this sim: sky-fall adds matter shaped by elsewhere (cold-formed nucleotides, space organics, exotic minerals). A panspermia arrival can jump-start cells directly — but the field log will always mark them as arrivals, not natives. Provenance matters.

What the soup shows you

Energy + matter + time is not a guarantee — it's a budget. Lightning spends energy to build bonds. Heat speeds everything, including falling apart. Lipids make boundaries, nucleotides store instructions, and a cell is what happens when boundary and instructions end up in the same place. After that, selection does the bookkeeping: change one condition and a different branch of the tree wins.

Abiogenesis ↗How life is thought to arise from non-living chemistry. Miller–Urey experiment ↗The 1953 spark-in-a-flask experiment behind the first preset. The RNA world ↗Why nucleotides in this sim matter before there are cells. Hydrothermal vents ↗Hot, mineral-rich, dark — a completely different starting bet. What is a phylum? ↗Body plans, and how biologists group them. LUCA ↗The last universal common ancestor of everything alive today. Panspermia ↗The controversial extra step: that living organisms themselves travel between worlds. The delivery of water and ingredients, by contrast, is established. Late Heavy Bombardment ↗The era when falling rock delivered a large share of Earth's water — the oceans as sky-fall, at planetary scale. Murchison meteorite ↗A real carbon-rich meteorite carrying real amino acids — proof space delivers ingredients, whatever you think of the rest. Cosmic dust ↗The constant rain from above: interplanetary and interstellar grains falling into every pond.

A teaching model, not a claim about exactly how it happened. Real abiogenesis is still an open question — this pond lets you feel why conditions matter.
