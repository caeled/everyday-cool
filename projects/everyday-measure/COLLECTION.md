# Collection origin

Original Everyday Cool workshop, added 19 November 2026 at Steven Powell's request. In the Everyday Cool ideas side chat that morning he proposed the room in his own words: "The metric system (for most Americans) and how it compares to English system. Converters examples stem articles. Weights and volume comparison tools ect new room" — and gave the banner's art direction with it: "Image robot with scales (and a chart behind it with what each prefix means". He chose the name himself from the options offered — "Everyday Measure" — and said build it. Built by Muse (AI assistant) and added directly to the collection at his direction as the twenty-third room; this folder is the maintained original, and there is no upstream copy.

The room follows the Science block's shape (benches that work right on the page and show their working, a verified shelf of legitimate places, Help ▾ menu with full documentation, banner verified on the page and inside the desktop app). It enters the catalog under "Science," immediately after Everyday Body, so the hub's Science run reads Physics, Chemistry, Electronics, Body, Measure. The updated Everyday Cool desktop app ships as app v1.0.17 through the app repo's existing build workflow.

Everything interactive is computed in the visitor's browser from data baked into `measure.js` — the page sends nothing anywhere. The conversion factors are the exact legal definitions published by NIST (the inch is 2.54 cm by definition; the pound is 0.45359237 kg by definition; the US gallon is 3.785411784 L by definition), and `test.cjs` re-checks them, the ladder, the temperature anchors, and the comparison shelf's two readings of each thing on every run. Facts and their sources — the US Mint's 5.000 g nickel, the World Aquatics (formerly FINA) Olympic pool minimum, the NASA and Canadian investigation accounts behind the two breakage stories — are recorded in THIRD-PARTY.md. One deliberate scope line: the volume bench covers US liquid measure (plus the Imperial gallon, as a labelled trap); dry measure and troy weights are named as out of scope in THIRD-PARTY rather than silently absent.

The rules live in `measure.js` (no DOM), checked by `test.cjs`.

Banner: `everyday-measure-banner.jpg`, AI-generated watercolor to Steven's concept — a friendly robot beside a large brass balance scale with brass weights in its pans, and on the wall behind it a framed chart drawn as a staircase labelled with what the prefixes are: KILO, HECTO, DEKA, METRE, DECI, CENTI, MILLI — generated 19 November 2026 as part of adding this room, in the style and dimensions (2736×912) of the collection's other banners. The first generation was rejected: its staircase duplicated DECI and swapped DEKA and HECTO — a chart about prefixes cannot ship with the prefixes wrong. The second generation, with the step order spelled out in the prompt, reads correctly top to bottom, each word once, and that is the banner that ships.

Documentation update, 20 November 2026: `index.html`, `style.css`, `README.md`, and `COLLECTION.md` gained a professional documentation treatment — a Source on GitHub link in the Help menu, a documentation lede and action bar in the Help section, matching styles, and a Documentation section in the README — at Steven Powell's direction ('the documentation section is the only part that doesn't look like a real program'). No bench, lesson, or behaviour changed.

The same documentation update also added a Steve's Profile link (Steven Powell's LinkedIn) at the foot of the Help section — `index.html` and `style.css`.

Game (21 November 2026): at Steven Powell's direction, the room gained its first game — `anchors.html`, **Anchors**: estimate sixteen real quantities across four rounds of widening scale (tabletop, the room, the town, and by-chains) against the room's own anchor quantities — thirteen harvested verbatim from the room's benches, three marked additions. Scoring is by factor (points = round(100 × (1 − log₁₀(factor)/2))), because estimation error is multiplicative; feedback expresses every truth back in anchors by computation from the same table that drives the shelf and the proof's estimator, so the three can never drift apart. The build's proof caught a floating-point banding bug (an exact ×1.5 tipping over the Close edge in binary) — the core now carries a commented 1e-9 band tolerance, and the lesson's How it's built reports it. `anchors-lesson.html` is its back door (Fermi estimation, ratio scoring, chaining, the anchor catalogue with sources), opened from inside the game by a Lesson pop-up. `index.html` gained a game card in the Feel It bench. The game also visits the Physics Game Table, the Arcade shelf, and both lesson shelves. Changed files: `anchors.html`, `anchors-lesson.html`, `index.html`, `README.md`, `COLLECTION.md`.

Staleness fix, 22 November 2026: the Mars Climate Orbiter story now prices the orbiter itself at about $125 million (the $327.6 million figure was the whole two-spacecraft Mars Surveyor '98 program) in `index.html`, `README.md`, and `THIRD-PARTY.md`; FINA is now named World Aquatics (formerly FINA) in `measure.js`, `anchors.html`, `anchors-lesson.html`, `THIRD-PARTY.md`, and this `COLLECTION.md`.
