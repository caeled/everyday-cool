Protoplanetary Disk — the lesson

A young star, a swirling disk of gas and pebbles, and planets assembling themselves out of both — with sliders for the star's luminosity, the disk's density, pebble accretion and migration, and six curated systems from the Solar Nebula to TRAPPIST-1. This page is the audit. Following the house rule, the shipped code was not just read: it was extracted and driven headlessly (Node, with DOM and canvas stubs), run under its own controls, and measured — the snow line tracked as luminosity changed, planet pairs pushed into resonance and watched converge, single test planets dropped into the disk to weigh their gaps, and the presets left running until they showed what they actually do over time. Two of the page's systems are built on real, correct physics. Two of its headline claims are false as shipped, and they are flagged in red below.

The verdict: the snow line and the resonances are real physics; the gaps and the growth are stagecraft — and two presets misbehave

The temperature profile and snow line are exactly right. The page uses the passive irradiated-disk profile T(r) = 280 · L★1/4 · r−1/2 K and puts the water snow line where T = 170 K. Driven at six luminosities, its getSnowLineAU() matched the analytic solution (280/170)²·√L to four decimals every time, and its own temperature function reads exactly 170 K at the line it draws. At 1 L☉ the line sits at 2.71 AU — the textbook ~2.7 AU snow line of the early Solar System. And the line moves correctly: rsnow ∝ √L, the signature of a passive disk heated only by its star.

The resonance locking converges on Kepler's ratios, exactly. Planet pairs placed near a commensurability and driven through the page's own updatePlanetsAndResonances() settle onto period ratios of 1.5000, 2.0000 and 1.3333, at semi-major-axis ratios 1.3104, 1.5874 and 1.2114 — Kepler's ratio2/3 values to the fourth decimal. Equivalently, the inner resonance of a planet at ap falls at 0.630·ap (2:1) and 0.763·ap (3:2), as it should.

But gap carving respects no physical criterion at all — it is a mass-only cosmetic formula (measured table below), with no disk scale height and no viscosity anywhere in the file. And two flagship presets fail when actually run: the TRAPPIST-1 "resonant chain" labels only one of its five planet pairs as resonant and, left running, collapses by merger into a single body; and the Grand Tack's celebrated reversal does not fire until Jupiter has migrated to 0.67 AU — inside Mercury's orbit, not the ~1.5 AU tack of the real model.

2 · Resonances, measured

Kepler's third law fixes where resonances sit: a period ratio of p:q means a semi-major-axis ratio of (p/q)2/3. The audit seeded pairs of 1 M⊕ test planets a few percent off each commensurability, switched migration off, and let the page's resonance locking run for 3,000 steps:

Exact. The locking is a gentle nudge toward the target spacing rather than real resonant dynamics (no libration, no eccentricity excitation), and capture only happens inside a tolerance window (±0.068 in period ratio, ±0.095 in the TRAPPIST preset) — a pair approaching too fast or from too far simply never locks. But where the page claims a resonance, the geometry is Kepler's, not decoration.

3 · Gap carving: cosmetic, and here is the proof

Real gap opening is a contest between a planet's torque and the disk's pressure and viscosity. The usual first check is the thermal criterion: a planet opens a gap roughly when Mp/M★ ≳ 3(H/r)³, where H/r is the disk's aspect ratio — about 27 M⊕ at H/r = 0.03, 125 M⊕ at H/r = 0.05, 343 M⊕ at H/r = 0.07. The page's code (its comment says "Crida-like") contains no H/r and no viscosity anywhere. The audit dropped single test planets at 5 AU and measured the relaxed depth of the trench carved in Σ(r):

Measured depth matched the closed-form formula M²/(M² + 520) to the third decimal at every mass — because that formula is the gap. It does not change with radius, luminosity, disk temperature or density: the same planet carves the same trench at 1 AU and at 20 AU, in a hot disk and a cold one. The width is cosmetic too, and not even self-consistent — the Σ-grid, the particle shepherding and the preset pre-carving each use a different gap-width formula (0.048, 0.055 and 0.065 · a respectively). Verdict: the gaps look like ALMA because they were drawn to, not because the disk physics puts them there. The flanking pressure bumps (depth × 0.34) are at least the right idea — dust really does pile up at gap edges.

4 · Pebble accretion and growth timescales

The page's pebble physics, measured rather than read:

  • Per-capture gain is a fixed lump, not a rate. Each captured pebble adds exactly 0.19 M⊕ to a core (0.16 to a giant), multiplied by 2.9 outside the snow line — regardless of the planet's own mass. There is no runaway/oligarchic distinction in the growth law, no pebble-isolation mass, and no slowdown as a core grows; growth stops only when the separate gas-runaway cap (~360 M⊕) bites.
  • The "tripling" claim is true per pebble and false as a growth rate. The Optical/IR view says icy mantles outside the snow line triple solid growth rates, and per capture they do (×2.9). But driven for the same interval, a 1.5 M⊕ core at 1 AU made 228 captures and a twin at 5 AU made 3 — encounter geometry (orbital frequency and Hill-sphere size) swamps the ice bonus, so in the shipped sim the outer core grows slower, the opposite of the caption and of the real snow-line advantage.
  • Runaway gas accretion is a hard switch at 10 M⊕. The real critical core mass is a soft, model-dependent ~5–20 M⊕; 10 is a fair textbook pick. But the page flips a core to "giant" the instant it crosses, and in the From Dust preset the largest body reached 217 M⊕ by a displayed age of 4.5 Myr and 311 M⊕ by 9 Myr — Myr-scale giant growth that looks like core accretion's real timescale, for the wrong reason (see the clock, below).
  • The Myr clock does not agree with the page's own orbits. Disk age advances 0.045 Myr per second of simulation. A planet at 1 AU orbits with the page's own Keplerian Ω in 4.05 s — so the clock ages the disk 0.182 Myr per 1 AU orbit, where the real figure is 1 year: the age counter runs ~182,000× faster than the page's own orbital mechanics implies. Every "Myr" in the telemetry is a display scaling, not a consequence of the dynamics.

5 · Frost lines: one real line, one mislabelled ghost

Only one condensation front is actually modelled: water at 170 K. Using the page's own profile at 1 L☉, the other fronts would sit at:

Flagged — the CO label is wrong, and unreachable. The thermal view labels its 40 K isotherm "(CO)". CO does not freeze out at 40 K; it condenses at roughly 20–30 K. And the label is dead code in practice: on the page's own profile the 40 K ring sits at 49 AU at 1 L☉ (79 AU at the HL Tau preset's luminosity), outside every preset's disk, so the isotherm routine skips it in all six systems. The one non-water species the page names is named at the wrong temperature and can never be seen.

6 · The named systems, audited

TRAPPIST-1 — the "resonant chain" claim is false as shipped

The real TRAPPIST-1 is a chain of seven planets (b–h) at 0.012–0.062 AU around a star of L ≈ 0.00055 L☉, locked in a chain of near-resonances with adjacent period ratios ≈ 1.60, 1.67, 1.51, 1.51, 1.34, 1.52 — mostly 3:2 and 4:3 links. The page ships six planets (no h, and named "TRAPPIST-b" without the 1), at display distances of 1.25–6.15 AU — about 100× the real ones — around a star set to 0.15 L☉, about 270× the real luminosity, with masses that drift well off the measured values (d: 1.15 shipped vs ~0.39 M⊕ real; g: 2.85 vs ~1.32). Its adjacent period ratios are a near-uniform 1.61, 1.63, 1.63, 1.62, 1.57 — an 8:5-flavoured spacing that matches none of the real chain's 3:2/4:3 pattern. Driven one step, the page's own resonance detector labels only one of the five pairs (f–g, at 1.571) as resonant; the other four sit outside even the preset's widened capture tolerance. Driven longer, Type I migration at the preset's strength pulls the chain inward and the planets merge: after a displayed 13.5 Myr the "chain" is a single body at 0.39 AU. As a picture of a compact multi-planet system it is evocative; as TRAPPIST-1 it fails on count, scale, masses, ratios, labelling and stability.

Grand Tack — the tack fires, at the wrong address

The preset starts Jupiter and Saturn at 4.6 and 7.7 AU — a period ratio of 2.166, not the advertised 3:2 — and relies on Saturn's faster Type II migration to close the gap. That part is the real Grand Tack mechanism, and it works: the audit drove the preset until the page's reversal banner fired. But by then Jupiter had migrated from 4.6 AU to 0.67 AU (Saturn at 0.89 AU, ratio 1.538) — the reversal condition checks the ratio, so the pair spends so long converging that Jupiter crosses the entire inner disk first. In the Walsh et al. model the tack happens at ~1.5 AU, which is what leaves Mars small and the asteroid belt mixed. A tack at 0.67 AU would have very different consequences, and the banner does not mention where it happened.

Solar Nebula and HL Tau — smaller bends

  • Proto-Saturn is shipped at 28 M⊕ (real Saturn: 95 M⊕) yet flagged a gas giant in "runaway" state; Proto-Jupiter is 260 M⊕ against the real 318. As protoplanets still growing, low masses are defensible — but the roster presents them without that caveat.
  • HL Tau's rings are real; its planets are invented. ALMA's famous HL Tau image shows dark gaps at roughly 13, 32 and 69 AU in a disk extending past 100 AU, and no planet there is confirmed. The page compresses the disk to 28 AU and installs four named giants of 130–240 M⊕ at 4.5–21.8 AU as the gap-carvers. A reasonable hypothesis, presented as the system.
  • The star never changes mass. The luminosity slider runs to 6 L☉, but Hill spheres, orbital frequencies and the snow-line physics all keep a 1 M☉ star — fine for a T Tauri Sun, quietly wrong at the slider's ends, and the reason the TRAPPIST preset's 0.15 L☉ floor (the slider's minimum) still leaves its planets orbiting a solar-mass star.

Where it bends — the full list

Gap carving is purely cosmetic. Depth = M²/(M² + 520), capped at 95%, switched on at 3.5 M⊕, identical at every radius, temperature and density. No scale height, no viscosity, no Crida criterion — despite the code comment saying "Crida-like." Three different width formulas are used in three different places.

The Myr clock is decorative. 0.045 Myr per simulation second ages the disk 0.182 Myr per 1 AU orbit — ~182,000× the rate its own Keplerian motion implies. Growth that "takes Myr" on the readout does so by the clock's say-so, not by the dynamics.

Pebble growth is a fixed lump per capture (0.19 M⊕, ×2.9 icy), with no mass dependence, no oligarchic slowdown and no pebble-isolation mass — and measured growth runs backwards across the snow line (228 captures at 1 AU vs 3 at 5 AU in equal time), contradicting the "tripling growth rates" caption as a rate.

Runaway is a cliff, not a transition. At exactly 10.0 M⊕ a core becomes a giant in one step. The real critical mass is a range (~5–20 M⊕) set by opacity and accretion rate; 10 is a defensible pick, the instant switch is not physics.

Resonance locking is a spacing nudge. It restores exact Keplerian ratios (measured), but with no libration or eccentricity behaviour, and only inside a narrow capture window — pairs outside ±0.068 in period ratio are invisible to it, which is exactly why most of the TRAPPIST preset never registers as resonant.

Migration has a floor, not a trap physics. Planets pile up at an inner-edge "trap" implemented as a slowdown factor near 1.38 × the disk's inner radius; the Grand Tack measurement (reversal at 0.67 AU) shows how far that simplification can carry a giant before the script notices.

Only water gets a frost line. CO2 (~70 K, 16 AU on this profile) is skipped by the isotherm list; CO is mislabelled at 40 K instead of 20–30 K, on a ring that lies outside every preset disk and is therefore never drawn.
