Cosmology Expansion — the lesson

A cosmic web of about 600 galaxies expanding under the Friedmann equations: pick a universe — our flat ΛCDM, a closed Big Crunch, an open coasting cosmos, a phantom Big Rip, or the matter-only Einstein–de Sitter model — and watch its scale factor a(t) run. This page is the audit. Following the house rule, the shipped code was not just read: its own cosmology engine was extracted and driven headlessly in Node (DOM and canvas stubbed), every preset was integrated under the page's own parameters, and the resulting a(t) curves were checked against independent numerical integrations of the same Friedmann equations done for this lesson. The engine turns out to be remarkably exact. The bends are almost all in the display layer on top of it — and one displayed number is simply invented.

The verdict: a real Friedmann engine, integrated correctly

The core is genuine physics, not an animation faked to look like it. The page integrates the second Friedmann (acceleration) equation with RK4 — radiation (Ωr = 9.2×10−5), matter, curvature and a dark-energy term with a free equation of state w — starting from a = 1, ȧ = H0 today and running both backwards to the Big Bang and forwards to the fate of each model, gently re-projecting onto the first Friedmann constraint as it goes. The audit's independent integrals (different code, different method) agree with the shipped trajectories to three or four significant figures on every preset — ages, turnaround, collapse and rip times all match (tables below). That is the strongest kind of receipt this collection knows how to give.

(ȧ/a)² = H₀² [ Ω_r a⁻⁴ + Ω_m a⁻³ + Ω_k a⁻² + Ω_Λ a⁻³⁽¹⁺ʷ⁾ ] The equation as printed on the page — and, unusually, the equation the page actually solves. Curvature is never a free slider: Ωk = 1 − Ωm − ΩΛ − Ωr, as it should be.

1 · The Hubble law: exact — because it is the definition being plotted

The default Hubble constant is H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc (slider 40–100), squarely inside the real, still-argued-over 67–73 km/s/Mpc range. Sampling the shipped engine at a = 1 under the ΛCDM preset returns H = 70.0 km/s/Mpc exactly, and recession velocity is computed everywhere — tooltips, vectors, the v vs d plot — as v = H(t)·d:

Two honest qualifications. First, this linearity is tautological, not empirical: recession velocity in cosmology is defined as H·d, so the straight line in the plot can never bend. The observed Hubble diagram — redshift against distance — is not a straight line beyond low z, and the page never plots that one; its galaxies live at Gpc distances where cz = H0d would already be wrong. Second, the scatter the plot sprinkles around the line is exaggerated — see the bends.

  • Matter-only power law: exact. A log–log fit to the shipped Einstein–de Sitter trajectory gives a ∝ t0.6665 — the theoretical t2/3. The young ΛCDM universe does the same (fit slope 0.6669 between 0.3 and 2 Gyr), because matter ruled then. The preset's own banner claim "a(t) ∝ t^(2/3)" is true of its own sim.
  • Λ-dominated late time: exponential, as required. Under ΛCDM the measured H falls from 70 today toward a constant: 61.5 at 20 Gyr, 58.7 at 30 Gyr, 58.2 at 40 Gyr — against the de Sitter asymptote H0√ΩΛ = 58.15 km/s/Mpc. The logarithmic growth rate of a settles at 0.0597–0.0611 Gyr−1 against the theoretical 0.05947 Gyr−1. That is exponential expansion arriving.
  • The Big Crunch really crunches. ȧ was tracked along the whole shipped trajectory: it changes sign at t = 31.79 Gyr, at a maximum scale factor amax = 1.714 — exactly the analytic Ωm/(Ωm−1) = 2.4/1.4 — and a then falls back to zero at t = 63.57 Gyr (independent total lifetime: 63.58 Gyr). After turnaround the telemetry H goes properly negative (−10.5 km/s/Mpc at 40 Gyr) and the fate badge flips to "Big Crunch ↓". No cosmetic collapse: the integration turns around and comes home.
  • The Big Rip really diverges in finite time. With w = −1.45 the shipped run flags a rip at 38.36 Gyr and caps the trajectory there; the independent integral of the remaining time from today gives the same figure. H at 30 Gyr is already 173 km/s/Mpc and climbing — phantom behaviour, correctly signed.

4 · Redshift: 1 + z = 1/a, honoured — including on the light cone

Redshift is the best-handled quantity on the page. The headline readout uses z = 1/a − 1 exactly; photon wave-packets stretch as λobs = λemit·(anow/aemit); the CMB temperature runs as T = 2.7255 K / a (at the Early preset's z = 10 start: 30.0 K, matching its banner). Better still, a galaxy's displayed "light-cone z" is not the naive formula — the page integrates conformal time η = ∫ c dt/a along the trajectory and inverts it, asking at what scale factor was light emitted that arrives now from comoving distance χ? The audit compared that inversion against independent comoving-distance integrals for the same cosmology:

5 · Horizons: one computed well, one misnamed, one missing

  • Particle horizon — real, and 2% small. The "Observable Horizon" sphere is the conformal-time integral itself: measured η(today) = 13.43 Gpc comoving under the sim's parameters, against 13.71 Gpc from the independent integral (the shortfall comes from the analytic seed used for the first trajectory step). With Planck parameters the real figure is ≈14.4 Gpc (≈47 billion light-years); the sim's H₀ = 70 / Ωm = 0.31 universe genuinely has a slightly smaller one, so most of the gap to the textbook number is parameters, not error.
  • Hubble sphere — right radius, wrong surname. RH = c/H(t) is computed correctly (4.283 Gpc today), and the page's note that photons emitted beyond it can still reach us as the sphere grows is correct and well put. But the toggle labels it "(v = c Horizon)". The Hubble sphere is not a horizon of any kind — nothing is causally cut off there — and calling it one teaches the exact misconception the page's own note then has to undo.
  • Event horizon — absent. No preset computes or draws the cosmic event horizon (≈4.9 Gpc proper today in real ΛCDM — the boundary beyond which light emitted now will never reach us). In a page this thorough about horizons, the omission is worth naming: the one horizon that decides what we can ever see in future is the one not shown.

6 · The age of the universe: 13.34 in the sim, 13.8 in reality — and both are "right"

The sim's ΛCDM age, measured from its own integration, is 13.34 Gyr. The independent integral for the sim's exact parameters (H₀ = 70, Ωm = 0.31, ΩΛ = 0.69) is 13.339 Gyr — so the integrator is not drifting; the parameters are what differ from Planck's (H₀ = 67.4, Ωm = 0.315), which give the familiar 13.8 Gyr. Dial H₀ down toward 67 on the page's own slider and its age climbs toward the real one. One inconsistency is the page's own: the static label beside the sliders reads "t₀ = 13.5 Gyr", a rounded figure the live engine immediately replaces with its computed 13.34 — the printed number and the computed number never quite agree.

Where it bends

Flagged plainly — one displayed redshift is invented. For any galaxy beyond the particle horizon, the page stops computing redshift and fabricates one: the code sets z = max(4, 2.5 × |v/c|). A galaxy at recession velocity 2c is labelled z = 5, at 3c, z = 7.5 — numbers with no derivation behind them, shown in the same typeface as the exact light-cone redshifts everywhere else. Worse, those galaxies are drawn at all (dimmed to 52%): by definition nothing beyond the particle horizon has ever been observable, so their presence in the sky — let alone their redshifts — is fiction. The hover tooltip, to its credit, refuses this: it says "Beyond Horizon". The galaxy tint and the scene do not.

The Hubble diagram's scatter is exaggerated 18×. Each galaxy carries a peculiar velocity drawn from ±0.0012c (±360 km/s — genuinely realistic, and the code comment says so). The v vs d plot then multiplies that term by 18, scattering points up to ±6,480 km/s off the Hubble line. As a picture of "galaxies have small random motions" it reads clearly; as data it overstates the effect by more than an order of magnitude, with no label saying so.

"Hubble Sphere (v = c Horizon)" — see §5. A misnomer in a toggle label, contradicted by the page's own (correct) explanatory note two panels later.

The particle horizon runs 2% small (13.43 vs 13.71 Gpc for the sim's own parameters) because the conformal-time integral is seeded with a matter-era approximation for its first step instead of integrated from zero. Everything built on η — light-cone redshifts included — inherits a share of that offset, though as §4 shows, the redshifts land within ~0.1% anyway.

The cosmic web is a stage set, and says so only quietly. The ~608 galaxies carry famous names (Milky Way, Andromeda, Virgo A…) attached to seeded-random comoving positions out to 13.5 Gpc, in a web gently flattened (y × 0.78) "for richer 3D perspective". No galaxy is where its namesake really is, and at early epochs most of the web sits beyond the then-particle horizon. As a cosmology demonstrator that is a fair trade — but the names invite a trust the positions haven't earned.

Display caps, not physics caps. The a(t) plot clips its axis at a = 8 and the Big Rip trajectory is halted at a = 24–28 with the rip time finished off by an analytic remainder (which the audit verified: 38.36 Gyr). Both are reasonable engineering; neither is labelled on the page.

Static placeholder telemetry disagrees with the boot state. The HTML as shipped shows 4.20 Gyr / a = 0.462 / H = 148 km/s/Mpc before the first frame, while the ΛCDM preset actually starts at t = 3.6 Gyr. The live engine overwrites these within a frame, so a visitor never sees the mismatch — but the file's printed numbers are not the sim's numbers.
