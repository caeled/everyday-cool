Gravitational Lensing — the lesson

A galaxy bends light the way a lens bends light — and when a background source sits almost exactly behind it, the bent light arrives as arcs, multiple images, or a complete Einstein ring. This lab ray-shoots every pixel through the real lens equation. The audit extracted the shipped physics core, drove it headlessly through the page's own controls, and measured image positions, deflections and magnifications from the shipped math. The core is genuinely exact — the point-lens solver reproduces the analytic answers to three decimals. But two headline numbers the page displays are wrong, and they are flagged in red below: the lens mass is understated by a factor of about 2.2 for the page's own stated geometry, and a centred circular elliptical lens reports a magnification of 2,068× that is a solver artefact, not physics.

The verdict: exact lens equation, honest ring — two broken headline numbers

The deflection law is the real one, measured. For the point-mass model the shipped code computes α = θE² ⁄ θ — the reduced form of Einstein's deflection α̂ = 4GM ⁄ (c²b). Driving evalDeflection() directly at θE = 1.65″:

Impact parameter bMeasured αθE²/b
0.50″5.440″5.445″
1.00″2.722″2.723″
2.00″1.361″1.361″
4.00″0.681″0.681″

Doubling b from 1″ to 2″ changed α by a factor of 1.9996 (the tiny shortfall is the code's 0.016″ softening core, there to keep the centre finite). Doubling the mass — which in this page means doubling θE² — doubled α by a factor of exactly 2.000; quadrupling the mass quadrupled it, exactly 4.000. And at b = θE the measured deflection is 1.6498″ ≈ θE itself, which is precisely the self-consistency condition that defines the Einstein ring.

The ring lands on θE three independent ways. (1) Solving the shipped lens equation β = θ − α(θ) = 0 along an axis gives a root at θ = 1.64992″. (2) The page's own critical-curve extraction (marching squares on det A = 0) draws 132 segments whose radii run 1.646–1.657″, mean 1.652″ — the grid's resolution, centred on 1.65″. (3) The actual rendered picture: the audit injected a compact bright source into the page's texture slot, ran its ray-shooter, and measured the radial brightness profile of the output frame — the annulus peaks at 1.653″ against the dialled θE of 1.65″, with essentially zero brightness at 0.5″, 1.0″ and 2.2″. The ring you see is the ring the formula promises.

θE = √[ 4GM/c² · Dls ⁄ (Dl·Ds) ] The worked scale: a 10¹² M☉ galaxy at Dl = 1 Gpc with a source at Ds = 2 Gpc gives θE = 2.02″ (audit-computed) — galaxy lenses really do make rings of an arcsecond or two, so the lab's 0.40–2.75″ slider range is the right regime.

Image formation: two images, an arc, a ring — all measured

For a point lens the images sit at θ± = (β ± √(β² + 4θE²))/2. The page's grid-plus-Newton solver was fed sources at four offsets (u = β/θE) and its roots were read back:

The behaviour the lesson asks for is all there. Slightly off-axis (u = 0.1–0.3) you get two images straddling the lens, and because the page's source is an extended spiral, those two images stretch and merge into the familiar arc in the ray-shot picture. At u = 1 the counter-image has faded to μ = 0.17 against the main image's 1.17 — the dominant-plus-faint pairing of a well-off-axis source. At perfect alignment the solver's ring flag fires and the picture is the full 360° ring. Image parities come out right too: the outer image is +, the inner −, in every case.

The Einstein Cross: four images only where physics allows them

A point lens can never make four images, and neither can a round one — a quad needs the four-cusp astroid caustic that only an elliptical (or sheared) lens produces, with the source inside it. The audit tested the shipped SIE model (θE = 1.72″) across that boundary:

So the Cross is earned, not scripted: dial the ellipticity to zero and the quad disappears; move the source out of the astroid and the images annihilate in pairs, 4 → 3 → 1, exactly as caustic theory says. The four preset images sit roughly north/south/east/west at ~1.5–1.8″ with two positive and two negative parities — the correct anatomy of a quad. Scanning the source inward along an axis, the total magnification jumped from 4.76× (2 images, β = 0.35″) to 19.36× (4 images, β = 0.25″): the fold-crossing spike, in the page's own numbers.

Magnification near caustics: divergence, honestly capped — mostly

For a point source the magnification formally diverges on a caustic (μ = (u²+2)/(u√(u²+4)) → 1/u for the point lens). The page follows the exact formula while it can — measured μ at u = 0.5, 0.2, 0.1, 0.08, 0.07 was 2.18, 5.08, 10.04, 12.53, 14.31, matching the analytic values to the second decimal at every step — and then caps it, because a real source has size: the point-lens total is capped at min(65, 2.4θE/source-size term), and each solved image at min(75, 14/source size). That is the right physics in spirit — finite sources smooth the infinity away — though the cap formulas are heuristics fitted to the source sprite's bright core, not an integration over the source disc. Treat the capped numbers as "tens, not thousands," which is the true statement for galaxy-sized sources.

Where it bends

FALSE — the lens mass is understated by a factor of ≈ 2.2. The telemetry converts θE to mass with a coefficient of 1.23×10¹¹ M☉ per arcsec², stated in the code to be for a fiducial zL = 0.5, zS = 1.5 — so the default 1.65″ lens reads 3.35×10¹¹ M☉. Recomputing that exact geometry in standard ΛCDM (Dl ≈ 1,259 Mpc, Ds ≈ 1,746 Mpc, Dls ≈ 990 Mpc at H0 = 70) gives 2.73×10¹¹ M☉ per arcsec², i.e. the 1.65″ lens should read ≈ 7.4×10¹¹ M☉. Even the simple 1 Gpc / 2 Gpc geometry gives 2.46×10¹¹ per arcsec² — the page's coefficient is about half of every honest version of itself. The θE slider is fine; every mass the page derives from it is roughly half what its own stated geometry implies.

FALSE — a centred round SIE lens reports μ = 2,068×. Set the SIE model to ellipticity 0 with the source at the centre and the telemetry magnification reads 2068.2×. What happened, measured: at exact alignment the grid solver returns ~65 sample points strung around the ring (it did the same for the point lens — 63 roots), and while the point model overrides the total with a finite-source estimate (14.9×), the SIE branch simply sums the per-image caps: 65 × 31.82 = 2,068. A near-centre round SIE is likewise reported at 63.6×, which is just two images each pinned at the 14/source-size cap. The picture in both cases is a correct Einstein ring; the magnification number is a counting artefact. Related artefact: at low ellipticity (e = 0.10, source near centre) the solver simultaneously reports 4 roots and the "Aligned Ring!" flag — the two diagnostics disagree with each other.

A counter-image quietly vanishes at wide offsets. The solver discards any image with μ < 0.06 (a filter written for the demagnified central image of an SIE). For a point lens at u = 3 that eats the genuine counter-image (analytic μ = 0.008 at θ = −0.50″): the page then displays "1 Image / Weak Lensing," even though a point lens always forms two images and the page's own analytic total (1.017×) still includes the missing one. The picture barely changes — the image is 100× fainter than the main one — but the multiplicity readout is wrong there.

The Cross preset is the right shape at twice the real scale. The page never names the real system, but "Einstein Cross" is one specific object: Q2237+0305 (Huchra's Lens), a quasar at z = 1.695 behind a spiral galaxy at z = 0.039, whose four images span 1.8″ in total around an Einstein radius of 0.89″. The preset uses θE = 1.72″, so its cross spans ~3.5″ — about double. As a demonstration of quad geometry it is correct; as a portrait of the actual Cross it is scaled up ~2×.

No named real system is actually modelled. There is no Q0957+561 double quasar anywhere in the page, no named Einstein ring, and the "Abell Cluster" is five invented halos (BCG Core, Halo E-2, W-3, S-4, SW-5), not a catalogued Abell cluster. SMACS 0723 — the real JWST cluster (SMACS J0723.3-7327, z = 0.39, ~8×10¹⁴ M☉, dozens of multiply-imaged galaxies) — is mentioned only in a code comment about where the background galaxies are sprinkled; the cluster preset is not a model of it. Cluster masses carry a further invention: after the (already low) coefficient, the cluster total is multiplied by an unexplained ×38 "dark-matter halo + ICL multiplier," and the displayed cluster θE is √(ΣθE,i²) = 1.95″ for the default halos — a bookkeeping convention, not a measured cluster Einstein radius.

"Microlensing" at galaxy scale. The sparkline is labelled a microlensing light curve and one preset is a "Binary Microlens / Twin BH," but everything runs at θE ≈ 1.5″ — galaxy-lens (macro) scale. True microlensing Einstein radii are milliarcseconds. The caustic-crossing phenomenology the page shows is real and shared by both regimes; the label borrows the micro name for a macro picture, and the drifting source whose μ(t) is plotted is an extended galaxy, not the point star of a microlensing event.

Small bends. The point model wears a "Schwarzschild" badge — a point mass is a Schwarzschild lens only in the black-hole sense; a galaxy is not one, though the deflection formula is the same. The Cross preset's status pill reads "Caustic Spike!" rather than "Inside Astroid Caustic" because the μ ≥ 14 label check runs before the 4-image check. The SIE lens carries a small softening core (s = 0.045θE), so it is strictly a softened isothermal ellipsoid — which is also why it can show a third, central image at some offsets where a perfectly singular SIE would hide one. None of these touches the ray-shot picture, which is an honest backward ray-shoot through β = θ − α(θ) with surface brightness conserved, as the page claims.
