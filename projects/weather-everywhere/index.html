THE ANTARCTICA QUESTION

How can it be summer
on a frozen continent?

Summer means a season, not a promise of warmth. Antarctica’s coast can approach freezing in summer; its high interior stays much colder. The ocean, elevation, latitude, and sunlight all matter.

OPPOSITE WEATHER / THE OTHER SIDE OF THE PLANET

Somewhere, the weather is opposite.

Most of Earth is water, so the point exactly opposite a place is usually ocean weather. This tool does not move the point to the nearest city. It shows the real opposite point, with its coordinates, and says plainly when it is open ocean.

No opposite weather loaded yet. Nothing is sent until you choose a comparison.

The antipode lies through the center of the Earth from the selected place: latitude reversed, longitude shifted by 180 degrees. The mirror point keeps the same longitude and reverses only the latitude. Conditions are Open-Meteo model values, not station observations.

ILLUSTRATIVE MODEL

What this little model can—and cannot—tell us

Density is a linear approximation near ordinary seawater: warmer water is less dense; saltier water is denser. Our circulation index combines an invented wind contribution and density contrast. The coast relaxes toward an invented heat-influence target over 30 model steps. Changing salinity affects that pathway, not rain directly.

The moisture index uses warm-water evaporation potential and wind direction. It is not rainfall in millimeters. This model omits Earth's rotation, real geography, ocean depth structure, clouds, storms, and many feedbacks. Steps have no real-world time unit. Never read this as an AMOC forecast or a prediction for a named place.

THE CLOUD MAKER / MAKE THE INVISIBLE VISIBLE

A mountain.
A little moisture.
A different sky.

Follow a parcel of air uphill. Find the height where cooling makes water vapor condense—and discover why clouds sometimes never appear.

Build a cloud

The science behind the picture

We estimate dew point using the Magnus formula, then cloud base as about 125 meters for each degree Celsius between temperature and dew point. Unsaturated air cools about 9.8°C per kilometer; above cloud base we use a fixed 6°C per kilometer to illustrate latent heat release. The real saturated cooling rate varies.

This is a single parcel sketch. It doesn’t predict rain, cloud thickness, or the full weather behind the mountain. Cooling to saturation can form a cloud; having a cloud does not guarantee rainfall.

SUN AND TILT / WHAT IF THE EARTH LEANED DIFFERENTLY?

Lean the planet.
Slide the year.

Seasons come from a lean of about 23.5 degrees. Change the lean, move a town to a new latitude, or slide through the days of the year and watch the sunlight change. Everything in this room is computed on this device. No internet is needed, and none of the values is measured weather.

WHAT-IF TILT

Computed teaching math: the sun's declination from the tilt, then day length from the sunrise hour angle. Twilight is not included. These are hours of sunlight geometry, not weather.

Only the latitude changes. The town keeps its name, and the sunlight follows the new line around the planet.
