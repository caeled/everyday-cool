Radiative Forcing, Greenhouse Photons & Climate Feedbacks — the lesson

The exhibit is a one-layer energy-balance climate you can fly by hand: gold solar photons rain in, crimson infrared photons rise and get intercepted and re-emitted by greenhouse molecules, polar ice grows and retreats, and sliders drive CO2, solar luminosity, clouds and aerosols through six presets. Its constants and its core equations are the real ones — the Stefan–Boltzmann law in its code is exact to five decimal places. But when the audit drove the shipped model to equilibrium, preset by preset, the climates it actually settles into were not the climates on its labels. Verdict up top: the physics engine is honest equation-by-equation, but the calibration is not — the “balanced” 14 °C pre-industrial Earth is not a stable state of its own model at all. Left alone it runs away to +32 °C, and its bare-rock Earth misses the famous −18 °C in both directions at once.

False claims — flagged prominently

FALSE — Pre-Industrial is not “280 ppm · ~14 °C” balanced. The preset starts at 14.0 °C under a pill that reads “Balanced Equilibrium,” and a code comment claims it is calibrated so 280 ppm balances at 14 °C. Driven as shipped (dt = 0.016 s, the browser's own step), it does not stay there: the ice relaxes toward its 14 °C target faster than the temperature can respond, albedo falls, and the planet warms through 18.7 °C (sim-year 29), 24.4 °C (year 58) and 28.1 °C (year 87) to an equilibrium of 32.3 °C (305.5 K, ice 0.8%, albedo 0.185). Worse, 14 °C is not an unstable point you can nudge back to — under pre-industrial forcing the model is bistable with no temperate attractor: starting the same forcing at 280 K or below settles at −36.9 °C (a snowball), and starting at 287 K or above settles at +32.3 °C. The observed Earth — 288 K, ≈ +33 K of greenhouse effect over the 255 K bare-rock temperature — appears in this exhibit only as the preset's opening frame, never as a state its physics will hold.

FALSE — “No Atmosphere · 0 ppm · −18 °C bare” does not produce −18 °C. The textbook number needs albedo α = 0.30: absorbed sunlight is 1361 × (1 − 0.30) / 4 = 238 W/m², and σT⁴ = 238 W/m² gives Te = 254.6 K ≈ −18.6 °C. The exhibit's bare preset never has that albedo. Its surface albedo is 0.08 + 0.54 × (ice fraction), so with the ice–albedo feedback on (the shipped default) the airless planet freezes, ice runs to 100%, albedo climbs to 0.62, and it equilibrates at 218.5 K = −54.6 °C. Switch the ice feedback off and the code hard-codes ice to 28%, albedo 0.231, and it equilibrates at 260.6 K = −12.5 °C. −18 °C would need 40.7% ice in its own albedo formula — a setting no preset or toggle produces. The −18 on the button is the right textbook number pasted onto a model that lands 36 degrees below it or 6 degrees above it, depending on a checkbox.

FALSE — “Runaway Venus” does not run away, and is not Venus. Driven to equilibrium, the Venus preset (2,000 ppm, sun at 130%, 70% cloud) settles at 331.0 K = 57.9 °C — a very hot Earth, 406 degrees short of Venus's 464 °C surface. No runaway is possible in this code at any setting: the trapping fraction ε is capped at 0.985, so outgoing radiation always keeps at least ≈ 0.505 × σT⁴, and T⁴ always catches up. (The status pill still announces “Runaway Greenhouse” above 60 °C.) Venus's actual runaway needed a steam atmosphere and a CO2 inventory the slider cannot express — it tops out at 2,000 ppm against Venus's ≈ 96% CO2 at 92 bar.

FALSE by labelling — the “🌋 Pinatubo Eruption” button is not Pinatubo. The button sets stratospheric aerosols to 78%, which at fixed temperature cuts absorbed sunlight by 42.5 W/m². The real 1991 Pinatubo eruption peaked at roughly −3 to −4 W/m² and cooled the planet ≈ 0.5 °C for about two years; in this model's own units, one Pinatubo is ≈ 7% on its aerosol slider, not 78%. And because the model is bistable, the button's overshoot is not temporary: pressed once from the pre-industrial preset and left alone while the aerosol decays away on the code's own schedule, the planet dips to −45.6 °C and ends in the permanent snowball state at −36.9 °C, ice 99.9% — a temporary veil becomes a one-way door.

What this is

  • A zero-dimensional energy-balance model with a photon diorama on top. One global temperature, one ice fraction, one trapping fraction. Each step computes absorbed sunlight, outgoing infrared, and warms or cools the planet by the imbalance divided by a thermal-inertia constant. That is the legitimate first model of climate science — the one that produces 255 K for a bare rock and ≈ 303 K for a planet under one fully absorbing atmospheric layer — so it can be graded against closed-form answers, and this audit graded it that way.
  • The greenhouse effect is a single number, ε (“IR Trapped %”). ε is built from a logarithmic CO2 term, a temperature-dependent water-vapour term, and a cloud term, capped at 0.985. Outgoing radiation is then (1 − ε/2)·σT⁴ — which, as the credit side notes, is exactly the standard single-layer result, not an invention.
  • Feedbacks are toggles, and they are strong. Ice follows a smooth sigmoid of temperature, water vapour adds trapping as the planet warms, and an optional permafrost switch adds methane (as CO2-equivalent ppm) above 17 °C. Together they create the bistability measured above — real behaviour for this class of model, tuned here until the temperate middle falls out.

How the audit measured it

The shipped page's script was extracted unedited and run in Node with the DOM and canvas stubbed and a seeded random generator; a harness-only hook exposed its own state, stepClimateODE() and applyPreset() so the audit could call the exhibit's own energy-balance step directly — at dt = 0 (fluxes at a fixed temperature, nothing integrates), at dt = 0.016 s (the browser's trajectory), and in long runs to equilibrium (|imbalance| < 0.01 W/m²). Every preset was applied through the page's own preset function with the shipped default toggles (ice feedback on, water-vapour feedback on, methane off), and feedback combinations were then separated toggle by toggle. Independent checks were computed from σ = 5.670374×10−8 and S0 = 1361 W/m² — the same constants the page itself uses. Nothing in the guest file was edited.

absorbed = S0(1 − α) / 4  ·  emitted = σT⁴  ·  one layer: OLR = (1 − ε/2)·σTs⁴ The three equations this exhibit is built from — and the three it was graded against. With α = 0.30 they give 238 W/m² and Te = 254.6 K; with ε = 1 they give Ts = 21/4·Te ≈ 303 K.

What's true — the credit side

  • The constants are exact. σ = 5.670374e-8 is the CODATA value and S0 = 1361 W/m² is the modern solar constant — not the rounded 1367 or 1400 of older textbooks.
  • The Stefan–Boltzmann core is perfect. With the greenhouse zeroed, outgoing radiation matched σT⁴ to five decimal places at four temperatures, and a perturbation test recovered the exponent 4.000. Whatever else is miscalibrated, the engine's heart is the real law.
  • The one-layer model is the textbook one. (1 − ε/2)·σT⁴ is the correct top-of-atmosphere result for a single atmospheric layer of emissivity ε radiating both up and down, and ε < 1 is handled properly throughout — the layer is grey, not a blackbody switch. At ε = 1 this formula is the one that yields the standard ≈ 303 K, and the code's 0.502 in place of ½ costs less than half a percent.
  • CO2 forcing is logarithmic in shape. Each doubling costs the same 7.51 W/m² as the last — the saturation behaviour that makes forcing go as ln(C/C₀) is genuinely in the code, via a 0.056·ln(C/280) term in ε. Only the coefficient is doubled (see bends).
  • The tipping point is real. The ice sigmoid plus albedo feedback produces true hysteresis: drag the planet into the snowball state and restoring pre-industrial forcing does not thaw it (−36.9 °C, ice 99.9%). That is the actual behaviour of energy-balance models since Budyko and Sellers, and the exhibit earns its “Ice-albedo tipping!” label — it just doesn't warn you the same trapdoor sits under the temperate presets.
  • Clouds net-cool, the sun is linear, and the photon picture is honest. Cloud cover cools on net by about the real amount; solar luminosity scales absorbed energy linearly; and the diorama's infrared photons really are intercepted and re-emitted isotropically — half of them back down — which is the correct cartoon of the greenhouse mechanism.

Bends named honestly

The forcing coefficient is doubled, silently. The page prints no forcing number, so nothing stated is false — but every CO2 experiment a visitor runs is powered by Δε = 0.056·ln 2 per doubling, which its own OLR formula converts to 7.51 W/m², twice the 5.35·ln(C/C₀) ≈ 3.7 W/m² of the real atmosphere. That single coefficient is why even its feedbacks-off “Planck” response comes out at 2.32 °C instead of ≈ 1.15 °C. The page never says which sensitivity it models; measured, it models a hot one at every toggle combination.

ε is a calibration bundle, not a measurement. The “IR Trapped %” readout mixes a 0.56 CO2 base, a 0.14 water-vapour base that is present even with the H2O feedback switched off, and up to 0.145 from clouds. At the pre-industrial settings these happen to sum to 0.7725 — close to a defensible effective emissivity — but no slider maps to any one physical gas, and removing all CO2 while keeping clouds still leaves ε = 0.0725 of pure cloud trapping.

The feedbacks are tuned past the middle. Ice relaxes toward its temperature target at 0.85 per simulated second while the planet's thermal inertia is far slower, so in any race the ice wins — that asymmetry is what melts the pre-industrial Earth out from under its own starting temperature, and what makes the model's only two resting places a snowball and a hothouse. Real ice sheets are slower than oceans, not faster.

Methane is CO2 in a costume. The permafrost feedback adds “ppm-eq” methane directly into the CO2 logarithm, 1 ppm for 1 ppm — real methane forcing follows a square-root law at ppb concentrations and is far stronger per molecule. In practice the bend barely matters here: at the 850 ppm preset it adds only +0.6 °C (40.6 → 41.1), because ε is already near its 0.985 cap. The 17 °C release threshold is an invention, labelled with a real mechanism's name.

Aerosol percent is an abstract dial wearing a volcano's name. The slider's percent maps linearly onto albedo (+0.16 at 100%); nothing on the page converts it to the W/m² a visitor would need to know that 7% ≈ Pinatubo and 78% ≈ eleven Pinatubos held at once. Held at 78% indefinitely, the model equilibrates at −56.4 °C.

The visual photon flux scales as T³, not T⁴. The infrared spawn rate in the diorama goes as (T/287)³ while the energy code it illustrates goes as T⁴ exactly. A visitor counting photons undercounts the warming signal — a small bend, in the safe direction, but a bend.

No Milankovitch, no orbital anything. Despite the room it lives in, the page makes no claim about eccentricity, obliquity or precession — the only solar lever is a luminosity percentage (75–135%, far outside the real solar cycle's ±0.1%). Nothing false; just know the ice ages are not in this room.

The clock and the clamps are stage machinery. A simulated year passes every ≈ 0.22 real seconds (4.5 years per second), the thermal inertia is an unlabelled divisor of 18, and temperature is hard-clamped to 185–740 K. The 740 K ceiling sits above Venus's surface — the one place a clamp could have hidden a runaway — but the ε cap makes it unreachable anyway.
