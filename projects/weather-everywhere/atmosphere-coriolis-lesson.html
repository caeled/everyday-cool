Coriolis, Global Circulation & Hurricanes — the lesson

The exhibit has two rooms. In Global mode, a 3-cell circulation with Rossby jet streams wraps a globe (or a Mercator map), and a “Coriolis Cannon” fires tracer air parcels whose curved paths are drawn against a dashed no-Coriolis ghost. In Hurricane Lab, a vortex spins over an interactive sea-surface-temperature ocean, intensifying over a warm pool and decaying over land. The parcel integrator at its heart is genuinely good — driven headlessly, it reproduces an independent integration of the spherical rotating-frame equations to about a percent, with the right sign in both hemispheres and a Coriolis parameter that, read on the page's own clock, lands within 2% of the real 2Ω sin φ. Verdict up top: the cannon is honest physics; the circulation wrapped around it is not — the Ferrel cell's surface winds blow backwards, and the “straight at the equator” parcel visibly curves in the exhibit's own simulation.

False claims — flagged prominently

FALSE — the Ferrel cell's surface winds blow the wrong way. The Earth 3-Cell preset promises “Hadley, Ferrel, Polar & Rossby jets,” and the code's own comment states the intent: Ferrel-cell surface flow is poleward and deflects east into the Westerlies. The audit averaged the shipped sampleGlobalWind() over all 36 longitudes (Rossby meander switched off) at mid-Ferrel latitude, 45°. Measured in the Northern Hemisphere: u = −0.73 (westward, i.e. easterly wind) and v = −0.28 (equatorward) — exactly backwards on both components. The Southern Hemisphere at −45° is the mirror image (u = −0.73, v = +0.28, also equatorward). The mechanism is visible in the code: the zonal and meridional terms are built from cellSign · sin(phase), and across the Ferrel cell sin(phase) is negative, cancelling the Ferrel cellSign the comment relies on. The Hadley cell (15°: u = −0.99 trades, v = −0.28 equatorward, NH) and the Polar cell (75°: u = −0.41, v = −0.28 equatorward, NH) are both correct — and the eastward jet cores the code plants at the 30° and 60° cell boundaries (u = +1.58 and +1.08 in the same average) partly mask the reversal, because the map's most visible mid-latitude arrows are those jets, not the surface flow between them.

FALSE (as a trajectory claim) — the equatorial parcel does not fly straight. The volley banner announces “Deflects Right in NH · Left in SH · Straight at Equator,” and the equatorial parcel is labelled “EQ → Straight.” Instantaneously the claim is true: at φ = 0 the Coriolis acceleration is exactly zero. But the parcel does not stay at the equator. Fired north from 0° in the shipped code, it measured +0.63° of eastward offset from its ghost after 0.5 sim-seconds and +5.37° after 1.0 sim-second — because the moment it gains latitude, sin φ (and with it f) grows, and the real rotating-sphere physics the code correctly implements bends it east. That is also what a real parcel leaving the equator does. What is false is the page's framing of “straight” as the equatorial trajectory rather than as a single instant's force balance at φ = 0.

What this is

  • The parcel cannon integrates motion on a rotating sphere: du/dt = f·v + u·v·tan φ, dv/dt = −f·u − u²·tan φ, with f = 1.65 · Ωrel · sin φ, a small drag (0.025 /s), and a twin “ghost” parcel integrated with Ω = 0 as the inertial straight-line (great-circle) comparison. Those metric terms are the real spherical terms — this is not a flat f-plane toy.
  • The circulation field is a separate, hand-built wind function: alternating cells whose count grows with Ω, trade/westerly zonal terms, and Gaussian jet cores on the cell boundaries with a Rossby-wave meander. It is a picture assembled from parts, not an integration of the equations of motion — which is exactly where its one reversed cell comes from.
  • The hurricane is a Holland/Rankine-style vortex (solid-body inside the radius of maximum wind, r−0.64 decay outside) whose target intensity is set by an Emanuel-flavoured potential-intensity formula in SST, latitude, shear and land fraction, with pressure from an Atkinson–Holliday-style wind–pressure relation.
  • What is not in the exhibit: there is no rotating-turntable mode (the globe plus ghost is its rotating-frame demonstration), no eastward/westward projectile launch — the cannon fires only north, south, poleward or equatorward — so there is no Eötvös effect to test, and no bathtub, sink or drain claim anywhere on the page. On the sink question the exhibit is silent, and silence is the right call: at sink scale (U ≈ 0.1 m/s, L ≈ 0.01 m) the Rossby number is ≈ 97,000, so Coriolis is utterly negligible there — the page neither repeats the myth nor debunks it, and this audit credits it for not repeating it.

How the audit measured it

The shipped page's script was extracted and run in Node with the DOM and canvas stubbed out and a seeded random generator. Its own launchAirParcel() / stepParcels() were driven at dt = 1/240–1/60 s, its sampleGlobalWind() and sampleCycloneWind() were sampled directly, and every preset was applied through its own applyPreset(). The independent check was a separate RK4 integration (1 ms steps, written for this audit) of the spherical rotating-frame equations du/dt = f·v + u·v·tan φ, dv/dt = −f·u − u²·tan φ, plus the standard real-Earth values f = 2Ω sin φ with Ω = 7.2921×10−5 rad/s. Nothing in the guest file was edited.

aCoriolis = f · v,   f = 2Ω sin φ,   Ω = 7.2921×10⁻⁵ rad/s At 45° and v = 10 m/s the real deflection acceleration is 1.03×10⁻³ m/s² and the inertial period is 16.9 h. The exhibit's clock advances 3.2 sim-hours per sim-second; on that clock the real f at 45° would be 1.19 per sim-second — the code uses 1.65·sin 45° = 1.167, within 2%.

What's true — the credit side

  • The integrator is real spherical physics, verified independently. At every latitude and direction tested, the shipped Euler-substepped integrator tracked this audit's RK4 integration of the rotating-frame equations to within ~1.5% over the first second — including the u·v·tan φ metric terms that a flat-earth model would miss, and the ghost, which matched the Ω = 0 great-circle integration (45° N ghost at t = 1 s: 76.69° in both).
  • The magnitude is calibrated, not just the sign. The page's own clock runs 3.2 sim-hours per sim-second. On that clock the true Coriolis parameter at 45° is 2Ω sin 45° × 11,520 s ≈ 1.19 per sim-second; the code's 1.65·sin φ gives 1.167 — 1.8% low, and because both sides are proportional to sin φ and to Ω, that agreement holds at every latitude and every rotation-rate setting, not just at 45°.
  • Deflection direction is right in all four compass cases — northward and southward motion, in both hemispheres — and correctly weakens toward the equator at fixed early time (+9.59° at 45° vs +4.44° at 26° after 0.5 s).
  • The hurricane spins the right way, both ways. NH counterclockwise, SH clockwise, flipping cleanly with the hemisphere toggle, with a calm eye, maximum winds at the RMW in all directions, surface inflow spiralling inward and upper outflow that reverses anticyclonically aloft — the full textbook vortex anatomy is present and measurable.
  • The supporting numbers are honest: the Saffir–Simpson thresholds, the Earth Rossby number (0.14 against a computed 0.139), the 1 → 3 → 7 cell-count progression from a non-rotating planet to Jupiter, the 26.5 °C SST genesis threshold built into the ocean, and warm-pool SSTs that actually reach the advertised ~32 °C (measured 32.8 °C at the pool centre in the Cat 5 preset).
  • No sink myth. Nothing on the page claims toilets or bathtubs drain by hemisphere. Given how often Coriolis exhibits reach for that example, leaving it out is a correct editorial decision.

Bends named honestly

Parcels fly inertial circles, not gentle deflections. With launch speed 0.56 rad/s and f ≈ 1.17 /s at 45°, the inertial radius v/f is 27.5° of arc (44° at 26°) and the period is 5.4 s, while parcels live 14 s — over two full loops. The graceful curve in the first second is the honest part; after that the tracer is orbiting its inertial circle, which is real physics for an unforced parcel but is not how a pressure-driven air parcel behaves. On the page's own clock that launch speed is ≈ 1,115 km/h — roughly a jet airliner, about a hundred times a trade wind.

The integrator is Euler, despite its comment. The code says “Sub-stepped RK2/Euler”; it is forward Euler at 4 substeps per frame. At frame dt that is accurate to ~1.5% for the first second (measured against RK4), but the error compounds over the 14 s parcel life, which is part of why the long volley paths wander so far from any clean circle.

The pole is a wall, not a crossing. Latitude is clamped at ±83.65° and tan φ at ±1.35 in both the parcel and its ghost. A true great-circle ghost fired north from 45° crosses the pole; the exhibit's ghost sticks at 83.65° (measured: pinned there from t = 1.5 s onward), so near-pole ghost comparisons — and any parcel that loops up there — are artefacts of the clamp.

The HUD jet speed and pressure are formulas, not measurements. Global-mode telemetry computes jet speed as 88 + 52Ω kt and equatorial pressure as 1008 − 4·(ΔT/42) hPa without ever sampling the wind field — hence 140 kt displayed over a field whose measured maximum is 97 kt. The cyclone HUD, by contrast, does report the storm's actual simulated wind and pressure.

Cat 5 overshoot. The spin-up preset peaks at 176 kt and 872 hPa — beyond Cat 5 and within 2 hPa of the lowest sea-level pressure ever observed on Earth (870 hPa, Typhoon Tip) — reached from a tropical storm in about twenty sim-seconds. The direction and mechanism are right; the ceiling and the clock are theatrical.

Basin walls bounce storms. Steering reflects off the basin edges, so the Landfall preset's storm, having decayed to 24–27 kt over land, bounces back over water and re-intensifies to 73 kt within a minute. Real storms do re-intensify after re-emerging over water; they do not ricochet.

No Eötvös, no turntable. Because the cannon only fires meridionally, the genuine eastward/westward asymmetry (an eastward parcel feels a slightly weaker effective gravity and deflects differently from a westward one) is outside the exhibit's scope — not wrong, just absent, and worth knowing before citing the cannon as the complete Coriolis story.
