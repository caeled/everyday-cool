Basaltic Shield Volcanism
Low silica (45–52 wt%) produces short silicate chains and low viscosity (~10² Pa·s). Dissolved H₂O and CO₂ bubbles rise freely and escape gently at the vent, driving effusive lava fountains and broad fluid pahoehoe flows.
Volcanology Physics
Why Silica Controls Explosivity
SiO₄ tetrahedra link into long polymer networks as SiO₂ increases from Basalt (48%) to Rhyolite (72%+), boosting viscosity by 5–6 orders of magnitude. Sticky magma traps expanding gas bubbles until chamber overpressure shatters the summit plug.
Plinian Columns & PDC Collapse
Hot fragmented ash mixes with air and rockets buoyantly past the Tropopause (~12–14 km), spreading into a stratospheric Umbrella Cloud. When dense ash jets lose buoyancy, margins collapse into incandescent ground-hugging Pyroclastic Density Currents (PDCs).
Caldera Collapse & Basalt Injection
Rapidly evacuating a large silicic magma chamber drops roof support, activating inward-dipping ring faults that drop the summit into a sunken Caldera. Injecting hot Basalt into a cooler silicic chamber triggers volatile exsolution and seismic swarms.
