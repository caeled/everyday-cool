River Erosion, Meanders & Delta Builder — the lesson

A 180 × 130 landscape (each cell ≈ 220 m, heights scaled at 4,200 m per unit) carved by three engines working together: Lagrangian erosion droplets that pick up sediment downhill and drop it in standing water, a shallow-water pipe-flow model that moves water and suspended sediment cell to cell, and a meander step that erodes cut banks and builds point bars. The directions are right almost everywhere this audit pushed: erosion grows with slope and discharge, the outer bank of a bend loses and the inner bank gains, sediment drops where the river meets the sea, and the shoreline builds outward. Verdict up top: the fluvial geometry is honest — but the sediment is not conserved. One engine mints sediment out of nothing and another quietly destroys about a quarter of what it moves, so the spectacular delta is built substantially from material that was never eroded, and the uplift dial delivers nine times the rate printed on it.

False claims — flagged prominently

FALSE — the delta is not built from the eroded sediment; a large share is created in the water. When a droplet enters the sea it deposits its real load honestly (65% to a fan cell just ahead, 35% under itself). But the same step also adds dropAmt × 14 to the suspended field of the fan cell — a turbid-plume visual, capped at 1.0 per cell — and suspended is not just a colour: it is the shallow-water engine's transport inventory, which that engine then deposits as real terrain at 4.2× its land rate in sea cells. Driven in isolation for 240 frames, the droplet engine lost 410.7 height-units of terrain yet gained 597.1 units of suspended sediment — total sediment in the system rose by 186.4 units with no source. In a full Alpine run of 240 frames (10.1 kyr on the page's clock), terrain mass grew by +1,080.8 height-units (≈ +194 m averaged over the whole map), land cells grew from 15,126 to 23,374 of 23,400 — and 5,690 of the new-land cells started the run more than 200 m under water. A real delta progrades at its mouths; it does not raise an entire deep ocean basin to sea level in ten thousand years. The “Delta Prograding” status pill is reporting a process whose volume is largely fabricated.

FALSE — the Tectonic Uplift slider's mm/yr is not the rate the terrain receives. The slider is labelled in mm/yr. Driving the thermal/tectonics step alone for 119 steps — the number that occur in 10 kyr of the page's own age clock (0.042 kyr per frame, tectonics every second frame) — at the setting labelled 1.00 mm/yr raised mask-1.0 terrain by 89.9 m, an equivalent rate of ≈ 9.0 mm/yr, nine times the label. The direction is right and the antecedent-gorge preset still works qualitatively, but any reading of “uplift vs erosion balance” off that dial is off by an order of magnitude.

What this is

  • The droplet engine is the classic hydraulic-erosion droplet: it follows the height gradient with inertia, carries sediment up to a capacity, erodes when under capacity and deposits when over it or when forced uphill. Its capacity rule, extracted from the code, is the stream-power idea in droplet clothing:
capacity = max(−Δh, 0.0022) × speed × water volume × 3.2 × Sediment-Capacity slider −Δh is the height drop per step (the local slope), so capacity — and therefore erosion — rises with slope, with flow speed, and with the droplet's water volume (its discharge). That is the E ∝ discharge × slope shape of the stream-power law E = K·Am·Sn, with the erodibility slider playing the role of K. There is no printed formula on the page; this is the rule the shipped code actually applies, and the audit tested its monotonicity directly (below).
  • The shallow-water engine moves water through virtual pipes between cells, derives a velocity field, picks up sediment where capacity exceeds load (land only), and deposits where load exceeds capacity — with capacity cut to 8% and the deposit rate multiplied by 4.2 in sea cells, which is exactly the right instinct: a river entering standing water loses competence and dumps its load.
  • The meander engine samples the velocity two cells downstream, computes the bend's turn (a cross product), and transfers sediment from the bank on the outside of the turn to the bank on the inside, while slightly deepening the channel centre. Oxbow lakes are detected afterwards: cells that once carried strong flow, now run slow, and sit in a local hollow are progressively flagged as oxbow and given standing water.
  • What is not in the model at all: grain sizes. There is one sediment scalar and one suspended scalar — no coarse/fine fractions — so the real-world sorting of a delta (coarse bedload drops at the mouth, fines travel farthest) is not modelled and not claimed; do not read the plume as sorted sediment. Likewise there is no avulsion mechanism: delta lobes spread because each submerged droplet step sprays its fan at a random angle (±1.15 rad), not because a channel ever abandons its course for a steeper one.

How the audit measured it

The shipped page's script was extracted and run in Node with the DOM and canvas stubbed out and a seeded random generator; a test hook appended in the scratch copy (the exhibit file was not touched) exposed its own runDroplet(), stepShallowWater(), stepMeanderAndOxbow(), stepThermalAndTectonics(), stepSimulation() and loadPreset(). Each engine was driven in isolation on synthetic terrain built for the question (uniform sloping planes for stream power, an isolated three-cell-wide arc channel for bend direction), and then all six presets were run whole for 240 frames while total terrain mass, suspended mass, land-cell count and the page's own eroded-volume counter were recorded externally.

What's true — the credit side

  • The stream-power shape is real. Erosion rose monotonically with slope (×12 over a ×16 slope range), with discharge (≈ ×3 for a ×4 water-volume boost), with the capacity slider and — saturatingly — with erodibility. A student who plays with the rain and capacity sliders is feeling the actual E ∝ Q·S relationship, not a scripted animation.
  • The bend geometry is exactly right. In the isolated-arc test the inner bank gained +23.6 m per cell while the outer bank lost −8.8 m per cell, in both flow directions around the arc (the outer bank is the outer bank either way), and a straight reach with the same speed and depth changed by precisely zero. Cut bank, point bar, thalweg deepening — all present, all on the correct side.
  • Deposition happens where competence dies. Droplets dump 58% of their load per step once submerged; the shallow-water engine cuts capacity to 8% and quadruples the deposit rate in sea cells; a short droplet run put +48 m/cell on the shoreline band and literally zero below −200 m. Reservoirs behind dams trap sediment by the same machinery.
  • Base level pushes the system the right way, both ways. +300 m of sea produced hundreds of metres per cell of extra coastal back-filling relative to control; −300 m collapsed shelf deposition and turned the lower hills net-erosional — the exposure-and-incision response the page's own note promises.
  • Thermal slumping is a model citizen — talus and scree moves conserved terrain mass to the last decimal over 240 steps, at two honest angles of repose (soft sediment 0.019, bedrock 0.052 height-units per cell ≈ 20° and 42° at this map's scales).
  • Sediment erodes in the right order of stubbornness: unconsolidated sediment is taken 2.2× faster than bedrock by droplets (and preferentially by the other engines), soft alluvium in the meander preset (0.045 thick, hardness 0.65) migrates far more readily than the canyon's banded strata — which is why the presets feel as different as they look.

Bends named honestly

The clock runs at 2.52 kyr per real second. Age advances 0.042 kyr per frame at speed 1×, so the 10.1 kyr of the audit's Alpine run is four seconds of watching. Nothing printed is false — the age readout does advance at exactly that rate — but every rate a viewer intuits from watching (a basin filling, a canyon cutting) is compressed by that clock, on top of the minted volume.

The “Eroded Volume” counter only counts one engine. totalErodedKm3 is incremented inside runDroplet() alone. Sediment picked up by the shallow-water engine and banks eroded by the meander engine never register, so the telemetry's eroded km³ is a partial ledger — and it is the number a viewer would naturally compare against the growing delta.

The oxbow counter counts cells, not lakes. The probe readout is (flagged cells ÷ 14), where a cell is flagged for once having run fast and now running slow in a hollow. That is why the Volcanic Island — radial drainage, no meanders — reports 25 oxbows and the Grand Canyon 58. The meander preset does ship with one genuinely pre-carved crescent oxbow, and real cutoffs do get flagged; the number is a heuristic index, not a census.

Meander migration is a height transfer, not a bank retreat. The engine moves one cell of sediment per event from outer to inner bank and deepens the centre; the channel migrates because the outer side gets lower and the inner side higher until the flow field follows. There is no neck-cutoff event that suddenly reroutes the river — “cutoffs” in the preset's subtitle arrive gradually, through the oxbow-flagging heuristic above, not through an avulsion.

Droplets that die mid-map take their load with them. A droplet that evaporates (water volume < 0.05), exhausts its 48 steps, or stalls in the sea deposits nothing further — its carried sediment simply leaves the accounting. Some of that is a fair model of export off the map edge; the mid-map evaporation share is sediment vanishing, and it partly masks the plume mint in the whole-run totals.

Erodibility saturates. Per-step droplet erosion is capped at 0.92 × the step's height drop, so past a modest setting the Rock Erodibility slider mostly stops changing droplet behaviour (0.243 → 0.298 km³ across its full useful range, against ×4.9 for the capacity slider). The dial works; it is much weaker than its neighbours.

Delta Area % is a band census, not a shoreline survey. The telemetry counts cells that gained > 0.004 height-units while sitting within −0.025…+0.09 of sea level. It is a reasonable proxy on a young delta and a generous one on a filling basin — one more reason the headline delta numbers read larger than the geomorphology underneath them.
