# Sources & limitations

## Bundled material

The compass-rose trainer and all lesson text are original work written for this workshop (code MIT, lessons CC BY 4.0). The banner image is AI-generated art made for this room, to Steven Powell's concept. No third-party code, fonts, or data are bundled in this folder.

## Loaded at view time (not bundled)

- **Leaflet 1.9.4** — the map library, loaded from the unpkg CDN with pinned integrity hashes. BSD 2-Clause license, © Vladimir Agafonkin and contributors. https://leafletjs.com
- **OpenStreetMap tiles** — map pictures served by the OpenStreetMap Foundation's tile servers. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License (ODbL). https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright
- **Nominatim** — OpenStreetMap's search service, used for ZIP code and town lookups. Its usage policy asks for light, user-driven use, which is how this page uses it: one search per button press, no bulk lookups. https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org
- **pdf.js 3.11.174** — Mozilla's PDF reader, loaded from the unpkg CDN only when a visitor opens a PDF in Build a Map. Apache License 2.0. It reads the file in the browser; the file itself is never uploaded. https://mozilla.github.io/pdf.js/

## Linked shelves (not bundled, each under its own terms)

- OpenStreetMap (openstreetmap.org) — the volunteer-built world map; free to view, editing needs a free account.
- The National Map viewer and program pages (USGS, usgs.gov) — US government topographic maps and data, free.
- Compass Dude (compassdude.com) — free compass lessons by an independent enthusiast; the site carries its own advertising.

## Limitations

Find-me accuracy belongs to the visitor's device: phones with a clear sky view are usually close; desktops and indoor locations can be off by streets. The page says so where the button is. The PDF coordinate hunter sees only coordinates written as decimal-degree text pairs (like 41.2845, -84.3558); a PDF that is a picture of a map, or writes coordinates in degrees-minutes-seconds, will honestly report that none were found. Printed maps carry whatever tiles had loaded on screen. Place search is at its best for US ZIP codes and town names (the search is limited to US results, as stated on the page). Bearing and pace-counting lessons are honest simplifications for beginners; a real trail deserves a real compass, a paper map, and practice somewhere safe first. Shelf descriptions were checked on 15 November 2026 and can change; each site's own pages are the authority.
