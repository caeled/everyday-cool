Set a bearing (click the rose, drag the dial, or type the degrees) and a pace count, then Walk. The teal marker is where your notebook says you are — dead reckoning, worked from your own calls. The ground truth is less tidy: your stride veers a little and wanders as you go, so the walker on the ground (you) drifts off the notebook line. A fix — taken near a landmark — tells you where you actually stand and gives you a fresh bearing from there. Fixes are few. Wandering is free. Navigation is not a precision thing. It is a “go thataway” — and you get there.
