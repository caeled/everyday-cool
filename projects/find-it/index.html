Find
it.

On the road and you need a thing — coffee, a pharmacy, a place to stretch your legs? Say where you are (or let the map find you), tap one big button, and get the nearest matches on a real map, each with directions. No account, no fuss.

Sister room to The Map Room: that room teaches you to read and make maps; this one just finds the thing.

Start with a place.

02 / WHAT DO YOU NEED?

Tap one big button.

Nearest first

    Results come from OpenStreetMap, the volunteer-built map, and are searched within about 5 miles (8 km) of the map's centre spot. A place that nobody has added to OpenStreetMap yet will not show up — the Help section below says what to do then.

    03 / FLIGHT TRACKER

    Where is that plane?

    Meeting someone at the airport, or wondering what just flew over? Type the flight number from the ticket or the arrivals board. Find It puts the plane on the map above, with its height, speed and heading, and draws its usual route between the two airports. US airport delays come from the FAA.

    Live positions come from volunteers' ADS-B receivers (adsb.lol), so a plane over an ocean or far from any receiver may not show. Routes are the flight number's usual route on adsbdb.com — on a rare day the airline may fly it differently. For schedules, gates and arrival times, use the FlightAware or Flightradar24 link under the result.

    The short version

    Three steps

    1. Set your place with Find me or the ZIP / town box. 2. Tap a category button, like Coffee or Pharmacy. 3. Pick from the list — nearest first — and tap Directions to get turn-by-turn routing in Google Maps, or via OpenStreetMap if you prefer.

    That really is the whole app

    Find me and your privacy

    What the button does

    Find me asks your device where it is and uses the answer only to centre this map and search around it. The map moves as soon as the device gives a first answer, then sharpens for up to 20 seconds; the blue ring shows how sure the device is. The last good spot is remembered only inside this open tab (or app window) for up to 10 minutes, so pressing Find me again is instant; closing the tab or app forgets it, and it is never saved to a file or sent anywhere else. If the device cannot answer at all, Find It asks a free "where is this internet connection" service (geojs.io, or ipwho.is as a backup) for a rough, town-level guess and labels it as rough. If you say no to location, nothing else is tried on its own: the ZIP box does the same job with a place name, and a Use a rough guess button appears if you would like the town-level guess instead. The footer gives the full plain-language version.

    Nothing about you is kept after you close it

    Nothing found?

    When the list is empty

    Three usual reasons: the search area is small (about 5 miles), the place is not on OpenStreetMap yet, or the connection hiccuped. Try moving the centre to a bigger town, try a neighbouring category (a clinic for urgent care, a convenience shop for groceries), or wait a minute and tap again.

    Bigger town, second try

    Distances

    Nearest first

    Distances are straight-line — as the crow flies — from your centre spot, the same maths the Map Room uses for trails. Driving distance will be a bit longer; the Directions link gives the real road route.

    Crow flies; car follows roads

    Wrong or missing place?

    OpenStreetMap is editable

    Every result comes from OpenStreetMap, which anyone can improve. If a shop has closed or a new one opened, you can fix it at openstreetmap.org with a free account — and the next traveller's Find It gets better.

    You can fix the map

    Flight tracker

    Type a flight number

    Type the airline code and number, like UA123, DL 1234 or the radio callsign form UAL123. If the plane is broadcasting, it appears on the map as an orange plane pointing the way it flies, with height, speed, heading and when it was last heard. It updates every 20 seconds while this page is on screen, pauses when you switch away, and stops after an hour. "Not broadcasting right now" usually means it is on the ground, not flying at this hour, or over an ocean. Only the flight number is sent (to adsbdb.com, adsb.lol and the cors.dev relay); nothing about where you are. The FAA does not offer a public flight-number search, so Find It shows the airport delay and ground-stop notices the FAA does publish.

    Only the flight number leaves this page

    Works offline?

    Honest answer: no

    Find It needs the internet: map pictures, place searches, and directions are all live services. The Help you are reading is part of the page, so once the page has loaded, it stays readable if the connection drops.

    05 / KEEP GOING

    Shelves worth your time.

    • The Map Room — the sister room: read any map, build your own from pins, and learn the compass (included in the full collection).
    • OpenStreetMap — the map and place data this room runs on, drawn by volunteers. Free to look; anyone can add a missing place.
    • Weather Everywhere — knowing what you need pairs well with knowing what weather is coming (included in the full collection).
    • A Library — Find It has a Library button for a reason: free wifi, bathrooms, charging, and a quiet chair in almost every town (included in the full collection).
    • Shelf It! — the Dewey game — this room finds places by name; the Library's game finds books by subject: type what you're after and it kicks out the Dewey number, the address you walk to the shelf (included in the full collection).

    About location and privacy, plainly: the Find me button asks your device for its location and uses the answer only to centre the map and search around it; it is remembered only in the open tab or app window (for up to 10 minutes, forgotten when you close it) and not sent anywhere except as the centre of the place search described next. Only if the device cannot answer — or you press Use a rough guess — Find It asks geojs.io (or ipwho.is) for a town-level guess from your internet address; those services see that address, as any website does. ZIP and town searches are sent to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service to find the place. Category searches are sent to the Overpass API (or, if it is slow, OpenStreetMap's Nominatim search as a backup) (overpass-api.de first; if that stalls, the community mirrors overpass.kumi.systems and overpass.private.coffee are tried) as a map centre and a radius — that is how it finds what is nearby. The flight tracker sends only the flight number you type: to adsbdb.com for the airline and usual route, and to adsb.lol for the live position through the cors.dev public relay (adsb.lol does not answer web pages directly); FAA airport status comes from nasstatus.faa.gov through the same relay. The map's pictures (tiles) come from OpenStreetMap's servers, which see the area you are viewing, the way any map site does. Directions links open Google Maps or OpenStreetMap in a new tab; where they take you is then their service, under their terms. The map library, Leaflet, loads from the unpkg content network. No accounts, no trackers, no ads, and nothing about you is kept by this page.
