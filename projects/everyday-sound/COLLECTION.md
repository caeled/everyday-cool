# Collection origin

Original Everyday Cool workshop, added 20 November 2026 at Steven Powell's direction. In the Everyday Cool ideas side chat that morning, Muse offered six candidate rooms; Steven picked four and placed two of them himself — "sound and time knots too, and light. (knots go in survival)" — then set the order: "start with sound and time." This room is the first of that pair. Built by Muse (AI assistant) and added directly to the collection at his direction as the thirtieth catalog entry and the eleventh workshop inside The STEM Room; this folder is the maintained original, and there is no upstream copy.

Two scope additions arrived from Steven while the room was being built and are folded in. First, the music wing: his framing, in his words, is that "music is basically math geometry with sound," with a cross-link to Math — so the room carries intervals as ratios (with the equal-temperament compromise tabulated in cents, not hidden), a rhythm bench where a 4/4 measure must sum to exactly 1, and an SVG circle of fifths. Second, the piano grew both ways: what began as a playable scale became a two-octave little piano with five synthesized voices, computer-key and Web MIDI input, and then a "Take a song with you" area — a Standard MIDI File player (formats 0 and 1, tempo changes honoured, keys lighting as notes sound) and a recorder that writes the visitor's own playing out as a real, downloadable `.mid`. The MIDI reader and writer are original code in `sound.js`, written from the MIDI Association's specification; no library is bundled, in keeping with the collection's no-dependencies rule.

It enters the catalog under "Science" with `hubParent: stem-room`, immediately after Everyday Physics, so the STEM Room's bench now reads Physics, Sound, Chemistry, Electronics, Measure, Engineering, Math, Earth, Computing, Body, Radio. The room follows the Science block's shape: benches that work right on the page and show their working, a verified shelf of legitimate places, Help ▾ menu with full documentation, banner verified on the page. Its cross-links: Everyday Physics (waves in general — and Physics' Keep Going list now links back here), Everyday Math (ratios, fractions, logarithms — linked from the music benches, and Math's Keep Going list links back here), Everyday Body (the ears, and the bone-conduction cards), The Radio Room (sound at a distance), Everyday Measure (the units), and Everyday Earth (the thunder the counting trick measures). The STEM Room's own page gained this room's card, recorded in that room's COLLECTION.md.

Everything interactive is computed in the visitor's browser from rules baked into `sound.js` — the page sends nothing anywhere, and a MIDI file the visitor opens is read locally and never uploaded. The decibel ladder's values and the NIOSH safe-exposure rule, the tuning mathematics, the membrane overtones, and the MIDI specification sources are recorded in THIRD-PARTY.md. `test.cjs` re-checks the arithmetic — including a MIDI round trip (write a file, parse it back, compare the notes) and a hand-built format-1 file with a mid-file tempo change — on every run.

Files in this room: `index.html` (the room itself), `style.css`, `sound.js` (the pure rules and the MIDI reader/writer), `app.js` (the wiring: WebAudio benches, the piano and its voices, Web MIDI, playback, recording), `test.cjs`, `README.md`, `THIRD-PARTY.md`, `COLLECTION.md`, `LICENSE`, `LICENSE-CONTENT.md`, and `everyday-sound-banner.jpg`.

Banner: `everyday-sound-banner.jpg`, AI-generated watercolor to the concept in Steven's idea sketch — a friendly robot with one hand cupped to its ear, sound waves drawn as teal and gold ribbons carrying music notes, an acoustic guitar and a drum on the workbench — generated 20 November 2026 as part of adding this room, in the style and dimensions (2736×912) of the collection's other banners. The first generation matched the brief and ships.

The room ships web-first, as the recent STEM rooms have: it reaches the desktop app in a later release, not this one.

Cross-link added 20 November 2026: when Everyday Light joined the collection — the third of Steven's wave-2 picks, built to his note that light gets the same treatment as sound, colour theory and its chords beside the physics as music theory and its chords stand beside the acoustics here — this room's circle-of-fifths section gained its return link to Light's colour-chord wheel, the twin circle: twelve hues arranged by angle as these twelve notes are arranged by fifths — `index.html` and `COLLECTION.md`. No bench, lesson, or behaviour changed.

Game added 20 November 2026: **Tune the String** — `tune-the-string.html`, an original Everyday Cool game built by Muse, the strings bench with a win condition: a target note plays and the visitor matches it with the length and tension sliders under the real string formula f = (1/2L)·√(T/μ), early rounds with a tuner needle to read, later rounds ears-only, won by killing the beating between the two notes — the tuner's real method. Its simplifications (an ideal string with no inharmonicity, μ fixed within a round, equal-tempered targets, synthesized stand-in voices chosen so the beating is easy to hear) and its proof (formula, cents, and beating tests pass; every round's target reachable within the slider ranges, witnesses within 1.2 cents; the audio itself unverifiable headless — Steven's ears are the receipt, as with the piano bench) are recorded in `THIRD-PARTY.md`. The room side of the integration touched `tune-the-string.html` (new), `index.html` (the play link at the strings bench), `README.md`, `THIRD-PARTY.md`, `COLLECTION.md`, and `test.cjs`.

Lesson added, 20 November 2026: `tune-the-string-lesson.html`, `tune-the-string.html`, and `COLLECTION.md`. At Steven Powell's direction the game became a frontdoor/backdoor lesson pair: a lesson page carrying the string formula and cents math, worked examples from the game's proof runs (including the 122.694 N A2 string), the beating method, and the coding choices — plus a 📖 Lesson pop-up inside the game that opens the lesson while playing. The game's pitch-math core is untouched. One correction the lesson carries: a fixed tension step is coarsest in cents at LOW tension, not high — the square root flattens the steps as tension grows — and the page shows the arithmetic.
