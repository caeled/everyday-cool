What you are looking at

Geometry Deep Dive is the high-school geometry shelf of Everyday Math, rebuilt around one argument the whole internet is having: memorize the formulas, or derive them? The formula canon is here in full — but every major formula is also wired into a bench where you can take it apart. The centerpiece slices a circle into sectors and rearranges them until πr² falls out, the way the two most-discussed geometry posts on the social feeds both do it.

The benches

Formula canon — the major formulas as cards. Cards marked with a bench button load straight into that bench with the formula ready to pull apart.

Derive-it-yourself — three views of where πr² comes from. Rearrange the slices: n sectors, straightened into triangles, form a near-parallelogram whose base tends to πr and whose height tends to r; the sliver error is printed live and shrinks as n grows. Unroll into a triangle: the circumference laid flat as a base, the radius as height — area ½ · 2πr · r. Count the squares: a grid over the circle; full squares are certainly inside, touched squares bracket the truth. That one is an estimate, labelled as such — the first two are limit arguments, and the page shows convergence numbers rather than pretending a picture is a proof.

Pythagoras lab — drag the legs (or use the sliders); the squares on the three sides are drawn to scale and their areas computed, so a² + b² = c² is something you see balance. The converse tester takes any three lengths and delivers the verdict by the numbers: right, acute, or obtuse.

Rearrange lab — pick a formula, pick which variable to solve for, and watch the algebra move: divide both sides, take the root, each step printed, and both sides evaluated live under test numbers you enter. Trace mode walks a value through a formula one operation at a time, showing every intermediate number.

Angles & shapes — polygon angle sums for n = 3…12 sides, and two parallel lines cut by a transversal: set one angle and all eight are computed, with the pair names (corresponding, alternate interior, same-side interior, vertical) on call.

Honesty notes

The rearranged-parallelogram picture is a limit argument: at every finite n the straightened figure is a little short of πr², and the page prints exactly how short. Nothing here is a proof by picture alone — the numbers carry the claim, and the proof table at the bottom re-checks every one of them when the page loads.

Geometry Deep Dive

"Don't memorize πr². Watch it happen."

The classic high-school geometry formulas — perimeters, areas, volumes, Pythagoras, angle sums, circle theorems — each one on a card, and each major one wired into a bench where you can rearrange it, feed it test numbers, and watch the value move through it. Built at Steven Powell's direction, with the social feeds' loudest geometry argument — cheatsheets vs derivations — settled the only honest way: derive it yourself, then keep the cheatsheet.

1 · The formula canon — the cheatsheet, with doors in it

Every formula here is the standard high-school form. Cards with a bench button load the formula into a bench below; the rest are reference — and each is stated exactly as the cheatsheets state it, because the cheatsheets, on these, are right.

2 · Derive-it-yourself — where πr² comes from

The two biggest geometry posts in the social feeds are both this derivation. This bench is those animations with the numbers left in: nothing is smoothed, and the error is printed, not hidden.

3 · Pythagoras lab — a² + b² = c², drawn to scale

Check a triple
Converse tester — any three lengths

Drag the two glowing corners of the triangle, or use the sliders. The converse of the theorem is a theorem too: if a² + b² = c², the angle opposite c is a right angle — and if the sum falls short or overshoots, the numbers say obtuse or acute.

4 · Rearrange lab — move the terms, feed it numbers

Test numbers

In Rearrange, the lab isolates your chosen variable one legal move at a time, then substitutes the answer back into the original formula to prove nothing was lost. In Trace, one set of numbers walks through the formula and every intermediate value is shown in place.

5 · Angles & shapes — sums and the transversal

Name that pair

The polygon sums are computed, not quoted: interior angles add to (n − 2) · 180°, and the exterior angles of any convex polygon walk you exactly once around — 360°, whatever n says. On the transversal, one angle determines all eight; the pairs are the vocabulary the proofs are written in.
