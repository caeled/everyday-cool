# Collection origin

Original Everyday Cool workshop, added 19 November 2026 at Steven Powell's direction. In the Everyday Cool ideas side chat that evening he named the gap himself — "I keep having a nagging feeling that there is a STEM subject we are missing" — and ordered the four candidates saved so all four will be built ("save that list I want to do them all"). Everyday Engineering went first; then, in his words, "Then we can do math." Built by Muse (AI assistant) and added directly to the collection at his direction as the twenty-seventh catalog entry and the eighth workshop inside The STEM Room; this folder is the maintained original, and there is no upstream copy.

The room is built to Steven's own design notes from that evening, kept in the STEM queue file: the tricks bench led by his pick — "the 09 in one column and 9 - 0 in the other" for the nines table — with the other classics (×11, squares ending in 5, divisibility rules, casting out nines) and number theory in plain language; simple algebra and the common formulas, each with a worked example; the nod to Chemistry through Avogadro's number and scientific notation; "a simple javascript scientific calculator"; "just a button that produces a spreadsheet printable with the times to 16/16" and a small log table with interpolation notes, so that "even with numbers to 1000 you can interpolate"; and his standing order for the STEM rooms: "be sure to include the stem wikipedia and other links on the pages." The room ships web-first under the plan Steven set the same evening: the four queue rooms go live on the web one at a time and reach the desktop app together in a single later release — so no app build carries this room yet, by design.

It enters the catalog under "Science" with `hubParent: stem-room`, immediately after Everyday Engineering, so the STEM Room's bench now reads Physics, Chemistry, Electronics, Measure, Engineering, Math, Body, Radio. The room follows the Science block's shape: benches that work right on the page and show their working, a verified shelf of legitimate places, Help ▾ menu with full documentation, banner verified on the live page.

**The Engineering log cross-link closes here, both ways, as recorded there.** Everyday Engineering's COLLECTION.md carried a "recorded, not done" note: its pocket log table's natural sister is this room, and the loop would close in the Math build. It does: this room's log section carries the full table and links to Engineering's pocket bench (`../everyday-engineering/index.html#logs`), and Engineering's log section gained the return link in the same pass (recorded in that room's COLLECTION.md). The Avogadro bench cross-links to Everyday Chemistry, the room where the mole is spent.

Everything interactive is computed in the visitor's browser from rules baked into `math.js` — the page sends nothing anywhere. The calculator is a chain calculator over plain functions in the same file, so `test.cjs` can press its keys without a page. The printable sheet is generated from the same tables the benches read (`timesTable(16)` and `LOG_TABLE_FINE`), so the paper on the wall and the page on the screen can never drift apart. Facts, sources, and the deliberate scope lines (two-digit ×11, the checkers' stated limits, the tables' fourth-decimal wobble, floating point's last-digit lie) are recorded in THIRD-PARTY.md.

The rules live in `math.js` (no DOM), checked by `test.cjs`.

Banner: `everyday-math-banner.jpg`, AI-generated watercolor generated 19 November 2026 as part of adding this room, in the style and dimensions (2736×912) of the collection's other banners: the plush-style robot holding a chalk at a green chalkboard in a warm classroom, drawing the two columns of the nines trick — 0 to 9 counting down one column, 9 to 0 up the other — beside a chalk multiplication grid and a right triangle with its square corner; a wooden abacus with amber beads on the desk. The chalk digits are digits, not words; the image shipped on its first generation.
