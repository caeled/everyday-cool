Radio never went away — it moved into the internet, and it brought everything with it. 02 / THE LISTENING SHELF

Everywhere else radio lives.

The big services can't play inside this page — they need their own accounts and licences — so the shelf opens each one in a new tab, the way the Games Room's shelves do. Every link was checked live on 17 November 2026.

Music, made for you

Services that learn what you like and build the listening around it.

  • Pandora

    Name a song or an artist you love; Pandora builds a whole station around it and learns from your thumbs up and down.

  • iHeartRadio

    Live stations from across the country plus made-for-you mixes, in one account.

  • Spotify Free

    The giant song library with ads on the free tier: albums, playlists, and podcasts.

  • YouTube Music

    Official songs alongside the live versions, covers, and oddities only YouTube has.

Real stations, live online

Actual broadcast stations, streaming what is on the air right now.

  • Audacy

    Hundreds of local stations — music, news, and sports talk. You may know it by its old name: this is the service that used to be Radio.com.

  • iHeart Live Radio

    The live-station directory: pick a city, pick a station, listen to what's on air there now.

  • TuneIn

    A worldwide directory of stations — local radio, news, sports, and music from almost everywhere.

  • myTuner

    Tens of thousands of stations and podcasts, sorted by country and style.

  • Radio Garden

    Spin a globe of the Earth and listen to live radio from wherever you land. Pure Everyday Cool.

Talk, news & public radio

Voices, stories, and the news of the day.

  • NPR

    US public radio: news, stories, and music shows, with live streams from member stations.

  • BBC Sounds

    The BBC's radio home: live stations, drama, comedy, and documentaries from Britain.

  • CBC Listen

    Canada's public radio, live and on demand.

Scanners & live feeds

Public broadcasts, shared by volunteers with radios. These are public broadcasts — anyone with a scanner may listen — and feeds can be delayed; the workshop below explains why.

  • Broadcastify

    The biggest scanner shelf: police, fire, EMS, aviation, and railroad feeds from thousands of places, run by volunteers.

  • OpenMHz

    Public-safety radio systems recorded call by call, so you can scroll back and replay a single transmission.

Real radios you can tune

Not recordings and not stations — actual radios, connected to the internet, that strangers kindly share. Turning the dial here retunes a real receiver, right now, in someone else's town. This is the direct descendant of the ham-radio world Steven's dad has been part of since 1956 — and the same radio sky that Tonight's Sky listens to with its radio telescope.

  • WebSDR

    A directory of shared shortwave receivers all over the world. Pick one, drag across the band, and listen to what's really in the air there.

  • KiwiSDR Public Radios

    Hundreds more shared receivers, with a map to choose by place — broadcasts, hams, and mysterious signals alike.

Old-time & archive

Radio from before television, kept safe — plus the station on this room's dial.

  • Internet Archive — Old-Time Radio

    Thousands of classic broadcasts: mystery, comedy, and adventure shows from radio's golden age, free to stream and download.

  • SomaFM

    The station playing on the wooden set above — dozens of hand-run channels, no commercials, supported by its listeners.

03 / HOW RADIO WORKS HERE

Three kinds of listening.

A station stream

One broadcast, everyone hears the same moment

A real station — or an internet-only one like SomaFM — sends one stream, and every listener hears the same song at the same time, like radio always worked. The wooden set above is a station stream: press Lush anywhere on Earth and you hear what Lush is playing now. There is nothing to skip, because there is nothing stored — it's live.

Live means live for everyone

A personalized service

Pandora & friends

Pandora doesn't broadcast at all. It keeps a giant library of recordings and, the moment you ask, builds a private playlist shaped by what you told it you like. No two listeners hear the same "station," and nothing is live — which is exactly why it can't play inside this page: the library and the learning live behind its own account and licences.

A playlist pretending to be a station — cleverly

A scanner feed

Real radios, shared by volunteers

Police, fire, EMS, pilots, and train crews talk on radios anyone may listen to. A volunteer near you keeps a scanner (or a small software-defined radio) running and sends what it hears to a shelf like Broadcastify. You're hearing the real thing, second-hand, through a stranger's kindness.

Real broadcasts, relayed

Why scanner feeds lag

The delay is normal — sometimes on purpose

Expect a feed to run from half a minute to several minutes behind the real moment: the audio has to travel from the volunteer's radio to the service and back out to you, and some services add a deliberate delay before publishing public-safety audio. So a scanner is for understanding what's happening in your town — never treat it as an emergency service, and never act on it as if it were happening this second.

Interesting, not instant

What is WebSDR?

A real radio, shared

SDR means software-defined radio: a receiver whose dials and filters are software. A WebSDR or KiwiSDR is one of those receivers, in a real place with a real antenna, connected to the internet so visitors can tune it. When you drag the frequency, you are retuning that radio and hearing what its antenna hears — static, faraway stations, hams chatting, numbers stations being strange. Hams have shared their world this way for years; these sites open the door to everyone.

04 / HELP

Help — how The Radio Room works.

The retro player

The wooden set at the top

Press any cream station button. The needle slides to that station's spot on the dial, the dial window shows its name, and the stream starts. Stop halts it. The player is a normal web audio player wearing a wooden cabinet — everything it does, your browser's own controls could do; the cabinet just makes it feel like a radio.

Buttons play, Stop stops

Presets & the tuning knob

Six stations, one dial

The six presets are SomaFM channels, laid out left to right along a make-believe FM dial. The round Tuning knob steps to the next station (and wraps around at the end); the Volume slider is the set's volume knob, flattened so it's easy to grab. Your pick isn't saved anywhere — close the page and the set forgets, like a real radio with the power cut.

Nothing is remembered

The shelf & linking out

Why Pandora opens a new tab

The big services — Pandora, iHeart, Spotify, Audacy and the rest — only play inside their own sites and apps, under their own accounts and music licences. No page is allowed to embed them, so the shelf links out honestly instead of pretending. In the desktop app, those links open in your default browser, like every link that leaves the workshop.

Their licences, their living room

Scanners, briefly

Public, delayed, volunteer-run

Scanner feeds carry public broadcasts — dispatch and service radio that anyone with a receiver may hear. They can run minutes behind real time (see the workshop above), they depend on a volunteer keeping a radio on, and a silent feed usually just means a quiet hour in that town. Listen for understanding, not for emergencies.

Public broadcasts, kindly relayed

Works offline?

Honest answer: no

Radio is the internet's live wire: every stream, station, and feed needs a connection, because the whole point is hearing what's happening now. The Help and workshop text are part of the page, so once it has loaded they stay readable if the connection drops — the music, sadly, stops with it.

Live means connected

About privacy, plainly: this page keeps nothing. Pressing a preset asks SomaFM's public stream server (somafm.com) for that channel's audio — it sees your internet address, the way any website does — and nothing else is sent anywhere by the player. There is no account, no sign-in, and no record of what you listened to; the volume and station you picked live only while the page is open. Every shelf link opens its own service in a new tab; what happens there is that service under its own terms, and most of them do use accounts and recommendations of their own. Scanner feeds carry public broadcasts and can be delayed; they are not emergency services.
