ROOM 4 · THE COIN THAT LIES — THE GAME

The Boring Bet Wins

Three doors. Every price is printed on the door — the game hides nothing, including the answer. You start with 100 coins. Each round, pick a door and pay its stake. The question the game asks is not whether you can read the prices. It is whether, round after round, you will walk through the door the prices point to.

Every door stakes the same 10 coins, so the only difference between them is the deal itself. The number that matters is the expected value (EV): the average profit or loss per play if you played the same door forever. Each door's card shows its EV — and shows the arithmetic, so you can check it by hand.

The back door: expected value worked by hand, variance, the law of large numbers, and gambler's ruin — readable while you play.

THE THREE DOORS

Pick a door, any door

DOOR ONE · THE STEADY DEAL

The Boring Bet

Stake 10. Nine times in ten, the steady deal pays out: +4 profit. One time in ten, the week goes bad and you lose the 10. No headline. No story. Just a small edge, collected over and over.

EV = (9/10)(+4) + (1/10)(−10) = 3.6 − 1.0

+2.6 per play

DOOR TWO · THE HONEST TREADMILL

The Fair Coin

Stake 10. Flip a coin: heads you profit +10, tails you lose the 10. Perfectly fair — which means, on average, perfectly still. Excitement included at no extra charge; progress not included at all.

EV = (1/2)(+10) + (1/2)(−10) = 5 − 5

0 per play

DOOR THREE · THE BIG HEADLINE

The Flashy Game

Stake 10. One chance in twenty, the wheel pays the headline: +140 profit. The other nineteen times, you lose the 10. The prize is real. So is the price — it is printed right here:

EV = (1/20)(+140) + (19/20)(−10) = 7 − 9.5

−2.5 per play

The Play buttons act on whichever game is open below — Free Rounds or The Challenge. If your bankroll drops below 10 coins, no door can take your stake, and the run ends: zero is not a low point, it is a full stop.

THE GAMES

Three ways to meet the doors

Free Rounds — twenty rounds, no target

Play any door, in any order, for 20 rounds. The ledger keeps two bankrolls side by side: the one you actually have, and the one the doors' prices said you should expect. The gap between them is luck — watch how wide it swings, and how it behaves as the rounds pile up.

    The Challenge — finish 30 rounds holding 150 or more

    Same 100 coins, same three doors, 30 rounds. When round 30 ends, be holding 150 coins or more. Run out of stake money first and the run ends on the spot. Before you start, do the arithmetic the doors printed: only one line of play expects to get there. The others can arrive only by luck — and luck, unlike an edge, cannot be ordered twice.

      The Long Run — let the doors play themselves

      Pick one door and the machine plays it 1,000 rounds, 100 separate times, each run starting with 500 coins (a deeper pocket, so the fair line has room to wander). Same doors, same prices — the only thing removed is you, choosing. What is left is the price, collecting.

      Discovery. A good bet can lose and a bad bet can win — once. Expected value is not a prediction about the next round; it is a price, and prices collect in the long run. The boring bet's whole secret is that its small edge is still there on round 900, quietly compounding, while the flashy game's price has been collected 900 times.
