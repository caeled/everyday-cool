# Decisions Everywhere

### An Everyday Cool Workshop Proposal

**Author:** Steven Powell
**Design brief prepared by:** Copilot (31 October 2026)
**Collection:** Everyday Cool
**Status:** Proposed Core Workshop
**Audience:** Curious children, teens, adults, families, homeschoolers, libraries, community centers, lifelong learners

---

# Mission

Most people are taught how to read, write, and calculate.

Very few people are taught how to *decide*.

Every day we make decisions:

- What information should we trust?
- What should we buy?
- What risk should we take?
- Which explanation makes the most sense?
- How do we know if something is likely or unlikely?
- How do we recognize when information is missing?

The purpose of Decisions Everywhere is to make decision-making visible.

Not politics.

Not ideology.

Not persuasion.

The workshop teaches observation, reasoning, evidence, probability, troubleshooting, and consequences.

The goal is not to tell visitors *what* to think.

The goal is to help visitors understand *how* to think.

---

# Core Theme

### SEE → QUESTION → TEST → DECIDE → REVIEW

Everything in the workshop follows this cycle.

Visitors repeatedly discover that:

- Good decisions come from good information.
- Good information comes from careful observation.
- Confidence is not the same thing as certainty.
- Every decision has tradeoffs.
- Mistakes are information.

---

# Visual Style

The workshop should feel different from a textbook.

Imagine:

- A detective notebook
- A field journal
- A troubleshooting bench
- A scientist's notebook

The tone should feel curious and exploratory.

Never preachy.

Never academic.

Never intimidating.

Visitors should feel invited to experiment.

---

# Room 1
## Guessing and Knowing

### Big Question

How do we know what we know?

### Visitors Explore

Statements are sorted into categories:

- Observation
- Fact
- Prediction
- Opinion
- Guess

Examples:

> The grass is wet.

> It probably rained.

> It will rain tomorrow.

> Rainy weather is pleasant.

---

### Discovery

Many arguments happen because people confuse:

- observations
- conclusions
- predictions

with one another.

---

# Room 2
## The Broken Flashlight

### Big Question

How do we find the real cause of a problem?

Visitors investigate several flashlight failures.

Examples:

- Dead batteries
- Backward batteries
- Dirty contacts
- Defective switch
- Broken bulb

Each clue removes possibilities.

---

### Discovery

Troubleshooting is decision-making.

Good troubleshooters do not guess randomly.

They eliminate possibilities.

---

# Room 3
## The Tradeoff Store

### Big Question

Can we always get everything we want?

Visitors compare imaginary products.

Examples:

- Cheap
- Fast
- Reliable

- Powerful
- Portable
- Affordable

---

### Discovery

Most decisions involve tradeoffs.

Choosing one advantage often means giving up another.

---

# Room 4
## The Coin That Lies

### Big Question

Are humans naturally good at probability?

Visitors predict outcomes:

- Coin tosses
- Dice rolls
- Card draws

Then compare expectations with reality.

---

### Discovery

Human intuition often misjudges probability.

Random events often look non-random.

---

# Room 5
## The Missing Puzzle Piece

### Big Question

What information is missing?

Visitors receive incomplete stories.

Example:

> A graph rises dramatically.

What does not appear:

- Timescale
- Sample size
- Starting value

---

### Discovery

The most important information is sometimes absent.

A careful thinker learns to ask:

> What am I not being shown?

---

# Room 6
## The Time Machine

### Big Question

What happens later?

Visitors compare decisions over time.

Examples:

- Save versus spend
- Practice versus procrastinate
- Repair versus replace

---

### Discovery

Many consequences are delayed.

Immediate results and long-term results are often different.

---

# Room 7
## Confidence Meter

### Big Question

How certain should we be?

Visitors examine claims and assign confidence.

Examples:

- Very uncertain
- Some evidence
- Strong evidence
- Extremely likely

---

### Discovery

There is a difference between:

- certainty
- confidence
- evidence

---

# Room 8
## The Big Decision Lab

Visitors receive real-world scenarios:

### Example

Your school wants:

- Faster computers
- Better internet
- More playground equipment

Budget allows only one.

What do you choose?

Why?

What information would help?

---

### Discovery

Good decisions require:

- information
- priorities
- reasoning
- review

---

# Field Missions

Visitors leave the workshop with challenges.

### Mission 1

Observe three advertisements.

What information is missing?

---

### Mission 2

Watch a weather forecast.

Which parts are observations?

Which are predictions?

---

### Mission 3

Find a broken object.

List three possible causes before testing.

---

### Mission 4

Ask a trusted adult:

> What was a decision that looked smart at first but turned out differently later?

---

# Educator Guide

Visitors should finish with these skills:

- Observation
- Verification
- Troubleshooting
- Risk assessment
- Tradeoff analysis
- Probability awareness
- Reflection

No prior technical knowledge required.

Appropriate for:

- Upper elementary
- Middle school
- High school
- Adult learners

The current collection teaches:

- Computers
- Networking
- Weather
- Robotics
- Information systems
- Verification
