The one idea: you don't steer a submarine up and down. You make it heavier or lighter than the water it pushes aside — and the water decides. Flood the tanks and your sub's mass grows past the mass of its displaced water: down you go. Blow them and the water wins: up you come. The dive planes only bite while water flows over them, so they are a nudge, never a lift. Keys if you have them: ↑/↓ blow & flood, Space propeller, A/D planes.

Simplifications, named plainly: the hull displaces a fixed 10 m³ (a real hull compresses slightly as pressure grows, which is one reason real trim is a constant chore); the tanks answer at a game pace, not a real boat's; there are no currents — the only surprise in the water is the density layer's effect on buoyancy itself; and the ocean is a 2D side view. The arithmetic is not simplified: net force really is (displaced water mass − sub mass) × g, with drag opposing motion, and the HUD shows you every number the physics is using.
