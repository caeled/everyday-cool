Carnot — the lesson

Four textbook heat engines — Otto, Diesel, Stirling, and Carnot — run on one 0.500 L swept cylinder, with a piston you can watch and a pressure–volume diagram drawn live beside it. The claim at the centre of the page is the oldest one in thermodynamics: the area enclosed by the loop is the work, W = ∮P dV. This page is the other half of the exhibit: the physics of the four cycles, the exact formulas the code runs, and the audit that checked them across a 21-case parameter grid — formula against energy ledger, ledger against the drawn loop, vertex by vertex against the ideal-gas law.

Verdict: PASS-grade: exact air-standard thermodynamics across a 21-case grid, and the drawn loop is the computation itself (area within 0.71% of ∮P dV) — the bends are cosmetic: a peak-temperature slider silently overridden in two corners and an invented exhaust temperature.

The physics

A cycle is a closed walk, and its area is work

Plot pressure against volume as a piston runs and the state of the gas traces a loop. On the expansion leg the gas pushes the piston out and does work; on the compression leg work is done on the gas. Because pressure is higher on the way out than on the way back, the two do not cancel — the difference is the net work, and geometrically it is exactly the area the loop encloses: W = ∮P dV. That is not a metaphor the page uses for decoration. In this build, as the audit shows below, the polygon the page draws and the work number the page reports are the same quantity, measured two ways, agreeing to within sampling error.

Four ways to walk the loop

The Otto cycle is the air-standard model of the spark-ignition engine: two adiabatic legs (compression and power) joined by two constant-volume legs, heat added in a flash at the top. The Diesel replaces that flash with constant-pressure heat addition — the audit confirmed the distinction is real in the code, not just in the label: at the defaults the Diesel vertex table shows state 2 and state 3 both at 58.0 bar. The Stirling walks isothermally — expanding and compressing at constant temperature — joined by constant-volume legs, and passes its heat from one leg to the other through a regenerator. The Carnot cycle is the limiting case: two isotherms and two adiabats, the most efficient loop any engine operating between the same two temperatures can draw, and the yardstick the other three are scored against.

The Carnot bound is a ceiling, not a target

No heat engine working between a hot reservoir at TH and a cold one at TC can beat η = 1 − TC/TH. The exhibit evaluates that bound at each cycle's true extreme temperatures — for Otto and Diesel, 1 − T1/T3 — and reports an “η vs Carnot” ratio beside every efficiency. Across the whole 21-case grid, no setting lets a cycle exceed its displayed bound. The ratio is honest, which matters: a page that grades itself against a ceiling it computes loosely could flatter itself, and this one does not.

Why Stirling meets the ceiling

With ideal regeneration — the heat shed on one constant-volume leg returned in full on the other — the Stirling cycle's efficiency is the Carnot efficiency, exactly. The exhibit claims that, and the claim holds in its own numbers: at the defaults the live readouts show η 68.4% = Carnot 68.4%, a ratio of 100%. The regenerated heat is not hidden, either: Qreg = Cv(TH − TC) = 669 J at the defaults, shown on the page as “Recycled.”

The math the game runs

The model runs throughout with R = 8.314462618 J/(mol·K), intake pressure P1 = 1.01325 bar, and the fixed 0.500 L swept volume.

Otto

ηOtto = 1 − r1−γ r is the compression ratio and γ the heat-capacity ratio. At the defaults, r = 10 and γ = 1.40, this gives η = 60.19% — the live readout shows 60.2%. Across the grid the formula, the energy ledger η = Wnet/Qin, and the equation panel agree to all digits. Try the helium preset: γ = 1.67 at r = 10 gives η = 78.6% = 1 − 10−0.67, live.

Diesel

ηDiesel = 1 − r1−γ · (rcγ − 1) / [γ(rc − 1)],   rc = TH/T2 rc is the cutoff ratio — how far into the power stroke the constant-pressure burning continues. At the defaults, r = 18 gives rc = 2.41 and η = 61.31%, live 61.3%.

Stirling and Carnot

ηStirling = ηCarnot = 1 − TC/TH  ;  Qin = TH·ΔS,   Qout = TC·ΔS For the Carnot cycle the T–S loop is the claimed rectangle, and the bookkeeping is exact at the defaults: ΔS = 0.154 J/K gives Qin 117 J, Qout 46 J, and W 71 J. The Stirling charge is set at 1.5 bar at (Vmax, TC); its vertex pressures match the ideal-gas law — state 1 at 12.5 bar, state 2 at 4.8 bar.

Power and mean effective pressure

Power = Wnet × (RPM/60) / revs-per-cycle  ;  MEP = Wnet/Vswept Both exact at the Otto defaults: 408 J × 10 /s = 4.08 kW live, and MEP 8.17 bar live. Mean effective pressure is the constant pressure that would do the same work over the same swept volume — the fair way to compare engines of different sizes, and here it is computed from the model's own work, not fitted.

Receipt 3: every vertex is a real state

Every vertex of every cycle satisfies PV = nRT to a relative error of at most 4.3×10−16 — machine precision. The live animation keeps the same faith mid-stroke: Otto compression at 35% phase reads 1.86 bar / 357 K against 1.86 bar / 357 K computed from the crank position, and the power stroke at 63% phase reads 1086 K against 1091 K computed (readout rounding). Power and MEP, above, are likewise exact.

Simplifications and divergences, named

The model itself is the air-standard idealization, and it owns that: these are the textbook cycles — adiabatic legs perfectly adiabatic, heat added on the Otto's constant-volume leg and the Diesel's constant-pressure leg, ideal regeneration in the Stirling. Within that model nothing is fudged; the divergences below are where the display departs from the model, not where the model departs from the textbook.

The “Peak Combustion TH” slider is silently overridden in two corners. Otto computes T3 = max(T2 + 350, TH): at r = 14 with the slider at 1200 K the label still reads “1200 K” while the actual Peak Temp is 1212 K; with γ = 1.67 and TH = 1200 K the actual peak is 1753 K. Diesel clamps the cutoff ratio to rc ≥ 1.25: with γ = 1.67 (T2 = 2080 K) and TH = 2300 K, the unclamped rc would be 1.11, so the page runs T3 = 2601 K against a slider reading 2300 K. The model stays internally consistent — the Carnot bound is computed from the true peak, and the page's own Peak Temp readout reports the true value, which is why these are bends rather than falsehoods — but in those corners the control does not do what it says.

The exhaust-sweep temperature is invented. During the exhaust sweep the live T readout shows a “warm residual” decay — measured 432 K at 90% phase and 407 K at 99% — where the modelled state after blowdown is T1 = 300 K, and the intake stroke correctly reads 300 K. Display only: the loop, the ledger, and the vertices are unaffected.

Stale first-paint placeholders. The static HTML carries Peak Pressure 62.4 bar, Work 412 J, Qin 684 J, Qout 272 J, MEP 8.24 bar; the true boot values are 74.3 bar, 408 J, 679 J, 270 J, 8.17 bar. The model replaces them on the first frame — they are visible only if the scripts fail.

One slider corner leaves physics behind. With isothermal ratio 4.2, TC = 240 K, and TH = 1400 K — all within the Carnot sliders' ranges — the fixed 0.5 L swept volume forces a total volume ratio of ~345:1 and Pmax = 2040 bar. The math stays self-consistent in a physically absurd regime, and nothing on the page warns.

Small print: the blurb says diesels compress “16:1–22:1”; the sim's own compression slider runs 14:1–22:1.
