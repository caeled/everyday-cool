Chapter 01 · The Fabric in Motion

Gravitational Waves: Not Waves IN Space, But Waves OF Space

Light waves are oscillations of electric and magnetic fields traveling through spacetime. Gravitational waves (`h_+`, `h_×`) are something even more astonishing: rhythmic ripples of spacetime curvature itself propagating outward at the exact speed of light c!

Because gravity is a spin-2 tensor field, a passing gravitational wave does not push objects forward — it stretches space along one transverse axis while simultaneously squeezing it along the perpendicular axis (quadrupole strain h = ΔL / L). Watch the LIGO test-mass ring in the oscilloscope panel below the simulation deform as crests and troughs sweep across it! (The bench's field is a scalar stand-in for the true tensor wave; the spin-2 character lives in how the ring answers it.)

Chapter 02 · Gravity Acting on Gravity

Why Gravitational Waves Bend Around Centers of Mass (Shapiro Delay)

In the regime this bench simulates, a gravitational wave is a linear ripple travelling on the curved spacetime a mass has already made — it does not need gravity acting on gravity to bend. When a gravitational wave passes near a star, black hole, or galaxy cluster of mass M, it travels along curved null geodesics where coordinate clocks tick more slowly (Gravitational Time Dilation / Shapiro Delay). The delay and the bending are siblings — both are the same metric potential at work — not cause and effect.

Effective Coordinate Speed of a Wave Passing Mass M (Schwarzschild r_s = 2GM/c²): c_eff(r) ≈ c · ( 1 − r_s / r ) ⟺ Effective Refractive Index: n_eff(r) ≈ 1 + 2GM / (r c²) Just like a glass lens slows the middle of a light wavefront and bends it inward, a gravity well slows the portion of a gravitational wavefront passing closest to the mass — steering the entire wavefront around the center of mass! (The printed c_eff is the radial coordinate speed; the tangential one is c·√(1 − r_s/r). The delay itself accumulates as Δt ≈ (2GM/c³)·ln(4r₁r₂/b²) — for a signal grazing the Sun on an Earth–Mars-scale path, ≈119.5 µs one way.)
Chapter 03 · Wave-Optics Diffraction

When Wavelength Matches the Black Hole (`λ ~ r_s`): The Gravitational Arago Spot!

When starlight passes a galaxy, its wavelength (~500 nm) is trillions of times smaller than the galaxy — so light behaves like crisp geometric rays, forming distinct lensed arcs and Einstein rings.

Gravitational waves are enormous: a binary black hole merger detected by LIGO radiates waves thousands to ten-thousand kilometers long (`f ~ 30–300 Hz` — λ = c/f runs from 9,993 km at 30 Hz down to 999 km at 300 Hz), and LISA's supermassive mergers radiate waves hundreds of millions of kilometers long at millihertz frequencies (≈3×10⁸ km at 1 mHz) — right on the same scale as a black hole's Schwarzschild radius r_s!

In this Wave-Optics Regime (`λ ~ r_s`), gravitational waves diffract around the black hole like ocean swells wrapping around an island! In three dimensions, the ripples traveling around the top and bottom meet constructively dead-center behind the black hole, creating a bright **Gravitational Arago–Poisson Focus** and glowing interference fringes. This bench is a 2D slice of that world, and the slice is honest about its limits: measured on its own ⟨h²⟩ view, the axis behind the mass stays dark at λ ≈ r_s, and an extended axial caustic brightens only at longer wavelengths (λ ≳ 1.7 r_s). The sharp spot belongs to the 3D geometry; the 2D bench shows the diffraction that creates it, at the wavelengths a slice can.

Chapter 04 · Spinning Spacetime

Kerr Black Holes, Frame-Dragging & The River Model of Spacetime

Real astrophysical black holes spin — many of them rapidly, some near the Kerr limit, though measured spins span the range with wide uncertainties (the spinning case is described by Roy Kerr's exact 1963 solution, with dimensionless spin a/M ∈ [−1, +1]). A spinning black hole doesn't just curve space inward — it twists spacetime itself into a relentless relativistic whirlpool called Lense–Thirring Frame-Dragging!

The River Model of Black Holes (Gullstrand–Painlevé / Doran Coordinates): Space flows radially inward like a river toward a waterfall at speed: v_radial(r) = −c · √( r_s / r ) (Hits v = c at the Event Horizon r = r_s!) Plus a spinning Kerr black hole drags that river into an azimuthal vortex: v_swirl(r) ∝ (a / M) · (r_s / r)² (the bench's schematic scaling — true frame-dragging is an angular velocity, Ω ≈ 2aM/r³ far away in geometric units, and a swirl speed also needs the polar angle) • Prograde side (wave moving WITH the swirl): whipped smoothly around closer to the horizon! • Retrograde side (wave fighting the swirl): braked and deflected outward at a wider radius! Result: A dramatic ASYMMETRIC SPIRAL WAKE and shifted lensing caustic!
Chapter 05 · Capture & Cosmic Bell Ringing

The Photon Sphere (`1.5 r_s`), Capture Cross-Section & Quasinormal Ringdown

Unstable Orbit at `r = 1.5 r_s`

At 1.5 times the Schwarzschild radius lies the Photon Sphere — where both light and gravitational waves can loop in tight unstable circular orbits! Any wave arriving with impact parameter smaller than b_crit = (3√3 / 2)·r_s ≈ 2.60 r_s cannot escape: it spirals inward across the Event Horizon and is swallowed permanently! (Exact for a ray, in geometric optics. A wave packet in the λ ~ r_s regime of Chapter 3 partially diffracts and scatters instead — watch this bench's own downstream field.)

Black Hole Ringing (Quasinormal Modes)

Wavepackets that graze right along the unstable photon sphere orbit multiple times, continuously leaking energy both inward (into the horizon) and outward (to distant observers) as exponentially damped ripples — the exact Quasinormal Mode "Ringdown" that lets LIGO measure a black hole's mass and spin! Reference numbers (the unitless bench doesn't compute these): the fundamental mode is Mω ≈ 0.3737 − 0.0890i, so a 60 M☉ remnant rings at ≈201 Hz with a damping time of ≈3.3 ms — and the capture cross-section the photon sphere implies is σ = 27π(GM/c²)² ≈ 84.8 (GM/c²)².

Chapter 06 · Observational Frontier

Lensed Gravitational-Wave Echoes in LIGO, Virgo & LISA

Because gravitational waves pass right through dust, stars, and entire galaxies almost without being absorbed — the interaction is extraordinarily weak, though the waves are still lensed and scattered, as this chapter itself shows — a distant binary merger lensed by an intervening galaxy produces multiple echoes arriving days to months apart (a galaxy cluster stretches the delays to years; minutes-scale delays belong to ~10⁵–10⁶ M☉ lenses), and the two pictures must not be merged: separated echoes are the geometric-optics regime — and in the wave-optics regime (`M ~ 100–10,000 M_☉` intermediate-mass black holes), the overlapping lensed paths imprint unmistakable frequency-dependent beating patterns directly onto the chirp waveform! Hunting for these lensed GW diffraction signatures is one of the most exciting searches in modern gravitational-wave astronomy.
