Everyday Physics · The back door to Magnetosphere

Magnetosphere — the lesson

Four rooms, one engine: a magnetic bottle built from two current coils, Earth's dipole with its radiation belts and aurora, a tokamak, and a reconnecting current sheet — all of them stepped by a single Boris pusher through a single field function, with up to 860 protons and electrons live at once. The exhibit's own physics note claims a "symplectic, energy-conserving Boris Lorentz pusher… preserving Larmor gyration, grad-B/curvature drift, and adiabatic mirror invariance μ = mv⊥²/2B." This lesson is the audit of that sentence, run against the shipped code.

The short version: the pusher is genuine and excellent, the mirror physics is honest to half a degree, and the belts' drifts match theory's ratio to two decimals. The bends live where they usually live — in unit conversions, in readouts that restate the sliders, in an aurora that is partly painted on, in a reconnection that is staged rather than computed, and in the one room whose field is a thousand times stronger than the other three, where the gyration becomes an alias while everything slower than the gyration survives.

Magnetosphere was built by Google Antigravity (Gemini) at Steven Powell's direction; this lesson and its audit were written by Muse. The exhibit itself ships exactly as built — the audit runs its code, it does not repair it.

How to play

The exhibit opens on the Mirror Bottle: two glowing coils and a few hundred protons (orange-red) and electrons (cyan) corkscrewing between them. Everything else is a control away.

  • Four rooms, one engine — the preset buttons (or keys 1–4) switch between Mirror Bottle, Van Allen Belts, Tokamak Reactor and Reconnection Flare. Each room re-labels the same sliders: the "Coil pinch" of the bottle becomes "Dipole tilt" around Earth, "Safety factor" in the tokamak, "Sheet thinness" in the flare.
  • Sliders — Field strength scales the whole field. Temperature rescales every particle's velocity live (it also moves the speed ceiling). Injection pitch sets the centre of the spawn pitch distribution — the code adds a Gaussian scatter of σ ≈ 12.6° and clamps it, so a "trapped" setting always leaks a few particles into the loss cone. Particle count runs 120–860. Solar wind / drive feeds the convection field, the MHD perturbation, or the reconnection drive, depending on the room.
  • Buttons — Inject plasma adds a burst of 34 particles at the slider pitch. The event button changes its name per room: Loss-Cone Beam fires 38 particles straight down the bottle's throat, Solar Storm Gust and Trigger Flare slam their room's boost to 1.85 for a few seconds (the flare also spawns 44 particles already moving at jet speed — remember that when you read the audit), the tokamak's ELM Burst does the same to its perturbation. Re-seed plasma rebuilds the room.
  • Drag tools — pick Magnet to plant your own bar magnet (its field joins the same field function, and the particles really do respond to it), Dipole for a second Earth-style dipole, Coil for a movable current loop, or Orbit to just move the camera. Click a placed object to select it; Delete removes it.
  • Telemetry (left panel) — trapped percentage, mirror ratio and loss-cone angle in the bottle, peak field, mean Larmor radius, a live counter of mirror bounces and escapes, and a per-room status line. The audit below checks which of these numbers are measured and which are theatre.
  • Views — toggles for field lines, the |B| heatmap, the aurora, a top-down camera, auto-rotate, a Cinema mode that hides the panels (H), and speeds from 0.25× to 2×. Space pauses, R reseeds.

Three first experiments. (1) In the bottle, set the injection pitch to 90° and watch the escapes counter stay near zero; then walk the pitch down toward 25° and find the angle where particles start pouring out of the ends — that cliff is the loss cone, and the audit found it within half a degree of the textbook value. (2) In the belts, turn the solar wind up and watch green light pool at the poles; then ask, while you read the honest box, how much of that glow any particle earned. (3) In the tokamak, lower the safety-factor slider and compare the HUD's q against how many times a traced field line actually wraps — the two disagree, and the audit measured by how much.

The physics

A charged particle in a magnetic field goes in circles

The Lorentz force, F = q(E + v × B), is perpendicular to the velocity whenever only the magnetic part acts — so a magnetic field can bend a particle's path but never speed it up or slow it down. The perpendicular motion is a circle: the gyration, with Larmor radius r_L = m·v⊥ / (|q|·B) and gyro-frequency Ω = |q|·B / m. The parallel motion is free. Circle plus drift-along-the-line equals the corkscrew every particle in this exhibit draws. Two consequences matter everywhere below: electrons, being light, gyrate far faster and tighter than protons in the same field (in nature, 1,836 times faster at the same temperature); and any honest simulation has to resolve that circle — take steps small compared with the gyro-period — or the circle it draws is not the circle physics prescribes.

The magnetic mirror, and the cone that leaks

Make the field stronger at the ends of a tube than in the middle — two coils do it — and a spiralling particle feels a force pushing it back toward the weak field. The quantity that governs this is the magnetic moment μ = m·v⊥² / (2B): as long as the field changes slowly over one gyration, μ stays constant, so as B rises the perpendicular energy must rise with it — and since the total energy is fixed (a static magnetic field does no work), the parallel energy falls. If B rises enough, the parallel velocity reaches zero and the particle reflects. That is the magnetic mirror. Whether a particle reflects depends only on its pitch angle θ — the angle between its velocity and the field — and the mirror ratio R_m = B_max / B_min: it reflects if sin²θ > 1/R_m. Particles inside the loss cone, θ < θ_c = asin(1/√R_m), have too much of their motion along the field; they reach the end before the field can turn them, and they leave. No mirror bottle traps everything — the loss cone is the price of open ends, and it is why the real mirror-fusion programme struggled and why Earth's poles leak aurora.

Drifts: the slow walk across the field

Average over the gyration and the guiding centre still moves. Where the field strength varies, the Larmor circle is tighter on the strong side than the weak side, and the orbit fails to close — the grad-B drift, sideways, in opposite directions for opposite charges, at a rate proportional to m·v⊥² / q. Field-line curvature adds a cousin, the curvature drift, with the same charge dependence. Around Earth these drifts are what sort the Van Allen belts: protons drift one way around the planet, electrons the other, forming two counter-rotating current rings. An electric field adds the E×B drift, v = E×B / B² — the same for both charges, and the engine that convects plasma through the magnetosphere and into a reconnection site. All of these are gyro-averaged effects: they exist only because the gyration underneath them exists. A simulation that fails to resolve the gyration has no right to the drifts — which makes them a sharp test, and the audit uses them as one.

Earth's bottle, the belts, and the aurora

Earth's field is, to first order, a dipole — the field of a bar magnet at the planet's centre, tilted about 11° from the spin axis. Field lines leave one polar region, loop out to several Earth radii at the equator, and re-enter at the other pole: a natural magnetic bottle, and particles trapped in it form the Van Allen radiation belts, bouncing pole to pole while drifting around the planet. The mirror at each end is imperfect exactly as the loss cone says: particles whose equatorial pitch angle is too small reach the atmosphere instead of reflecting, and when they strike air molecules high over the poles, the molecules glow — the aurora, organised into ovals around the magnetic poles at roughly 65–70° magnetic latitude. A solar storm that enhances the convection and dumps more particles into the loss cone brightens and expands the ovals. That chain — dipole, trapping, loss cone, precipitation, glow — is the honest skeleton of the exhibit's second room.

The tokamak: a bottle bent into a ring

Close the tube into a torus and the ends — and the loss cone — go away, but a purely toroidal field drifts particles vertically off the surfaces. The fix is to add a poloidal field, from a current flowing in the plasma itself, so field lines wind helically around the torus, each one tracing a nested flux surface. The winding is counted by the safety factor q: the number of toroidal turns a field line makes per poloidal turn. Too low a q and the configuration is unstable to kinks — real tokamaks run q above ~2–3 at the edge for a reason. The toroidal field itself falls off as B ∝ 1/R across the bore, which is also what makes the inner side of the torus a stronger mirror than the outer side, trapping some particles in "banana" orbits.

Reconnection: when field lines change their minds

Bring two oppositely directed fields together and they press into a current sheet — the Harris sheet, whose field reverses across a thin layer as B_x = B_0·tanh(y/L). Plasma drifts in from both sides (E×B inflow), and at the X-point, where the field passes through zero, the frozen-in rule that ties plasma to field lines breaks: lines cut and rejoin in a new topology, stored magnetic energy converts explosively into two jets of plasma fired along the sheet, and a reconnection electric field along the X-line does the accelerating. It powers solar flares and magnetospheric substorms — and it is intrinsically a time-dependent change of field topology, a fact to hold onto when the audit reaches the fourth room.

The math the game actually runs

The exhibit's own physics note (Visuals tab) says: "symplectic, energy-conserving Boris Lorentz pusher (F = q(E + v × B)), preserving Larmor gyration, grad-B/curvature drift, and adiabatic mirror invariance μ = mv⊥²/2B." That is a strong claim, and most of this lesson is the audit of it. Here is what the code does, exactly.

The pusher

It is the textbook Boris push — the same algorithm the collection's Cyclotron runs. Each substep: a half electric kick, then the magnetic rotation built from t = ½·(q/m)·B·dt and s = 2t/(1+t²) applied as two cross products, then the second half electric kick, then the position update. The magnetic part is a pure rotation of the velocity vector, which is why — as in the Cyclotron audit — the speed in a static field is conserved to roundoff rather than merely approximately. One frame is at most 0.04 s of simulation time (times the speed setting), split into 4 substeps.

The couplings

Fields come from one function, evaluateFields, shared by the particles, the field-line tracer, the heatmap and the telemetry — the same single-source-of-truth pattern as the Event Horizon exhibit. The Boris pusher consumes them with two scale factors: the magnetic term uses (q/m) × 8.4, while the electric kick uses (q/m) × 8.4 × 12. Hold onto that extra 12; receipt 2 is about it. Species: protons q = +1, m = 1; electrons q = −1, m = 0.28 — a comment in the code says the electron mass is raised "for numerical stiffness," which makes the coded charge-to-mass ratio 3.57 instead of nature's 1,836. Spawn speeds are 42 units/s for protons, 66 for electrons, times the temperature slider; a hard ceiling rescales any velocity that exceeds 230 × temperature.

The four fields

  • Mirror bottle — two circular coils at x = ±128, radius 64, each drawn as a 14-segment polygon and summed with the real Biot–Savart law per segment, with a softening of 14² added inside the distance squared. Coil strength is B₀ × (12,000 + 68,000 × pinch); a weak uniform guide field B₀ × (0.58 − 0.44 × pinch) runs along the axis. Nothing here is a look-up or a fake gradient — the mirror well is the coils' actual field.
  • Magnetosphere — an analytic dipole, moment B₀ × (0.7 + 0.65 × pinch) × 1.45×10⁶ pointing mostly along −Y with a tilt of 0.20 + (pinch − 0.5) × 0.34 radians, softened at a radius of 22 (Earth's radius in the scene is 46, so the softening reaches halfway to the surface). Added on top: an interplanetary-field offset that grows with the wind, a tail-stretching term, and a convection electric field E_z = 0.045 × wind.
  • Tokamak — toroidal field with the true shape, B_φ = 1.35 × B₀ × R₀/ρ (R₀ = 124, minor radius 46), plus a poloidal field whose strength is set by qInv = 0.22 + 0.72 × pinch and tapered so it vanishes at the magnetic axis, plus — when the drive is on — an m = 2, n = 1 MHD perturbation and its electric field.
  • Reconnection — an analytic, static Harris sheet: B_x = 1.38 × B₀ × tanh(y/L) with sheet half-thickness L = 54 − 26 × pinch, a B_y component shaped to open an X-point, a constant guide field B_z = 0.22 × B₀, and imposed electric fields: a reconnection field E_z proportional to a drive factor 0.4 + 0.95 × wind + 1.8 × boost, and an "E_x Hall assist" shaped like the outflow jets. Static is the load-bearing word; see receipt 6.

The telemetry

The bottle's mirror ratio is computed honestly from the field itself — |B| on-axis at the coil plane divided by |B| at the centre — and the loss-cone angle from asin(1/√R_m). Other readouts are conversions and formulas: peak field multiplies the code's |B| by a per-room display scale (×3.6 "T" in the bottle, ×48 then ×0.35 "μT" at Earth); the mean Larmor radius averages only particles whose last-computed radius falls in the window (0.1, 120); the Kp index is 1 + 0.85 × auroraEnergy + 1.4 × wind + 2.4 × boost; the solar-wind speed prints 320 + 360 × wind + 280 × boost "km/s"; the flare room's output prints a megawatt figure assembled from the sliders. Which of these survive contact with measurement is the audit.

The audit — what the receipts show

Method, same as the rest of the room: the game's own functions were extracted verbatim from the shipped file and run headlessly in Node against instrumented particles — no reimplementation of anything under test. (A fidelity check first: the extracted pusher and an independent implementation of the same algorithm agreed to zero deviation over 200 frames, so every number below is the game's arithmetic, not a port of it.)

Receipt 1 — the pusher is real Boris, and it behaves like it

One particle, uniform B = 2, no electric field, 60 fps frames — the Cyclotron test, rerun on this exhibit:

CaseGyro-period errorLarmor radius errorSpeed drift

Electron, B = 6+4.5%+6.8%exact

The errors are not noise; they are Boris's known fingerprint. The pusher rotates the velocity through 2·atan(Ω·dt/2) instead of Ω·dt, so the gyration always runs a fraction slow, by an amount the formula predicts: at B = 6 the prediction is +4.5% on the period and the measurement is +4.5%; at 2× speed the prediction is +2.0% and the measurement +2.1%. Where the step is small compared with the gyro-period, this exhibit's gyration is as honest as the Cyclotron's. Where it isn't — receipt 7 — is a property of the fields, not the pusher. One wording correction to the in-page note: Boris is volume-preserving, not strictly symplectic, and "energy-conserving" holds exactly only for the magnetic rotation; the electric kicks add and remove energy by design, as they should.

Receipt 2 — the electric field is coupled twelve times stronger than the magnetic

Put a proton, then an electron, in a uniform field with both E and B, and measure the guiding-centre drift. Both species drifted at exactly 1.500 units/s — identical, as E×B drift must be, and exactly the value predicted from the code's own couplings. But compute E×B from the field values the page displays, and you get 0.125. The drift runs 12× fast, because the electric kick carries that extra factor of 12 relative to the magnetic term. Every E-driven phenomenon in the exhibit — magnetospheric convection, the reconnection inflow and acceleration, the tokamak's MHD electric field — is twelve times stronger, relative to the magnetism, than the fields on the tin say. In the reconnection room the effective electric field actually exceeds the magnetic field in the inflow region, which is a regime where E×B drift ordering doesn't merely bend, it stops applying: particles there are directly accelerated by the imposed E, and the visible inflow is mostly the speed particles are spawned with (34 units/s, against a drift of −4.2 with the code's coupling, or −0.35 with the naive one).

Receipt 3 — the mirror keeps its promises, to half a degree

At the bottle's defaults (B₀ = 1.15, pinch = 0.74), the game's diagnostics report Rm = 5.37 and θc = 25.6°. An independent scan of the shipped field along the axis gives B = 1,241 at the centre and 6,660 at the coil plane — ratio 5.366, the diagnostics exactly. Then the experiment the room exists for: protons fired from the centre at speed 42, pitch angle by pitch angle, two gyro-phases each, watched for 120 s. At 25° every particle escaped; at 26° and above, none did. The measured trapping boundary sits between 25° and 26°; theory says 25.57°. The loss cone in the shipped bottle is where the textbook puts it, to within half a degree. A trapped proton at 64° pitch was followed to its reflection points: it turned around where B = 1,483, against the mirror condition's prediction B_start/sin²θ = 1,483 — agreement to 0.03%. And μ, sampled at eight centre crossings, held a spread of 0.02% for a proton and 0.14% for an electron, with the speed band at 0.000% throughout. The adiabatic invariant the physics note advertises is, in the slow dynamics, genuinely there.

Receipt 4 — in the belts, the drifts are real, opposite, and quantitatively right

Around the exhibit's Earth, at radius 160 where the dipole's field is 0.49, single particles were placed with velocity purely perpendicular to B and their guiding centres tracked for 90 s. The proton drifted azimuthally at +1.58°/s; the electron at −1.09°/s — opposite directions, as grad-B/curvature drift demands. Better: the ratio of the two rates, 1.45, matches the theoretical scaling m·v⊥²/|q| for the two coded species — (1 × 42²) / (0.28 × 66²) = 1.45 — to two decimal places. The drift claim in the physics note is true in this room, and not just qualitatively. In the bottle the same measurement gives −0.000°/s (proton) and +0.001°/s (electron) over 300 s — but there the physical drift rate is itself only of order a degree per 300 s, because the drift shrinks as 1/B and the bottle's B is enormous; the audit reports the bottle's drift as not there to be seen, and convicts nothing.

Receipt 6 — reconnection is staged, not computed

The field in the flare room was sampled at a fixed point at simulation time 0 and again at 37.5 s: bit-identical. The field never evolves; the X-point and its B_y structure exist from the first frame; no flux is transferred, no topology changes in time. What moves is particles, under a scripted electric field — and on that narrower claim the code is exact: a proton placed at rest at the X-point (where, notably, B is purely the guide field and E is parallel to it) reached v_z = −62.6 after one second, precisely the coded coupling × the imposed E_z. A proton at rest near the sheet was genuinely thrown into a jet by the Lorentz force, reaching |v| = 76 with v_x = +57 in 2.5 s. The "Trigger Flare" button raises the boost — E_z at the centre goes from −0.62 to −2.15 — and simultaneously spawns 44 particles already travelling at 95–160 units/s along the jet direction: the explosion's most photogenic particles never needed accelerating. And in the flare run, the fastest particle observed hit 218.5 — exactly the speed ceiling of 230 × temperature (at temp 0.95) — so the one room advertising explosive acceleration is the one room where the non-physical velocity cap demonstrably bites, quietly rescaling velocities the pusher would otherwise have grown.

Three rooms live at |B| ≈ 0.5–1.5 and their gyration is fully resolved — that's why receipts 1 and 4 pass there. The bottle lives at |B| ≈ 1,241–6,660, a thousand times stronger. Each substep there rotates a proton's perpendicular velocity by ~175°, an electron's by ~179°: the gyro phase is aliased to noise, and the true Larmor radius — about 0.002–0.005 scene units, a fraction of a pixel — is nowhere in the picture. The corkscrews you see in the bottle are the guiding-centre motion wearing alias jitter, not Larmor helices. Yet receipt 3 stands: |v| is preserved exactly (a rotation is a rotation, however coarse), the bounce period is ~22 s against a 1/240 s substep, and the mirror condition, the loss cone and μ all come out right, because they are properties of the slow dynamics. The precise verdict on the physics note is therefore split: preserving Larmor gyration — true in three rooms, an alias in the fourth; adiabatic mirror invariance — measured true where it was claimed, in the room where it matters most.

How it's built

One self-contained HTML file (130 KB; no libraries, no network calls, no storage — the audit verified the file contains no external URL at all). A single field function, evaluateFields, is the source of truth for everything: the Boris pusher steps particles through it, an RK2 midpoint tracer draws field lines from it, the heatmap samples it on a grid in the camera plane, and the telemetry reads the mirror ratio off it. The bottle's coils are genuine Biot–Savart segment sums — on the axis, the 14-segment polygon reproduces a 720-segment loop to four significant figures, so the polygon is not the approximation; the softening is (honest box). Particles are never destroyed: one that escapes a room's boundary, falls into Earth, or crosses the flare room's edge is recycled into a fresh spawn, which is why the population holds steady for hours — and why "escaped" in the bottle means "left and was replaced," a modelling choice the telemetry is upfront about in its counter. The aurora is a pair of 72 × 18 latitude–longitude bin arrays filled by precipitation deposits and drawn as additive curtains at 67.6° latitude; a seeded value-noise field animates the shimmer. The ambient hum and the aurora chime are WebAudio oscillators driven by the same state that drives the pictures. Custom magnets, dipoles and coils that you drag into the scene are added into the same field function, so the particles, the tracer and the heatmap all agree about your magnet too — the same discipline the Event Horizon exhibit keeps, and the reason most of this audit could be run against the exhibit's own arithmetic instead of a reconstruction of it.

The honest box — what this exhibit bends

The gyration in the bottle is an alias. Receipt 7 is the big one: at the bottle's field strengths each Boris substep rotates the velocity by ~175–179°, the true Larmor radius is ~0.002–0.005 units, and the visible corkscrews are guiding-centre motion plus aliasing. The slow physics — bounce, mirror condition, loss cone, μ — is measured honest; the fast physics in that one room is a drawing of itself.

The electric coupling is 12× the magnetic. E and B are consumed with different scale factors, so every E×B drift and E-acceleration runs twelve times stronger than the displayed fields imply (measured exactly: 1.500 vs 0.125 units/s). In the reconnection room the effective E exceeds B outright, and the flows are spawn velocity plus direct acceleration, not drift physics.

The electron weighs 0.28 protons. Disclosed in a code comment ("for numerical stiffness"), nowhere on the page. The coded electron-to-proton gyro-frequency ratio is 3.57; nature's is 1,836. Electron gyration everywhere in the exhibit is ~500× too slow relative to the protons, and at equal speed its Larmor radius is 0.28× a proton's instead of 1/1,836.

Two peak-field readouts are mis-calibrated. The bottle's display scale (×3.6) was evidently set for a field a thousand times weaker than the coils produce: at defaults the HUD computes a peak field of 23,975 T — about a million times a tokamak's toroidal field, in a room whose placeholder text says 4.20 T. At Earth the chain ×48 then ×0.35 lands on 312 μT, five times Earth's real polar surface field of ≈60 μT. The underlying code fields are self-consistent; only the unit conversions are fiction.

The dipole is softened halfway to the surface. The 22-unit softening radius (Earth's radius is 46) flattens the near field: the audit measured the pole-to-equator surface field ratio at 1.40, against the dipole's exact 2.00. The belts, at r = 160 where the softening costs ~2%, are unaffected.

The mean Larmor radius in the bottle never updates. The telemetry averages only particles whose radius falls in (0.1, 120); the trapped population's radii are ~0.002–0.005, below the gate. Over 30 s × 420 particles — 756,000 particle-frames — exactly 3 ever entered the window, and the readout sits at its initial placeholder, displaying "1.01 cm" regardless of the sliders. In the magnetosphere the same code works properly (7.97 units → 143 km, consistent with the formula value).

The bounce counter never counts. A bounce registers only if the parallel velocity flips sign across a frame in which its previous magnitude exceeded 6 and the local B exceeds a threshold — but a smooth mirror reflection changes v∥ by only ~0.1–0.7 per frame, so the gate essentially never opens: 17 true reflections in the bottle and 8 in the belts were counted by the audit; the game's counter said 0 both times. The mirror-bounce flash on the HUD therefore never fires for a bounce.

The aurora is partly painted. Three separate bends: the precipitation gate accepts any particle reaching r < 1.14 RE with |latitude| > 41.3° — a polar cap more than twice the angular radius of the oval actually drawn at 67.6° — and it tests latitude against the untilted axis while the dipole and the oval are tilted; with the wind slider above 0.15 the aurora bins are held at a baseline wave pattern whether or not anything precipitates; and the storm button adds ~0.65 or more directly to every bin and +3.2 to the aurora energy, without a single particle being involved. The glow that particles earn is real; it shares the sky with glow that was simply applied.

Several readouts are formulas in the sliders, not measurements. The Kp index, the solar-wind "km/s," the flare room's "MW output," the tokamak's "Fusion Q-Index" (a confinement score: trapped% − 16 × wind − 22 × boost — not fusion gain), and the tokamak's q (receipt 5) all restate the controls. The mirror ratio and loss-cone angle, by contrast, are computed from the field and are exact.

The coil softening is a real, if modest, field error. Adding 14² inside the Biot–Savart distance cuts the on-axis field by 6.8% at the coil plane, 5.5% at 32 units out, 3.5% at 64, 1.4% at 128 — the mirror ratio and throat field inherit the centre figure — and it caps the field near the wire at ~15% of the true value, which no particle much minds but a field purist should know.

The speed ceiling breaks the energy claim where it bites. Velocities are rescaled at 230 × temperature; in a static field that is the one place the pusher's exact speed conservation is overridden. It bit measurably in the flare room (fastest particle pinned at exactly the cap) and nowhere else the audit ran.

Small print. The "uniform guide field" of the bottle is decorative — 0.29 units against a coil field of 1,241 at the centre (0.02%); the pinch slider works almost entirely through coil strength. The initial HTML telemetry is pre-boot placeholder paint (it even shows 220 + 220 particles for a 420-particle room) and is replaced within a frame or two. And the physics note's "symplectic" should read "volume-preserving" — Boris's energy behaviour is a bounded oscillation, which is better than most integrators manage, but it is not the symplectic property.

Set against the bends: a textbook-exact Boris pusher, coils summed from the real Biot–Savart law, a loss cone measured within half a degree of theory, a mirror condition good to 0.03%, μ steady to 0.02%, drifts in the belts that match theory's ratio to two decimals, and flux surfaces that hold for a hundred turns. The exhibit's engine room is honest. Its sins are concentrated where exhibits usually sin — in the labels, the unit conversions, the one room whose field outran its integrator, and a reconnection that is choreography wearing physics' clothes.
