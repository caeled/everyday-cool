Wavefunction — the lesson

The header on the game says iℏ ∂ψ/∂t = Ĥψ, and the first question an audit has to ask is whether anything in the file actually solves that equation — or whether the waves are animation wearing a lab coat. The answer, measured six ways below, is that the quantum mechanics is real. Both solvers are genuine time-dependent Schrödinger propagators (the Visscher staggered leapfrog, real and imaginary parts stepped a half-period apart), and the 1D lab is a precision instrument: its tunneling probabilities track the exact square-barrier formula across a 125-to-1 range of barrier heights and widths, its wave packet spreads at exactly the theoretical rate, and its conserved norm holds to three parts in 1015. The 2D lab is the same solver on a coarser grid, and there the grid itself becomes part of the physics — its fringes are genuine interference, but spaced by the lattice's dispersion, not the textbook formula. This page is the other half of the game: the physics, the math the code runs, and the audit — including the one mode whose percentages cannot be trusted, and why its picture still can.

Wavefunction was built by Google Antigravity (Gemini) at Steven Powell's direction; this lesson and its audit were written by Muse, from reading the game's code and running its solvers headlessly.

How to play

There are two laboratories sharing one page. The 2D Double-Slit Lab is a chamber 192 × 116 cells: a wave packet flies right, meets a wall with slits or a barrier, and a detector screen near the right edge accumulates the pattern — both as a smooth envelope and as discrete dots, one sampled hit at a time. The 1D Tunneling Lab is a single line of 512 points where the same physics runs on a finer grid, with the wave itself drawn (real and imaginary parts) and the numbers — Transmission T, Reflection R, ⟨E⟩/V₀, Norm — kept to laboratory precision. The telemetry is live in both.

  • Choose an experiment. Six curated presets: Double-Slit Interference, Which-Path Observer (the same slits with a sensor at Slit A), Quantum Tunneling (1D, E < V₀), Resonant Double-Barrier (a quantum Fabry–Pérot cavity), Harmonic Slosh (a coherent state in a parabolic well), and Single-Slit Diffraction.
  • Fire packets. The stream auto-fires, or fire one yourself. Sliders set the momentum k₀ (and with it the energy), the packet width σ, and the simulation speed.
  • Shape the potential. Barrier height V₀, barrier width L, slit separation d, slit width a, cavity width — then Apply & Relaunch. Or paint your own: aim, paint barrier, paint well, erase, with a brush.
  • Turn the observer on and off in the double-slit chamber and watch the detector histogram change character — fringes with the observer off, a smooth classical-looking band with it on. Clear Detector Screen starts the count over.
  • Read the tallies. In 1D, T and R are accounted to the last scrap of probability: what is still flying, plus what the absorbing edges have eaten on each side, always totals the packet you launched. In 2D the same tallies run — with one exception, named in the honest box, in Which-Path mode.

The physics

A wave packet is a thing that spreads

The packet the game fires is a Gaussian wave packet: a bell-shaped envelope carrying a wave, ψ ∝ e−x²/(2σ²) · eik₀x. It has a position (the bell) and a momentum (the wave inside it), and quantum mechanics charges for holding both at once: squeeze σ and the momentum spread grows. Left alone in empty space the packet does something no classical object does — it spreads, its width growing as:

s(t)² = s₀² · (1 + (ħt / 2ms₀²)²) The variance of |ψ|² for a free Gaussian. The packet does not spread because anything pushes it; it spreads because it was always a sum of waves with slightly different speeds, and they drift apart. The audit measures the game's packet against this formula point by point — see Receipt 2.

Tunneling: the wave does not stop at the wall

Send the packet at a barrier taller than its energy — V₀ > E — and classical physics gives a clean verdict: 100% reflection, every time. The Schrödinger equation gives a different one. Inside the barrier the wave does not oscillate; it decays exponentially, ψ ∝ e−κx with κ = √(2m(V₀ − E))/ħ. If the barrier is thin enough that some wave is still alive at the far side, it re-emerges as a traveling wave: the particle is found on the other side of a wall it never had the energy to climb. For a square barrier the exact transmission probability is:

T = [ 1 + V₀² sinh²(κL) / (4E(V₀ − E)) ]−1 L is the barrier width. Every symbol in this formula is a slider or a readout in the 1D lab, which is what makes the lab auditable: set V₀ and L, read T, and compare against this expression. The famous approximation T ≈ e−2κL (the WKB or Gamow factor) drops the prefactor — and at these barrier sizes that shortcut is wrong by a factor of two or more, as Receipt 3 shows. The game agrees with the exact formula, not the shortcut.

Two barriers with a gap between them make a cavity, and the cavity has resonances: when the wave inside the gap fits a whole number of half-wavelengths, the reflections conspire to cancel and transmission spikes — the quantum Fabry–Pérot effect, the same physics as an anti-reflection coating. Off resonance, two barriers are far more opaque than one. Receipt 4 scans the cavity width and watches T swing by a factor of ten.

Two slits, and the observer who breaks them

With both slits open and nothing watching, the packet passes through both at once — it is a wave — and the two halves interfere: bright fringes where the path lengths differ by whole wavelengths, dark lines where they differ by half. The textbook fringe spacing, for wavelength λ, slit separation d, and screen distance D, is Δy = λD/d. Two honest complications live in this room. First, the game's screen sits at D = 102 cells while d²/λ = 168 — inside the Fresnel (near-field) zone, where the textbook formula has not yet taken over. Second, and larger: the 2D grid's own dispersion bends the spacing further. Both are measured in Receipt 6.

Now switch the observer on. A sensor at Slit A that can tell which slit the particle used destroys the fringes — not by jiggling the wave, but by entangling with it: the total state becomes (wave through A)·(detector says A) + (wave through B)·(detector says B), and when you look only at the particle, the cross term that makes fringes is exactly cancelled by the orthogonality of the detector states. What remains is a mixture — the sum of two single-slit patterns. The game implements precisely this, and Receipt 6 measures how completely the fringes die: visibility 0.959 with the observer off, 0.003 with it on.

The harmonic well and the state that does not spread

One packet is exempt from spreading: the coherent (Glauber) state of the harmonic oscillator, a Gaussian of exactly the ground-state width, displaced from the centre. It sloshes back and forth at the classical frequency, period 2π/ω, keeping its shape — the closest quantum mechanics comes to a pendulum. The Harmonic Slosh preset launches exactly that state, and Receipt 5 checks the period, the amplitude, the energy, and the width that refuses to grow.

The math the game runs

Both solvers work in units ℏ = 1, m = 1, so the Hamiltonian is Ĥ = −½∇² + V and the energy of a wave is E = k²/2. They differ in resolution, and the difference matters:

1D lab2D lab
Grid512 points, spacing h = 0.5 (domain 256 long)192 × 116 cells, spacing h = 1
Kinetic operator−(1/2h²)·(ψi−1 + ψi+1 − 2ψi)−½·(four neighbours − 4ψ)
Time stepdt = min(0.085, 1.15/(8 + max V))dt = min(0.11, 1.15/(4 + max V))
Frame28 substeps ≈ 2.38 time units9 substeps ≈ 0.99 time units
Packet energymeasured ⟨Ĥ⟩ on the grid at launchanalytic formula ½k² + ¼/σx² + ¼/σy²

The propagator is the Visscher scheme (1971): split ψ into real and imaginary parts and stagger them in time — Im lives at half-steps, Re at whole steps — then leapfrog them through each other using Ṙ = Ĥ·Im and İ = −Ĥ·Re:

Im(t + dt/2) = Im(t − dt/2) − dt·Ĥ·Re(t) ;   Re(t + dt) = Re(t) + dt·Ĥ·Im(t + dt/2) This is a real TDSE solver — not split-operator FFT, not Crank–Nicolson, and not an animation. It is symplectic, exactly time-reversible, and it conserves a staggered norm Σ[Re² + Im(t+dt/2)·Im(t−dt/2)] to machine precision (Receipt 1). The dt rule keeps dt·λmax = 1.15, comfortably inside the stability limit of 2; in the presets the caps (0.085 / 0.11) are what actually bind. The launch code staggers Im to −dt/2 with the same Ĥ, so the first step is already centred.

The edges of both chambers are sponges: 30 cells (1D) or 13 cells (2D) where the wave is multiplied each step by e−0.045d² (resp. e−0.042d²), d the depth into the sponge. The sponge is also the bookkeeper — the probability it removes, density × (1 − s²), is tallied on the correct side of the barrier, which is how T and R can be accounted exactly. The slit walls in 2D are not a potential at all but a mask: ψ is held at zero inside the wall, a hard Dirichlet boundary. In Which-Path mode the game runs two wavefunctions under two masks — branch A with Slit B closed, branch B with Slit A closed — and displays the incoherent sum |ψA|² + |ψB|², which is the mixture the entangled state leaves behind. The detector dots are drawn by sampling: probability accumulating at the screen column releases one dot per 0.011 of accumulated |ψ|²·time, its height drawn from the screen's instantaneous |ψ|² profile.

The audit — claims checked against the code, with receipts

The audit lifted the game's own script out of the shipped file — same constants, same Ĥ, same Visscher step, same sponge accounting, same presets — and ran it headlessly in node with the DOM stubbed. Every number below is measured from the game's code, not quoted from its labels.

Receipt 1: the solver is genuine, and its norm is conserved to 3 × 10−15

The Visscher scheme conserves an exact invariant — the staggered norm, with Re and Im paired at their proper half-step offset. Computed with that pairing, over 30,000 steps of the 1D solver with a barrier present, the invariant's total spread was:

SolverStepsStaggered invariant spread
1D (h = 0.5)30,0003 × 10−15
2D (h = 1, closed box, no sponge)1,5001.7 × 10−14

That is machine precision: probability is not leaking, full stop. Hold this receipt next to the game's own Norm readout, which pairs the fields half a step apart and therefore breathes — ±1% in 1D, and in a closed 2D box (sponge off, wave sloshing) between 0.920 and 1.078. The breathing is the readout's, not the physics': the conserved quantity underneath never moves. ⟨E⟩ behaves the same way — bounded oscillation of about ±1% in the naive estimator, no drift.

Receipt 6: fringes — real interference, lattice spacing, and an observer that truly kills them

The detector envelope was accumulated at three distances from the slit wall (slits at y = 44 and 72, openings 7 cells, λ = 2π/1.35 = 4.65):

The fringes are unmistakably interference — visibility 0.959, minima at the zeros — but their spacing follows neither the textbook formula nor a constant error. It follows the 2D grid's own dispersion. On the lattice, a wave's energy is (1 − cos kx) + (1 − cos ky), not (kx² + ky²)/2; at k₀ = 1.35 the wavelength is only 4.65 cells, deep in the dispersive zone, and the wavevector that actually carries energy toward a screen point at 7.8° has ky = 0.134 instead of the continuum's 0.183. The fringe spacing, set by that transverse wavenumber, stretches by the same factor — predicted 23.5, measured 23. (The same dispersion makes the 2D packet travel at group velocity sin(k₀) = 0.98 instead of k₀ = 1.35 — 28% slow.) A player who checks the screen against λD/d will think the game is wrong; the game is instead solving a slightly different universe — the lattice one — exactly.

Then the observer. With Which-Path on, the same accumulation gives fringe visibility 0.003 — the fringes are not damped or blurred, they are gone, and what remains is the flat sum of the two single-slit patterns, exactly the mixture the entangled state predicts. The mechanism in code is the honest one: two complete TDSE evolutions under complementary wall masks, densities added, so the interference cross term is absent by construction rather than suppressed by a decoherence knob.

Receipt 7: 2D tunneling agrees with the grid's energy, not the label's

The 2D tunneling configuration (k₀ = 1.05, V₀ = 0.68, L = 5) measured T = 0.0087. The exact formula at the telemetry's displayed energy (⟨E⟩ = 0.5541, the analytic continuum value) predicts 0.0157, and spectrally averaged, 0.0247 — the game looks low. But the wave on the grid actually carries ⟨Ĥ⟩ = 0.5046 (measured with the game's own Ĥ), because at h = 1 the discrete kinetic energy 1 − cos k sags 9% below k²/2. At that energy the formula predicts 0.0082, spectrally averaged 0.0104 — and the game's 0.0087 sits between them. The 2D solver is solving its own lattice universe consistently; it is the displayed ⟨E⟩, computed from the continuum formula, that belongs to the other universe. In the 1D lab (h = 0.5) the same sag is 0.6% and the distinction vanishes — which is why the 1D energy is measured on the grid at launch, and why Receipt 3 could be so exact.

The asymmetry is structural: each branch is launched as a full packet, so the tallies average the two branches on the left of the barrier (where each branch carries the whole incoming packet) but add them on the right (where each branch contributes only its own slit's transmitted piece — the choice that makes the screen pattern come out right). The price is that in this one mode the percentages are bookkeeping, not measurement: they total 113% of a particle, and the displayed T (0.232) sits between the two defensible answers (the mixture's 0.13 and the unobserved geometry's 0.263) without being either. The picture in this mode is exact; the percentages are not. Everywhere else the accounting closes to 1.000–1.001.

How it's built — the coding choices

One Hamiltonian as the source of truth

Everything flows through two functions, applyH1D and applyH2D: the launch staggering uses them, the Visscher step uses them, the 1D energy measurement uses them — and the audit used them, which is why the audit's ⟨Ĥ⟩ and the game's agree by construction and the comparisons above are about physics, not about two implementations drifting apart. The step functions themselves are twenty lines each: update Im from Re, update Re from the new Im, apply the sponge, tally what it ate. There is no separate "display physics."

The presets are launch conditions, not animations

Each preset is a potential builder plus a launch: the tunneling preset is k₀ = 0.62, V₀ = 0.24, width 8 cells — and its comment in the code ("V0/E = 1.25 (E < V0!)") is borne out by the measured ⟨E⟩ = 0.1924. The harmonic preset computes the coherent state's width from ω itself, σ = 1/√ω, rather than hard-coding a Gaussian and hoping. The double-slit stream, the auto-relaunch when a packet has exited, the detector's dot sampler — all bookkeeping around the same two step functions. The file is fully self-contained: no external references, no storage, no network.

The rendering is honest about being a picture

The 2D view colours |ψ|² by phase through a precomputed lookup table and applies a gamma-boosted, auto-scaled brightness (reference peak re-measured from the live field) so that a tunneled tail a hundred times fainter than the incident packet stays visible. That is a display choice, and it cuts both ways: the faint wave you see beyond a barrier is real — Receipt 3 counts it — but its brightness on screen is boosted, not proportional. The 1D view, with Re(ψ) and Im(ψ) drawn as curves and the energy line marked, is the lab's honest face.

Simplifications and divergences, named

The 2D grid is a universe with its own dispersion: at h = 1 and k₀ = 1.35, the lattice kinetic energy (1 − cos k) sits 14% below k²/2, the packet's group velocity is sin(k₀) = 0.98 rather than 1.35, and the double-slit fringes space at 23 cells where the textbook formula says 17 — all one cause, all measured in Receipts 6 and 7. The 2D lab's displayed ⟨E⟩ is the continuum formula's value (0.5541 where the wave carries 0.5046), so a 2D tunneling T checked against the displayed energy will look wrong by ~2×; checked against the carried energy it is right. The 1D lab, at h = 0.5, has none of this at any size that matters (0.6%).

The Norm readout breathes: the telemetry's ∫|ψ|² pairs Re and Im half a step apart, so it oscillates — ±1% in 1D, and between 0.920 and 1.078 in a closed 2D box — while the solver's exactly conserved staggered norm holds to 3 × 10−15 (Receipt 1). In normal play with the sponge on you will mostly see it decline as packets are absorbed, which is real; the wobble on top of the decline is the readout, not a leak.

Which-Path percentages total 113%: in that one mode the T/R counters average the two branches on the left and add them on the right (Receipt 8), so they are not measurements. The screen pattern in the same mode is the correct incoherent sum and its fringe visibility is a true 0.003. Trust the picture, not the percentages, exactly there.

The observer is a representation, not a simulation: no detector degree of freedom is evolved; the decoherence is imposed by the two-branch structure at launch, which is exactly equivalent to tracing out an entangled detector at the level of the displayed density — but the flashing sensor icon at Slit A is a threshold lamp (it lights when |ψ|² at that pixel passes 0.00018), not the mechanism. Nothing reaches back and disturbs the wave; in reality nothing needs to — but the game shows the entanglement's result, not its dynamics.

The detector dots are samples, not collapses: a "hit" is drawn from the screen's instantaneous |ψ|² profile (density at the column, not probability current) at a rate of one per 0.011 of accumulated density·time, at most five per frame. The wavefunction is never reduced, re-launched, or otherwise touched by a hit — the dots and the histogram are a Monte Carlo portrait of the same |ψ|² the envelope integrates, accumulating alongside the evolution. The explainer's "collapsing into discrete dots" is the standard shorthand for what a real detector does; the game models the statistics, not the collapse.

The "Resonant" preset parks in a trough: its default cavity (18 cells) sits near a transmission minimum for its own energy — T ≈ 0.104 with its own packet, against 0.353 at 14 cells and 0.300 at 22 (Receipt 4). The resonance it advertises is real and one slider-drag away, but the factory setting demonstrates the anti-resonance.

"Two classical bands" merge into one: with the observer on, the explainer pictures two classical bands on the screen; at the preset geometry each single-slit pattern is so broad (the envelope's first zero lies off the detector) that the two bands overlap into a single nearly flat distribution — the audit's accumulated profile reads ≈ constant across the whole detector. The fringes' death is the point and it is real; the two-band silhouette is not what this geometry produces.

Small print: the coherent state's variance breathes ±5% on the grid instead of holding perfectly (Receipt 5). The screen sits in the Fresnel zone (D = 102 < d²/λ = 168), so even a continuum solver would not show the textbook λD/d spacing at this distance — the lattice effect in Receipt 6 is on top of that. The 2D harmonic well uses ω = 0.022 where the 1D well uses 0.0085 — the two labs' "same" preset is two different wells. And the auto-scaled rendering (see How it's built) means brightness in 2D is visibility, not proportion.
