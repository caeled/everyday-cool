Chapter 01 · Microscopic Electrodynamics

What Reflection Really Is: Oscillating Electrons, Not Billiard Balls

In introductory physics, we draw a light ray bouncing off a mirror like a pool ball off a cushion: θ_r = θ_i. That geometric shortcut predicts the direction accurately, yet it hides the real electromagnetic miracle happening inside the mirror!

Light is a coupled, self-propagating wave of oscillating electric (E) and magnetic (B) fields. When an EM wave hits a surface — whether glass, water, or polished silver — its oscillating electric field grabs the electrons inside the surface atoms and shakes them back and forth at the exact frequency f of the incoming wave. An accelerating electric charge radiates its own new spherical electromagnetic wave!

Why Only One Angle Survives (Huygens–Fresnel)

Millions of atoms across a flat mirror radiate spherical wavelets slightly staggered in time (because the tilted wavefront arrives at one side of the mirror earlier than the other). In almost every outgoing direction, these wavelets arrive out of phase and destructively cancel to zero. There is only one magic reflection direction where every atom's wavelet arrives perfectly in step: θ_r = θ_i!

Why Metals Mirror & Glass Transmits

In a metal (silver, aluminum), free conduction electrons slosh effortlessly to cancel the electric field inside the metal (E_total = 0), forcing almost 100% of the energy to reradiate backward as a mirror reflection. In glass or water, electrons are tightly bound like little springs — they reradiate a weaker reflected wave and a slowed-down transmitted wave (v = c / n).

Maxwell's Curl Equations (The 2D TMz Engine Running Live in Lab 1): ∂E / ∂t = (1 / ε) (∇ × H − σE) (Changing magnetic curl drives electric field) ∂H / ∂t = −(1 / μ) (∇ × E) (Changing electric curl drives magnetic field) Wave speed inside medium of refractive index n = √(ε_r μ_r): v = c / n
Chapter 02 · Shadow Geometry & Radiosity

The True Anatomy of Shadows: Umbra, Penumbra, Antumbra & Bounce Light

A shadow is not a physical "thing" — it is a sculpted 3D volume of space where an occluder interrupts the flow of radiant energy. Yet almost no real-world shadow is a uniform black cutout, for two profound reasons: finite light-source size and secondary bounce light (radiosity).

Umbra (Total Blockage)

The dark inner core where no point on the light source is visible past the object. When the lamp is larger than the obstacle (like the Sun relative to the Moon or your hand!), the umbra tapers to a pointed cone and eventually ends!

Penumbra (Soft Partial Shadow)

The glowing gradient transition zone where part of the lamp peeks around the obstacle's edge. Wider lamps (overcast sky, studio softbox) produce huge, buttery penumbras; tiny lamps (bare LED, laser) produce razor-sharp shadows.

Antumbra ("Ring of Fire")

Beyond the tip of a tapering umbra cone, the obstacle appears smaller than the light source, leaving a bright ring around it. Stand in the Moon's antumbra and you witness an Annular Solar Eclipse!

Additive Colored Shadows & Radiosity Bounce

Aim a Red, Green, and Blue lamp at an object on a white floor: where all three overlap, the floor looks white. Where the object blocks only the Red lamp, Green + Blue still illuminate that shadow — turning the shadow electric Cyan! Blocking Green makes a Magenta shadow; blocking Blue makes a Yellow shadow!

Meanwhile, real rooms aren't pitch black inside shadows because walls, floors, and mirrors act as secondary light emitters — bouncing photons back into the umbra (a physics principle known as Global Illumination / Radiosity). Notice how a sunlit red brick wall casts a warm crimson glow straight into the shadowed side of a white statue!

Chapter 03 · When Ray Optics Breaks

Light Bending Into Shadows: Fresnel Diffraction & the Arago–Poisson Spot

Geometric shadow rays work magnificently when obstacles are much larger than the wavelength of light (a ≫ λ). Shrink the obstacle toward the wavelength scale — or switch to radio waves / microwaves where λ is centimeters to meters — and shadows behave in a way that shocked 19th-century physicists: electromagnetic waves bend around edges directly into the geometric shadow!

The Famous 1818 Arago–Poisson Bet: • Augustin-Jean Fresnel submitted his wave theory of diffraction to the French Academy of Sciences. • Judge Siméon Denis Poisson (a die-hard Newton particle theorist) computed Fresnel's integral for a circular shadow and jeered: "Your equations predict an absurd BRIGHT SPOT right at the dead center of a solid disk's shadow!" • Committee head François Arago actually ran the experiment in the lab — AND THE BRIGHT SPOT WAS RIGHT THERE! Why? Every point along the circular rim of the obstacle is the EXACT same distance from the shadow's centerline. Therefore, all diffracted wavelets wrapping around the edges travel equal paths and arrive 100% IN PHASE! One honest wrinkle: that argument is exact for a real 3D disk. This bench is 2D — its disk is an endless strip — so here the on-axis spot is real but weaker and wanders with distance, peaking at roughly a fifth of the lit level instead of matching it.

This same wave-shadow diffraction explains why AM radio (λ ≈ 300 m) bends right over mountains and around buildings into "radio shadows," whereas 5G millimeter waves (λ ≈ 1 cm) cast sharp, optical-like shadows behind walls and trees!

Chapter 04 · Maxwell Boundary Conditions

Fresnel Equations, Grazing Mirrors & Brewster's Zero-Glare Angle

Look straight down into a clear pond and you see the pebbles on the bottom (only 2% reflects). Look across the same pond near sunset at a shallow grazing angle, and the water turns into a blinding silver mirror — about 60% reflective at 85°, and 90%+ only within a degree of grazing! Why does reflectance depend so dramatically on angle — and on the direction the electric field vibrates?

Because Maxwell's equations require the tangential E and H fields to match smoothly across the boundary, yielding the exact Fresnel Amplitude Reflection Coefficients for s-polarization (electric field perpendicular to the plane of incidence) and p-polarization (electric field parallel to the plane of incidence):

Snell's Law: n₁ sin(θᵢ) = n₂ sin(θₜ) s-Polarized (⊥): r_s = (n₁ cos θᵢ − n₂ cos θₜ) / (n₁ cos θᵢ + n₂ cos θₜ) ⇒ R_s = |r_s|² p-Polarized (∥): r_p = (n₂ cos θᵢ − n₁ cos θₜ) / (n₂ cos θᵢ + n₁ cos θₜ) ⇒ R_p = |r_p|² Normal Incidence (θᵢ = 0°): R₀ = [ (n₁ − n₂) / (n₁ + n₂) ]² (Air→Glass ≈ 4.3%, Air→Diamond ≈ 17.2%) Brewster's Angle (R_p = 0!): θ_B = arctan( n₂ / n₁ ) (Air→Water = 53.1°, Air→Glass = 56.7°)

The Physical Secret Behind Brewster's Angle

At θ_B = arctan(n₂/n₁), the reflected ray and the refracted ray meet at an exact 90° right angle (θ_B + θₜ = 90°). For p-polarized light, the electrons shaking inside the water oscillate directly along the would-be reflected direction. Because an oscillating electric dipole radiates zero energy along its own axis (like trying to see the waves of a rope shaken straight toward your eye), the p-polarized reflection vanishes completely! That leaves 100% horizontally (s) polarized glare — which vertical polarized sunglasses block effortlessly!

Chapter 05 · Phase Flips & Quantum-Like Tunneling

180° Phase Inversion, Total Internal Reflection & Evanescent Waves

Hard vs. Soft Bounce (Phase Flip)

Just like a wave pulse on a rope tied to a rigid brick wall flips upside-down when it bounces, an EM wave moving from a lower index to a higher index (n₁ < n₂, e.g. Air → Glass) undergoes an exact 180° (π radian) phase inversion on reflection! Going the opposite way (n₁ > n₂, Glass → Air) is like a rope with a free ring end — 0° phase shift! (These flip rules are the s-polarization rules; p-polarization follows them only outside its own Brewster geometry — the lab's per-polarization phase readout shows both.)

Total Internal Reflection & Evanescent Leak

When light travels from glass into air past the Critical Angle θ_c = arcsin(n₂/n₁), Snell's law demands sin(θₜ) > 1 — impossible for a real propagating angle! 100% of the energy reflects back into the glass (the magic behind fiber-optic internet cables). Yet Maxwell's boundary conditions still demand a non-zero field on the air side: an Evanescent Wave that glides along the boundary and dies off exponentially within ~1 wavelength!

Critical Angle (n₁ > n₂): θ_c = arcsin( n₂ / n₁ ) (Crown Glass → Air: 41.1°) Evanescent Decay Depth: E(z) = E₀ · exp( −z / δ ), δ = λ / ( 2π √(n₁² sin²θᵢ − n₂²) ) Frustrated TIR (Optical Tunneling): Bring a second glass prism within a fraction of a wavelength (d ~ δ) of the boundary, and the decaying evanescent field catches onto the second prism and resumes propagating — the exact electromagnetic twin of Quantum Mechanical Tunneling!
Chapter 06 · When Beams Intersect

Crossing EM Waves: Superposition, Interference Lattices & Zero Collisions

Cross two garden hoses and water splashes violently in every direction. Cross two laser beams in air or vacuum and… neither beam deflects, slows down, or loses a single photon! Why?

Because Maxwell's equations in vacuum and linear media are strictly linear: if E₁(r,t) and E₂(r,t) are each valid solutions, then their exact sum E_total = E₁ + E₂ is automatically a valid solution. Inside the overlap zone, however, something spectacular happens:

Combined Intensity of Two Crossing Coherent Waves: I(r) = I₁ + I₂ + 2 √(I₁ I₂) cos( Δk · r + Δφ ) • Where cos = +1 (Constructive): E doubles (2E₀), so Intensity QUADRUPLES (4I₀)! • Where cos = −1 (Destructive): E cancels to ZERO — dark nodal stripes right inside overlapping light! • Energy Conservation: The missing energy from the dark stripes didn't vanish — it piled up into the 4× brighter constructive peaks! Average energy = I₁ + I₂.

Engineers actually exploit crossing laser interference lattices to build Optical Lattices ("egg cartons made of pure light") that trap ultracold atoms in quantum computers, and to etch holographic diffraction gratings with nanometer precision!

Chapter 07 · Standing Waves & Structural Color

Resonance: Fabry–Pérot Cavities, Lasers & Iridescent Thin Films

What happens when an EM wave doesn't just bounce once, but bounces back and forth between two surfaces? Now the wave interferes with itself!

Thin-Film Interference (Bubbles & AR Coatings)

Light reflecting off the front of a soap film (π phase flip) interferes with light that enters the film, bounces off the back surface (0 flip), and exits. Model note: this bench's film reflectance is computed from the s-polarization amplitudes — exact at normal incidence; at steep angles it reads above the true unpolarized average. Because the round-trip optical path is 2 n d cos(θₜ), certain colors reinforce constructively (brilliant turquoise, gold, violet) while others cancel completely! Camera lenses use a quarter-wave MgF₂ coating (d = λ / 4n) where both bounces flip by π, making the two reflected waves cancel to a whisper of glare (~1.3% residual — the two bounce amplitudes are not quite equal, so the cancellation is deep but not perfect)!

Fabry–Pérot Optical Cavity Resonance

Trap light between two high-reflectivity mirrors spaced by L. At arbitrary wavelengths, successive bounces arrive out of step and cancel out. At exact Resonant Eigenmodes where an integer number of half-wavelengths fit snugly inside the mirrors (2 n L cos θ = m λ), every single bounce adds in phase — building a massive standing wave inside the cavity and letting 100% of resonant light tunnel cleanly through both mirrors! This exact resonance powers every Laser, gravitational-wave LIGO arm, and fiber-optic wavelength filter!

Thin-Film Round-Trip Phase Difference: Δφ = (4π n_film d cos θₜ) / λ + Δφ_reflection Fabry–Pérot Airy Transmission Formula (Mirror Reflectivity R): T(λ) = 1 / [ 1 + F · sin²(δ / 2) ], where F = 4R / (1 − R)² (Coefficient of Finesse) Resonance Condition: δ = 2πm ⟺ L = m · (λ / 2n) (m = 1, 2, 3, …)
Chapter 08 · Quantum Electrodynamics (QED)

Feynman's QED Mirror: Why Light Actually Reflects Off the ENTIRE Mirror!

In his legendary lectures QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter, Richard Feynman revealed a truth far wilder than "angle in = angle out": light does NOT just bounce off the middle of a mirror. A photon traveling from Source S to Detector D explores every single atomic patch across the entire mirror — even the far left and far right corners where θ_r ≠ θ_i!

Each possible path carries a tiny rotating quantum amplitude arrow ("clock hand") e^(i·k·L) whose angle equals the travel time t = L/c multiplied by the light's frequency. To find the probability that light reaches the detector, you place every path's arrow tip-to-tail into a grand vector sum (the Cornu Spiral) and square the final length: P = |Σ e^(i k L_j)|².

Why Only the Center Appears to Reflect (Stationary Phase / Fermat's Principle): • Near the edges: moving 1 millimeter left or right changes path length L rapidly, so neighboring clock arrows point in opposite directions and curl into tight circles — nearly canceling, leaving only a whisper of residue! • At the center (θ_r = θ_i): path length L hits a minimum (dL/dx = 0, Fermat's Principle of Least Time!). Because L barely changes there, dozens of neighboring arrows point the SAME way and add into a giant vector! THE SMOKING-GUN PROOF (Try it in Lab 2!): Block the middle of the mirror completely, then scrape away every OTHER half-wavelength stripe on the far edge (removing the arrows that point backward). Suddenly the far edge reflects brilliantly at an "impossible" angle! That's literally how a Diffraction Grating — and a shimmering CD/DVD — works!
Chapter 09 · Geometry of Curved & Multiple Reflections

Curved Mirrors, Coffee-Cup Caustics, Chirality & Infinite Mirror Halls

Bend a mirror — or place two flat mirrors facing each other — and simple θ_r = θ_i rules generate breathtaking mathematical envelopes and optical paradoxes:

Why Coffee Cups Have a Glowing Heart-Shaped Caustic

A circular (spherical) mirror is cheap to manufacture, but parallel rays hitting its outer rim reflect slightly closer than rays near the center (Spherical Aberration). Where adjacent reflected rays bunch together and cross, they trace a blazing envelope called a Nephroid Caustic — the exact kidney/heart shape glowing on the surface of your morning coffee! Morph the curve into a Parabola (e = 1) and the caustic collapses to a single razor-sharp focal point — why every radio telescope and satellite dish is parabolic!

The Mirror Chirality Paradox (Why Do Mirrors Flip Left-Right?)

Stand in front of a mirror: your right hand looks like your reflection's left hand — yet your head doesn't flip to your feet! Why? A mirror does NOT flip left and right! It flips front-to-back along its surface normal (z → −z), turning a right-handed glove inside-out into a left-handed glove (Parity Inversion, det(M) = −1)! Bounce light off two angled mirrors (like a kaleidoscope or corner reflector), and two flips cancel ((−1)² = +1), restoring your true non-reversed appearance!

Kaleidoscope Virtual Image Count (Two Mirrors Hinged at Angle θ): N_virtual = (360° / θ) − 1 (e.g., θ = 90° ⇒ 3 images; θ = 60° ⇒ 5 images; θ = 45° ⇒ 7 images) Infinity Mirror Brightness Decay (Two Parallel Mirrors of Reflectivity R): I_k = I₀ · R^k (At R = 92%, the 10th reflection has 43% brightness; the 25th has 12%!) And because ordinary soda-lime glass absorbs tiny amounts of red/blue per pass, deep reflections turn emerald green!
Chapter 10 · Real-World Materials & Microfacets

Specular vs. Diffuse Reflection, BRDF & Why Gold Is Yellow

If every surface obeys Maxwell's equations microscopically, why does polished silver show your face while a sheet of white paper just glows softly? Zoom in 10,000× with an electron microscope and the mystery vanishes: rough surfaces are mountain ranges of tiny tilted mirrors (Microfacets)!

Microfacet BRDF & Lambertian Diffuse

The Bidirectional Reflectance Distribution Function (BRDF) measures how much light arriving from direction ω_i scatters toward viewer direction ω_o. As roughness α increases, the microfacets tilt randomly, spreading the mirror spike into a soft glossy lobe (GGX/Beckmann). When light penetrates inside paint, paper, or skin and bounces randomly off pigment granules before escaping, it forgets its original angle completely — emerging as isotropic Lambertian diffuse light!

Why Glossy Red Paint Has a WHITE Highlight, While Gold Has a YELLOW Highlight!

A glossy red car has a clear dielectric lacquer coat over red pigment. Light bouncing off the top clearcoat obeys dielectric Fresnel equations — reflecting all colors equally (~4% white glare)! Only light that penetrates into the pigment comes back red! In contrast, metals have no diffuse subsurface; their reflection comes directly from free + bound electrons governed by a complex refractive index ñ(λ) = n(λ) + iκ(λ). In Gold (Au), relativistic 5d → 6s electron transitions absorb blue/violet photons (< 500 nm), leaving pure gleaming yellow-gold specular reflections!

Complex Metallic Fresnel Reflectance at Normal Incidence (Conductors): R(λ) = [ (n(λ) − 1)² + κ(λ)² ] / [ (n(λ) + 1)² + κ(λ)² ] • Large extinction coefficient κ(λ) forces R → 100% (Silver: κ ≈ 3.5 across visible ⇒ R ≈ 97% white mirror) • Where quantum interband transitions switch on (Gold in blue/violet, ≈2.4 eV), the metal leaves the good-metal limit — n(λ) climbs toward κ(λ) — so R collapses and those wavelengths are absorbed!
Chapter 11 · Planetary-Scale EM Reflection & Shadows

Ionospheric Radio Skipping: Plasma Mirrors, Skip-Zone Shadows & Space Escape

How did Guglielmo Marconi send the first radio signal across the Atlantic Ocean in 1901 — right around the curved bulge of the Earth — when radio waves travel in straight lines like light? Because 80 to 350 kilometers overhead, solar ultraviolet radiation strips electrons off upper-atmosphere atoms, wrapping Earth in an invisible electrically charged shell: the Ionosphere!

When an HF (shortwave) radio wave enters this ionized plasma, its electric field shakes the free electrons — lowering the plasma's effective refractive index below 1.0! As the wave climbs into denser electrons, n(h) drops smoothly toward zero, continuously bending the radio wave via Snell's law until it undergoes gradual Total Internal Reflection right back down to Earth — where the conductive oceans and soil bounce it back up for 2nd, 3rd, and 4th hops around the globe!

Appleton–Hartree Plasma Refractive Index (Collisionless): n(h) = √( 1 − ( f_p(h) / f )² ), where Plasma Frequency f_p(h) ≈ 9 · √(N_e(h)) [Hz] Critical Frequency (Vertical Bounce Cutoff): f_oF2 = max f_p(h) (typically 4–12 MHz) Maximum Usable Frequency (Secant Law): MUF ≈ f_oF2 · sec(θ_incidence) • If f < f_oF2: Even straight-up vertical rays bounce back down (NVIS — NO Skip Zone!). • If f_oF2 < f < MUF: Steep rays ESCAPE into space, while shallow rays REFLECT far away — creating a silent RADIO SHADOW called the SKIP ZONE right between Groundwave and 1st Hop! • If f > MUF (VHF/FM/GPS): The plasma can't react fast enough; ALL rays punch straight into space!

The Silent Skip Zone (A Planetary Radio Shadow)

A shortwave station at 14 MHz might be completely inaudible 150 km away (beyond the groundwave, inside the silent Skip Zone where steep rays escaped into space), yet thunder in crystal-clear 2,200 km away where the first ionospheric skip touches down!

Why AM Radio Travels Hundreds of Miles at Night

During the day, sunlight creates a dense, low-altitude D-Layer (60–90 km) where electrons collide constantly with air molecules — acting like a warm sponge that absorbs low-frequency AM/80m radio before it can reach the F-layer mirror. The instant the Sun sets, the D-layer vanishes in minutes, unlocking the high F-layer mirror so distant AM stations skip across entire continents!
