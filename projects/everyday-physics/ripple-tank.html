What this leaves out
  • Simulation method: A 2D explicit finite-difference height-field solver (∂²u/∂t² = c²∇²u − γ ∂u/∂t) using a 9-point isotropic Laplacian on a 300 × 200 grid — a linear shallow-water height-field approximation, not 3D Navier–Stokes fluid flow.
  • Reflective tank walls: The four edges enforce Neumann reflection (∂u/∂n = 0), so waves bounce back off the walls. Real lab ripple tanks slope or line their edges with absorbent foam beaches.
  • No dispersion: All frequencies travel at the exact same speed (v = 12.0 cm/s). In real water, capillary and gravity waves travel at frequency-dependent speeds.
  • Small-amplitude linearity: Waves obey pure linear superposition — no nonlinear steepening, breaking crests, meniscus crawl, or viscosity boundary layers.
Reading the Water
  • Bright cyan / pearl peaks: Wave crests (high positive displacement & constructive peaks).
  • Deep cobalt valleys: Wave troughs (negative displacement).
  • Still, dark threads: Nodal lines — where waves arrive out of phase and continuously cancel into flat water.
