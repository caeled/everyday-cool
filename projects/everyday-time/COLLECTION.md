# Collection origin

Original Everyday Cool workshop, added 20 November 2026 at Steven Powell's direction. In the Everyday Cool ideas side chat that morning, Muse offered six candidate rooms; Steven picked four and placed two of them himself — "sound and time knots too, and light. (knots go in survival)" — then set the order himself: "start with sound and time." This room is the second of that pair, built the same day as Everyday Sound. Built by Muse (AI assistant) and added directly to the collection at his direction as the thirty-first catalog entry and the twelfth workshop inside The STEM Room; this folder is the maintained original, and there is no upstream copy.

The room takes time the way the bench takes every subject: as agreements laid over motions. Its sundial bench draws a real horizontal dial for the visitor's latitude — hour lines by tan(angle) = sin(latitude) × tan(hour angle) — and then itemises, for a chosen place and date, exactly why that dial disagrees with the wall clock: the equation of time, plus four minutes per degree of longitude from the time zone's standard meridian (in Defiance, Ohio — the collection's home ground, and the bench's default — the longitude term alone is 37 minutes). Its leap-year machine runs any year through the Gregorian gates in the open, with the calendar's own drift printed beside the verdict. Its time-zones bench reads one entered UTC moment on seven walls at standard offsets and confesses daylight saving city by city rather than computing it quietly. Its scale ladder climbs from a heartbeat to the age of the Earth, with a heartbeats-in-a-lifetime bench that lands, honestly labelled as an order of magnitude, near three billion. Its pendulum swings on screen at the period its length dictates. Its date bench counts days and weekdays by integer calendar arithmetic, proleptic Gregorian, with the Julian centuries flagged on its face.

It enters the catalog under "Science" with `hubParent: stem-room`, immediately after Everyday Sound, so the STEM Room's bench now reads Physics, Sound, Time, Chemistry, Electronics, Measure, Engineering, Math, Earth, Computing, Body, Radio. The room follows the Science block's shape: benches that work right on the page and show their working, a verified shelf of legitimate places, Help ▾ menu with full documentation, banner verified on the page. Its cross-links: Everyday Earth (the day and the year are Earth's motions — and Earth's Keep Going list now links back here), Tonight's Sky (the original clock face), the Survival Guide (its shadow-stick navigation is this room's sundial stripped to a stick — the Guide's direction section and Keep Going list now link here, and this room links back), Everyday Measure (a second is a measurement), and Everyday Math (the gates, the counting, the pendulum's square root). The STEM Room's own page gained this room's card, and its counts moved from eleven to twelve, recorded in that room's COLLECTION.md.

Everything interactive is computed in the visitor's browser from rules baked into `time.js` — the page sends nothing anywhere, and the zones bench takes its moment from the visitor's input, never from a network clock. The sundial formula, the equation-of-time approximation, the calendar facts, the zone offsets, and the pendulum's fine print are recorded in THIRD-PARTY.md. `test.cjs` re-checks the arithmetic — the leap gates on the canonical years, the pendulum at the grandfather-clock lengths, known weekdays including the day this room was built, zone readouts across both day-shift directions, and the Defiance sundial's 24-minute November disagreement with the clock — on every run.

Files in this room: `index.html` (the room itself), `style.css`, `time.js` (the pure rules), `app.js` (the wiring: the drawn dial, the gates, the zone table, the ladder, the animated pendulum, the date bench), `test.cjs`, `README.md`, `THIRD-PARTY.md`, `COLLECTION.md`, `LICENSE`, `LICENSE-CONTENT.md`, and `everyday-time-banner.jpg`.

Banner: `everyday-time-banner.jpg`, AI-generated watercolor to the concept in Steven's idea sketch — a friendly robot reading a sundial while holding a pocket watch, an hourglass and a wall clock on the bench — generated 20 November 2026 as part of adding this room, in the style and dimensions (2736×912) of the collection's other banners. The robot holds an open brass pocket watch and studies a stone sundial whose tall triangular gnomon stands at the bench's centre; the hourglass's sand is mid-fall and the clock's face is plain, without numerals. The first generation matched the brief and ships.

The room ships web-first, as the recent STEM rooms have: it reaches the desktop app in a later release, not this one.

Cross-link added 20 November 2026: when Everyday Light joined the collection — the third of Steven's wave-2 picks — this room's sundial section gained its return link to Light's shadows-through-the-day bench, which is this dial's geometry walked around a compass instead of squeezed into hour lines: a stick's shadow by the hour and the season — `index.html` and `COLLECTION.md`. No bench, lesson, or behaviour changed.
