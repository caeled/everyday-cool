04 / Check your thinking

What did you notice?

Educator's corner

Try this without a screen

Put two fingers gently on your wrist and feel your pulse at rest. Walk around the room for a minute, then check again. What changed? What other systems helped you move? Compare observations, not competition scores.

Discussion prompt: If one body system is a network, what happens when one part has to work harder? Which other systems respond?

The body atlas

Use the system filter chips to fade the organs that belong to other systems, then tap an organ on the figure — or use the organ buttons under it. The Discover panel tells that organ’s story: a summary, what it does, and one thing to notice. The map is a simplified front view and not to scale; some organs are drawn through others so you can reach them.

The brain explorer

Choose the Brain and a side-view diagram appears with six coloured surface structures you can tap: the four lobes, the cerebellum, and the brainstem. Below it, twelve region buttons add the deeper structures a side view cannot show — hippocampus, amygdala, thalamus, hypothalamus, basal ganglia, corpus callosum. The room says the honest part out loud: complex functions emerge from networks across regions, not from one region working alone.

Connect the dots

Pick an everyday action — running to catch a bus, eating an apple, hearing your name, cooling off on a hot day — and the chain shows which organs cooperate, in order, and what each one contributes. The effort slider beside it is an illustrative model of demand, not a measurement of your body.

Check your thinking

Six questions drawn from the atlas. Pick an answer and the page marks it and explains why; “Another question” cycles through the set. Nothing is scored, saved, or sent anywhere.

Honest limits — not medical advice

This workshop teaches anatomy. It does not diagnose health conditions or provide medical advice, and its descriptions are educational simplifications of a schematic atlas. For questions about your own health, each organ’s “Explore further” shelf — OpenStax, Wikipedia, NINDS — and a clinician are the authority.

Works offline?

Yes. The atlas, the brain explorer, the scenarios, and the quiz all run from this single file in your browser — including inside the desktop app, and including this Help. Only the “Explore further” links need the internet, because those pages live on other people’s sites.
