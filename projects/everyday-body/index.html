The Biology Wave — eight labs and an artificial-life zoo

Eight interactive biology exhibits built by Google Antigravity (Gemini) at Steven Powell's direction, then audited by Muse the collection's way: the shipped code driven headlessly, every claim measured, and the bends — and the falsehoods — printed in the lesson beside each exhibit. The wave stands complete at eight labs, with the Lenia Automata Zoo riding along as its artificial-life companion. Start anywhere; open the lesson when you want the receipts.

BioPower — ATP & photosynthesis

Spin the same proton turbine twice: as a leaf splitting water, and as a mitochondrion burning NADH. The proton bookkeeping sums clean to the modern 2.5 ATP per NADH — but the page prices ATP two different ways, and its DNP uncoupler never actually collapses the ATP counter. The carbon half of the story (the photosynthesis equation, the Calvin cycle) is not here at all. The lesson prints the measurements.

Helix — DNA & CRISPR Lab

Type a gene, watch the ribosome translate it, and send Cas9 hunting for a PAM. The audit drove all 64 codons through its translator with zero mismatches, and its scissors cut exactly 3 bp upstream of the PAM — but one flagship preset guide is shifted a single base and can never fire. The lesson shows both.

▶ Play · 📖 The lesson

Cardio — heart hemodynamics

A beating heart with live pressures, valves, an ECG, and a pressure–volume loop. Its pressure model is genuinely good — but as delivered, its mitral valve never opened, and the resting heart’s output decayed to failure numbers within seconds of loading. Steven sanctioned a one-line fix — the third in the collection — and the valve opened and the ventricle filled; when that unmasked a second defect capping ejection, he sanctioned that repair too (two lines, the fourth). The resting heart now measures the stroke volume and cardiac output on its tin — 70 mL, 5.04 L/min — while its ejection fraction reads 53% against a printed ≈62%, for an arithmetic reason the lesson states plainly. The lesson documents the failure, both fixes, and what still doesn’t hold, with receipts. Educational only — not medical advice.

Darwin — evolution terrarium

A living pond of clones that drift, adapt, split, and speciate. There is no Hardy–Weinberg machinery inside — reproduction is asexual — but the drift is real (variance matched haploid theory within 3%) and selection flips direction with the food supply. One claim on its own page, a “cubic” metabolic cost, measured size^1.55. The lesson has the numbers.

▶ Play · 📖 The lesson

Physarum — slime mold networks

Be the brainless mold: 32,000 tiny agents with three noses each lay scent trails that become roads between food. Driven headlessly, its maze route hit 0.99× the true shortest path and its detour around a UV wall 1.03× optimal — but the famous Tokyo web measured 1.91× the minimum length and never connected all 18 cities. The lesson separates the real model from the decoration.

▶ Play · 📖 The lesson

Axon — Hodgkin–Huxley neuron

Stimulate a squid axon running the actual 1952 Hodgkin–Huxley equations. The audit drove the shipped code and found the physiology real — rest at −65.00 mV, an all-or-none threshold, a ≈2 ms refractory wall, gates moving at their proper relative speeds — but the myelinated preset freezes into a checkerboard at the page's own frame rate, its “bursting train” fires exactly once, and nothing spikes at 37 °C. The lesson prints the measurements.

▶ Play · 📖 The lesson

Immunity — epidemic outbreak lab

Seed patient zero in a living SEIR city and reach for the vaccine dial. The audit counted every agent on every step: the population ledger never leaks (S+E+I+R+V = 320, error zero) and the latent and infectious timers are honest to the tenth of a second — but the city's true R₀ measures ≈11 at every setting of its 1.5–5.0 dial, so the herd-immunity and quarantine presets fall to their own streets. The lesson shows the counts.

▶ Play · 📖 The lesson

Mitosis & Genetics — the eighth lab

Divide a cell, then breed one. The audit ran its crosses in bulk — 20,000 offspring at a time — and its bookkeeping is the most exact of the wave: six chromosomes stay six through mitosis, with twelve chromatids counted honestly; Punnett ratios land on 3:1 and 9:3:3:1 within sampling noise; and its Hardy–Weinberg engine holds p = 0.600000 for 180 generations. Its one false note is a button labelled “Meiosis (2n→4n)” — meiosis makes four cells of n, and the rest of the page knows it. The lesson prints the counts.

▶ Play · 📖 The lesson

Lenia — an automata zoo

Continuous cellular automata that crawl, pulse, and glide. The audit verified this is the real Lenia: kernel, growth curve, and update rule match Bert Chan's mathematics to float precision (largest difference 2.9×10⁻⁷), and Orbium genuinely glides at a constant 0.64 cells per step. But the Geminium Twin dies within about 31 steps of its own preset, the “Hydro Swimmer” never swims, and Orbium lives only at σ = 0.017 — not the 0.015 the page calls canonical. The lesson shows the zoo's receipts.

Human Body Lab

Use Front/Back and the region selector or diagram to explore 25 regions across four topics. Shapes locate regions, not individual tissues. Body Detective offers five fictional observations: write a prediction, reveal evidence, compare an explanation, and follow links to the imager or organ atlas. Notes survive case switching only; reload clears them. All core activities work offline with no accounts or network requests.

The body atlas

Use the system filter chips to fade the organs that belong to other systems, then tap an organ on the figure — or use the organ buttons under it. The Discover panel tells that organ’s story: a summary, what it does, and one thing to notice. The map is a simplified front view and not to scale; some organs are drawn through others so you can reach them.

The brain explorer

Choose the Brain and a side-view diagram appears with six coloured surface structures you can tap: the four lobes, the cerebellum, and the brainstem. Below it, twelve region buttons add the deeper structures a side view cannot show — hippocampus, amygdala, thalamus, hypothalamus, basal ganglia, corpus callosum. The room says the honest part out loud: complex functions emerge from networks across regions, not from one region working alone.

Connect the dots

Pick an everyday action — running to catch a bus, eating an apple, hearing your name, cooling off on a hot day — and the chain shows which organs cooperate, in order, and what each one contributes. The effort slider beside it is an illustrative model of demand, not a measurement of your body.

Check your thinking

Six questions drawn from the atlas. Pick an answer and the page marks it and explains why; “Another question” cycles through the set. Nothing is scored, saved, or sent anywhere.

Honest limits — not medical advice

This workshop teaches anatomy. It does not diagnose health conditions or provide medical advice, and its descriptions are educational simplifications of a schematic atlas. For questions about your own health, each organ’s “Explore further” shelf — OpenStax, Wikipedia, NINDS — and a clinician are the authority.

Works offline?

Yes. The atlas, the brain explorer, the scenarios, and the quiz all run from this single file in your browser — including inside the desktop app, and including this Help. Only the “Explore further” links need the internet, because those pages live on other people’s sites.
