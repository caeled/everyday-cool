Evolution Terrarium — the lesson

A living pond of swimming cells with six heritable traits — size, speed, sense radius, diet, armor, hue — eating, hunting, starving and splitting in two under mutation, predation and scarcity. This page is the audit. Following the house rule, the shipped code was not just read: its own functions were driven headlessly in Node (DOM and canvas stubbed, RNG seeded) — mutateGenes sampled tens of thousands of times, the full stepSimulation engine run for hundreds of simulated seconds across replicate ponds, and its speciation and asteroid functions called directly. The headline finding comes first, because it changes what every other test can mean.

The verdict: a real evolution sim — but not a Hardy–Weinberg one

Flagged plainly, up top: this exhibit contains no alleles, no genotypes, no mating, and no Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium — at all. A full-text search of the shipped file finds zero occurrences of allele, genotype, Hardy, dominant or recessive. There is no p to set to 0.5 or 0.8, and no p², 2pq, q² anywhere to check. Reproduction is asexual mitosis: a well-fed cell splits, and the daughter's genome is the parent's genome plus Gaussian noise (mutateGenes). So the classic population-genetics exam questions cannot be asked of this page — and any lesson that pretended to verify Hardy–Weinberg here would be inventing the result.

What the page does contain is genuine, and it measures well: real random drift, real environment-dependent selection, honest Gaussian mutation whose measured spread matches its slider to under 1%, a working speciation threshold, and an asteroid bottleneck that kills almost exactly the 80% its button promises. The right audit is to test the claims this kind of model can make — haploid, asexual, continuous-trait claims — against the corresponding theory. That is what the sections below do, number by number.

child trait = parent trait + N(0, σ · ktrait), then clamped to range The entire inheritance law of the terrarium, as shipped in mutateGenes: k = 0.65 (size), 0.72 (speed), 55 (sense, px), 0.45 (diet), 0.48 (armor), 58 (hue, degrees). One parent, no segregation, no recombination — a clone with noise.

1 · Hardy–Weinberg: the neutral test that can be run

Hardy–Weinberg's core promise is that, with no selection, mutation or drift, frequencies stay put. The terrarium's analogue of an allele frequency is a founder-lineage frequency: seed a pond with two lineages identical in every trait except a neutral hue marker (hue affects nothing metabolic or predatory), mutation set to σ = 0 so daughters are exact clones, no predators, abundant food, and watch the marker's frequency p over 90 simulated seconds in the shipped engine itself:

Founders (p₀ = 0.50)Final p, mean of 8 replicate pondsVariance across pondsVerdict
16 founders (8 / 8)0.4780.0025 (SD 0.050)Stays put, small wander ✓
100 founders (50 / 50)0.5060.0008 (SD 0.028)Stays put, tighter ✓

No systematic drift in either case — the mean stays on 0.50 within noise, exactly the neutral behaviour Hardy–Weinberg describes, in the only form this model can express it. One honest qualification: the wander is smaller than a Wright–Fisher population of 16 would show, because the pond does not stay at 16 — it grows toward the carrying-capacity cap (~180), and a growing population freezes its founder ratios in. Drift in this terrarium is strongest exactly where the page says it is: in the bottleneck preset, after a crash, before the regrowth.

2 · Genetic drift: variance matches haploid theory — and is 2× the diploid formula

To measure drift cleanly, the audit drove the shipped inheritance function (mutateGenes at σ = 0, verified below to return exact clones) inside a standard Wright–Fisher wrapper: each generation, N offspring each copy a randomly drawn parent. 300 replicate populations, 20 generations, at two sizes:

Population NVar(Δp) per generation, measuredHaploid theory pq/NDiploid theory pq/(2N)Var(p) after 20 gens, measuredFixed or lost by gen 20
200.012080.012500.006250.175 (haploid theory 0.160)55% of replicates
2000.001230.001250.000630.026 (haploid theory 0.024)0% of replicates

Drift is real, correctly sized, and correctly N-dependent — variance falls tenfold as N rises tenfold, and at N = 20 a neutral marker starting at p = 0.5 is already fixed or lost in over half of all populations within 20 generations. But note which theory it matches: the haploid pq/N, not the textbook diploid pq/(2N). That is not an error in the sim — an asexual clone has one genome copy, not two — but it means drift here runs twice as fast as the diploid formula a student would bring from a Hardy–Weinberg chapter. Starting the same test at p₀ = 0.8 (N = 50) gives the same story: mean p after 20 generations 0.804 (no directional pull), variance 0.0485 against haploid theory 0.0532.

3 · Mutation: not a rate — a Gaussian σ, and an honest one

Textbook mutation is a per-allele probability (u, v) with equilibrium p̂ ≈ v/(u+v). The terrarium has no such thing: its slider is labelled, accurately, "Mutation Rate (Gaussian σ)", and each birth adds a Gaussian step to every trait. The audit sampled mutateGenes 20,000 times from a mid-range parent at the default σ = 0.12:

  • Zero mean, correct spread, everywhere. The underlying randGaussian itself measures mean 0.002, SD 0.997 over 20,000 draws. At σ = 0, mutateGenes returned the parent genome unchanged in 1,000 of 1,000 trials — mutation truly off means clones.
  • The one bend: the clamps push back. Traits are clamped to their ranges after mutation, so a parent near a boundary produces asymmetric steps: a founder at diet 0.02 gains on average +0.0124 per birth, and one at diet 0.98 loses −0.0132; armor at 0.02 gains +0.0145, size at 0.50 gains +0.0132. Near the edges, "mutation" quietly becomes a restoring force pulling traits toward mid-range — a mean-reversion no Gaussian is supposed to have, and the closest thing this model has to a mutation equilibrium: not v/(u+v), but the balance point where the clamps stop pushing.

4 · Selection: no fitness numbers at all — and it still selects, in both directions

There is no fitness variable, no selection coefficient, and no normalisation by mean fitness anywhere in the shipped code. Fitness here is entirely emergent: an organism reproduces when its energy crosses ~84% of its maximum (a crowding factor can raise that to 98.5%), whereupon mitosis splits its energy 47 / 47 between parent and daughter. Traits help or hurt only through the energy ledger — food found, bites taken, metabolic burn paid. The audit tested whether that ledger actually selects, using two lineages identical except for sense radius (225 px vs 70 px), mutation off, starting at 50 / 50:

EnvironmentHigh-sense lineage after ~5–6 generationsReplicates
Abundant food (regrowth 3.0×)0.50 → 0.208 (range 0.183–0.218)6 ponds, 90 sim-s each
Scarce food (regrowth 0.30×)0.50 → ≈0.75 (range 0.54–0.86)5 ponds, 120 sim-s each

Selection is real, strong — and its direction is set by the environment, exactly as it should be. Sense is not free: the measured burn of a test organism rises from 2.327 energy/s at sense 45 to 3.857 energy/s at sense 225 (the code charges 0.0085 per pixel per second). In a rich pond, where even a near-blind cell bumps into food, the wide-eyed lineage pays for vision it does not need and is out-bred two-to-one within six generations. In a scarce pond the same eyes find food the others never see, and the same lineage takes three-quarters of the pond. That flip — one trait, opposite fates, environment deciding — is the most genuinely Darwinian thing in the exhibit, and it is nowhere advertised on the page.

5 · Speciation & the asteroid: two claims that check out

  • Speciation threshold — real, and slow at default settings. A daughter whose geneticDistance from her species' founder exceeds 0.44 is registered as a new species. Measured per-birth distance at σ = 0.12 is 0.111 ± 0.035, and 0 of 10,000 single births crossed 0.44 — speciation here is cumulative drift along a lineage, taking a median of 14 generations (mean 15.9) of mutation steps to cross. At the Cambrian preset's σ = 0.30, per-birth distance rises to 0.279 and 5.0% of births branch immediately — the "adaptive radiation" preset earns its name.
  • Asteroid "−80%" — measured 80.3%. Calling the shipped triggerAsteroidBottleneck on the balanced pond (66 organisms) left 13 survivors: survivors are set to max(6, round(0.20 · N)) by an unbiased shuffle, so the kill fraction is exact by construction, and it also wipes roughly half the algae (52% survive a coin flip). The survivors are a genuinely random sample — a proper drift bottleneck, not a fitness test.

Where it bends

Flagged plainly — one printed claim is false: the "cubic" metabolism. The Genome Key states that larger cells pay a "steep cubic-like metabolic cost". The shipped basal burn is 1.65 · size1.55 — and the audit's measurement says the code, not the caption, is what runs: a lone organism with no food measured 1.240, 2.327 and 5.508 energy/s at sizes 0.5, 1.0 and 2.0. The difference ratio is 2.928 — exactly the size1.55 prediction; a cubic cost would give 8.000. Large cells do pay more, roughly size-and-a-half power — steep, but not cubic, and a student quoting "cubic" from this page would be quoting the caption against the sim.

"Lotka–Volterra food web waves" — one boom and a bust, not waves. In a seeded run of the Balanced Pond preset, herbivores boomed 44 → 76 within 5 simulated seconds, predators lagged behind to a peak of 45 — and then herbivores collapsed to zero by ~70 s and stayed extinct for the remaining 165 s of the run while the pond settled at 10–25 mostly omnivorous organisms. Real Lotka–Volterra cycles repeat; this run produced a single overshoot and a local extinction. Individual ponds will differ, but nothing in the shipped dynamics guarantees the repeating waves the sparkline header promises.

"Gene Flow restored" — there is no gene flow to restore. Lowering the speciation barrier shows that banner, but in an asexual mitosis model genes never flow between lineages at all. What the barrier's removal restores is migration and mixing — the populations can again eat, hunt and compete across the line. The allopatric split itself is honestly built (a hard wall, 78% of algae spawning west, measured divergence tracking in the drawer); only the vocabulary overreaches the mechanism.

The "Diversity %" readout is an invented composite. It is computed as clamp(traitStd · 2.45 + hueDispersion · 0.45) — defensible as a dashboard gauge, but the weights 2.45 and 0.45 are chosen, not derived, and the number is not heterozygosity, Shannon diversity, or any standard index. Read it as "spread, roughly", never as a population-genetics statistic.

Drift runs at haploid speed (§2): twice the diploid pq/(2N) rate a textbook leads you to expect. Correct for a clonal model; worth knowing before comparing its bottleneck preset against textbook bottleneck examples.

Mutation near the trait boundaries is not neutral noise (§3): the range clamps add up to +0.012 to +0.015 per birth of inward push at the edges, so extreme traits quietly regress toward the middle even with selection off.
