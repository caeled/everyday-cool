ATP & Photosynthesis — the lesson

The exhibit is a chemiosmosis machine with two faces: a chloroplast thylakoid running the light reactions of photosynthesis, and a mitochondrial inner membrane running oxidative phosphorylation. In both, electrons fall downhill through protein complexes, protons are pumped uphill, and the protons' fall back through ATP synthase — a real rotary turbine — is what actually makes ATP. That central idea is taught correctly, and its modern P/O ratios (2.5 ATP per NADH, 1.5 per FADH2) are the current ones, not the retired textbook numbers. This page is the other half: what the exhibit actually claims, what driving its own code measured, and where the accounting does not reconcile. Verdict up top: the proton bookkeeping sums correctly — the ATP bookkeeping does not, because the page quietly uses two different prices for one ATP.

False and missing — flagged prominently

FALSE in effect — DNP does not collapse ATP production. The DNP preset promises "Proton Gradient Dissipated as Heat," and the heat readout does saturate (measured heat level 1.00). But driven for 60 simulated seconds, the DNP run still forged 273 ATP (4.55 ATP/s), against 382 (6.37 ATP/s) for plain aerobic respiration — about 71% of normal output, in steady 46–47 ATP slices every 10 s, with the proton pool parked at 77.6 and the rotor at 389 RPM. A real uncoupler collapses ATP synthesis toward zero while respiration races; here DNP is modelled as extra leak that the boosted electron flow mostly out-pumps. The glow is right; the ATP collapse the label implies never happens.

FALSE display — the mitochondrial intermembrane space at pH 4.45. The engine gives both organelles the same ΔpH scale (up to 3.35). Saturated in mitochondria mode it displays matrix pH 7.80 against an IMS pH of 4.45, with a proton-motive force of 234 mV. A real mitochondrion holds most of its PMF as membrane voltage (Δψ), not ΔpH: its ΔpH is roughly 0.5–1 unit and the IMS sits near pH 6.8–7.0. A ΔpH of 3.35 is a chloroplast-sized gradient worn by the wrong organelle.

MISSING — the headline equations and yields this topic is usually judged by are not in the exhibit at all. There is no overall photosynthesis equation (6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2) anywhere in the page, no Calvin-cycle stage (the Calvin cycle appears only as a zone label, "CALVIN CYCLE FUEL ZONE," and one sentence saying NADPH is "required for carbon fixation"), no CO2 fixation, no glucose, no glycolysis or Krebs cycle, and therefore no per-glucose ATP yield at all — neither the modern ~30–32 nor the old 36–38. Photorespiration, C4 and CAM are likewise absent. The exhibit teaches the membrane half of photosynthesis and respiration, honestly and well; the carbon half is simply not built. Do not cite it for the missing half.

What this is

  • Chloroplast mode — the Z-scheme. Photosystem II splits water (O2 out, protons into the lumen), plastoquinone ferries electrons to cytochrome b6f, which pumps more protons via the Q-cycle, and Photosystem I re-boosts the electrons so ferredoxin–NADP+ reductase can make NADPH. Light-dependent stage only: it consumes H2O and light, and produces O2, NADPH and a proton gradient.
  • Mitochondria mode — oxidative phosphorylation. Complex I takes NADH, Complex II takes FADH2 (and pumps nothing — the page says so, correctly), Complex III runs its Q-cycle, and Complex IV reduces O2 to water. Cyanide blocks Complex IV; DNP uncouples. This is the electron-transport stage of respiration only — glycolysis and the Krebs cycle that supply the NADH/FADH2 are upstream and off-stage.
  • The shared turbine. In both modes the gradient spins one ATP synthase. The exhibit's counter adds exactly 1 ATP per 120° of rotor turn — three per revolution, matching the three catalytic β-subunits it describes.

The accounting mismatch — two prices for one ATP

Inspector, both modes: ADP + Pi + ~3.3 H+ → ATP + H2O The price printed on the turbine itself. At that price, the page's own proton tallies buy 10 / 3.3 = 3.03 ATP per NADH and 12 / 3.3 = 3.64 ATP per O2 in the chloroplast.
Summary panel: 10 H+ → ~2.5 ATP · 6 H+ → ~1.5 ATP · 12 H+ → ~3 ATP Every one of those conversions prices ATP at exactly 4.0 H+ — the effective cost once ADP/ATP and phosphate transport across the mitochondrial membrane is included. That is the defensible price for the 2.5/1.5 headline. It is just not the price printed on the turbine.

So the stage subtotals sum, but the conversion factor changes mid-page: 3.3 H+/ATP in the inspector, 4.0 H+/ATP implied in the summaries — a 21% discrepancy in the exhibit's own accounting, worth about half an ATP per NADH. Neither figure is absurd on its own (real c-rings differ: the mammalian synthase's 8-subunit ring implies ~2.7 H+ per ATP before transport costs; the spinach chloroplast's 14-subunit ring implies ~4.7, which would make linear electron flow yield ~2.6 ATP per 2 NADPH — the shortfall the Calvin cycle's ATP demand is famous for). But a page cannot spend both prices at once, and this one never says it is doing so.

What's true — the credit side

  • Modern yields, not museum yields. 2.5 ATP/NADH and 1.5 ATP/FADH2, Complex II pumping zero, the Q-cycle doing real work in both organelles — all current.
  • Stage order and direction are right. Light reactions before (and feeding) carbon fixation; electron flow I → III → IV with II entering sideways at the CoQ pool; protons pumped stroma→lumen and matrix→IMS, and ATP made on the stroma/matrix side as they return.
  • Cyanide and darkness behave. Both measured collapses above are the real physiology: a blocked chain or a dark chloroplast can only spend the gradient already stored, and then it stops.
  • The PMF arithmetic uses a real constant. Its 61.5 mV per pH unit is 2.303·RT/F at body temperature (37 °C) — not a made-up scale factor.
  • The turbine is honest mechanics. 1 ATP per 120° step, three per turn, Open/Loose/Tight β-subunit cycling — that is the binding-change mechanism as established.

Bends named honestly

The ATP counter counts rotor steps, not chemistry. In the code, ATP rate is the displayed rotor speed × 0.24 (a legibility slow-down) × 3 ATP per turn ÷ 60 — which is why peak chloroplast (22.1 e⁻/s) and peak mitochondria (7.4 e⁻/s) both land at ≈6.3 ATP/s, and why the counter can never testify about stoichiometry. The ATP/s number is a tachometer reading, not a yield. There is no per-glucose, per-O2 or per-NADH tally anywhere to sum.

One ΔpH scale for two different organelles. ΔpH is derived as proton-pool × 3.35 in both modes, and the PMF then adds a second pool term on top (8 mV chloroplast, 28 mV mitochondria) — a Δψ-flavoured patch that double-counts the same pool. It lands near plausible totals (214 / 234 mV) by construction, while producing the impossible IMS pH flagged above. Even the chloroplast's saturated lumen pH of 4.65 is at the very low edge of reported values.

Substrate floors. Water and oxygen enter the flux formulas as (0.12 + 0.88 × slider) and (0.05 + 0.95 × slider): at a flat 0% the machine still runs at 5–12% — the source of the water-free photosynthesis measured above.

Green light, solution vs leaf. The 6.4% absorption at 532 nm is roughly true of chlorophyll in a test tube. A real leaf absorbs most green light too (commonly ~70% or more) — it looks green because green is absorbed least, not barely at all. "Chlorophyll reflects green" is the textbook simplification, and the exhibit's dramatic red-vs-green contrast (6.28 vs 0.42 ATP/s) inherits it.

Rotor speed is theatre-scaled. The 540 RPM cap and the 0.24× visual slow-down are display choices the code comments admit to; treat RPM as a relative gauge.

No leaks in the biology beyond the slider. No photorespiration, no cyclic electron flow around PSI (the chloroplast's real fix for its ATP/NADPH shortfall), no alternative oxidase, no maintenance costs. The membrane is the whole world of this exhibit — which is exactly why the carbon half flagged at the top is missing rather than wrong.
