Mitosis, Meiosis & Genetics — the lesson

The exhibit has three rooms sharing one cell. On the division stage, a 2n = 6 cell (or 2n = 4, your choice) walks the full mitosis timeline, or the two-division meiosis timeline with crossing-over, independent assortment and an optional nondisjunction error. Beside it sit a Punnett Cross Studio (monohybrid and dihybrid, any of the standard parental genotypes) and a Hardy–Weinberg population lab with selection, drift and mutation sliders. This is the eighth and final lab of the Biology Wave, and it is the most quantitatively honest of the set: the audit drove its own division, cross and population functions headlessly, in bulk, and the famous textbook trap — confusing sister chromatids with chromosomes — is one this page does not fall into. Verdict up top: the chromosome bookkeeping is correct at every phase, the Punnett ratios land on Mendel's numbers within sampling noise, and the Hardy–Weinberg engine reproduces the theory's closed-form answers essentially exactly. One label on one button is flatly false, and it is flagged below.

False claims — flagged prominently

FALSE — the mode button reads “Meiosis (2n→4n)”. Meiosis does not produce a tetraploid (4n) anything. It produces four cells, each haploid (n) — and the rest of the page knows it: the meiosis preset banner says “2n = 6 → 4 unique gametes,” the final phase is named “Telophase II & 4 Haploid Gametes,” the inspector badge reads “4 × n (3),” and the audit's drive of computeOutcomeCells() confirms four gametes of 3 chromosomes each at 2n = 6. The button has confused “4 cells” with “4n.” Read it as 2n → 4 × n; everything the button switches you to behaves that way.

MISLABELLED — the nondisjunction gametes are tagged “Trisomy (n+1 = 4)”. The counts are right (below), but the word is wrong for the object it is attached to. A gamete carrying two copies of chromosome 1 is a disomic (n+1) gamete; trisomy is the condition of the diploid zygote that results if that gamete is fertilised by a normal one. The same shorthand appears in the meiosis preset (“Jammed fiber → Trisomy”). In mitosis mode, by contrast, the label is used correctly: the daughter cell itself is diploid, and 2n+1 really is a trisomic cell.

What this is

  • The division stage is a phase-scrubbed animation, but its telemetry is computed, not decorative: ploidy, chromosomes per cell, chromatids, chiasmata count, gamete combinations (2n) and cell count are all rewritten from the timeline position every frame, and a separate outcome function, computeOutcomeCells(), builds the actual daughter cells or gametes — chromatid by chromatid, with maternal/paternal segment origins — that the Inspect tab lists. That function is what the audit drove.
  • The Punnett studio is a deterministic grid, not a sampler: it lists each parent's gametes, combines every pair, and counts phenotypes and genotypes over the grid. To test it the way the task demands — statistically — the audit sampled offspring by drawing gametes at random from the exhibit's own gamete lists, thousands at a time, and compared the tallies with Mendel's ratios and with an independent enumeration.
  • The Hardy–Weinberg lab is a genuine Wright–Fisher simulator: each generation applies selection against aa, then one-way mutation A → a, then (for finite N) binomial sampling of 2N alleles. That ordering and those formulas have exact theoretical predictions to check against, and the audit checked them.
  • What is not offered: no incomplete dominance, no codominance, and no sex-linked inheritance anywhere in the page — phenotypeInfo() implements complete dominance only. A testcross is available, but only as an ordinary genotype choice (Aa × aa, AaBb × aabb), never named as one. Those are absences, not errors; they are recorded here so nobody credits the lab with modes it does not have.

How the audit measured it

The shipped page's script was extracted and run in Node with the DOM and canvas stubbed out and Math.random replaced by a seeded generator (mulberry32). The exhibit's own computeOutcomeCells(), buildPairChromatids(), gametesFromGenotype(), combineGametes(), phenotypeInfo(), stepHWGeneration(), sampleBinomial(), getCurrentPhase() and applyPreset() were called directly; the telemetry readouts were captured by driving updateTelemetryAndUI() at sampled timeline positions in both modes, at both chromosome counts, with and without nondisjunction. Every preset the page ships was applied. Nothing in the guest file was edited.

Division bookkeeping, phase by phase

  • Mitosis holds 2n the whole way. Sampled at nine timeline positions, the readout never leaves 2n = 6 (2n = 4 in the two-pair setting): 6 chromosomes and 12 chromatids through metaphase, “12 (sep)” while the separated sisters are still in one cell, then 6 chromosomes and 6 chromatids per cell in two cells. That is exactly the convention textbooks insist on — a replicated chromosome is one chromosome with two chromatids, and a separated chromatid becomes a chromosome — and the page applies it consistently in both the telemetry and the outcome function.
  • Meiosis halves once, at the right place. The ploidy readout stays 2n through Metaphase I and Anaphase I, drops to n = 3 with 3 chromosomes but still 6 chromatids per cell after the first cytokinesis (the reduction is in chromosome number, not yet in chromatid number), and ends at 3 chromosomes / 3 chromatids per gamete after the second division. The Prophase II note correctly states there is no S phase between the divisions (“Interkinesis (No S-phase)”).
  • The checkpoint and spindle claims are real biology, correctly placed. The G₂ note invokes the replication checkpoint at 4C; the Metaphase note puts the spindle assembly checkpoint (Mad2, APC/C, amphitelic attachment) before anaphase; the Anaphase note has separase cleaving cohesin, with Anaphase A and B distinguished; the Meiosis I note protects centromeric cohesin with shugoshin so homologs, not sisters, separate. None of these is simulated as a mechanism — they are annotations — but every annotation the audit checked is accurate and attached to the right phase.
  • All six presets do what their labels say when driven through applyPreset(): Mitosis Clonal (mitosis, 2n = 6, no error), Meiosis Crossing-Over (all three pairs crossing over), Assortment Shuffle (assortment and Meiosis II flips re-randomised), Spindle Nondisjunction (error armed, meiosis mode), Hardy–Weinberg Eq. (p₀ = 0.60, s = 0, N = ∞, μ = 0) and Recessive Selection (p₀ = 0.22, s = 0.65, N = 2,000, μ = 0.001).

What's true — the credit side

  • The chromatid/chromosome distinction — the single most-mistaught point in this unit — is right everywhere it is tested: telemetry, phase notes, inspector and outcome cells all agree that replication doubles chromatids and DNA, not chromosome number.
  • The Punnett engine is exact, including the case most implementations get wrong. Keeping duplicate gametes in the list ([AB, Ab, AB, Ab] for AABb) makes the naive-looking grid a correctly weighted one for every one of the 3 monohybrid and 9 dihybrid genotypes offered, in every pairing — the audit enumerated all of them and sampled the headline crosses at N = 20,000.
  • Crossing-over is modelled at the right granularity: one chiasma per pair swaps the distal segment of exactly two non-sister chromatids, so half the gametes are recombinant at that pair — and because the locus shown sits distal to the chiasma, the recombinant chromatids genuinely carry the other parent's allele.
  • The Hardy–Weinberg lab is not a cartoon. Its selection step is the textbook recursion (p′ = (p² + pq) / w̄), its mutation step is exact, and its drift is a true Wright–Fisher binomial draw whose measured variance matches theory. Two of its predictions matched closed-form theory to four decimal places.
  • Nondisjunction arithmetic is right in both divisions — 7/5 in mitosis, 4/4/2/2 gametes in meiosis I — including the detail that in meiosis the two n−1 gametes are missing chromosome 1 entirely, which the inspector shows as an empty strip.

Bends named honestly

DNA content is asserted, never shown. There is no G₁ or S phase on either timeline and no C-value in the telemetry, so the 2C → 4C doubling and its halving at cytokinesis — a third of the standard bookkeeping trio — exists only as the (correct) “4C DNA content” phrase in the G₂ note. A learner can finish this lab able to count chromosomes and chromatids, and never have seen DNA content change.

The telemetry flips on thresholds, not on phase boundaries. The counts are computed from fixed timeline cut-points that sit slightly inside the named phases: mitosis registers its split at t = 0.94, well inside Cytokinesis (0.85–1.00); meiosis registers the Meiosis I reduction at t = 0.48, inside Cytokinesis I (0.39–0.50), and the final gamete counts at t = 0.955. A related wrinkle: through telophase the mitosis chromosome count is shown as a whole-cell total (“12 (sep)”) rather than 6 per forming nucleus, and in the mitosis nondisjunction ending the chromatid readout still says “6 / cell” for both daughters while the chromosome readout correctly says 7 / 5.

One chiasma per pair, one locus per chromosome. Each pair can cross over at most once, and each chromosome carries a single shown locus, distal to the chiasma. A consequence the “2³ = 8 (+XO)” readout glosses: at these three loci the allele haplotype pool is still exactly 8 — crossing-over here creates new segment-origin patterns (64 distinct ★-annotated haplotype strings in the audit's full enumeration), not new allele combinations. Real recombination's extra diversity needs at least two loci on the same chromosome, which this model doesn't carry.

Complete dominance is the only genetics on offer. No incomplete dominance or codominance (which would make the monohybrid phenotypic ratio 1:2:1), no sex-linkage, no linked genes — the two dihybrid loci always assort independently, by construction of the gamete lists. The lab teaches Mendel's two laws correctly and stops exactly there.

“Seed from Meiosis” builds its parents backwards, arbitrarily. It pairs gamete 1 with gamete 3 to fabricate Parent 1 and gamete 2 with gamete 4 for Parent 2. Driven at the audit's settings it seeded a monohybrid cross of AA × aa — two homozygotes whose cross cannot segregate at all — out of a meiosis whose actual parent was heterozygous at every locus, and a dihybrid cross of AABb × aaBb. The seeded cross is a legal cross; it is just not the cross the meiosis on stage came from.

Hardy–Weinberg simplifications, all standard but worth naming: selection acts only against the recessive homozygote (heterozygotes are exactly as fit as AA — no overdominance, no selection on Aa), mutation runs one way only (A → a, never back), the N = 2,000 drift step uses a normal approximation to the binomial (its measured behaviour was still on theory), and the run stops at 180 generations whether or not anything has finished evolving.

Small display lags: with crossing-over switched on, the chiasmata counter reads 0 until the timeline passes t = 0.10 — the swaps are presented as an event mid-Prophase I rather than a state of the cell — and prophase and prometaphase are merged into a single mitosis phase, so nuclear-envelope breakdown shares a stage with condensation.
