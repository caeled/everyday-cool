Lenia Automata Zoo — the lesson

The exhibit is four cellular-automaton engines in one arena — Conway's Game of Life, Brian's Brain, Wireworld, and the headline act: a continuous Lenia zoo on a 256×256 grid, with an FFT convolution, a ring kernel, a Gaussian growth function, and a bestiary headed by Orbium, Bert Wang-Chak Chan's famous swimming soliton. The audit drove the shipped engine headlessly and checked it against Chan's canonical rule (2018–2020). Verdict up top: this is the real Lenia. The update rule, the growth Gaussian and the normalised ring kernel all match the canonical formulas to float precision, and Orbium genuinely glides at a constant velocity for as long as you care to watch. But the zoo's labels are another matter: the Geminium Twin — a named creature — dissolves in about 30 steps under every parameter set the page offers it, the “Hydro Swimmer” never swims, and Orbium only lives because the page quietly runs it at σ = 0.017, not the σ = 0.015 its own code comment calls canonical — at 0.015, the creature dies.

False claims — flagged prominently

FALSE — the Geminium Twin does not orbit, divide, or even live. The stamp is sold as a “Dual-core cell,” and the Geminium preset promises “Twin colonies & interacting Orbium pairs that split, orbit, and pulse.” Stamped alone and driven with the exhibit's own engine, the twin's biomass collapses from 161.4 to 15.2 in 25 steps and reaches zero at step ≈ 31 — under the Geminium preset's own parameters (μ = 0.146) and again under the default μ = 0.150 (dead at step ≈ 30). It never splits, orbits or pulses; it evaporates. The preset tells the same story in aggregate: its biomass falls from 529 to ≈ 226 within 100 steps and then holds flat for 500 more — 226 is, to within a unit, exactly three Orbiums' worth of biomass (≈ 76 each). Everything that survives in the “Geminium” preset is an Orbium; both twins are gone in the first half-minute of simulated time.

FALSE — Orbium is not run at the canonical σ = 0.015 the page cites. The code comment over the creature matrix reads “Authentic Orbium unicaudatus … (R=13, T=10, mu=0.15, sigma=0.015)”, but the engine's default, the slider's default and every Orbium preset actually set σ = 0.017. That substitution is load-bearing: driven at σ = 0.015, the shipped matrix swells briefly (68.8 → 77.9), crashes to 52.7 by step 50 and is extinct at step 63. A σ scan in this engine finds a narrow survival window — σ = 0.016, 0.017 and 0.018 live; 0.013 dies at step 23, 0.014 at step 80, 0.020 at step 72, and 0.022 doesn't die at all, it explodes (biomass 12,405 by step 300). So the page's Orbium is real and genuinely gliding, but it is a retuned Orbium: the parameter printed as canonical would kill it here, whether because the transcribed matrix is approximate or because the discretised kernel shifts the window. The honest label is “σ = 0.017, tuned until it swam.”

FALSE — the “Hydro Swimmer” does not swim. Stamped alone under the Hydro preset's own parameters (R = 15, μ = 0.255, σ = 0.036, two-ring kernel), the creature persists beautifully — and goes nowhere: over 800 steps its centroid stays within 0.3 cells of where it started while its biomass breathes between 128 and 164. It is a stationary pulsing oscillator wearing a swimmer's name. (Its stablemate the Ring Soliton does eventually wander — see the creature table — at roughly a sixth of Orbium's speed, and only after sitting still for ~200 steps.)

What this is

  • Lenia (Chan, 2018–2020) generalises Conway's Life by making everything continuous: cell states are real numbers in [0, 1] instead of dead/alive, time advances in small steps dt instead of generations, and the neighbourhood count is replaced by a smooth ring-shaped kernel K convolved with the whole field. The exhibit implements exactly that, in a single channel with one kernel — standard Lenia, not the multi-channel “extended Lenia” variant. The Hydro preset's two-peaked kernel (peaks [1, 0.5]) is still plain Lenia: Chan's own formulation allows multi-peaked kernels.
  • The other three engines (Life with editable B/S rules, 3-state Brian's Brain, 4-state Wireworld) are discrete automata sharing the arena, the brushes and the telemetry. They are context for the comparison below; the deep audit here is the Lenia engine, which is the exhibit's headline claim.
  • The canonical reference is Chan's rule: A(t+dt) = clip(A + dt·G(K∗A), 0, 1), with G(u) = 2·exp(−(u−μ)²/(2σ²)) − 1 and K normalised to sum 1 — for Orbium, R = 13, μ = 0.15, σ = 0.015, dt = 0.1 (T = 10) in the original parameterisation. The test below is whether the shipped code's structure is that rule and whether its creatures do what their labels say.
A(t+dt) = clip( A + dt · G(K ∗ A), 0, 1 ),   G(u) = 2·exp(−(u−μ)²/(2σ²)) − 1 The canonical Lenia update. K ∗ A is the convolution of the ring kernel with the field; G rewards neighbourhood densities near μ and punishes everything else; clip keeps states in [0, 1].

How the audit measured it

The shipped page's script was extracted and run in Node with the DOM and canvas stubbed out; nothing in the guest file was edited. The engine's own FFT2D and LeniaEngine classes were driven directly: (1) the stored kernel spectrum was inverse-transformed back to a spatial kernel and summed; (2) uniform fields were stepped once to read the growth function back out of the update; (3) a random blobby field was stepped once by the engine and once by an independent direct (non-FFT) circular convolution plus the canonical formula, and the two results compared cell by cell; (4) every Lenia stamp was seeded alone at the centre of an empty 256×256 arena and run for up to 800 steps, tracking biomass, active-cell count and toroidal centroid; (5) all four Lenia presets were run for 600 steps (Hydro to 1,200), and Orbium was re-run across a σ scan. One engine step took ≈ 14 ms in Node on this machine, so these runs are thousands of real steps, not spot checks.

What's true — the credit side

  • The mathematics is not a costume. Many “Lenia” demos on the web are a smoothed threshold rule or a reaction–diffusion wearing the name. This one is the actual algorithm: Chan's bump-function ring kernel, normalised to sum 1; the Gaussian growth function with the advertised μ and σ, verified point by point to 3×10⁻⁷; the Euler update with clipping; and a genuine radix-2 FFT doing the convolution, verified against a brute-force convolution. The rule structure is Lenia's, full stop.
  • Orbium really swims. A constant-velocity glider is the hardest thing to fake in a continuous automaton — most seeds pulse, crawl or dissolve. This one holds its biomass within ±0.5 and its velocity within measurement noise for 800 steps, wrapping the torus and continuing. Whatever retuning it took to get σ = 0.017, the result is a true soliton of the rule.
  • The two-ring Hydro kernel is legitimate Lenia, correctly built — two shells at the right radii, the second at half height, an exact node between them, and the whole thing still normalised. Its creatures persist for hundreds of steps under it.
  • The Life comparison is honest. Putting binary, generation-stepped Life beside continuous-valued, dt-stepped Lenia in one arena is exactly the right way to show what Chan changed — and the page never claims Lenia's space is continuous, which it isn't, here or in the original.

Bends named honestly

σ = 0.017 is a retune, and the window is narrow. Beyond the false comment, the practical bend: Orbium's survival window in this engine is roughly σ ∈ [0.016, 0.018]. The slider runs from 0.008 to 0.065, so most of the slider's travel is creature-killing territory — drag σ a little and the zoo's star dies (0.020) or the field explodes (0.022). That sensitivity is true to real Lenia, where creatures live in thin parameter islands, but a visitor should know the dial is loaded.

The non-Orbium “creatures” are procedural inventions, not Chan's bestiary. The twin, hydro and soliton seeds are generated by Gaussian-blob formulas in the code (buildTwinCreature, buildRingCreature, buildSolitonRing), not transcribed organism data like the Orbium matrix. Real Lenia species (Hydrogeminium, Geminium) exist in Chan's taxonomy, but these seeds are original constructions borrowing the names — which is why one of them (the twin) turns out not to be a viable organism at all, and another (hydro) behaves as an oscillator rather than the swimmer its namesake is.

“60 fps” is a design target, not an audit result. The page claims the FFT computes the convolution “at 60fps”, and the loop does run one step per frame at 1×. Headlessly, one engine step (no rendering) took ≈ 14 ms in Node on a shared VM — consistent with 60 steps/s being plausible on a desktop browser, but this audit did not and cannot verify the rendered frame rate. Relatedly, the 4× speed setting is capped at 2 Lenia steps per frame by the loop, so at 60 fps “4×” delivers at most 2× the steps.

Lenia's edges always wrap, whatever the toggle says. The “Toroidal Wrap Edges” switch is wired to the three discrete engines; the Lenia engine has no wrap setting at all — an FFT convolution is periodic by construction, so the Lenia arena is a torus permanently. Harmless, but the toggle's silence about it could mislead.

The “Generation” readout for Lenia is actually continuous time. The telemetry shows time (steps × dt, so 10 steps = 1.0) under the Generation label. Arguably the right number for a continuous automaton — just not a generation count.

Everything is float32. The field, the kernel and the FFT are all single-precision. The measured agreement with the canonical formulas (≈ 3×10⁻⁷) is exactly float32 noise, so nothing is lost at this scale — but long-run creature behaviour can drift from a float64 reference Lenia, one more reason the σ window here need not match Chan's exactly.
