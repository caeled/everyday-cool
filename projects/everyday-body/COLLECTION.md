# Collection origin

A workshop built by **ChatGPT** (OpenAI) at Steven Powell's direction, added to the collection 18 November 2026 as its twenty-first room. Steven proposed the room in the Everyday Cool ideas side chat that day and shared ChatGPT's full proposal there: an interactive anatomical explorer — Whole Body → Organ System → Organ → Region/Structure → Function — with the brain given a dedicated sub-explorer, standard information panels for the other organs, curated educational sources (OpenStax, NCBI, Wikipedia, NINDS), and a "How Everything Connects" scenario mode, explicitly framed as connecting to the collection's physics, chemistry, and robotics workshops rather than standing as a disconnected encyclopedia.

ChatGPT built the workshop as a single self-contained HTML page and committed it directly to this repository as `body/index.html` (commit `e1a9613`, "Add Everyday Cool Body interactive anatomy workshop v1"). Muse integrated it into the collection the same day: the page moved unchanged in substance to `projects/everyday-body/index.html`, and the root `body/` folder was removed. This folder is now the maintained original.

Integration changes made by Muse, and nothing else: the page `<title>` and header/footer branding aligned to the house style ("Everyday Body · Everyday Cool" — ChatGPT's H1, "Your body is a universe you carry around," is untouched); a banner image added at the top of the page; a Help ▾ menu in the header and a Help section in the page, in the room's own visual idiom, documenting the atlas, the brain explorer, the scenarios, the quiz, the honest limits, and the offline answer; and the house files in this folder (README, this note, THIRD-PARTY, licenses, tests) plus catalog, hub, provenance, and ZIP wiring. At initial integration, ChatGPT's script was byte-identical to the committed original, and its content and visuals are otherwise exactly as delivered — notably, the page arrived with no hotlinked images at all (every diagram is inline SVG), which keeps the room's offline claim and the desktop app bundle honest, as Steven had flagged when the proposal leaned on external image URLs.

What v1 contains, against the proposal: twelve selectable organs (brain, heart, lungs, liver, stomach, pancreas, kidneys, intestines, urinary bladder, eyes, skeleton, muscles) across seven systems; the brain explorer with six tappable surface structures and all twelve proposed region topics; and the connections mode, which ships under the name "Connect the dots — One body. Many systems." with four scenarios (running to catch a bus — the proposal's "Run a Race" in everyday dress — eating an apple, hearing your name, cooling off on a hot day). Ears, skin, and reproductive organs appear within scenarios but are not selectable organs in v1; more scenarios and organs are natural later adds, the way The Radio Room grew.

The room carries its limits on its face: the atlas is a simplified schematic, not to scale; the deep brain structures are explored by labelled buttons because a side view cannot show their positions; descriptions are educational simplifications; and the workshop does not diagnose conditions or give medical advice.

Banner: `everyday-body-banner.jpg`, AI-generated watercolor — a friendly robot with goggles pushed up on its head, pointing to the glowing brain on a large anatomical atlas chart of the human body, heart and lungs lit in soft colour, in a warm lamplit study — generated 18 November 2026 as part of integrating this room, in the style and dimensions (2736×912) of the collection's other banners.

Documentation update, 20 November 2026: `index.html` (its CSS is inline), `README.md`, and `COLLECTION.md` gained a professional documentation treatment — a Source on GitHub link in the Help menu, a documentation lede and action bar in the Help section, matching styles, and a Documentation section in the README — at Steven Powell's direction ('the documentation section is the only part that doesn't look like a real program'). No bench, lesson, or behaviour changed.

The same documentation update also added a Steve's Profile link (Steven Powell's LinkedIn) at the foot of the Help section — `index.html` only.

## The Biology Wave · 23 November 2026

At Steven Powell's direction, five biology exhibits built by Google Antigravity (Gemini) — wave 1 of the five-wave challenge Steven set Antigravity — were integrated into this room byte-for-byte: `atp-photosynthesis.html` (BioPower), `dna-crispr-lab.html` (Helix), `heart-hemodynamics.html` (Cardio), `evolution-terrarium.html` (Darwin), and `physarum-ecosystem.html` (Physarum). No guest file was edited; sha256 hashes of the integrated copies match Steven's uploads exactly. Muse audited each by driving its shipped code headlessly and wrote the five lessons: `atp-photosynthesis-lesson.html`, `dna-crispr-lab-lesson.html`, `heart-hemodynamics-lesson.html`, `evolution-terrarium-lesson.html`, and `physarum-ecosystem-lesson.html`.

Headline audit results, printed in full in the lessons: BioPower's proton stoichiometry sums clean to modern P/O ratios but prices ATP two ways on one page and its DNP preset never collapses ATP; Helix's codon table is perfect (64/64) and its Cas9 cut is canonical, but its HDR preset guide is shifted one base and can never fire; Cardio's pressure model settles at 111/81 but its mitral valve never opens, so the resting heart decays to heart-failure numbers within seconds — broken as shipped, documented, not repaired (any fix is Steven's call); Darwin is a genuine asexual evolution sim whose drift matches haploid theory within 3%, with no Hardy–Weinberg machinery and a "cubic" cost that measures size^1.55; Physarum is a real Jones (2010) agent model that solves its maze at 0.99× optimal, while its Tokyo web measures 1.91× the minimum and never fully connects.

Wiring in this pass: `index.html` gained the Biology Wave block at the top of the room and a nav link; `README.md` gained a Biology Wave section; Dad's Arcade reached 112 games / 112 lessons; the Nerd Heaven shelf reached 97 lessons; Java Young Robot Builders back doors 92–96.
