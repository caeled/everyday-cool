Physarum Ecosystem — the lesson

A brainless slime mold that builds transport networks: tens of thousands of chemotactic agents foraging between oat flakes, carving veins around UV-light walls, re-creating the Tokyo rail experiment, solving a hex maze, and warring or braiding in two- and three-species modes. This page is the audit. Following the house rule, the shipped code was not just read: it was extracted and driven headlessly (Node, with DOM and canvas stubs) at its own full settings — all 32,000 agents, its own presets, its own telemetry — and measured: a two-food-source test weighed against the straight line, the settled network cut across by a UV wall to see if it re-routes, the Tokyo web skeletonised and compared to the minimum spanning tree of its own 18 cities, the maze timed against the true shortest navigable path, and the species modes watched for real ecosystem dynamics. The engine is genuine — one of the most honest in the collection. The headline comparison to railway engineering is not earned by what the sim actually settles into, and that is flagged in red below.

The verdict: a real Jones (2010) agent model that genuinely pathfinds and re-routes — but its Tokyo web is twice the minimum length and never fully connects, and the "ecosystem" has no populations in it

The engine is the real thing. This is not a drawn approximation, not a pre-computed graph, and not a minimum-spanning tree dressed in slime colours. It is Jeff Jones's 2010 agent model, faithfully implemented: each agent samples the trail field at three sensors (left, centre, right — 28° apart, 15 px ahead at the Tokyo settings), steers toward the strongest reading, moves, and deposits pheromone; the field then diffuses through a 3×3 Gaussian blur and decays (×0.945 per tick at the default). Networks emerge from that loop and nothing else — the audit verified there is no graph solver anywhere in the file.

It really does find near-short paths. Between two food sources 405 px apart, the route through the settled trail measured 449.7 px — 1.11× the straight line. In the hex maze, the trail route from Start Oat to Goal Oat measured 692.8 px against a true shortest navigable path of 700.7 px — the maze is solved, essentially at the optimum. And when a UV wall was painted across a settled two-node route, the network re-routed around it to within 1.03× of the best possible detour.

But the Tokyo claim is overclaimed as shipped. The page's biological note says the mold "solves Steiner shortest-path transport networks comparable to human railway engineering (Tero et al., Science 2010)," beside a preset named "Tokyo Rail Solver." Driven for 3,000 ticks, the web it settles into has a skeleton length of ~1.9× the minimum spanning tree of its own 18 cities — a loopy, spur-covered web, not a near-Steiner network — and by the page's own connectivity telemetry it never stably connects all 18 nodes: it peaked at 88% (16 of 18) and flickered between 65% and 88% for the whole run. The real 2010 experiment earned that comparison with a different mechanism (see §4); this sim borrows the headline without reproducing the result.

And the "ecosystem" is decorative. There are no populations to have dynamics: agent counts are fixed at spawn (assigned round-robin, i % speciesCount), and there is no birth, death, predation or consumption anywhere in the code. The species modes partition territory — measured in §5 — but nothing oscillates Lotka–Volterra-style, because nothing can be born or eaten.

1 · The engine, identified

sense (trail + 1.45·nutrient − 5.5·barrier) → steer → move → deposit 0.72 → diffuse (3×3, /16) → decay The shipped step, in its own constants: sensor angle 28°, sensor distance 15 px, turn 34°, speed 1.45 px/tick, trail decay 0.055 — the Jones (2010) parameter family, exposed on the page's own "Jones (2010) Sensor & Motor" panel. The label is honest.
  • What it is: a particle chemotaxis model. Positive feedback is real — agents follow trails agents laid — so veins genuinely self-organise, prune (by decay where traffic stops), and re-form. The audit's step timing at full settings: ~14 ms per tick for 32,000 agents on an 810×506 grid.
  • What it is not: the Tero flow model behind the actual Tokyo rail paper. Real Physarum network refinement — and Tero's equations — run on flow: tubes carrying more protoplasm thicken, tubes carrying less shrink. There are no tubes, no conductivities and no flux anywhere in this file. Pruning here is trail evaporation plus agents wandering off, which produces a different, looser kind of web (§3).
  • The scent is honestly local. Each oat projects a Gaussian chemoattractant field of radius ≈121 px (24% of the grid's short side). Measured midway between two nodes 405 px apart, the nutrient field is exactly 0.000 — the gradient does not secretly bridge the gap. The agents have to find each other with their own trails, and they do.
  • One helping hand: food nodes inject pheromone directly into the trail field every tick (0.24 × strength, into every species' channel), on top of the scent field. Real oats emit attractant; they do not lay the mold's own trail chemical for it, and they certainly don't lay it in a rival species' colour. A small, load-bearing cheat — it seeds the first veins.

So the shortest-path claim is true at the scale of a route and false at the scale of a network. A traveller following the veins goes nearly the shortest way; the system builds about twice as much vein as the minimum — which, to be fair, is also true of the real mold on a good day, and is exactly why the real Tokyo result was reported as a cost/efficiency/fault-tolerance trade-off, not a Steiner optimum. The page's note keeps the triumph and drops the trade-off.

3 · Pruning and re-routing, measured

Pruning is the signature Physarum behaviour: build too much, then withdraw from the routes that don't pay. The audit counted "ink" — grid cells above trail threshold — as two-node colonies settled:

RunEarly inkLate inkChangeShape of the curve
Two nodes, trail > 0.14226,680 cells @ tick 10072,900 @ tick 600−68%Fast early collapse, still slowly falling
Two nodes, long run, trail > 0.391,297 cells @ tick 30020,993 @ tick 2,400−77%Never fully stabilises inside 2,400 ticks — still thinning at the end

Pruning is real. The colony massively over-builds, then withdraws — the right shape, driven by decay where agents stop travelling. Two honest qualifications. First, it is slow and unfinished: even at tick 2,400 the deposit is still thinning, and only 20.4% of the remaining ink lies inside the ±15 px corridor around the direct line — what settles is a broad, narrowing fan, not the single clean tube of the textbook photographs. Second, the mechanism is evaporation, not economics: no route is withdrawn because another route carries more flow; routes fade because agents stopped wandering down them.

Re-routing is real, and it is the best result in this audit. After the two-node network settled (route 449.7 px), a UV wall 220 px long was painted straight across the corridor. The trail route lengthened to 549 px within 100 ticks and refined to 517.8 px by tick 1,000, against a best possible detour of 501.9 px — 1.03× optimal. Agents genuinely retreat from the barrier field (weight −5.5 in the sensor sum, plus a hard turn-away when the next step lands in strong UV), the old crossing evaporates, and the detour that replaces it is nearly the shortest one available. Blocked-path adaptation: demonstrated, not asserted. Food sources, by contrast, are never consumed — nodes emit forever at full strength — so removal adaptation is only testable by deleting a node, which simply ends its scent; there is no hunger in the model to adapt with.

4 · Tokyo, audited

  • The 18 cities check out. The preset ships exactly 18 nutrient nodes (Tokyo, Shinjuku, Shibuya, Ikebukuro, Ueno, Kawasaki, Yokohama, Kamakura, Odawara, Machida, Hachioji, Tachikawa, Kawagoe, Omiya, Tsukuba, Funabashi, Chiba, Narita), hand-placed in normalised dish coordinates that roughly mirror the real map, with a UV "Tokyo Bay" exclusion blob curves the veins around the water. As a stage set for the Tero experiment, it is carefully done.
  • The connection never completes. By the page's own telemetry (BFS through trail > 0.14), connectivity over a 3,000-tick run read: 88% at tick 300 (16 of 18 nodes), then 71%, 76%, 76%, 65%, 71%, 65%, 65%, 65%, 65% — a flickering two-thirds, not a solved network. The "Network Solved" status pill (≥85%) can only flash, never hold. A second run reached 82% (15 of 18) at tick 900 with the same flicker. Outlying cities — the audit's traces point at the far corners of the map — repeatedly lose their last faint vein to decay and are re-found later.
  • The telemetry itself is generous. The connectivity BFS steps 2 px at a time, seeds a 5×5 patch around each node, and accepts trail as faint as 0.14 — a forgiving yardstick, and it still won't say 100%. When a lenient referee won't call it solved, it isn't solved.
  • What Tero actually showed. In the 2010 Science paper, the mold's network scored comparably to the rail network on a combined cost/efficiency/robustness measure — similar total length to the railway, which is itself far longer than any minimum tree, because railways buy redundancy. This sim's web, at 1.91× the spanning-tree minimum with loops and spurs, is arguably in that spirit — but the page never makes that argument; it says "Steiner shortest-path," which is the one comparison the measured web clearly fails.

5 · The "ecosystem": territories, not populations

Mode (600 ticks each)Trail mass by species (sampled)What the numbers show
Territorial War (2 species, repel −0.9)Tick 100: 8,550 / 7,853 → tick 600: 16,221 / 17,579Shares drift from 52/48 to 48/52 — one slow crossing, then rough parity. Territory being carved, not a population cycle.
Symbiotic Braids (3 species, attract +0.55)Tick 100: 6,702 / 7,064 / 6,335 → tick 600: 2,088 / 2,112 / 2,105Three near-identical masses decaying in lockstep to near-equality — the opposite of out-of-phase oscillation.

Lotka–Volterra dynamics need the one thing this engine does not contain: change in population. Predator and prey cycle because prey reproduce and predators eat them. Here the agent count is a slider (15,000–45,000), assigned to species round-robin at spawn, and no agent is ever created or destroyed; food is never eaten; the "Species Interaction" slider only changes how much one species' sensors discount (or court) another's scent. What the modes genuinely produce is pattern: with repulsion, two colonies partition the dish into territories with a contested border — a real and pretty piece of self-organisation, honestly labelled "Territorial War" as a picture. As ecology, there is nothing alive in it to compete: no generations, no hunger, no death. Call it a territorial pattern generator and it is a good one; call it an ecosystem and the word is doing unpaid work.

Where it bends — the full list

"Steiner shortest-path… comparable to human railway engineering" is overclaimed for this sim. Measured: Tokyo web skeleton ≈1.91× the minimum spanning length, connectivity flickering at 65–88% and never holding all 18 nodes across 3,000 ticks. The sentence is roughly true of the real organism in the real 2010 paper (as a trade-off, not an optimum); placed beside a "Tokyo Rail Solver" preset, it promises a result the shipped preset does not reproduce.

It is Jones, not Tero. The network refinement the Tokyo paper is famous for is flow reinforcement — high-flux tubes thicken. This engine has no flow, tubes or conductivities; pruning is trail decay (0.055/tick) plus agent redistribution. Same family portrait, different mechanism, measurably looser networks.

Food nodes lay pheromone. Every tick, each node deposits trail (0.24 × strength) into all species' channels within a 5×5 patch, in addition to its scent field. It jump-starts colonisation, and in multi-species modes it feeds both sides equally at every oat — one reason "war" fronts stall at parity.

Food is never consumed and never runs out. Node strength is constant forever; there is no satiety, no depletion, and so no foraging economics — the colony's only scarcity is its own attention.

The ecosystem has no populations. Fixed agent counts, round-robin species assignment, no birth, death, predation or reproduction anywhere in the code. Species modes are territorial pattern generators; any predator–prey or population reading of them is decoration.

Connectivity % is a lenient, flickery gauge. 2 px BFS stride, 5×5 seeding patches, threshold 0.14 — and it still oscillates by ±20 points between telemetry samples on a settled dish, because faint veins perpetually cross and recross the threshold. Treat the number as weather, not as a score.

Pruning never quite finishes. Ink was still falling at tick 2,400 in the long two-node run, and the settled deposit is a broad fan (20.4% of ink inside the direct corridor), not a single tube. The veins you see are the bright core of a much wider deposit — the palette's gamma curve does the tidying-up visually.
