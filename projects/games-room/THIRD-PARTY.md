# Sources & limitations

## Bundled material

Stack-a-Bot is original code written for this workshop (MIT). The banner image is AI-generated art made for this room. No third-party code, fonts, images, or data are bundled.

## Linked shelves (not bundled, each under its own terms)

- The Internet Arcade and the MS-DOS Games Library — Internet Archive (archive.org), a nonprofit library. Games there are presented by the Archive for preservation and research.
- AARP Games — AARP (games.aarp.org)
- Lichess — lichess.org, a nonprofit, volunteer-built chess server; free and ad-free by design.
- Pogo — Electronic Arts (pogo.com)
- MSN Play — Microsoft (msn.com)
- New York Times Games — The New York Times (nytimes.com)
- Poki — Poki B.V. (poki.com)
- CrazyGames — CrazyGames (crazygames.com)
- Kongregate — Kongregate (kongregate.com)
- itch.io HTML5 games — itch.io, a marketplace for independent makers; individual games carry their own prices and licenses.

## Learning links

- MDN Web Docs (developer.mozilla.org) — the 2D breakout tutorial is CC-BY-SA content by Mozilla contributors.
- p5.js (p5js.org) and freeCodeCamp (freecodecamp.org) — free learning projects; linked, not copied.

## Limitations

Shelf descriptions (ads, accounts, subscriptions) were checked on 15 November 2026 and can change; each shelf's own pages are the authority. The Internet Archive shelves present classic games for preservation; availability of individual titles can change. Stack-a-Bot's robot-progress and scoring are teaching simplifications of the falling-block genre, stated as they are.
