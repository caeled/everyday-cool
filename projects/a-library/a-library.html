A Library

Places you can visit and read without stepping into a building. Books, wikis, knowledge bases, and archives. General shelves and odd ones. Each keeps its own rules.

A link and a label. Nothing here is a course, and nothing here replaces a librarian.

Books

  • Project GutenbergBooks free to read; public-domain status varies by country.
  • Standard EbooksThe same old books, carefully set.
  • Open LibraryA catalog of everything, and borrows when a copy is free.
  • Internet Archive textsScanned books, magazines, and the wayback of the web.
  • LibriVoxBooks read aloud by volunteers; check local public-domain rules.
  • Directory of Open Access BooksScholarly books with no paywall.

Reference

  • WikipediaThe general shelf. Start anywhere.
  • Wikipedia, ScienceThe STEM door.
  • WikisourceSource texts, not articles about them.
  • Stanford Encyclopedia of PhilosophyLong, signed entries. A real reference work.
  • World Factbook historyThe CIA retired this publication in February 2026. Its farewell describes the former reference; it is not a current country-data service.
  • Our World in DataNumbers about how people live, with the sources attached.

Science

  • arXivPreprints in physics, math, computer science, and more.
  • PubMedThe medical literature, abstracts free, many papers open.
  • Directory of Open Access JournalsJournals that do not charge the reader.
  • Biodiversity Heritage LibraryCenturies of writing about life on Earth.
  • NCBI BookshelfBiomedical books from the National Library of Medicine.
  • NASA Astrophysics Data SystemThe astronomy literature.
  • OpenStaxFree textbooks, mostly college STEM.
  • MIT OpenCourseWareCourse materials left open on purpose.

History and records

  • Library of Congress digital collectionsMaps, photos, newspapers, papers.
  • Chronicling AmericaHistoric American newspapers.
  • National Archives CatalogThe U.S. government’s records.
  • Digital Public Library of AmericaLibraries and archives across the country, one search.
  • EuropeanaEurope’s museums, libraries, and archives.
  • GallicaThe Bibliothèque nationale de France.
  • British Library collection storiesStories and highlights from the collections.

Earth, sky, and making

  • U.S. Geological SurveyMaps, water, quakes, and the ground under you.
  • NOAAWeather, oceans, and climate records.
  • NASA Image and Video LibraryThe public picture of the agency.
  • NASA SciencePlanets, Earth, and the missions.
  • ESAEurope’s space agency, in public.
  • Smithsonian Open AccessMillions of assets designated CC0; check each item’s rights and any separate restrictions.
  • Wikimedia CommonsPictures, sound, and video with item-specific licenses; keep required attribution.
  • IMSLPSheet music; access and public-domain status vary by country.
Built as a shelf, not a subscription. External sites keep their own licenses. Some lend a copy only while you are signed in, for free. Pirate libraries are not on this list.

The shelf itself works offline; opening these external resources needs internet. Some resources are advanced, and access rules vary.
