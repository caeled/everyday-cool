🎓 Phoenix Study Shelf

Free, authoritative reading for Steven's first four Master of Information Systems courses at the University of Phoenix (start December 15, 2026). Every link checked live on November 23, 2026. No paywalls, no sign-ups — the primary sources themselves.

IT Infrastructure

CMGTCB/554 — telecommunications & networks, the Internet, server and storage architectures, regulatory considerations

  • Information Systems for Business and Beyond (Open Textbook Library) — a free full textbook; its infrastructure and networking chapters are the plain-language backbone for this course.
  • Companion exhibits in this collection: Packet Playground — Route It (how data travels the network) and the new Servers & Storage — The Back Room (where data lives: RAID, tiers, and why backups are not RAID).

Systems Analysis and Development

CMGTCB/555 — analysis & design techniques, the Software Development Life Cycle, Waterfall and Agile

  • Systems Analysis and Design (Wikibooks) — a free textbook-length walk through the SDLC, requirements, and design, chapter by chapter.
  • Manifesto for Agile Software Development — the 2001 primary source itself: four values, twelve principles, one page. Read the original before anyone's summary of it.
  • Atlassian Agile Guide — a clear, practical explainer of sprints, backlogs, and ceremonies, useful when the textbook gets abstract.
  • Companion exhibit in this collection: Two Roads — Waterfall vs Agile — run the same project both ways and watch what a mid-project change costs each one.

Enterprise Models

CMGTCB/556 — organizational support systems, strategic metrics, digital transformation, enterprise software selection

  • Microsoft Cloud Adoption Framework — a real enterprise transformation playbook, free from Microsoft: strategy, plan, governance, and how organizations actually decide.
  • DORA — the research program behind the famous delivery metrics (deployment frequency, lead time, change failure rate, recovery time) — what "strategic use of metrics" looks like with receipts.
  • Information Systems for Business and Beyond — its chapters on enterprise systems, CRM/ERP, and organizational decision-making map straight onto this course.

IT Architecture in the Age of Cloud

BSACB/531 — cloud solution architecture: resilient, performant, secure, cost-optimized, operationally excellent

  • NIST SP 800-145 — The NIST Definition of Cloud Computing — the two-page definition the whole industry quotes. If a vendor's "cloud" doesn't match this, it isn't.
  • AWS Well-Architected Framework — the pillars in the course description — operational excellence, security, reliability, performance, cost optimization — come from here.
  • Microsoft Azure Well-Architected Framework — the same pillars from the other big cloud, with checklists and reviews you can actually run.
  • Companion exhibit in this collection: The Cloud Balancing Act — push the resilience slider up and watch the cost follow; architecture is trade-offs.
