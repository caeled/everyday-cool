# AI Collaborators Briefing

One briefing for every collaborator on Everyday Cool — Muse, ChatGPT,
Copilot, future AI systems, and humans working alongside them. It is
deliberately model-neutral: nothing here depends on a single vendor's
tools, memory, or conventions. If you are an AI assistant picking up this
repository cold, read this file first, then [README.md](README.md),
[CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), and [MAINTAINING.md](MAINTAINING.md).

When a decision changes how this project works, record it in the
Historical Decisions Log below so the next collaborator inherits the
reasoning, not just the result.

## Mission and Vision

Everyday Cool is a free collection of portable workshops for children,
curious adults, and anyone who likes finding out how things work. It is
curated by Steven Powell and built with AI assistance, human curiosity,
and review.

Every workshop, whatever its subject, teaches how a system works: take
something apart, try it, and see the moving parts.

The vision is a curated home for the current collection and future
additions that anyone can open in a browser or carry away in a folder —
free learning that belongs to everyone, and that keeps growing toward a
curriculum and workshop distribution platform (see Known Future Goals).

## Project Principles

- **Free learning, always.** The official collection is free to access:
  no subscriptions, prompt-pack sales, ads, affiliate funnels, or required
  paid AI accounts.
- **Teach through doing.** Additions teach something through exploration,
  explanation, or practice — a concrete activity plus an understandable
  explanation.
- **Honest about models.** Label simulations; distinguish a toy model from
  a real-world prediction; identify simplifications.
- **Credit and provenance.** Sources are attributable, changes to
  educational material are identified, and origins are recorded (see
  Provenance Rules).
- **Human curation.** Steven Powell is the maintainer and final curator.
  Contributions — human or AI-drafted — are proposals, never automatic
  publication. AI assistance is disclosed, and AI-assisted material is
  checked by a human before it is proposed.
- **Privacy by default.** No accounts, no tracking, no personal data in
  examples. Prefer local processing; explain any external request.
- **Portability.** The collection works from a downloaded folder, offline
  wherever practical, as plain static files with no build framework.

## User Needs

- **Children and curious adults** who want to explore computing, robotics,
  weather, codes, math, and space without permission, payment, or an
  account.
- **Educators** running workshops in classrooms, clubs, and libraries,
  including on shared machines and school networks (see
  [educators.html](educators.html)).
- **Homeschool families** learning at a kitchen table, often with mixed
  ages and one computer (see [homeschool.html](homeschool.html)).
- **Low-connectivity users** who download the collection once and carry
  it; core activities must not silently require the internet.
- **Learners with accessibility needs**, served by the standards below.
- **Contributors and authors** who need clear rules for proposing work
  (see [WORKSHOP-AUTHOR-GUIDE.md](WORKSHOP-AUTHOR-GUIDE.md)).

## Repository Architecture

- `catalog.json` is the **source of truth** for the collection: one entry
  per project with its id, title, topic, hook, description, starter
  activity, entry point, original addresses, and download.
- `index.html` is **generated** by `tools/build-hub.py` from the catalog.
  Change the catalog or the generator, then regenerate — do not hand-edit
  the landing page without regenerating it, or the two will drift.
- `assets/hub.css` and `assets/hub.js` style and power the hub. The front
  page works without JavaScript; search and topic filters enhance it
  locally.
- `learning-paths.html`, `educators.html`, and `homeschool.html` are
  hand-written hub pages sharing the same stylesheet and navigation.
- `projects/<id>/` holds a self-contained copy of each workshop with its
  own `LICENSE`, `LICENSE-CONTENT.md`, `README.md`, and `COLLECTION.md`.
  These are independent source snapshots, **copied, not moved**, from the
  maintained originals; they do not silently update each other.
- `provenance/<id>.json` records each copy's origin (see Provenance
  Rules).
- `downloads/` holds per-project ZIPs generated by
  `tools/build-packages.py`, which also writes the complete portable
  archive beside the collection folder.
- `tools/check-collection.py` validates catalog links, bundled licenses,
  ZIP contents, and snapshot provenance. It inspects files and never
  executes contributed scripts.
- `templates/workshop/README.md` is the skeleton every new workshop
  starts from.
- The site is published by GitHub Pages from `main` / root. `.nojekyll`
  serves the static files with no framework build.

## Documentation Standards

- The README is the portable guide: it must stay accurate for someone
  holding only the downloaded folder.
- Process lives in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), curation in
  [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md), collection mechanics in
  [MAINTAINING.md](MAINTAINING.md), authoring in
  [WORKSHOP-AUTHOR-GUIDE.md](WORKSHOP-AUTHOR-GUIDE.md).
- Write plainly and warmly, in the collection's voice, and state honest
  boundaries (for example: this is not an accredited school).
- A catalog change, a provenance change, and the change itself land
  together, reviewed. If you modify a copied project file, record the
  intentional change in that project's `COLLECTION.md`.
- Keep this briefing current: new standing decisions go in the log below.

## Accessibility Standards

From [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), applied to every page and
workshop:

- Readable navigation, full keyboard access, visible focus, and labels.
- Sufficient contrast; meaning never carried by color alone.
- No compulsory motion or autoplay; honor reduced-motion preferences.
- Captions or an accessible alternative for essential media.
- Plain language wherever the subject allows it.

Accessibility improvements are first-class contributions, and reported
barriers are handled as corrections.

## Provenance Rules

From [MAINTAINING.md](MAINTAINING.md):

- `provenance/<id>.json` records original Git blob hashes and the source
  commit for repository imports.
- Author-supplied files use `format: sha256`, a source-file digest, and
  individual file digests; no Git commit or original history is claimed
  for them.
- Refresh a recorded upstream commit and provenance file only after
  verifying the new source. Never overwrite a collection copy with an
  upstream change without comparison and review.
- If a copied file is modified, the change must be listed in that
  project's `COLLECTION.md`; the checker reports unrecorded changes.
- A source record is an origin record, not a claim that future changes
  are byte-identical.

## Review Checklist

Before proposing or merging any change:

- [ ] The learning outcome is stated, and the change teaches through
      exploration, explanation, or practice.
- [ ] Any AI-assisted material was reviewed for accuracy by a human, and
      material AI assistance is disclosed.
- [ ] The learning experience is free to access, with no sales funnel,
      ads, affiliate links, or required paid accounts.
- [ ] Sources are accurate and attributable; simulations are labeled.
- [ ] Licenses and third-party permissions are documented, and no
      existing license was changed.
- [ ] Keyboard access, labels, contrast, and motion/media behavior were
      checked.
- [ ] Offline and internet requirements are stated honestly; external
      requests are explained.
- [ ] No private data, credentials, or tracking are included.
- [ ] `catalog.json`, packages, and this briefing's decision log were
      updated where the change requires it, and
      `python tools/check-collection.py` was run when a bundled project
      or the catalog changed.

## Historical Decisions Log

- **2026-10-05 — Everyday Cool launched.** Steven Powell published the
  collection as a curated home for free educational workshops; the first
  catalog is recorded in `catalog.json` (collection dated 2026-10-06).
- **At launch — Free licensing model.** Original hub code is MIT and
  original educational writing is CC BY 4.0; bundled projects keep their
  own licenses. The official collection stays free to access as a matter
  of policy. That policy governs what we publish; it does not restrict
  the license rights MIT and CC BY 4.0 grant anyone, including commercial
  reuse, and independent forks are not automatically endorsed.
- **At launch — Fork-and-contribute model.** Anyone may fork and publish
  under the applicable licenses. Inclusion in the official collection
  happens only through a reviewed pull request; Steven decides. There is
  no automatic merging, unreviewed ingestion, or contributor-controlled
  publishing.
- **At launch — Copies, not moves.** Existing projects were copied into
  `projects/` with provenance records; the originals remain maintained at
  their original addresses, and copies are reconciled deliberately,
  never by automatic sync.
- **2026-10-06 — Author-supplied additions.** Space Camp / For Christa
  and A Library were supplied by Steven as files and added with SHA-256
  origin records; no original Git history is claimed for those uploads.
- **At launch — Static, generated hub.** The landing page is generated
  from `catalog.json` by `tools/build-hub.py` and served as static files
  by GitHub Pages — no framework, no build pipeline, no accounts.
- **2026-10-31 — Paths, guidance, and author documentation.** Learning
  Paths (Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced), educator and homeschool
  guidance pages, the Workshop Author Guide, and this model-neutral
  collaborators briefing were added, so people and AI systems share one
  set of standards.
- **2026-10-31 — Learning-path levels are editorial, and a known check
  failure is pre-existing.** `catalog.json` carries no level metadata;
  the Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced groupings on the Learning Paths
  page are editorial suggestions ("a route, not a gate"), not catalog
  facts — do not treat them as schema. Separately, `tools/check-collection.py`
  reports a `.gitignore` change and hash mismatches on imported project
  files, but it fails identically on a pristine checkout of upstream, so
  it predates the 2026-10-31 change set; the provenance records were left
  untouched pending a deliberate reconciliation (see "Copies, not moves").
- **2026-10-31 — Decisions Everywhere added as a flagship workshop.**
  Steven Powell authored the workshop from a design brief prepared by
  Copilot; the full brief is preserved as
  `projects/decisions-everywhere/DESIGN.md`, and the workshop was
  implemented by Muse. Eight interactive rooms teach the decision cycle
  (SEE → QUESTION → TEST → DECIDE → REVIEW) that the rest of the
  collection quietly uses, with field missions and an educator guide. It
  enters the catalog under the new topic "Thinking & decisions" (with a
  matching hub filter) and leads the Beginner learning path, because
  decision skills are the entry skill for every other workshop.

## Known Future Goals

- **Grow from a collection into a curriculum and workshop distribution
  platform**: the direction set in Steven's repository recommendations —
  Everyday Cool as the educational content platform, with paths and
  guidance as the first steps.
- **Grow the collection**: new subjects and workshops, developed here or
  proposed through the fork-and-contribute model.
- **Deepen the paths**: richer learning paths, and continued educator and
  homeschool support as the catalog grows.
- **Widen access**: translations and accessibility improvements are
  explicitly welcomed contributions.
- **Keep collaboration model-neutral**: one shared briefing and decision
  log, so Muse, ChatGPT, Copilot, and future systems can all contribute
  under the same standards.
