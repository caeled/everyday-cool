How this started

The whole thing started with a question from a friend. From Nerd Heaven, and a few apps built frankly out of both curiosity and bordem. An earlier set of playing around had even been incorported in a couple of cases literally from a bordeom file. Then came the workshop ideas and deep dives into subjects as they suggested themselves usually relating or being lead to by others. The results no include rocketry, thrust, electronics and most recently math deep dives into some of the most vexing problems students and the teachers trying to teach them run into. The result is what you see today and continues to be built as the Wild Goose Flies.

— Steven Powell

THE PLUMBING / NEW · CATALOG 38

Play together, not at the same time.

The Baton Box is not a workshop — it is the collection’s relay server. It knows no games; it just carries the baton, so one player can set up a position and the next player inherits it, and any pass-and-play game can ride it. (It also serves the game folders with the isolation headers some exhibits need — it is how the DOS Console runs.) The Relay is its first passenger and the worked example: you don’t have to finish it. You have to set up the next person.

Everyday Home

A house as a system: what trips the breaker, where the water stops, and what to check before you call someone.

Three benches about living in a system. The breaker bench sums real appliance wattages on one 120 V, 15 A circuit — with the 80% planning line and an honest trip. The water bench rehearses the first move for four leaks: the fixture valve or the main shutoff, never the mop. The triage bench walks a silent furnace, a slow drain, a dead outlet, and a warm fridge through the free checks first, each ending in an honest found-it or call-now verdict. The safety lines are printed in its Help and outrank the benches.
